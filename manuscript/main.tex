\begin{abstract}
Can acceleration improve the condition-number dependence of local PageRank
without losing locality? We give deterministic and randomized algorithms
attaining the $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ running-time target for
$\ell_1$-regularized personalized PageRank posed by Fountoulakis and Yang at
COLT 2022. Here $\alpha$ is the lazy PageRank parameter, $\rho$ is the
regularization parameter, and optimization accuracy enters only
polylogarithmically. The graph is given by local adjacency-list access;
all discovery, repeated scans, arithmetic, state maintenance, and output are
charged, with no graph-wide preprocessing or supplied solution support.

Our deterministic algorithm combines regularization continuation with
accelerated corrections constrained by a degree-scaled box and a mass cap.
One energy proves accelerated convergence. A second energy, obtained from
the same recurrence in the PageRank metric, controls the response that can
activate vertices outside a small analytical comparison set. A signed-flow
argument then bounds cumulative scanned volume, including repeated visits.
A deterministic threshold data structure realizes this bound. We also give
a bounded-arithmetic realization with explicit encoding costs.

Our randomized algorithm instead solves a sequence of support-safe principal
systems. A block-Cholesky and Chebyshev argument bounds the number of
threshold batches by $\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$. Residual-certified SDD
solves yield a Las Vegas algorithm. Both methods imply
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ work for degree-normalized PPR
error $\epsppr$. The main results hold for point sources on finite connected
simple unweighted undirected graphs.
\end{abstract}
