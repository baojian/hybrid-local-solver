\section{Other pure-local acceleration failures and limitations}
\label{sec:signed-star-other-failures}

The FISTA star is not the only way an accelerated local method can lose.  Two
other proof-owned examples in this repository expose complementary costs.
They are included here as imports, with their original accuracy and algorithm
contracts preserved.

\subsection{Safe nested acceleration can pay a repeated-prefix tax}

Literal ASPR repeatedly solves an accelerated principal subproblem on a safe
active set, retracts to a lower solution, discovers new negative-gradient
coordinates, and restarts on the enlarged set.  This prevents the FISTA-type
spurious activation: every active set stays inside the true RPPR support.
Safety, however, does not prevent re-reading and re-solving the old prefix.

The standalone audit
\texttt{manuscript/notes/aspr23\_bound\_audit/} proves an endpoint-path
family on which exactly one new support coordinate is discovered per stage.
If the final support has $s$ vertices, stage $r$ solves a prefix of volume
$\Theta(r)$, and sufficiently accurate inner solves require
$\Omega(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ restricted-gradient iterations per stage.  Thus
\begin{equation}
  W_{\rm ASPR}
  =\Omega\!\left(
      \frac{s^2}{\sqrt\alpha}
    \right)
  \label{eq:signed-star-aspr-structural-lower}
\end{equation}
before the prescribed accuracy logarithm; fresh full-gradient discovery also
costs $\Omega(s^2)$.

The audit gives a literal same-tolerance separation by choosing
\begin{equation}
  \alpha=s^{-2},
  \qquad
  \rho=\frac\alpha{100},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_{\rm obj}=10^{-4}\alpha^2.
  \label{eq:signed-star-aspr-quantitative-family}
\end{equation}
On this RPPR path,
\begin{equation}
  W_{\rm ASPR}=\Omega(s^3\log s),
  \qquad
  W_{\rm FISTA}=O(s^2\log s).
  \label{eq:signed-star-aspr-fista-separation}
\end{equation}
So FISTA can be a factor $\Omega(s)$ faster on a full-support path, even
though FISTA loses arbitrarily badly on the tiny-support leaf-star of
\Cref{sec:signed-star-fista-failure}.  The two examples establish
incomparability:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the leaf-star punishes unsafe momentum and rewards support containment;
\item the path punishes nested restarts and rewards continuation of the
      accelerated state.
\end{itemize}
The path theorem is about literal ASPR, not every safe active-set method.  A
method that transports an estimate sequence across support expansions is
outside that lower bound.

\subsection{Accelerated outer stages do not by themselves bound local work}

The source AESP schedule performs $\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ accelerated
outer stages, each calling a local inner procedure.  The proof-owning note
\texttt{manuscript/notes/aesp\_locgd\_star\_lower\_bound/} specializes the
literal batched LocGD inner method to a center-seeded star.  Symmetry makes
every nonempty batch scan the entire active star.  Its source-compatible
theorem assumes
\begin{equation*}
 0<\alpha<\frac12,
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon_{\rm AESP}\leq\frac18,
 \qquad B=\left\lfloor\frac1{4\varepsilon_{\rm AESP}}\right\rfloor,
\end{equation*}
and gives
\begin{equation}
  W_{\rm AESP\text{-}LocGD}
  =\Omega\!\left(
      \frac{1}{\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm AESP}}
    \right)
  \label{eq:signed-star-aesp-locgd-lower}
\end{equation}
in the note's AESP accuracy and active-volume work model.

This is not a lower bound above the desired product scale and therefore does
not refute acceleration.  Its role is diagnostic: multiplying an accelerated
outer-stage count by an unproved notion of ``local inner work'' is invalid.
One must charge the volume of every actual inner batch.  The result also does
not cover the sequential LocAPPR inner method or arbitrary local inner
oracles.

\subsection{Classical monotone baselines fail for a different reason}

Under their own namespaces, the active manuscript proves two closely related
tightness results:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item lazy APPR has worst-case degree work
      $\Theta(1/(\alpha\varepsilon_{\rm appr}))$;
\item fixed-relative-accuracy residual-thresholded coordinate ISTA has
      worst-case work $\Theta(1/(\alpha\rho))$ for RPPR.
\end{itemize}
These results are consistent with
\Cref{thm:signed-star-monotone-lower}, but their tolerances are not identified
with $\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}$.  They show algorithm-specific tightness for
standard conservative propagation; they are not lower bounds against signed
relaxation, FISTA, block solves, or response methods.

\subsection{A strong certificate can fail even when the algorithm succeeds}

The residual-certificate behavior in
\eqref{eq:signed-star-certificate-log} deserves to be listed as a failure mode
in its own right.  If one defines the algorithm to continue until
$|r|\le\gamma_\alpha\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}d$, then optimal SOR really does
perform
\begin{equation}
  \Theta\!\left(
    \frac{\log(1/\alpha)}
         {\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}}
  \right)
  \label{eq:signed-star-certificate-work}
\end{equation}
work on this star.  The semantic theorem does not invalidate that statement;
it analyzes a different, predetermined stopping policy.  What is refuted is
the inference that certificate-stopped work is a lower bound on semantic
accuracy.  In signed dynamics, cancellation can make the solution error small
while coordinate residual magnitudes remain comparatively large.

\subsection{Why elimination is a distinct rung}

The star also prevents an overbroad lower-bound interpretation.  Once the
entire star has been exposed, its two-by-two aggregate system can be solved
exactly and materialized in $O(m)=O(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$ work,
independent of $\alpha$.  This does not contradict
\Cref{thm:signed-star-signed-lower}: an exact aggregate response is not a
one-coordinate relaxation.  It uses a stronger primitive whose cost must be
charged separately.

The three separately defined special-case regimes are therefore
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Theta(1/(\alpha\varepsilon))}_{
      \text{nonnegative one-hop}}
  \quad\text{vs.}\quad
  \underbrace{\Theta(1/(\sqrt\alpha\varepsilon))}_{
      \text{signed dissipative one-hop}}
  \quad\text{vs.}\quad
  \underbrace{\Theta(1/\varepsilon)}_{
      \text{full-star aggregate solve}}.
  \label{eq:signed-star-three-rungs}
\end{equation}
The comparison symbols are not class-containment arrows:
$\mathcal M_+$ and $\mathcal R_\pm$ are incomparable as defined, while the
aggregate solver uses a stronger primitive.  The genuinely nested signed
comparison is $\mathcal R_\pm^{\rm nn}\subset\mathcal R_\pm$ from
\Cref{sec:signed-star-monotone-failure}.  Passing to the aggregate solve is
not ``more momentum'' but a change in inverse primitive from local recurrence
to output-scale elimination.
