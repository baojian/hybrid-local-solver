# Repeated PeelingLC toward one fixed trial: a root-scale obstruction

Date: 2026-09-04

## Verdict

**Proved here.**  Repeating the support-linear `PeelingLC` pass toward the
same fixed safe-box trial does not reach the computable `ABoxStop` condition
in a universal constant or a polylogarithmic number of passes.  Already on
the canonical two-vertex unit graph, there is a positive supplied
single-source SafeBoxLC state at

```text
alpha=s^2,        s=1/m,
```

for which the first `m/32=Omega(1/sqrt(alpha))` repeated passes all fail

```text
(1+s) zeta <= A.
```

The graph, source, RPPR objective, outer state, fixed trial, and every
peeling event are explicit and rational.  This is stronger than a finite
two- or five-pass counterexample: the delay grows while the graph stays fixed
at two vertices.

There is also a general termination bound.  On a fixed canonical support of
size `k`, the number of failed repeated passes is strictly less than

```text
min{(1+s)sqrt(k)/s, (1+s)^2/s^2}.                (GenericPassBound)
```

Thus repeated peeling is finite and the two-vertex family is tight in its
`alpha` dependence for fixed dimension.  The bound is nevertheless much too
large to make refinement support-linear per outer product.

The scope is equally important.  The supplied state is a valid positive
canonical state, but it is not proved reachable from the prescribed
zero-start SafeBoxLC history.  One root-scale inner refinement can also fit
inside the *total* desired root-scale budget.  Therefore this theorem refutes
constant-pass and polylog-pass implementations of the proposed inner
shortcut; it is not an end-to-end lower bound and does not rule out an
across-outer-round amortization, signed persistent LCP state, or an exact
safe-box response primitive.

## Fixed canonical instance

Let `m>=256` be a multiple of 32, put `s=1/m` and `alpha=s^2`, and take the
simple connected graph consisting of one unit edge.  Both degrees are one,
so the canonical normalized PageRank matrix is

```text
Q = [ a  -b ],       a=(1+s^2)/2,
    [ -b  a ]        b=(1-s^2)/2.
```

Use source vertex zero and `rho=1/4`.  The canonical RPPR linear term and
strictly positive optimum are

```text
c = s^2(3/4,-1/4),
x_rho^*=(1/4+s^2/2, 1/4-s^2/2).
```

Let `eta=s^3` and prescribe the initial residual, current point, trial
correction, fixed trial, and estimate state by

```text
t_0 = c-Q x_0 = eta(4,0),
x_0 = x_rho^*-Q^(-1)t_0,
u   = eta(4/s,2/s),
y   = x_0+u,
z_0 = x_0+((1+s)/s)u.
```

Then

```text
y=(x_0+s z_0)/(1+s).
```

Moreover `x_0>0`, `x_0<=z_0`, and both states have full support.  Indeed,

```text
Q^(-1)t_0=4s(a,b),
```

which is coordinatewise smaller than `x_rho^*` for the stated range.  Thus
this is an exact canonical point-source SafeBoxLC input state, rather than an
arbitrary-load LCP.

Starting from `w_0=x_0`, repeatedly apply the literal event-driven peeling
pass from `w_k` toward the *same* `y`.  Set

```text
e_k=y-w_k,       t_k=c-Qw_k,       zeta_k=e_k^Tt_k,
```

and evaluate the original-round credit

```text
A_k=(1/2)||w_k-x_0||_Q^2
      +(s alpha/2)||z_0-w_k||_2^2.               (ABoxCredit)
```

## Exact two-event recurrence

### Lemma 1

For every pass covered by the bounds below, there are exactly two peeling
events.  There are positive scalars `E_(1,k),E_(2,k),r_k` such that

```text
e_k=(eta/s)(E_(1,k),E_(2,k)),
t_k=eta(r_k,0),
```

with initial values

```text
E_(1,0)=4,        E_(2,0)=2,        r_0=4.
```

Writing

```text
D_k=E_(1,k)-E_(2,k),
P_k=aD_k+s^2 E_(2,k),
N_k=bD_k-s^2 E_(2,k),
theta_k=s r_k/P_k,
lambda=b/a,
```

one pass gives

```text
E_(1,k+1)=(1-theta_k)E_(1,k),
E_(2,k+1)=E_(2,k)-theta_k lambda E_(1,k),
r_(k+1)=r_k lambda N_k/P_k.                      (TwoEventMap)
```

#### Proof

At the start of a pass, the two row pressures generated by the full
direction `e_k` are

```text
(eta/s)(P_k,-N_k).
```

The first residual therefore decreases from `eta r_k` and hits zero at time
`theta_k`; the second residual initially increases.  The first coordinate
then freezes.  The second coordinate continues alone and hits zero at total
time

```text
theta_k b E_(1,k)/(a E_(2,k)).
```

Substitution gives `(TwoEventMap)`.  The later motion of coordinate two
recreates a positive residual only in row one, so the same event orientation
recurs.  All comparisons are strict under Lemma 2.

## Root-scale failure of ABoxStop

### Theorem 2

Let `K=m/32`.  For every `1<=k<=K`, the repeated peeling output is an exact
feasible `(ABox)` pair but satisfies

```text
(1+s)zeta_k>A_k.                                 (RootStopFailure)
```

Hence at least `m/32=1/(32 sqrt(alpha))` peeling passes are required before
`ABoxStop` can possibly hold on this family.

#### Proof

The recurrence gives a short uniform induction.  As long as `D_k>=3/2`,

```text
theta_k <= 16s/3,
E_(1,k)-E_(1,k+1) <= 64s/3,
E_(2,k)-E_(2,k+1) <= 64s/3.
```

Also

```text
D_k-D_(k+1)
 =theta_k E_(1,k)(1-lambda)
 <=(128/3)s^3.
```

Summing for at most `1/(32s)` passes proves

```text
E_(1,k)>=10/3,       E_(2,k)>=4/3,
D_k>=2-(4/3)s^2>3/2.                              (StateBounds)
```

These inequalities close the induction, make `N_k>0`, and show that the
second event time is at most

```text
(16s/3)(E_(1,k)/E_(2,k)) <=16s<1.
```

Thus Lemma 1 applies throughout the claimed horizon.  Furthermore,

```text
P_k-N_k=s^2(D_k+2E_(2,k)),
lambda>=(1-2s^2),
N_k/P_k>=1-8s^2.
```

Bernoulli's inequality now gives

```text
r_k>=4(1-10s^2)^k
    >=4(1-10ks^2)
    >=15/4.                                       (ResidualBound)
```

It remains to compare the exact certificate units.  Put

```text
H_(1,k)=4-E_(1,k),       H_(2,k)=2-E_(2,k).
```

The preceding bounds give `0<=H_(i,k)<=2/3` and
`|H_(1,k)-H_(2,k)|<=4s^2/3`.  Diagonalizing the two-by-two matrix in its
common and difference modes yields

```text
A_k/eta^2
 = (H_(1,k)+H_(2,k))^2/4
   +(H_(1,k)-H_(2,k))^2/(4s^2)
   +||(4,2)+s(E_(1,k),E_(2,k))||_2^2/(2s)
 < 11/s.                                         (CreditUpper)
```

On the other hand, `(StateBounds)` and `(ResidualBound)` give

```text
zeta_k/eta^2=E_(1,k)r_k/s
             >=(10/3)(15/4)/s
             =25/(2s)>11/s.                      (GapLower)
```

Therefore `(1+s)zeta_k>A_k`, proving `(RootStopFailure)`.

## A general finite-pass upper bound

The lower bound above is not caused by nontermination.  A complementary
argument bounds the number of failed passes for every fixed canonical
support.

### Proposition 3

Let `S` be a fixed support with `k=|S|`, and repeatedly peel from a lower
point `x` toward one fixed trial `y`.  Put `u=y-x`, let `w_j` be the output
of pass `j`, and set

```text
e_j=y-w_j,        p_j=w_j-w_(j-1),
t_j=c_S-Q_SS w_j.
```

If the outputs of `N` consecutive passes all fail `ABoxStop`, then

```text
N < min{(1+s)sqrt(k)/s, (1+s)^2/s^2}.            (GenericPassBound)
```

In particular a successful certified output must occur after at most this
many failures plus one.

#### Proof

Write

```text
C=diag(Q_SS)-Q_SS
 =((1-s^2)/2)(D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2))_SS.
```

During one pass, let `tau_i` be the final fraction of the pass-start
direction moved in coordinate `i`.  If `e_(j,i)>0`, row `i` froze when its
residual was zero.  Only neighbors with `tau_l>tau_i` move afterward, so

```text
(t_j)_i
 =sum_l C_(i,l) (e_(j-1,l))(tau_l-tau_i)_+
 <=(C p_j)_i.
```

Coordinates with `e_(j,i)=0` contribute nothing to the complementarity gap.
The normalized adjacency has spectral norm at most one, also after principal
restriction, and hence `||C||_2<=(1-s^2)/2<=1/2`.  Therefore

```text
zeta_j=e_j^Tt_j
       <=e_j^TCp_j
       <=(1/2)||u||_2 ||p_j||_2.                 (MovementUpper)
```

The fixed linear-coupling relation gives

```text
z_0-w_j=u/s+e_j.
```

All three vectors are nonnegative.  If pass `j` fails `ABoxStop`, its second
credit term alone implies

```text
zeta_j>A_j/(1+s)
       >=s||u||_2^2/[2(1+s)].                    (FailureLower)
```

Combining `(MovementUpper)` and `(FailureLower)` yields

```text
||p_j||_2 > [s/(1+s)]||u||_2.                    (LargeMove)
```

The movements are coordinatewise nonnegative and telescope below `u`.
Consequently

```text
sum_j ||p_j||_2 <=sum_j ||p_j||_1
                 <=||u||_1<=sqrt(k)||u||_2,
sum_j ||p_j||_2^2<=||u||_2^2.
```

Applying `(LargeMove)` to the linear and squared ledgers gives the two terms
in `(GenericPassBound)`.

For `k=2`, Theorem 2 and Proposition 3 match up to an absolute constant:
the worst supplied-state dependence is `Theta(1/sqrt(alpha))`.  When the
support itself grows, the proposition only gives pass work

```text
O(vol(S) min{sqrt(|S|)/sqrt(alpha),1/alpha}),
```

before logarithmic implementation factors.  It is therefore a termination
theorem, not the desired support-linear inner primitive.

## What this changes in the proof search

The tempting refinement recipe

```text
one support-linear peel;
while ABoxStop fails, peel again toward the same trial
```

does preserve exact feasibility and eventually works on tested finite
instances.  It nevertheless cannot be treated as an `O(1)` or soft-logarithmic
replacement for the greatest-safe-box LCP.  A single fixed-support outer
round can spend the entire `Theta(1/sqrt(alpha))` outer product budget on
inner peeling, even when `M=2` and no graph discovery occurs.

This does **not** yet prove `Omega(M/alpha)` end-to-end work.  To obtain that
conclusion one would need either a reachable zero-start chronology with
root-scale refinement at root-scale many outer rounds, or a theorem showing
that the supplied state can be forced repeatedly without enough outer
Lyapunov credit.  Conversely, a positive route may still charge all failed
inner passes globally to the outer energy or retain a signed response that
does not restart peeling from its current feasible correction.
