\begin{abstract}
We isolate a short, modular alternative to transporting acceleration through
the changing support of regularized personalized PageRank.  Stage~I returns
either an accurate numerical point, a certified coordinate envelope, or a
low-energy inner RPPR face.  All momentum is then discarded.  Stage~II solves
one fixed restricted ordinary-PPR linear system by Chebyshev, conjugate
gradients, or accelerated gradient, but only when Stage~I returned a set
without an already accurate numerical point.  Envelope-linearization and a
new strong-convexity transfer show that this restricted
linear solution is already within the RPPR regularization scale of full PPR;
Stage~II need not solve another obstacle problem or identify the zero
coordinates inside the envelope.  If Stage~I uses work $W_{\rm disc}$ and
returns an envelope of degree
volume $V_E$, the complete point-source PPR work is
\[
 W_{\rm disc}+\widetilde O(V_E/\sqrt\alpha).
\]
Taking the RPPR regularization and terminal error both equal to half the PPR
target gives $V_E=O(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$.  Thus all numerical work after
discovery already has the desired
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}\sqrt\alpha))$ scale.  More sharply,
we prove that the numerical RPPR/APPR checkpoints returned by every concrete
Stage~I engine considered here are already valid PPR outputs; on them the
second stage is redundant.  A deterministic benchmark confirms that forcing
the optional tail costs roughly five times direct APPR work at equal accuracy.
We also give a complete set-only alternative: a hard-capped point-source
Green-ball screen exposes one certified ball, solves one principal system,
and races fairly against direct APPR.  Its successful branch has the target
product scale; cap failure loses only a constant factor to APPR.  Across 90
high-accuracy deterministic cases the standalone lane wins 22, including
stars, binary trees, a grid, a path, and a cycle.  Charging the concurrent
APPR lane preserves 15 strict wins.  The strongest non-star standalone ratio
is $0.103$.
On certified fixed-treewidth envelopes, one sparse factorization and
backsolve improve the successful tail to $O(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$; this
structural version wins 50 of 56 cap-successful large-graph cases standalone
and 35 after charging the fair APPR race.  Tuning the exact tail to use the
full truncation budget raises cap success to 56 of 75 cases.
For literal support identification, coarse APPR snapshots and
positive-residual SOR proposals are raced against the same direct lane.
Returning the exact RPPR verifier point gives four and five fair-race wins.
When a separate fresh ordinary-PPR face solve is charged to enforce a strict
two-stage execution, two APPR-proposal and all five SOR-proposal wins remain;
every failure stays within the factor-two fallback bound.
On trees, the point-source threshold-message reporter closes Stage~I
unconditionally.  A direct implementation certifies all $60$ large test
instances; among $51$ nontrivial direct comparisons it wins $27$ standalone
and $14$ after charging the APPR race.  A literal set-only then ordinary-PPR
version has the same counts: its exact fixed-face solve replaces rather than
duplicates RPPR recovery, and permits the tuned split $\rho=\varepsilon$.
Classical APPR remains the practical deterministic baseline; exact response
reporters attain the target on trees and several wider structural classes.
A companion threshold-batch theorem now closes the arbitrary-graph
product-work requirement in an exact-real randomized word model.  Its final
face may be a strict subset of the RPPR support: a new strong-convexity
transfer shows that low face-energy gap is already an approximate-envelope
certificate for one ordinary principal-PPR solve.  The literal two-stage
algorithm and the direct RPPR return both use
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}\sqrt\alpha))$ expected fully charged
work.  Deterministic finite-precision complexity and a practical persistent
implementation remain open.  A fair certificate race combines the earlier
engines with two complementary numerical handoffs: signed AESP burn-in followed by
SOR, and lower-safe AESP mass completion followed by APPR/SOR.  For the latter
we prove that any local envelope whose principal PPR solution captures
$1-O(\sqrt\alpha)$ mass yields the desired two-stage rate after a safe
Stieltjes retraction; it needs neither $S^\star$ nor an exterior-amplitude
certificate.  The observable trigger and unconditional fallback remain
available.  At intermediate accuracy, the adaptive AESP--APPR lane also gives
strict wins: on a source-centered 96-vertex star it uses
$570$ charged degree work versus $3610$ for either direct APPR ordering at
$(\alpha,\varepsilon)=(0.01,0.005)$, and $570$ versus $950$ at
$(0.04,0.005)$.  This is structural rather than universal: after the star is
exposed, its source lies in a two-dimensional invariant subspace and exact
CG terminates in two matrix applications.  The simplification is therefore
real but sharply located: a
terminal fixed-face solve is useful only after a genuinely cheaper set-only
screen, while the AESP--APPR mass lane is a safe instance-selective
alternative.  A star barrier shows why it cannot replace numerical early
return universally at coarser targets.
\end{abstract}
