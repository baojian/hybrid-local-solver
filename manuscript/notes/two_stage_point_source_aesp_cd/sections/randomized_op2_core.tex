\section{Randomized arbitrary-graph closure}
\label{sec:random-core}

Put
\begin{equation*}
 c_\rho=b-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one,
 \qquad
 F_\rho(x)=\frac12x^\top Qx-c_\rho^\top x
 \quad(x\geq0).
\end{equation*}
The companion \path{active_edge_lcp} note supplies the missing
arbitrary-graph discovery mechanism in an exact-real randomized word model.
The regime $\rho\geq1/d_v$ has the exact zero RPPR solution and is recognized
by one seed-degree query; below we discuss the canonical nonzero regime.
It does not maintain an accelerated trajectory through every face.  Instead,
on the current reachable RPPR face $U$ it approximately solves
\begin{equation}
 Q_{UU}x_U^U=c_{\rho,U},
 \qquad x_i^U=0\quad(i\notin U),
 \label{eq:random-core-face}
\end{equation}
certifies the active residual exactly, and admits in one batch every boundary
row whose residual clears a declared threshold.  Every admitted row is
support-safe, so $U\subseteq S^\star$ and $\vol(U)\leq1/\rho$ throughout.

Its threshold-batch depth theorem orders the unknown support by these batches,
block-factorizes $Q_{S^\star S^\star}$, and retains only the diagonal and
first block subdiagonal of its scalar Cholesky factor.  The retained
block-bidiagonal M-matrix $\widehat C$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\widehat C^{-1}}_2\leq\frac1{\sqrt\alpha},
 \qquad \norm{\widehat C}_2\leq\sqrt2.
 \label{eq:random-core-conditioning}
\end{equation}
Chebyshev inverse decay on this block chain gives, after $J$ complete faces,
\begin{equation}
 F_\rho(x^{U_J})-F_\rho(x_\rho^\star)
 \leq 8q_\alpha^{2J}+\frac{\vartheta^2}{\alpha\rho},
 \qquad
 q_\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{2/\alpha}-1}{\sqrt{2/\alpha}+1}.
 \label{eq:random-core-depth}
\end{equation}
Thus the threshold
$\vartheta=\sqrt{\alpha\rho\varepsilon_{\rm obj}}/8$ needs only
$O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\varepsilon_{\rm obj}))$ rebuilt faces.  A fresh
nearly-linear randomized SDD solve \citep{koutis2011nearly} is accepted only
when an exact active-row scan certifies its residual; capped independent
retries make all randomness an explicit failure flag.  The proved source
theorem therefore returns, except with declared probability $\zeta$, a
reachable face whose exact face point, as well as its projected feasible
numerical point, has RPPR objective gap at most
$\varepsilon_{\rm obj}$, and uses
expected fully charged work
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
  \log\frac1{\varepsilon_{\rm obj}}
  \log\frac2\zeta
 \right).
 \label{eq:random-core-source-work}
\end{equation}
This includes every face construction, fresh solver state, scan, comparison,
projection, and output; no support, global preprocessing, or complementarity
margin is supplied.

The following transfer is what makes the result a literal two-stage theorem,
rather than only an RPPR theorem.

\begin{lemma}[A low-energy inner face is a set certificate]
\label{lem:random-core-inner-face}
Let $U$ be any reachable face and
$\Gamma_U=F_\rho(x^U)-F_\rho(x_\rho^\star)$.  For the ordinary principal-PPR
solution $u_U=Q_{UU}^{-1}b_U$ and its zero extension $\widetilde u_U$,
\begin{align}
 \max_{i\notin U}\frac{x_{\rho,i}^\star}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 &\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\Gamma_U}{\alpha}},
 \label{eq:random-core-amplitude}\\
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(x^0-\widetilde u_U)}_\infty
 &\leq\rho+\sqrt{\frac{2\Gamma_U}{\alpha}}.
 \label{eq:random-core-envelope}
\end{align}
In particular, $U$ need not contain $S^\star$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Strong convexity gives
$\Gamma_U\geq(\alpha/2)\norm{x^U-x_\rho^\star}_2^2$.
Because the padded face point vanishes outside $U$ and $d_i\geq1$, this
proves \cref{eq:random-core-amplitude}.  Approximate-envelope linearization
then proves \cref{eq:random-core-envelope}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Randomized two-stage point-source PPR]
\label{thm:random-core-two-stage}
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, choose $\rho,\eta,\tau>0$ with
$\rho+\eta+\tau\leq\varepsilon$ and set
$\varepsilon_{\rm obj}=\alpha\eta^2/2$.  If the seed-degree test certifies
zero RPPR, return zero and omit Stage~II; its PPR error is at most $\rho$.
Otherwise run the threshold-batch algorithm
with failure budget $\zeta/2$, retain only its final face $U$, and discard its
numerical point and all solver state.  Solve $Q_{UU}u_U=b_U$ independently to
Euclidean error at most $\tau$, with failure budget $\zeta/2$, project the
approximation onto the nonnegative orthant, and zero-pad it.  With failure
probability at most $\zeta$, the result is $\varepsilon$-accurate and has
expected fully charged work
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\log\frac1{\alpha\eta^2}
      \log\frac4\zeta
  +\frac1\rho\log\frac1{\sqrt\alpha\tau}\log\frac4\zeta
 \right).
 \label{eq:random-core-total-work}
\end{equation}
The equal split $\rho=\eta=\tau=\varepsilon/3$ is therefore
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha))$ on every graph in this model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a successful first stage,
\cref{lem:random-core-inner-face} bounds exact truncation error by
$\rho+\eta$.  The terminal Euclidean error contributes at most $\tau$ to the
normalized $\ell_\infty$ norm, and projection cannot increase its distance
from the nonnegative exact principal solution.

For the charged terminal solve, the exact principal solution has
$Q$-energy at most $\alpha$.  A randomized relative-energy call at tolerance
$\sqrt\alpha\tau$ is accepted only if the exact active-row residual is at
most $\alpha\tau$ in Euclidean norm.  Since $Q_{UU}\succeq\alpha I$, every
accepted call then has Euclidean error at most $\tau$, regardless of its
random coins.  The source SDD bound, capped retries, and
$\vol(U)\leq1/\rho$ give the second term in
\cref{eq:random-core-total-work}; the first is
\cref{eq:random-core-source-work}.
\end{proof}

The fastest execution does not require the restart.  With
$\rho=\varepsilon/2$ and
$\varepsilon_{\rm obj}=\alpha\varepsilon^2/8$, the feasible numerical point
already produced by the threshold-batch solver is $\varepsilon$-accurate by
strong convexity and the RPPR--PPR bridge.  It has the same
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha))$ work.  The literal Stage~II is
useful when the desired architecture requires a set-only handoff; it is not
an asymptotic necessity.

This closure is randomized and exact-real.  It does not prove that AESP-CD's
original momentum recurrence survives changing faces, nor does it supply a
deterministic finite-precision or coefficient-bit theorem.  What transfers to
the hybrid is the safe threshold admission, hard phase cap, exact residual
certification, and restart discipline.  The result is best regarded as a new
threshold-batched active-set lane in the AESP-CD portfolio.
