\section{The complementary AESP-burn-in--SOR handoff}
\label{sec:two-stage-alternative-handoff}

The certificate-first algorithm is not the only sensible two-stage order.
The provider note \path{hybrid_aesp_locsor} studies the reverse lane:
AESP first reduces a numerical potential, all momentum is discarded, and
local SOR finishes the PPR system without identifying $S^\star$.

For the smooth PPR quadratic, a coordinate SOR update at $u$ is
\begin{equation}
 x^+=x-\frac{2\omega}{1+\alpha}\nabla_u f(x)e_u,
 \qquad 0<\omega<2,
 \label{eq:two-stage-sor-step}
\end{equation}
and has exact decrease
\begin{equation}
 f(x)-f(x^+)
 =\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{1+\alpha}\nabla_u f(x)^2.
 \label{eq:two-stage-sor-decrease}
\end{equation}
If the tail updates only coordinates with
$|\nabla_u f(x)|\geq\alpha\epsppr\sqrt{d_u}$, then from any finite handoff
$x_J$ it terminates with the semantic PPR certificate and work
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm SOR}
 \leq
 \frac{(1+\alpha)(f(x_J)-f(x^0))}
 {\omega(2-\omega)\alpha^2\epsppr^2}.
 \label{eq:two-stage-sor-tail}
\end{equation}
For $\omega=1$, a handoff gap
$f(x_J)-f(x^0)\leq\alpha^{3/2}\epsppr/(1+\alpha)$ makes the tail work at most
$1/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr)$.

For a lower-safe publication, the same cleanup admits a sharper scalar
interface that directly models ``AESP-CD plus APPR.''

\begin{proposition}[Mass-completion handoff to APPR/SOR]
\label{prop:two-stage-aesp-appr-mass-handoff}
Let $z$ be a PPR subsolution:
\begin{equation*}
 0\leq z\leq x^0,
 \qquad r:=b-Qz\geq0.
\end{equation*}
Put
\begin{equation*}
 \mu(z):=1-\langle D^{1/2}\one,z\rangle.
\end{equation*}
Starting from $z$, repeatedly choose, in any fair order, only rows with
$r_i/\sqrt{d_i}>\alpha\epsppr$ and make their exact monotone coordinate
pushes.  Stop when none remains.  The terminal vector is semantically
$\epsppr$-accurate and the cleanup degree work obeys
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm clean}
 <\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\epsppr}\,\mu(z).
 \label{eq:two-stage-mass-completion-cleanup}
\end{equation}
In particular, a certified handoff
$\mu(z)\leq c\sqrt\alpha$ gives
$W_{\rm clean}=O(c/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The PPR mass identity and $QD^{1/2}\one=\alpha D^{1/2}\one$ give
\begin{equation*}
 M(z):=\langle D^{1/2}\one,r\rangle
 =\alpha\langle D^{1/2}\one,x^0-z\rangle
 =\alpha\mu(z).
\end{equation*}
Write $a=(1+\alpha)/2$.  An exact update at $i$ adds $r_i/a$ to $z_i$,
zeros $r_i$, and increases only neighbor residuals.  Thus nonnegativity is
preserved and the weighted residual mass decreases by
$(\alpha/a)\sqrt{d_i}r_i$.  While row $i$ is eligible this is larger than
$(\alpha^2\epsppr/a)d_i$.  Summing the charged degrees proves
\cref{eq:two-stage-mass-completion-cleanup}.  At termination,
\begin{equation*}
 0\leq x^0-z=Q^{-1}r
 \leq Q^{-1}(\alpha\epsppr D^{1/2}\one)
 =\epsppr D^{1/2}\one,
\end{equation*}
which is the semantic certificate.
\end{proof}

This proposition is strictly more informative than saying ``run SOR after a
finite AESP handoff.''  It names the exact scalar that the accelerated prefix
must reduce.  The scalar is observable from the maintained sparse checkpoint;
it needs no global residual scan.  A safe AESP retraction can publish the
required subsolution, but
the current arbitrary-graph theory does not prove that it reaches
$\mu(z)=O(\sqrt\alpha)$ with output-sensitive local work.  If that scalar
mass-completion theorem is proved, APPR-style FIFO cleanup and priority-SOR
cleanup both attain the desired tail scale without identifying $S^\star$.

In fact, a fixed local set need not contain $S^\star$ to make this handoff
work.  It need only capture almost all PageRank mass.

\begin{theorem}[Mass-capturing envelope gives an AESP--APPR hybrid]
\label{thm:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope}
Let $E$ be any connected coordinate set containing the seed, let
$V_E=\vol(E)$, and let $u_E=Q_E^{-1}b_E$.  Suppose that for some $c>0$,
\begin{equation}
 1-\langle D_E^{1/2}\one,u_E\rangle
 \leq\frac{c\sqrt\alpha}{2}.
 \label{eq:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope}
\end{equation}
Then signed Chebyshev/AESP scratch restricted to $E$, followed by an
a-posteriori Stieltjes lower retraction, publishes a global PPR subsolution
$z$ with $\mu(z)\leq c\sqrt\alpha$ after
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\frac{V_E}{\sqrt\alpha}
   \log_+\frac{(1+\sqrt{V_E})\sqrt{V_E}}
                    {c\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \label{eq:two-stage-mass-capture-work}
\end{equation}
degree work.  The APPR/SOR cleanup of
\cref{prop:two-stage-aesp-appr-mass-handoff} then costs
$O(c/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$.  No support-containment or exterior-amplitude
certificate is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $v_E=D_E^{1/2}\one$.  Principal Stieltjes monotonicity gives
$0\leq u_E\leq x_E^0$, so its weighted mass is at most one, and
$Q_Ev_E\geq\alpha v_E$.  Run the usual degree-$k$ Chebyshev residual
polynomial on $Q_Eu=b_E$, allowing signed scratch $\widehat u$.  For its raw
residual $r=b_E-Q_E\widehat u$, define
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta:=\max_i\left[\frac{-r_i}{\alpha(v_E)_i}\right]_+,
 \qquad z_E:=[\widehat u-\Delta v_E]_+,
\end{equation*}
and extend $z_E$ by zero.

Because $r+\Delta Q_Ev_E\geq0$, inverse positivity gives
$\widehat u-\Delta v_E\leq u_E$.  The Stieltjes sign pattern shows directly
that taking the positive part preserves the subsolution inequalities:
$b_E-Q_Ez_E\geq0$.  Every exterior row also has nonnegative residual because
its source load is zero and its couplings to $z_E$ are nonpositive.  Hence
$0\leq z\leq x^0$ and $z$ satisfies the hypotheses of
\cref{prop:two-stage-aesp-appr-mass-handoff}.

Since $(v_E)_i\geq1$ and $\norm{v_E}_2=\sqrt{V_E}$,
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{u_E-z_E}_2
 \leq\frac{1+\sqrt{V_E}}{\alpha}\norm{r}_2.
\end{equation*}
It is therefore enough to make the right-hand side at most
$c\sqrt\alpha/(2\sqrt{V_E})$.  The spectrum of $Q_E$ lies in $[\alpha,1]$,
so the Chebyshev residual bound reaches this target in the logarithmic number
of $1/\sqrt\alpha$ iterations displayed in
\cref{eq:two-stage-mass-capture-work}.  Cauchy--Schwarz and
\cref{eq:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope} then give
\begin{align*}
 1-\langle v_E,z_E\rangle
 &\leq 1-\langle v_E,u_E\rangle
      +\sqrt{V_E}\norm{u_E-z_E}_2\\
 &\leq c\sqrt\alpha.
\end{align*}
The cleanup bound is
\cref{eq:two-stage-mass-completion-cleanup}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A residual interval screens mass attempts]
\label{prop:two-stage-mass-attempt-screen}
Let $z$ be a PPR subsolution supported on $E$, put
$r_E=b_E-Q_Ez_E\geq0$, $v_E=D_E^{1/2}\one$, and
$m(z)=\langle v_E,z_E\rangle$.  Define the observable row ratios
\begin{equation*}
 q_E^-:=\min_{i\in E}\frac{(Q_Ev_E)_i}{(v_E)_i},
 \qquad
 q_E^+:=\max_{i\in E}\frac{(Q_Ev_E)_i}{(v_E)_i}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\alpha\leq q_E^-\leq q_E^+\leq(1+\alpha)/2$ and
\begin{equation}
 m(z)+\frac1{q_E^+}\langle v_E,r_E\rangle
 \leq \langle v_E,u_E\rangle
 \leq m(z)+\frac1{q_E^-}\langle v_E,r_E\rangle.
 \label{eq:two-stage-mass-attempt-interval}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the lower endpoint at least
$1-\sqrt\alpha/2$ certifies the uniform half-slack premise before running
signed scratch.  More generally, a strict lower endpoint above
$1-\sqrt\alpha$ certifies an instance-dependent positive trigger margin and
the scratch may refine to that margin.  The upper endpoint below
$1-\sqrt\alpha$ certifies that even the exact principal solution cannot
trigger the cleanup lane.  Both scalars can be maintained with the monotone
coordinate updates without a full fixed-envelope solve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $v_E=D_E^{1/2}\one$, the principal Stieltjes rows obey
\begin{equation*}
 q_E^- v_E\leq Q_Ev_E\leq q_E^+ v_E.
\end{equation*}
Inverse positivity therefore gives
$v_E/q_E^+\leq Q_E^{-1}v_E\leq v_E/q_E^-$.  Since
$u_E-z_E=Q_E^{-1}r_E$ and $Q_E$ is symmetric,
\begin{equation*}
 \langle v_E,u_E-z_E\rangle
 =\langle Q_E^{-1}v_E,r_E\rangle.
\end{equation*}
The claimed interval follows from $r_E\geq0$.
For the normalized PageRank matrix,
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{(Q_Ev_E)_i}{(v_E)_i}
 =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
  -\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\frac{\deg_E(i)}{d_i},
\end{equation*}
which proves the declared range and makes the extrema maintainable under a
support insertion with local degree updates and a lazy heap.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A universal rooted-ball mass certificate]
\label{thm:two-stage-rooted-ball-mass}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 \kappa_\alpha:=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
If $E$ contains every vertex at graph distance at most $R$ from the point
source, then its principal PPR solution satisfies
\begin{equation}
 1-\langle D_E^{1/2}\one,u_E\rangle
 \leq \kappa_\alpha^{R+1}.
 \label{eq:two-stage-rooted-ball-mass}
\end{equation}
For $0<\alpha<1$, the half-slack premise of
\cref{thm:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope} is therefore guaranteed once
\begin{equation}
 R\geq
 \left\lceil
  \frac{\log(2/\sqrt\alpha)}
       {\log((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha))}
 \right\rceil-1.
 \label{eq:two-stage-rooted-ball-radius}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=1$, the seed alone has unit mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $P=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$ and
$h=D^{-1/2}e_v$.  Since
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}(I-\kappa_\alpha P),
 \qquad
 \frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}=1-\kappa_\alpha,
\end{equation*}
the principal solution has the nonnegative walk expansion
\begin{equation*}
 u_E=(1-\kappa_\alpha)
 \sum_{t\geq0}\kappa_\alpha^t(P_E)^th_E.
\end{equation*}
Put $v_E=D_E^{1/2}\one$.  The scalar
$v_E^\top(P_E)^th_E$ is the mass of the normalized walk killed when it leaves
$E$; it lies in $[0,1]$.  No length-$t$ walk can leave a radius-$R$ ball when
$t\leq R$, so this scalar equals one for those terms.  Consequently
\begin{align*}
 1-v_E^\top u_E
 &=(1-\kappa_\alpha)
   \sum_{t\geq0}\kappa_\alpha^t
     \left(1-v_E^\top(P_E)^th_E\right)\\
 &\leq(1-\kappa_\alpha)
   \sum_{t\geq R+1}\kappa_\alpha^t
 =\kappa_\alpha^{R+1}.
\end{align*}
Solving the last inequality for a deficit $\sqrt\alpha/2$ proves
\cref{eq:two-stage-rooted-ball-radius}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hard-capped mass screen or APPR fallback]
\label{thm:two-stage-hard-capped-mass-screen}
Let an adjacency-local proposal rule expose nested connected envelopes
$E_0\subset E_1\subset\cdots$ containing the seed.  Suppose successive
retained volumes at least double, all exposure and bookkeeping through volume
cap $B$ cost $W_{\rm grow}(B)$, and no proposed row beyond the cap is
committed.  On each accepted envelope run the fixed-envelope scratch and
retraction of \cref{thm:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope} to its half-slack
residual target.  If the published checkpoint satisfies
\begin{equation*}
  \mu(z)\leq\sqrt\alpha,
\end{equation*}
complete it by APPR/SOR.  If no checkpoint passes before the cap, discard the
lane and run direct APPR from zero.  The algorithm is correct on every finite
graph.  Its successful-branch work is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
   W_{\rm grow}(B)
   +\frac{B}{\sqrt\alpha}\log_+\frac{B}{\alpha}
   +\frac{1}{\sqrt\alpha\epsppr}
 \right),
 \label{eq:two-stage-hard-capped-mass-success}
\end{equation}
and the failed branch adds only the direct-APPR
$O(1/(\alpha\epsppr))$ fallback.  Thus with
$B=C/\epsppr$ and $W_{\rm grow}(B)=\widetilde O(B)$, every successful trace
has $\widetilde O(C/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$ work, while every failed trace
retains the classical unconditional guarantee.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each fixed-envelope publication is a global PPR subsolution.  Therefore the
observable mass trigger invokes
\cref{prop:two-stage-aesp-appr-mass-handoff} and proves the successful output
and cleanup term.  If it never triggers, no candidate has been returned, so
zero-start direct APPR proves the failed output independently.

The work of one fixed-envelope attempt is
\begin{equation*}
 O\!\left(
  \frac{\vol(E_j)}{\sqrt\alpha}
  \log_+\frac{\vol(E_j)}{\alpha}
 \right).
\end{equation*}
Geometric growth gives
$\sum_j\vol(E_j)\leq2B$ and the logarithm is monotone, which yields
\cref{eq:two-stage-hard-capped-mass-success}.  The cap makes every unsuccessful
speculation a declared prefix rather than hidden global preprocessing.
\end{proof}

The trigger $\mu(z)\leq c\sqrt\alpha$ is fully observable and does not
require knowing whether \cref{eq:two-stage-mass-capturing-envelope} holds.
Thus a practical lane may grow hard-capped envelopes geometrically, restart
the fixed-envelope scratch, and switch the moment the mass test passes;
otherwise it falls back.  What remains open is an arbitrary-graph proof that
such a mass-capturing envelope of output-sensitive discovered volume is
reached with fully charged exposure work.  This is a weaker target than exact
support discovery on favorable instances and is the cleanest
AESP-CD--plus--APPR formulation found in this note.  It is not universal:
\cref{prop:two-stage-star-mass-barrier} shows that diffuse small coordinates
can make mass capture far stronger than the semantic $\ell_\infty$ target.

\paragraph{Envelope diagnostic.}
On the same six deterministic graph families, the smallest rooted ball whose
exact principal PPR mass leaves half-slack deficit
$\sqrt\alpha/2$ retains a median $0.195$ fraction of graph volume.  Ten of
eighteen graph--$\alpha$ cases retain at most one quarter, whereas five
require the full graph.  The median fractions at
$\alpha=0.01,0.04,0.16$ are respectively $0.662$, $0.232$, and $0.056$.
Paths, cycles, and the broom handle are favorable; the star and the
low-$\alpha$ binary tree are not.  This diagnostic uses exact principal
solves and makes no work claim.  It shows both why the mass interface can be
substantially weaker than support discovery and why blind ball growth cannot
close the arbitrary-graph theorem.

The executable hard-capped lane is more sobering.  With cap
$2/\epsppr$, geometric BFS envelopes, restarted signed CG scratch, the safe
publication above, and priority-APPR cleanup, it triggers in $17$ of $36$
cases and falls back in $19$.  On the $30$ cases where direct APPR does
nonzero work, its median ratio is $5.99$ (range $2.67$--$12.25$) with no
win; even successful traces have median $5.17$.  Every output remains within
$0.523\epsppr$.  Hence the theorem identifies a legitimate new interface,
but naive balls and restart-from-zero scratch do not supply the missing
speedup.  Better envelope proposals or response reuse must reduce the prefix
constant and repeated work.

Replacing blind balls by the support exposed by priority APPR, maintaining
\cref{eq:two-stage-mass-attempt-interval}, testing only when its lower endpoint
proves a positive trigger margin, warm-starting the scratch, and continuing
the same priority state on failure reduces the median ratio to the better
direct baseline to $1.00$ (range $1.00$--$1.64$ on nontrivial cases).  It
triggers in only $3$ of $36$ cases; their ratios are $1.00$, $1.00$, and
$1.11$, so the coarse sweep has no strict win.  A second sweep at
$\epsppr\in\{0.005,0.01,0.02\}$ changes that conclusion in a narrow but
informative regime.  It triggers in $20$ of $54$ cases, has median ratio
$1.00$, and produces two strict wins.  On the 96-vertex source-centered star,
the ratios at $\epsppr=0.005$ are $570/3610\simeq0.158$ for $\alpha=0.01$
and $570/950=0.60$ for $\alpha=0.04$.  The maximum semantic error over the
sweep is $0.748\epsppr$.  The scalar screen therefore removes almost all
speculative overhead and sometimes exposes a genuine fixed-envelope win,
but it remains conservative and instance-selective.

\begin{proposition}[Why the star can be a genuine two-stage win]
\label{prop:two-stage-star-two-krylov}
Let the point source be the center of an $n$-leaf star.  Once the full star is
exposed, the ordinary PPR system in degree-unscaled coordinates preserves
the two-dimensional subspace of vectors that are constant on the leaves.
Consequently exact-arithmetic CG from zero solves the full system in at most
two iterations, and a set exposure plus the fixed solve costs $O(n)$ degree
work.  The resulting PPR checkpoint has zero mass deficit, so the cleanup
stage is empty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $b=(1-\alpha)/2$.  In degree-unscaled coordinates
the matrix is $\widehat Q=aD-bA$ and the right-hand side is
$\alpha e_c$.  Both belong to the invariant subspace
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal U=\{y:y_\ell\text{ is common to all leaves}\},
 \qquad \dim\mathcal U=2.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, for center value $y_c$ and common leaf value $y_\ell$,
\begin{equation*}
 (\widehat Qy)_c=an y_c-bn y_\ell,
 \qquad
 (\widehat Qy)_\ell=ay_\ell-by_c.
\end{equation*}
CG therefore terminates in at most two iterations in exact arithmetic.
Scanning the star and applying its matrix a constant number of times costs
$O(\vol(G))=O(n)$.  The exact full solution has weighted mass one, hence no
residual-mass cleanup remains.
\end{proof}

This proposition explains rather than contradicts the next barrier.  A
source-centered star is bad for support-sensitive mass capture because almost
every leaf may be needed, but it is excellent for a fixed solve because the
relevant minimal polynomial has degree two.  The high-accuracy win occurs
when the cap permits the whole star and direct coordinate pushes revisit the
same two global modes many times.  At looser targets the direct solver often
stops before exposure, and the same two-stage lane loses.

The direct PPR leakage gate of
\cref{prop:two-stage-leakage-certificate} was also run on the 54 high-accuracy
cases.  It certifies 44 envelopes and keeps every semantic error below
$0.566\epsppr$, including the same two star wins at ratios $0.184$ and
$0.700$.  Its median nontrivial ratio is nevertheless $7.52$ (maximum
$22.79$): restarting a fixed solve on every geometric BFS ball is much more
expensive than the adaptive scalar mass screen.  This comparison cleanly
separates the two issues.  The leakage certificate removes RPPR support
discovery from correctness, but a competitive arbitrary-graph proposal and
response-reuse mechanism is still required for work.

Using the monotone APPR checkpoint directly gives a much cheaper realization
of the same certificate.  The two-heap gate
\cref{eq:two-stage-two-heap-leakage-gate} is queried only when exposed support
volume doubles; on success, one warm-started fixed CG solve completes the
point.  On the same 54 high-accuracy cases this literal
``APPR/SOR discovery, AESP/CG solve'' lane switches only four times, all on
the star.  Its median nontrivial ratio is $1.061$ (range
$0.158$--$2.007$), and the two strict wins cost $572/3610$ and $572/950$.
The two units beyond the mass lane are the explicitly charged heap queries.
Thus conservative observability is beneficial here: it prevents almost all
speculative fixed solves while retaining the low-minimal-polynomial wins.

\begin{proposition}[A star barrier for mass capture]
\label{prop:two-stage-star-mass-barrier}
Let the point source be the center of a star with $n$ leaves, and put
$a=(1+\alpha)/2$, $b=(1-\alpha)/2$.  If an envelope contains the center and
exactly $k$ leaves, then its exact principal PPR solution has mass deficit
\begin{equation}
 \mu_E=\frac{ab(n-k)}{a^2n-b^2k}.
 \label{eq:two-stage-star-mass-deficit}
\end{equation}
For any $\eta<b/a$, the condition $\mu_E\leq\eta$ forces
\begin{equation}
 \frac{k}{n}\geq
 \frac{a(b-\eta a)}{b(a-\eta b)}.
 \label{eq:two-stage-star-mass-fraction}
\end{equation}
Meanwhile the full PPR vector in degree-unscaled coordinates has center value
$a/n$ and leaf value $b/n$.  Thus for $n\geq a/\epsppr$ the zero vector is
already semantically $\epsppr$-accurate, while a small-$\eta$ mass certificate
may still require nearly every leaf.  In particular, mass capture cannot be
a universal output-volume substitute for the numerical early-return lane.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By symmetry, the principal solution has center value $y_c$ and common
retained-leaf value $y_\ell$.  Its equations are
\begin{equation*}
 an y_c-bk y_\ell=\alpha,\qquad
 ay_\ell-by_c=0.
\end{equation*}
Hence
$y_c=\alpha a/(a^2n-b^2k)$ and $y_\ell=(b/a)y_c$.
The weighted mass in degree-unscaled coordinates is
$ny_c+ky_\ell$.  Using $a^2-b^2=\alpha$ gives
\cref{eq:two-stage-star-mass-deficit}; rearrangement gives
\cref{eq:two-stage-star-mass-fraction}.  Setting $k=n$ yields the stated full
PPR coordinates.  Their maximum is $a/n$.
\end{proof}

This route has different strengths and weaknesses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item it requires no support certificate and remains correct after a signed
  handoff;
\item its final SOR phase is safe and completely charged;
\item the graph-uniform local work of the early AESP phase remains open;
\item its black-box balance gives an instance-dependent
  $\alpha^{-3/4}$-type rate rather than automatically reaching the target.
\end{itemize}

The two lanes are complementary.  A set-only discovery lane isolates graph
exposure and makes an accelerated numerical tail routine.  A numerical
discovery lane normally returns early by
\cref{thm:two-stage-screen-or-solve}.  The burn-in-first lane avoids exact
discovery but asks AESP to remain local before the SOR handoff.
Including both in the fair race makes this complementarity algorithmic rather
than a pre-run guess.

\paragraph{Why the monotone lane does not become accelerated by a cosmetic
momentum term.}
The provider result
\texttt{prop:aesp-cd-positive-polynomial-stop} proves that any residual
polynomial that preserves the nonnegative cone for every nonnegative
transition matrix has nonnegative coefficients.  At a slow eigenvalue
$1-1/\mathcal K$, such a degree-$k$ polynomial is no smaller than
$(1-1/\mathcal K)^k$ and therefore needs
$\Omega(\mathcal K\log(1/\varepsilon))$ degree.  Square-root acceleration
must allow signed scratch.  In turn, signed scratch needs either a fixed
envelope plus a safe publication map, or a separately charged exposure and
retraction theorem.  This explains the architecture: monotone SOR is the
unconditional discovery/cleanup baseline, while AESP or Chebyshev is used
only where its signed state has a certified locality contract.

\begin{proposition}[Support versus numerical handoff certificates]
\label{prop:two-stage-handoff-dichotomy}
An implementation may safely switch when either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item a support/envelope certificate from
  \cref{def:two-stage-face-certificate,def:two-stage-envelope-certificate,def:two-stage-approximate-envelope}
  is available, possibly via \cref{prop:two-stage-gap-to-envelope}; a
  target-accurate numerical point returns immediately, while a set-only
  certificate invokes the ordinary principal PPR system; or
\item an objective-gap or weighted-residual-mass certificate for
  \cref{eq:two-stage-sor-tail} is available, in which case it discards
  momentum and uses local SOR.
\end{enumerate}
Neither switch needs to infer the other certificate.  Taking the first
certified switch preserves correctness, and fair scheduling gives the
constant-factor best-of bound of
\cref{thm:two-stage-certificate-race} when each full lane is treated as an
interruptible engine.
\end{proposition}
