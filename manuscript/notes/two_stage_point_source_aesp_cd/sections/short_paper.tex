\begin{abstract}
We give an at-most-two-stage framework for point-source personalized
PageRank.  A hard-capped first stage either returns an accurate numerical
point, certifies a coordinate envelope, or publishes a lower-safe point with
small residual mass.  In the set-only branch one fixed principal PageRank
system is accelerated; in the mass branch APPR/SOR completes the residual.
All momentum is discarded at the handoff and direct APPR is an unconditional
fallback.  Point-source Green decay yields a particularly short set-only
lane: expose one certified rooted ball, solve it once, and race this lane
against APPR.  On 90 high-accuracy deterministic instances this lane succeeds
in 85 and strictly beats the better direct baseline in 22 standalone.  After
charging the concurrent APPR lane, 15 remain strict wins.  Its strongest
non-star standalone ratio is $0.103$.
On certified fixed-treewidth envelopes, sparse elimination strengthens the
successful branch to $O(1/\varepsilon)$.  With the exact-tail budget tuned,
56 of 75 balls pass the cap, 50 win standalone, and 35 remain wins after the
fair APPR charge.
Coarse APPR snapshots and positive-residual SOR also give exact-support
races.  Returning the exact verifier point gives four and five fair-race
wins.  Charging a separate fresh ordinary-PPR solve to enforce literal
two-stage execution leaves two APPR-proposal and all five SOR-proposal wins;
failure stays within a factor two of direct APPR.
On trees, scalar threshold messages identify the exact support
unconditionally; the implementation certifies all $60$ large instances and
wins $14$ of $51$ nontrivial comparisons after the fair APPR charge.  A
literal second-stage ordinary-PPR solve has the same count because it replaces
the one RPPR recovery solve; it is not appended to it.
The framework requires neither exact RPPR support nor acceleration across
changing faces; exact support remains an optional margin-dependent verifier.
A threshold-batched active-set lane additionally gives the full
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha))$ bound on arbitrary graphs in an
exact-real randomized word model.  It returns a low-energy inner face rather
than identifying all of $S^\star$; deterministic finite-precision complexity
remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and the reduction}

Let $G=(V,E_G)$ be a finite simple undirected graph without isolates,
$A$ its adjacency matrix, and $D$ its degree matrix.  For
$0<\alpha\leq1$, put
\begin{equation}
 Q:=\alpha I+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
      \bigl(I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\bigr),
 \qquad b:=\alpha D^{-1/2}e_v.
 \label{eq:short-q}
\end{equation}
The point-source PPR vector in shared coordinates is $x^0=Q^{-1}b$ and the
semantic target is
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(\widehat x-x^0)}_\infty\leq\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:short-target}
\end{equation}
The matrix $Q$ is symmetric Stieltjes, has spectrum in $[\alpha,1]$, and
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 QD^{1/2}\one=\alpha D^{1/2}\one,
 \qquad Q^{-1}\geq0.
 \label{eq:short-comparison}
\end{equation}
Work is the sum of degrees of every scanned or updated row; all candidate
generation, boundary checks, matrix applications, and output writes count.
Classical APPR supplies the monotone local baseline
\citep{andersen2007using}; accelerated sparse PageRank motivates the target
product scale \citep{martinezrubio2023accelerated,huang2025accelerated}; and
the changing-support difficulty for $\ell_1$-regularized PageRank is the
reason for isolating the fixed-envelope continuation
\citep{fountoulakis2026complexity}.

The key reduction is to stop asking a single accelerated trajectory to both
discover graph coordinates and converge numerically.  A first stage may use
APPR, priority SOR, AESP-CD on temporary fixed envelopes, rooted balls, or a
structural response oracle.  It has three certified exits:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item an $\varepsilon$-accurate numerical point, which returns immediately;
\item an envelope $U$ whose exact principal PPR solution has small boundary
  leakage, followed by one fixed solve;
\item a PPR subsolution with $O(\sqrt\alpha)$ missing mass, followed by
  monotone APPR/SOR cleanup.
\end{enumerate}
No accelerated state crosses an envelope change.  A speculative lane retains
at most a declared volume cap $B$; if it cannot certify an exit, direct APPR
continues from its raced state (or safely restarts from zero if no concurrent
state was retained).  A constant-size fair race loses only a constant factor
to the first complete lane.

\section{A complete set-only two-stage lane}

The point-source restriction supplies a set-only proposal directly.  When
$\alpha=1$, $Q=I$ and the one-coordinate solution is returned immediately;
in the remaining case $0<\alpha<1$, put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\alpha:=\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha},
 \qquad
 R_\delta:=\left\lceil
  \frac{\log(2/\delta)}
       {\log((1+\sqrt\alpha)/(1-\sqrt\alpha))}
 \right\rceil .
 \label{eq:short-green-radius}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Hard-capped Green--CG lane]
\label{thm:short-green-cg}
Let $U=B(v,R_\delta-1)$ and abort before retaining a row that would make
$\vol(U)>B$.  If the ball completes, solve $Q_Uu=b_U$ from zero by CG,
stopping when
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{b_U-Q_U\widehat u}_2\leq\alpha\tau,
\end{equation*}
and return the zero extension of $[\widehat u]_+$.  The returned point is
$(\delta+\tau)$-accurate in \cref{eq:short-target}.  The successful lane uses
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(B+\frac{B}{\sqrt\alpha}
       \log\frac{C}{\tau}\right)
 \label{eq:short-green-work}
\end{equation}
degree work.  Racing it one-for-one against direct APPR costs at most twice
the faster complete lane; if the cap aborts, total work is at most twice the
direct-APPR work.  Thus $B=2/\varepsilon$ and
$\delta=\tau=\varepsilon/2$ give an unconditional adaptive algorithm with a
successful product-scale branch and the classical APPR fallback.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The point-source Green estimate gives
\begin{equation*}
 0\leq x_i^0\leq
  2\lambda_\alpha^{\operatorname{dist}(v,i)}\sqrt{d_i}.
\end{equation*}
Thus every coordinate outside $U$ has normalized amplitude at most $\delta$.
Principal inverse positivity gives $0\leq\widetilde u_U\leq x^0$; the same
Schur-complement comparison bounds
$\norm{D^{-1/2}(x^0-\widetilde u_U)}_\infty$ by $\delta$.
Since $Q_U\succeq\alpha I$, the residual stopping rule gives normalized
principal error at most $\tau$.  CG has the work in
\cref{eq:short-green-work}.  Finally, alternate one charged quantum of the
Green--CG lane with one quantum of APPR.  A successful lane finishes after at
most twice its own work.  If the screen aborts first, remove it and continue
APPR; its failed quanta are no more than the APPR quanta already executed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-treewidth tail]
\label{cor:short-green-structural}
If the completed Green envelope has a certified elimination order of width
$w$, sparse elimination and back substitution replace CG and give successful
work
\begin{equation*}
 O(B+w^3B).
\end{equation*}
For trees and unicyclic graphs this is $O(B)$; with $B=2/\varepsilon$, the
successful branch is $O(1/\varepsilon)$ and has no
$1/\sqrt\alpha$ factor.
Because this solve is exact, it may use the full truncation budget
$\delta=\varepsilon$ rather than the generic split
$\delta=\tau=\varepsilon/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every elimination bag contains at most $w+1$ live variables, so its dense
Schur update costs $O(w^3)$; a certified width-$w$ order has $O(|U|)$ bags and
$|U|\leq\vol(U)\leq B$.  Back substitution has no larger cost.  On a tree,
leaf elimination scans each incidence a constant number of times; a cycle has
width two.
\end{proof}

If an exact RPPR face verifier has already factored $Q_A$, a strict
ordinary-PPR Stage~II changes only the right-hand side.  It may reuse those
factors and pay one additional backsolve, $O(w^2\vol(A))$ in width $w$ and
$O(\vol(A))$ on a tree or unicyclic graph.  The measurements below are more
conservative: their literal two-stage columns charge a fresh structural solve.

This theorem is the cleanest answer to the exact-support question: Stage~I
need not identify $S^\star$.  It identifies a certified \emph{approximate
envelope} using only adjacency exploration.  Exact support is a discontinuous
contract without a KKT margin, whereas semantic PPR accuracy only needs this
Green envelope.

There is also a literal exact-support version.  With an exact structural
face solve, set $\rho=\varepsilon$ and reserve no terminal numerical budget;
run priority APPR toward its numerical $\varepsilon$ endpoint, and at charged
checkpoints snapshot its support or a charged one-hop expansion $A$.  Solve
$Q_Ap_A=c_{\rho,A}$ and test positivity plus every boundary KKT row.  A pass
proves $A=S^\star(\rho)$ and the returned point is already
$\varepsilon$-accurate for PPR.  Race all verifier work one-for-one against
the continuing APPR state; failure therefore costs at most twice direct APPR.
\begin{theorem}[APPR-to-exact-face race]
\label{thm:short-appr-face-race}
The preceding snapshot race is unconditionally correct and uses at most
$2W_{\rm APPR}(\varepsilon)$ work.  On a tree or unicyclic candidate, one
leaf/cycle elimination and one boundary scan implement the exact verifier in
$O(\vol(A))$ work.
\end{theorem}
In the fixed 90-case large-graph sweep, checkpoints at
$16\rho,8\rho,4\rho,2\rho$ complete 19 nontrivial exact-support certificates
before the direct lane; four have total-work ratios below one, namely $0.132$,
$0.276$, $0.590$, and $0.921$.  Thus APPR plus a one-shot AESP/structural verifier is a real
certificate strategy, although not a uniform acceleration theorem.  If a
fresh ordinary-PPR face solve is charged after certification, four cases
remain standalone wins and the two star cases remain fair-race wins.
Retaining the verifier factors raises the standalone count to six; the fair
count remains two.

Replacing APPR proposals by positive-residual SOR gives the AESP-CD version
of the same idea.  A lower-safe point can expose only true support rows:
for inactive $i$, $z\leq x_\rho^\star$ and the Stieltjes signs imply
$(c_\rho-Qz)_i\leq(c_\rho-Qx_\rho^\star)_i\leq0$ whenever
$(x_\rho^\star)_i=0$.  Hence priority SOR may grow a support-safe face,
launch the structural KKT verifier only at geometric work checkpoints, and
restart once when it passes.  The fair APPR fallback is unchanged.  In the
same sweep this lane finishes first in eight cases and, after charging both
lanes, strictly beats direct APPR in five, with best ratio $0.088$.  This is
the numerical early-return accounting.  A separately charged fresh
ordinary-PPR face solve leaves the same five fair-race wins, so this lane is
also a literal ``AESP-CD/SOR discovers, fixed face solves'' strategy; its
serial-frontier cases fall back rather than receiving a universal claim.
The retained-factor charge raises its standalone count from five to seven.

A more literal baseline solves the current RPPR face exactly, admits every
strictly positive exterior KKT row, and repeats.  Stieltjes inverse positivity
implies that the new face point dominates the padded old point, so all
admissions are support-safe; a face with no positive exterior row is exactly
the global obstacle KKT certificate.  The work is the sum of all face
solve-and-scan costs, so singleton paths prevent a universal product bound.
With $\rho=\varepsilon$, the structural implementation certifies $75$ of
$90$ cases; it finishes before APPR in $43$ standalone, and $13$ remain wins
after the one-for-one race when its exact RPPR point returns immediately.
After charging a fresh ordinary-PPR Stage~II, $33$ standalone and $8$ fair
wins remain; retaining the final factors raises these to $37$ and $9$.
Stars batch $1023$ leaves at once, whereas a
representative path still needs $38$ singleton refits.

The tree case is stronger and closes without a race assumption.
\begin{theorem}[Exact point-source tree closure]
\label{thm:short-tree-exact-support}
On a tree, the point-source RPPR support and its exact restricted point can be
found in
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
   \vol(S^\star)+\sum_{i\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(v,i)
 \right)
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
   \frac{1+\vol(S^\star)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right)
\end{equation*}
degree work.  If the terminal leaf solve is exact, choose
$\rho=\varepsilon$ and give it zero numerical error budget; the result is
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha))$.  If only the set is exported,
one leaf elimination solves the ordinary principal PPR system in an
additional $O(\vol(S^\star))$ work.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Root the tree at the source.  Eliminating the active descendants of $u$
gives a scalar affine response
$x_u=(\beta_u x_{p(u)}+B_u)/A_u$ with $A_u>0$.  An inactive child contributes
the threshold at which its KKT key becomes positive; an active child
propagates the minimum threshold in its subtree through the same affine map.
A handled heap at each vertex therefore names one genuinely positive
frontier row, or certifies that all frontier rows are nonpositive.  Admitting
the named row changes one record on its root path.  Each adjacency list is
scanned once, so the total route charge is the first display.  At termination
the active equations and exterior inequalities are the global obstacle KKT
conditions.  The point-source radius bound gives the second equality, and
$\vol(S^\star)\leq1/\rho$ gives the final specialization.
\end{proof}

This structural theorem explains precisely what the single-source
restriction buys: one rooted response hierarchy.  Multiple positive loads
can meet at a pinned row and must aggregate before activation, so independent
single-source reporters do not compose.

The implementation follows the proof literally and roots only rows when they
are admitted; it does not pre-scan the ambient tree.  Across $60$ path, star,
binary-tree, and broom cases of size $1024$--$2047$, every reported face passes
an independent fixed-face solve and global KKT scan.  On the $51$ nontrivial
direct-APPR comparisons, the complete lane wins $27$ standalone and $14$
after a one-for-one APPR race.  The literal set-only-then-PPR version has the
same charged work: the fixed PPR solve substitutes for recovery of the RPPR
point.  The exact terminal solve permits the tuned choice $\rho=\varepsilon$.
For example, at $(\alpha,\varepsilon)=(0.01,10^{-4})$ its path and broom each
cost $1630$ versus $6312$, while the full-support star costs $17391$ versus
$116622$.  At $\varepsilon=5\cdot10^{-4}$ the star support collapses to the
source and the ratio is $2046/34782=0.059$.  The independent RPPR solve and
KKT scan used to audit the implementation are not required by the exact
message algorithm.

\section{The shortest set-only certificate}

Let $U\ni v$ and write
\begin{equation}
 u_U:=Q_U^{-1}b_U,
 \qquad \widetilde u_U:=\text{the zero extension of }u_U.
 \label{eq:short-principal}
\end{equation}
Set $c=(1-\alpha)/2$.  Suppose a coarse fixed solve returns
$\widehat u_U$ and a certified error bar
\begin{equation}
 \max_{i\in U}\frac{|(u_U)_i-(\widehat u_U)_i|}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \leq\eta.
 \label{eq:short-error-bar}
\end{equation}
For every exterior boundary vertex $j$, compute
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\ell_j
 :=\frac{-Q_{jU}\widehat u_U}{\sqrt{d_j}}
 =\frac{c}{d_j}\sum_{i\in U\cap N(j)}
       \frac{(\widehat u_U)_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}.
 \label{eq:short-leakage-estimate}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-leakage gate]
\label{thm:short-leakage}
If
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\ell_j+c\frac{\deg_U(j)}{d_j}\eta
 \leq\alpha\delta
 \qquad(j\in\partial U),
 \label{eq:short-leakage-gate}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\widetilde u_U\leq x^0,
 \qquad
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(x^0-\widetilde u_U)}_\infty\leq\delta.
 \label{eq:short-leakage-result}
\end{equation}
It is sufficient to take the observable residual error bar
\begin{equation}
 \eta=\frac{\norm{b_U-Q_U\widehat u_U}_2}{\alpha}.
 \label{eq:short-residual-bar}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $r=b-Q\widetilde u_U$, the rows in $U$ vanish.  Since the sole positive
load lies in $U$, every exterior row is
$r_j=-Q_{jU}u_U\geq0$, and every nonboundary row is zero.  The error bar and
the Stieltjes edge weights give
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{r_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}
 \leq \widehat\ell_j
      +c\frac{\deg_U(j)}{d_j}\eta
 \leq\alpha\delta.
\end{equation*}
Thus $0\leq r\leq\alpha\delta D^{1/2}\one$.  Applying inverse positivity
and \cref{eq:short-comparison} proves
$0\leq Q^{-1}r=x^0-\widetilde u_U\leq\delta D^{1/2}\one$.
Finally, $Q_U\succeq\alpha I$ and $d_i\geq1$ imply
\cref{eq:short-residual-bar}.
\end{proof}

The gate can be evaluated before any signed scratch.  Let $z\geq0$ be a
global APPR/SOR subsolution with residual $r=b-Qz\geq0$ and
$U=\supp(z)$.  Define
\begin{equation*}
 \eta_U:=\max_{i\in U}\frac{r_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq u_U-z_U\leq\frac{\eta_U}{\alpha}D_U^{1/2}\one,
 \label{eq:short-monotone-box}
\end{equation}
because $Q_U(u_U-z_U)=r_U$ and
$Q_UD_U^{1/2}\one\geq\alpha D_U^{1/2}\one$.  Therefore the observable test
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}
 +c\frac{\deg_U(j)}{d_j}\frac{\eta_U}{\alpha}
 \leq\alpha\delta
 \qquad(j\in\partial U)
 \label{eq:short-monotone-leakage}
\end{equation}
certifies the same envelope.  If this test passes before APPR's global
stopping rule, the fixed accelerated continuation is genuinely useful: Stage
I has found the rows but has not yet solved their values to target accuracy.
The coarser test
\begin{equation}
 \max_{j\in\partial U}\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}
 +\frac{c}{\alpha}\eta_U\leq\alpha\delta
 \label{eq:short-two-heap-gate}
\end{equation}
is sufficient because $\deg_U(j)/d_j\leq1$.  Internal and boundary lazy heaps
make it an $O(1)$ checkpoint query; no boundary sweep is needed.
For an empty boundary the leakage gate is vacuous, so the second term is not
tested.

If the gate passes, continue the same principal solve or restart it once to
normalized error $\tau$.  Chebyshev, CG, or accelerated gradient then costs
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\frac{\vol(U)}{\sqrt\alpha}
          \log\frac{C_{U,v,\alpha}}{\tau}\right)
 \label{eq:short-fixed-work}
\end{equation}
and returns a $(\delta+\tau)$-accurate PPR point.  This is a literal
two-stage theorem with no RPPR parameter and no support classification.  If
$U=V$, the boundary gate is vacuous.

For comparison, RPPR can still propose an envelope: if its amplitude outside
$U$ is at most $\gamma$ at regularization $\rho$, then the same principal PPR
solution is within $\rho+\gamma$ of $x^0$.  This useful proposal lemma does
not make exact RPPR support part of the terminal contract.

\section{The complementary AESP--APPR mass exit}

A fixed set can also be useful without passing the boundary gate.  A vector
$z$ is a PPR subsolution if
\begin{equation}
 z\geq0,\qquad r:=b-Qz\geq0.
\end{equation}
Put $\mu(z)=1-\langle D^{1/2}\one,z\rangle$.

\begin{theorem}[Mass completion]
\label{thm:short-mass-cleanup}
From a PPR subsolution, exact monotone coordinate pushes on rows satisfying
$r_i/\sqrt{d_i}>\alpha\varepsilon$ terminate at an
$\varepsilon$-accurate point after degree work
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm clean}<
 \frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\varepsilon}\,\mu(z).
 \label{eq:short-mass-work}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mu(z)=O(\sqrt\alpha)$ gives
$O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\varepsilon))$ cleanup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weighted residual mass is
\begin{equation*}
 \langle D^{1/2}\one,r\rangle=\alpha\mu(z).
\end{equation*}
Writing $a=(1+\alpha)/2$, a push at $i$ decreases this mass by
$(\alpha/a)\sqrt{d_i}r_i>(\alpha^2\varepsilon/a)d_i$.  Summation proves
\cref{eq:short-mass-work}.  At termination,
$0\leq r\leq\alpha\varepsilon D^{1/2}\one$; comparison as in the previous
proof gives the semantic target.
\end{proof}

The subsolution can be published after signed fixed-envelope acceleration.
For $U\ni v$, let $u_U=Q_U^{-1}b_U$, $V_U=\vol(U)$, and
$v_U=D_U^{1/2}\one$.  Given signed scratch $\widehat u$ and residual
$q=b_U-Q_U\widehat u$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\max_i\left[\frac{-q_i}{\alpha(v_U)_i}\right]_+,
 \qquad z_U=[\widehat u-\Delta v_U]_+,
 \label{eq:short-retraction}
\end{equation}
and pad by zero.

\begin{theorem}[Safe AESP mass publication]
\label{thm:short-aesp-mass}
If the exact principal solution satisfies
\begin{equation}
 1-\langle v_U,u_U\rangle\leq\frac{C\sqrt\alpha}{2},
 \label{eq:short-mass-premise}
\end{equation}
then Chebyshev/AESP scratch followed by
\cref{eq:short-retraction} publishes a global PPR subsolution with
$\mu(z)\leq C\sqrt\alpha$ after
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\frac{V_U}{\sqrt\alpha}
   \log_+\frac{(1+\sqrt{V_U})\sqrt{V_U}}
                    {C\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \label{eq:short-aesp-work}
\end{equation}
degree work.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $Q_Uv_U\geq\alpha v_U$, the shifted residual
$q+\Delta Q_Uv_U$ is nonnegative.  Principal inverse positivity gives
$\widehat u-\Delta v_U\leq u_U$; the Stieltjes signs show that taking the
positive part preserves $b_U-Q_Uz_U\geq0$.  Exterior residuals are also
nonnegative, so the publication is global and lower-safe.  Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{u_U-z_U}_2
 \leq\frac{1+\sqrt{V_U}}{\alpha}\norm q_2.
\end{equation*}
The spectrum of $Q_U$ is in $[\alpha,1]$, so Chebyshev reduces the right-hand
side to $C\sqrt\alpha/(2\sqrt{V_U})$ in the work shown above.  Cauchy--Schwarz
and \cref{eq:short-mass-premise} then give $\mu(z)\leq C\sqrt\alpha$.
\end{proof}

The mass premise is screened before signed work.  For a current lower-safe
$z_U$, let $q_U=b_U-Q_Uz_U\geq0$ and
\begin{equation*}
 q^-:=\min_i\frac{(Q_Uv_U)_i}{(v_U)_i},\qquad
 q^+:=\max_i\frac{(Q_Uv_U)_i}{(v_U)_i}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\alpha\leq q^-\leq q^+\leq(1+\alpha)/2$ and
\begin{equation}
 \langle v_U,z_U\rangle+\frac{\langle v_U,q_U\rangle}{q^+}
 \leq\langle v_U,u_U\rangle
 \leq\langle v_U,z_U\rangle+\frac{\langle v_U,q_U\rangle}{q^-}.
 \label{eq:short-mass-interval}
\end{equation}
The row ratios are
$(1+\alpha)/2-c\deg_U(i)/d_i$, so a lazy heap maintains them locally.  A
strictly favorable lower endpoint triggers AESP; an unfavorable upper
endpoint rejects the attempt without a fixed solve.

\section{One algorithm, two continuations}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Hard-capped point-source certificate race}
\label{alg:short-race}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $G,v,\alpha,\varepsilon$ and cap $B=\Theta(1/\varepsilon)$
\STATE Race direct APPR, priority SOR, and one or more local proposal lanes.
\STATE Return immediately from any target-accurate numerical lane.
\FOR{each nested proposed envelope $U$ with $\vol(U)\leq B$}
  \STATE Update the mass interval and the coarse PPR leakage error bars.
  \IF{the leakage gate certifies budget $\delta$}
    \STATE Discard changing-envelope history; refine one fixed solve to
           $\varepsilon-\delta$ and return.
  \ELSIF{the mass interval certifies a positive $O(\sqrt\alpha)$ margin}
    \STATE Run fixed AESP scratch, retract safely, and complete by APPR/SOR.
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE If a proposal would exceed $B$, abandon that lane; direct APPR wins.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

With geometrically growing retained volumes, the sum of attempted volumes is
at most $2B$.  Hence either successful fixed-envelope branch has
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(\frac{B}{\sqrt\alpha}
              +\frac{1}{\sqrt\alpha\varepsilon}\right)
 =\widetilde O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt\alpha\varepsilon}\right)
 \label{eq:short-success-work}
\end{equation}
work when proposal and bookkeeping cost $\widetilde O(B)$.  A failed branch
adds direct APPR's classical $O(1/(\alpha\varepsilon))$ work.  Round-robin
scheduling of a fixed number of complete lanes costs at most a fixed multiple
of the best lane on each instance.

\section{What the data say}

All experiments use six deterministic graph families (path, cycle, star,
binary tree, broom, and grid) and charge degree work.  The coarse
$\varepsilon\in\{0.05,0.1\}$ sweep gives the following message:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Lane & median ratio to best direct baseline & strict wins \\
\midrule
Forced fixed tail after a numerical point & $4.93$--$5.48$ & $0$ \\
Hard-capped Green set screen & $3.13$ & $0$ \\
Blind BFS AESP mass screen & $5.99$ & $0$ \\
Adaptive APPR-shaped mass screen & $1.00$ & $0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The fixed tail should therefore never be forced after Stage~I has already met
the target.

The one-shot Green--CG lane behaves differently in the genuinely
high-accuracy range
$\varepsilon\in\{10^{-4},5\cdot10^{-4},10^{-3},0.002,0.005\}$.
It completes its capped ball in 85 of 90 cases and strictly beats the better
of FIFO APPR and priority SOR in 22 as a standalone lane.  Those wins comprise
13 stars, six binary trees, one grid, one path, and one cycle.  In a literal
one-for-one race, 15 remain strict total-work wins: ten stars, four binary
trees, and one grid.  Representative standalone ratios are

The monotone two-heap version is the literal requested two-stage algorithm:

fixed tail is forced.

The star mechanism is exact.  In degree-unscaled coordinates the full matrix
is $\widehat Q=aD-cA$, and the source belongs to the two-dimensional subspace
of vectors constant on the leaves.  Exact CG therefore terminates in at most
two iterations.  Exposing the star and solving it costs $O(n)$, with no mass
cleanup.  Yet a partial star retaining $k$ of $n$ leaves has principal mass
deficit
\begin{equation}
 \mu_U=\frac{ac(n-k)}{a^2n-c^2k}.
 \label{eq:short-star-deficit}
\end{equation}
Thus the star may require almost full exposure even when the semantic target
allows many tiny coordinates to be omitted.  Low numerical dimension, not a
universal support-volume law, explains the win.

\section{Scope and the remaining problem}

The result is deliberately point-source.  For linear PPR, solving every
source separately and optimally distributing the error gives the natural
factor
\begin{equation*}
 \left(\sum_{i\in\supp(s)}\sqrt{s_i}\right)^2,
\end{equation*}
which can equal $\operatorname{nnz}(s)$; it does not imply the desired
additive input cost.  RPPR is nonlinear in the source and does not superpose.

Exact RPPR support $S^\star$ is optional.  On trees and some cactus classes,
point-source response messages can find it at the desired product scale and
may serve as a deterministic proposal lane.  On arbitrary graphs,
\cref{thm:random-core-two-stage} instead returns a low-energy inner face and
closes the target in the exact-real randomized word model.  It charges every
fresh face, boundary scan, solver state, and exposure.  What remains open is a
deterministic finite-precision theorem, or a practical adjacency-local
proposal/reuse mechanism that produces such a certificate before doing the
work of direct APPR without rebuilding every face.

In a 10-case large-graph diagnostic, literal monotone RPPR discovery does
return smaller exact sets on paths and grids, but its median work is $7.92$
times Green--CG and $9.26$ times direct PPR.  This is not a lower bound on
future active-set acceleration; it is evidence that exactness is a poor
mandatory interface for the present algorithm.

If exact support is required and a candidate $A$ is already available, it can
still be certified cleanly.  Solve
$Q_Ap_A=c_{\rho,A}$ with an error box
$|p_A-\widehat p_A|\leq\eta D_A^{1/2}\one$.  Positive lower bounds on every
active coordinate and nonpositive upper bounds
\begin{equation*}
 c_{\rho,j}-Q_{jA}\widehat p_A
 +\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
  \frac{\deg_A(j)}{\sqrt{d_j}}\eta\leq0
 \qquad(j\in\partial A)
\end{equation*}
prove $A=S^\star$.  Fixed-face acceleration reaches this verifier with only
logarithmic dependence on a strict KKT margin.  Without such a margin, exact
support is not a uniform finite-precision contract; leakage is the correct
approximate alternative.

This short formulation removes the correction-by-correction changing-face
Lyapunov proof, Moreau face shocks, Perron alignment, permanent finite-inner
replay, and the catalogue of dynamic-reporter obstructions.  Those results
remain a companion audit.  They are not prerequisites for the two-stage
algorithm above.

\paragraph{Bottom line.}
The terminal fixed solve is closed, safe, and sometimes decisively useful.
The mass continuation is equally short and preserves direct-APPR fallback.
Threshold-batched safe discovery supplies the universal randomized exact-real
existence theorem.  The remaining breakthrough is a deterministic,
finite-precision, and practically competitive Stage~I implementation.
