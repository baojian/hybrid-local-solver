\begin{abstract}
This note formalizes a hybrid local method suggested by two complementary
lines of work on personalized PageRank. The locally evolving-set framework
shows how Gauss--Seidel/SOR, gradient descent, Chebyshev, and heavy-ball
iterations can be localized, but its accelerated variants face
momentum-localization errors. The AESP framework instead places Catalyst
acceleration outside short monotone local solves and exhibits particularly
fast early-stage convergence. The resulting design principle is to use AESP
as an early residual/objective compressor and then reset momentum and finish
with a direct local method.

For unregularized PageRank, we prove finite convergence from every signed
handoff, objective-gap and weighted-residual-mass tail bounds, and a master
handoff inequality. Combining the direct AESP amortization with the SOR tail
gives an unconditional instance-wise
$\bigO(R/(\alpha^{3/4}\eps))$ inner-plus-tail bound, parameterized by the
run-dependent AESP ratio $R$. A sharper trajectory analysis yields
$\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ under an early-locality condition; explicit
support-envelope and no-percolation conditions are given, but the premise is
not known for every graph. A KKT-slack charging lemma provides a third,
boundary-sensitive proof route.

For $\ell_1$-regularized PageRank, Catalyst is naturally compatible with the
weighted soft-thresholding term. We prove that the unit-step proximal map is a
$(1-\alpha)$ contraction in degree-weighted $\ell_\infty$ and $\ell_1$ norms,
and that its fixed-point residual divided by $\alpha$ certifies solution
error. Thus a finite composite Catalyst burn-in followed by momentum-free
ISTA is unconditionally convergent. The remaining open problem is a
graph-independent local-work bound from an arbitrary accelerated warm start,
not correctness of the hybrid itself.
\end{abstract}
