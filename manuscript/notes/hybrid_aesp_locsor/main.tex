\begin{abstract}
This note formalizes a hybrid local method suggested by two complementary
lines of work on personalized PageRank.  The locally evolving-set framework
shows how Gauss--Seidel/SOR, gradient descent, Chebyshev, and heavy-ball
iterations can be localized, but its accelerated variants face
momentum-localization errors.  The AESP framework instead places Catalyst
acceleration outside short monotone local solves and exhibits particularly
fast early-stage convergence.  The resulting design principle is to use AESP
as an early residual/objective compressor and then reset momentum and finish
with a direct local coordinate method.

We prove three statements that do not require a locality assumption.  First,
for any finite AESP handoff point, local SOR with any fixed relaxation
parameter in $(0,2)$ terminates under the standard degree-normalized gradient
certificate.  Second, with the proof-safe choice $\omega=1$, if the AESP
handoff objective gap is at most
$\alpha^{3/2}\eps/(1+\alpha)$, then the entire SOR tail costs at most
$1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps)$ degree-weighted operations.  Third, a stronger
weighted-$\ell_1$ handoff condition gives the same tail order and uniformly
bounds every tail active-set volume.  Combining these facts with the source
AESP work theorem yields a rigorous trajectory-dependent bound
\[
  W_{\rm hybrid}
  \leq
  \softO\!\left(\frac{\Lambda_J}{\sqrt\alpha}\right)
  +\frac{1}{\sqrt\alpha\eps},
\]
where $\Lambda_J$ is the largest early-stage AESP locality factor.  Hence the
desired $\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ total work follows whenever
$\Lambda_J=\bigO(1/\eps)$ during burn-in.  We give explicit confinement and
support-envelope conditions under which this implication is valid, and we
explain why the available literature does not yet establish the condition for
all graphs.  In particular, the latest $\ell_1$-regularized PageRank analysis
shows that momentum can activate a high-degree boundary node even when the
optimal support is tiny.  Thus unconditional convergence is established, but
a graph-uniform accelerated local-work theorem remains an open lemma rather
than a completed claim.
\end{abstract}
