\section{Relationship to the existing CF-Push analysis}
\label{sec:cf-relation}

The active repository already contains
\texttt{manuscript/sections/cf\_push\_two\_stage\_analysis.tex}, which studies a
different two-stage method: coarse monotone forward push followed by fixed
SOR. That note proves finite convergence and several handoff facts, and it
also records a long-spider obstruction to a graph-uniform accelerated work
bound for the current fixed-relaxation FIFO method.

The present note does not supersede that analysis. Its Phase I is AESP rather
than coarse forward push, and its principal handoff is an objective gap. The
long-spider obstruction remains relevant to claims about a plain optimal-SOR
tail, but it does not by itself refute \cref{thm:objective-tail}: after a
sufficiently small objective-gap handoff, even $\omega=1$ has the stated tail
budget. The unresolved question moves to whether AESP reaches that handoff
with $\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ local work on every graph.

\section{Conclusions and publication-level claim boundary}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The hybrid idea is mathematically coherent and at least convergent. For the
unregularized problem, the most robust proof architecture is
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{coarse AESP burn-in}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \text{momentum reset}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \omega=1\text{ LOCSOR cleanup}.}
\]
The SOR tail is fully controlled: an objective-gap handoff gives
$\bigO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ tail work, and a weighted-gradient-mass handoff
additionally controls every tail active set. The master inequality in
\cref{thm:master-handoff} makes the handoff tradeoff explicit.

Three levels of total-work result should be kept distinct. Path I gives the
closed, graph-structure-free but run-dependent rate
$\bigO(R/(\alpha^{3/4}\eps))$. Path II reaches the target
$\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ under an early-locality factor or support
envelope. Path III replaces hard confinement by boundary charging, but still
requires shifted-margin and localized path-length lemmas. Calling Path I
``unconditional'' is appropriate only with the explicit qualification that
$R$ remains in the bound and that outer initialization work must be charged.

For RPPR, the proof architecture becomes
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{composite Catalyst burn-in}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \text{momentum reset}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \text{proximal-gradient cleanup}.}
\]
The weighted proximal map is contractive, so correctness and convergence are
closed. What remains open is preserving the $1/\rho$ locality scaling from an
arbitrary accelerated warm start. This distinction is important: the hybrid
resolves the algorithmic compatibility question, but not yet the full COLT
2022 degree-work target.

A publication can therefore make a strong and accurate claim today: introduce
the acceleration--locality phase transition, prove the master handoff and the
$R$-parameterized rate, prove the conditional optimal theorem, establish the
composite convergence extension, and support the switch empirically against
AESP, LOCSOR, optimal SOR, and LOCCH. A universal
$\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))$ or
$\softO(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ theorem should be claimed only after the
corresponding locality lemma is closed.

\section{A compact dependency map}
\label{app:dependency}

The unregularized target theorem decomposes into the following implications:
\begin{align*}
  &\text{AESP inner certificates}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      f(x^{(t)})-f^\star\leq U_t,\\
  &U_J\leq\frac{\alpha^{3/2}\eps}{1+\alpha}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      W_{\rm tail}\leq\frac1{\sqrt\alpha\eps},\\
  &\Lambda_J\leq\frac{K}{\eps}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      B_J=\softO\!\left(\frac{K}{\sqrt\alpha\eps}\right),\\
  &\text{the previous two lines}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      W_{\rm hybrid}
      =\softO\!\left(\frac{K+1}{\sqrt\alpha\eps}\right).
\end{align*}
Only the third implication's premise is currently unresolved in a fully
graph-uniform form. Independently, the direct AESP amortization and the
objective tail imply the Path-I tradeoff
\[
  W_{\rm hybrid}^{\rm in+tail}
  \leq
  \frac{3000R_J^2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}{\Delta}
  +\frac{(1+\alpha)\Delta}{\alpha^2\eps^2},
\]
whose optimal black-box balance is
$\bigO(R_J/(\alpha^{3/4}\eps))$.

For RPPR, the closed dependency chain is
\begin{align*}
  &\text{coordinatewise nonexpansive soft thresholding}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      \mathcal T_\rho\text{ is a weighted }(1-\alpha)\text{-contraction},\\
  &x_\rho^\star=\mathcal T_\rho(x_\rho^\star)
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      \norm{x-x_\rho^\star}_{D,\infty}
      \leq\alpha^{-1}\norm{R_\rho(x)}_{D,\infty},\\
  &\text{finite Catalyst handoff plus full ISTA}
    &&\Longrightarrow\quad
      x^{(k)}\to x_\rho^\star.
\end{align*}
The missing implication is from this iteration contraction to a
$\softO(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ degree-weighted local-work bound.

\section{Useful inequalities}
\label{app:inequalities}

For reference, the analysis repeatedly uses:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item for a convex $1$-smooth function,
        $\norm{\nabla f(x)}_2^2\leq2(f(x)-f^\star)$;
  \item for $q=D^{1/2}g$ supported in $\Omega$,
        $\norm{q}_1\leq\sqrt{\vol(\Omega)}\norm{g}_2$;
  \item for $0<z<1$, $(1-z)^k\leq e^{-zk}$;
  \item for a thresholded set $S$ and support envelope $\Omega$,
        the average density on $S$ is at least the average density on
        $\Omega$, yielding \cref{eq:support-envelope-bound};
  \item for $a,b>0$,
        $\min_{\Delta>0}(a/\Delta+b\Delta)=2\sqrt{ab}$.
\end{enumerate}
