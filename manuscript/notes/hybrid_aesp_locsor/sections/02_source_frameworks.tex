\section{Source framework I: locally evolving sets}
\label{sec:work1}

The 2024 framework of \citet{zhou2024iterative} represents a local iterative
solver as a deterministic evolving-set process
\[
  (S_t,x^{(t)},r^{(t)})
  \longmapsto
  (S_{t+1},x^{(t+1)},r^{(t+1)}),
  \qquad
  S_{t+1}\subseteq S_t\cup\bigcup_{u\in S_t}\mathcal N(u).
\]
Its total work is $\sum_t\vol(S_t)$.  Two trajectory statistics summarize the
locality of a run:
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\vol}_T
  :=\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\vol(S_t),
  \qquad
  \overline\gamma_T
  :=\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_t,
  \label{eq:work1-trajectory}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_t$ is the fraction of the weighted residual mass processed in
round $t$.  For the monotone methods, the source proves
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\overline{\vol}_T}{\overline\gamma_T}\leq\frac1\epsppr.
  \label{eq:work1-locality}
\end{equation}

\subsection{LOCSOR and LOCGD}

Because $\diag(\widehat Q)=I$, a Work-I SOR coordinate update is
\begin{align}
  x^+&=x+\omega\widehat r_u e_u,
  \label{eq:work1-sor-x}\\
  \widehat r^+&=\widehat r-\omega\widehat r_u e_u
    +\chi\omega\widehat r_u D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}e_u.
  \label{eq:work1-sor-r}
\end{align}
For $\omega=1$, the source obtains a local work bound of order
\begin{equation}
  \softO\!\left(
    \frac{\overline{\vol}_T}{\alpha\overline\gamma_T}
  \right),
  \label{eq:locsor-source-bound}
\end{equation}
with the classical graph-independent fallback $\bigO(1/(\alpha\epsppr))$.
The localized gradient/Jacobi variant has the same structural bound and is
more parallelizable.

The globally optimal SOR parameter is
\begin{equation}
  \omega_\star
  =\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^2},
  \label{eq:omega-star}
\end{equation}
which yields a global asymptotic factor
$(1-\sqrt\alpha)/(1+\sqrt\alpha)$.  The source explicitly identifies the
obstacle to a matching local theorem: for $\omega_\star>1$, residual signs and
weighted-$\ell_1$ monotonicity may be lost.

\subsection{LOCCH and localization forcing}

The localized Chebyshev method masks both the residual and momentum updates on
$S_t$.  If $S_t=V$ for all $t$, the method reduces to the standard Chebyshev
iteration.  Under localization, skipped coordinates appear as a forcing term
in a nonhomogeneous second-order residual recurrence.  The source controls the
forcing through run-dependent quantities $\vartheta_k^{\rm CH}$ (denoted
$\beta_k$ in the source) and proves acceleration
when their geometric mean remains below a threshold corresponding to a
constant $c<2$.  Thus the result is informative and often effective in
practice, but it is not a graph-uniform unconditional accelerated theorem.

\subsection{Empirical active-set shape}

The project observation motivating the present hybrid is that accelerated
local trajectories often display an expansion--peak--contraction pattern:
active volumes are small initially, become largest in an intermediate regime,
and shrink near termination.  This pattern is visible in the source
experiments and is consistent with the locally evolving-set interpretation.
It must be treated as an empirical regularity, not as a property proved for
every graph or every accelerated method.

\section{Source framework II: AESP}
\label{sec:work2}

The AESP framework of \citet{huang2025accelerated} moves acceleration outside
the local process.  Each outer step solves a shifted system by a short,
weighted-$\ell_1$ monotone local method, then applies Catalyst extrapolation
between completed inner solves.

\subsection{Shifted subproblems}

At outer stage $t$, with center $y^{(t-1)}$, define
\begin{equation}
  h_t(z):=f(z)+\frac{\kappa_{\rm A}}2\norm{z-y^{(t-1)}}_2^2.
  \label{eq:aesp-ht}
\end{equation}
Its Hessian is $Q+\kappa_{\rm A}I$, with eigenvalues in
$[\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A},1+\kappa_{\rm A}]$.  For
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\rm A}:=1-2\alpha,
  \qquad \alpha<\frac12,
  \label{eq:aesp-shift}
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(Q+\kappa_{\rm A}I)
  =\frac{1+\kappa_{\rm A}}{\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A}}=2.
  \label{eq:aesp-condition-two}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, each inner shifted system has an effective PageRank damping
parameter $1/2$, independent of the original small $\alpha$.

Let
\begin{equation}
  q_{\mathrm{in},t}(z):=D^{1/2}\nabla h_t(z).
  \label{eq:aesp-inner-q}
\end{equation}
The AESP inner operators use the threshold
\begin{equation}
  \abs{\nabla_u h_t(z)}\geq\epsin^{(t)}\sqrt{d_u}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \abs{q_{\mathrm{in},t,u}(z)}\geq\epsin^{(t)}d_u.
  \label{eq:aesp-inner-active}
\end{equation}
The source derives a sufficient local stopping tolerance
\begin{equation}
  \epsin^{(t)}
  :=\max\left\{
    \sqrt{\frac{(\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A})\phi_t}{m}},
    \frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A})\phi_t}
         {\norm{q_{\mathrm{in},t}(z_t^{(0)})}_1}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:aesp-eps-t}
\end{equation}
which guarantees
$h_t(z_t^{(K_t)})-\min h_t\leq\phi_t$ whenever the weighted
$\ell_1$ inner gradient does not grow.

For AESP-LOCGD, the scaled inner gradient satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \norm{q_{\mathrm{in},t}(z_t^{(k+1)})}_1
  \leq
  \left(1-\tau\gamma_t^{(k)}\right)
  \norm{q_{\mathrm{in},t}(z_t^{(k)})}_1,
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A})}
              {1+\alpha+2\kappa_{\rm A}}.
  \label{eq:aesp-inner-contraction}
\end{equation}
With \cref{eq:aesp-shift}, $\tau=2/3$.  AESP-LOCAPPR obtains the
coordinatewise analogue using online Gauss--Seidel updates.  Importantly, the
argument allows signed inner gradients; it does not rely on nonnegativity of
the Catalyst center.

\subsection{Outer accelerated rate}

Let
\begin{equation}
  \vartheta_{\mathrm A}:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A}}
  =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
  \qquad
  \varpi_{\mathrm A}:=0.9\sqrt{\vartheta_{\mathrm A}}.
  \label{eq:aesp-q-rho}
\end{equation}
The outer momentum coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\rm A}
  =\frac{\sqrt{\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A}}-\sqrt\alpha}
         {\sqrt{\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A}}+\sqrt\alpha}
  =\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha}-\sqrt\alpha}
         {\sqrt{1-\alpha}+\sqrt\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aesp-beta}
\end{equation}
With the source accuracy schedule, the AESP theorem gives
\begin{equation}
  f(x^{(t)})-f(x^0)
  \leq
  U_t
  :=\frac{200(1-\alpha^2)}{\alpha}
       (1-\varpi_{\mathrm A})^{t+1}.
  \label{eq:aesp-outer-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus the number of outer stages needed for a prescribed objective target is
$\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$.

For stage $t$, let $\overline{\vol}(S_t)$ be the average inner active volume
and $\gamma_t$ the source-defined average active ratio.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_t
  :=\frac{\overline{\vol}(S_t)}{\gamma_t}.
  \label{eq:Lambda-t}
\end{equation}
The source work theorem has the stagewise form
\begin{equation}
  W_t^{\rm AESP}
  \leq
  \min\left\{
    \frac{\overline{\vol}(S_t)}{\tau\gamma_t}
      \log\frac{C_t^0}{C_t^{K_t}},
    \frac{C_t^0-C_t^{K_t}}{\tau\epsin^{(t)}}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:aesp-stage-work}
\end{equation}
where
$C_t^k:=\norm{D^{1/2}\nabla h_t(z_t^{(k)})}_1$.  Its published
worst-case local corollary is
\begin{equation}
  W_{\rm AESP}
  \leq
  \min\left\{
    \softO\!\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt\alpha}\right),
    \softO\!\left(\frac{R^2}{\sqrt\alpha\epsppr^2}\right)
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:aesp-published-bound}
\end{equation}
The $1/\epsppr^2$ factor arises from converting a quadratic objective target into
a coordinatewise inner threshold through an $\ell_\infty$--$\ell_1$
certificate.

\subsection{Figure 4 and the hybrid motivation}

Figure~4 of \citet{huang2025accelerated} reports error versus degree-weighted
operations on four very large real graphs.  AESP-LOCAPPR is the most efficient
AESP variant, attributed to online per-coordinate updates.  More importantly
for the present project, the shifted systems yield much faster early-stage
convergence than the direct baselines.  The hybrid hypothesis is therefore:
\begin{quote}
Use AESP only while its shifted local systems obtain large error reduction at
small active volume; before the later high-accuracy inner thresholds become
expensive, reset momentum and hand the remaining problem to a direct local
solver.
\end{quote}
This is a design hypothesis supported by experiments, not a consequence of
the AESP theorem alone.
