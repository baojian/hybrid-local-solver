\section{What the 2026 $\ell_1$ paper contributes--and what it does not}
\label{sec:l1-paper}

\citet{fountoulakis2026complexity} studies the shared RPPR objective
$F_\rho=f+g_\rho$ from \cref{eq:shared-rppr-objective}.  Its minimizer has
support $S^\star(\rho)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq\frac1\rho.
  \label{eq:rppr-support}
\end{equation}
For standard FISTA, the paper gives two complementary messages.

\paragraph{Positive conditional result.}
The analysis runs FISTA on a slightly over-regularized objective, uses the
support at the smaller regularization as a core, and charges transient
activations outside the core through KKT slacks.  If all spurious activations
are confined to a boundary set $B$, the work is bounded by
\begin{equation}
  \bigO\!\left(
    \frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\log\frac{\alpha}{\epsobj}
    +\frac{\sqrt{\vol(B)}}{\rho\alpha^{3/2}}
  \right).
  \label{eq:fmr-work}
\end{equation}
The paper also gives an explicit no-percolation condition ensuring that FISTA
iterates remain in a core plus its immediate boundary.

\paragraph{Negative result.}
On a seed-at-leaf star, the optimal support contains only the seed leaf, but
FISTA overshoots the seed coordinate and activates the degree-$m$ center after
two extrapolated steps.  It therefore incurs $\Omega(m)$ work before reaching
a fixed target, whereas ISTA remains local.  This shows that small optimal
support alone does not control accelerated transient work.

\paragraph{Implication for the AESP hybrid.}
The support guarantee \cref{eq:rppr-support} does not directly prove
$\Lambda_J=\bigO(1/\epsppr)$ for unregularized AESP.  AESP solves changing shifted
quadratics without the soft-thresholding map that enforces the RPPR support.
The KKT-slack argument also refers to an optimum of an over-regularized
composite problem, whereas AESP inner activity is governed by the shifted
smooth gradient.  The valid transfer is methodological: over-regularization,
margin separation, and a no-percolation induction identify the type of
structural lemma needed.  \Cref{lem:aesp-confinement} is one explicit smooth
AESP analogue, but it remains conditional on a bounded path norm and a graph
exposure inequality.

\section{Why a universal accelerated theorem is still open}
\label{sec:open-gap}

The conversation initially moved too quickly from ``AESP is fast early'' to
``the whole hybrid has $\bigO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$ work.''  The missing step is
not convergence of the SOR tail; that part is closed.  The missing step is a
graph-independent upper bound on the work needed for AESP to reach the
handoff.

There are three distinct obstacles.

\paragraph{Outer iterations are not local work.}
The AESP theorem gives $\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ outer stages, but each stage can
process a large active volume.  One must control $\Lambda_t$ or another
stagewise local-work quantity.

\paragraph{Objective convergence does not control weighted-$\ell_1$ mass
without a support factor.}
Since $f$ is $1$-smooth,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\nabla f(x)}_2^2\leq2(f(x)-f(x^0)).
  \label{eq:gradient-gap}
\end{equation}
If $\Omega_x:=\supp(\nabla f(x))$, then
\begin{equation}
  M(x)
  \leq\sqrt{\vol(\Omega_x)}\norm{\nabla f(x)}_2
  \leq\sqrt{2\vol(\Omega_x)(f(x)-f(x^0))}.
  \label{eq:mass-gap-support}
\end{equation}
Thus a function-gap rate becomes a mass rate only after controlling the
explored support volume--the very locality issue under study.

\paragraph{Momentum can cause high-degree transient exploration.}
The star construction of \citet{fountoulakis2026complexity} rules out the
principle that acceleration automatically preserves a small support envelope.
The active repository's separate coarse-forward-push/SOR analysis also finds
a long-spider obstruction for a fixed optimal-SOR FIFO tail.  These results do
not disprove the AESP burn-in hybrid, but they prevent a proof that ignores
transient graph geometry.

The central open statement can therefore be written cleanly as follows.

\begin{conjecture}[Early AESP locality]
\label{conj:early-locality}
For the AESP burn-in stopped at
$U_J\leq\alpha^{3/2}\epsppr/(1+\alpha)$, there exists a graph-independent constant
$K$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \max_{t\leq J}
  \frac{\overline{\vol}(S_t)}{\gamma_t}
  \leq\frac{K}{\epsppr}.
  \label{eq:early-locality-conjecture}
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

The available papers do not prove \cref{conj:early-locality}.  A valid paper
claim should therefore present either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
  \item a proof of \cref{conj:early-locality};
  \item a weaker structural theorem such as
        \cref{cor:confinement-total};
  \item a modified AESP mechanism that enforces confinement; or
  \item a different burn-in amortization that bypasses $\Lambda_t$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Possible mechanisms for closing the locality gap}
\label{sec:paths}

The following paths are serious research directions, but none is claimed as a
completed universal proof.

\subsection{Over-regularized AESP burn-in}

Introduce a temporary $\ell_1$ term with parameter $\rho\asymp\epsppr$ in the
burn-in shifted subproblems, use a slightly larger regularization to create a
uniform KKT margin, and switch to the unregularized safe tail after the core is
identified.  The hoped-for chain is
\[
  \vol(S^\star(\rho))=\bigO(1/\epsppr)
  \quad+\quad
  \text{no percolation}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Lambda_J=\bigO(1/\epsppr).
\]
The technical challenge is to preserve Catalyst's inexact proximal guarantee
while the temporary composite objective differs from the final unregularized
quadratic.

\subsection{Restart controlled by the original gradient mass}

At the end of each AESP stage, evaluate
$M(x^{(t)})=\norm{D^{1/2}\nabla f(x^{(t)})}_1$ and keep the best checkpoint.
Reject an
outer extrapolation that causes a prescribed mass explosion, or reset to
$x^{(t)}$.  A useful inner transfer inequality can be obtained because the
proximal term vanishes at the center and the inner weighted-$\ell_1$ gradient
contracts.  What remains is to prove that the restart frequency does not
destroy the $\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ outer rate.

\subsection{Priority inner updates}

Replace FIFO LOCAPPR by the density rule \cref{eq:priority-rule}.  Under a
support envelope, \cref{eq:priority-amortization} gives a direct work charge
to the active ratio.  This may trade a priority-queue logarithm for a cleaner
locality theorem.

\subsection{Adaptive $\ell_2$ inner certificate}

The AESP paper notes that the conservative mixed-norm stopping condition can
force a coordinate threshold of order $\epsppr^2$ near the final target.  During
burn-in one can test the local process at progressively finer thresholds and
stop as soon as
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\nabla h_t(z)}_2
  \leq\sqrt{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\rm A})\phi_t}.
  \label{eq:l2-inner-certificate}
\end{equation}
Because $\sqrt{\phi_t}$ rather than $\phi_t$ appears, this route may recover a
linear $1/\epsppr$ dependence.  The missing issue is evaluating and maintaining
the global $\ell_2$ certificate at genuinely local cost.

\subsection{Verified accelerated tail}

After an AESP checkpoint, one can experimentally try a block of optimal SOR
or LOCCH and accept it only if an observable residual norm decreases while a
weighted-mass envelope remains controlled.  A failed block is rolled back and
the algorithm returns to AESP or safe LOCSOR.  This is a practical state
machine, but its strongest theorem is a safeguarded/verified bound rather than
an unconditional theorem for plain LOCCH.
