\begin{corollary}[Online rent-or-buy orientation]
\label{cor:online-harmonic-rent-or-buy}
Use the certified adaptive banks of
\cref{thm:certified-adaptive-coded-bank-reuse}.  At level $\ell$, let
$p_\ell$ be its target-row count, let $\mathcal J_\ell$ be the queries
assigned to that level, and put
\begin{equation*}
 R_\ell^{\rm src}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}r_j.
\end{equation*}
There is an online exact orientation rule, requiring neither the future query
count nor $K_{\max}$, whose total number of anchor right-hand sides at this
level is at most
\begin{equation}
 2\min\{p_\ell,R_\ell^{\rm src}\}.
 \label{eq:online-orientation-rhs-count}
\end{equation}
Consequently the epoch-wide number is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
   \sum_\ell\min\{p_\ell,R_\ell^{\rm src}\}
 \right)
 \leq
 O\!\left(\min\left\{
   P_{\rm epoch},\sum_jr_j
 \right\}\right).
 \label{eq:epoch-orientation-rhs-count}
\end{equation}

The rule initially rents: it applies each query by the source orientation of
\cref{thm:bidirectional-harmonic-application}.  Immediately before the next
source block would make the cumulative rented width reach $p_\ell$, it buys:
it builds the $p_\ell$ target harmonic extensions and uses the target
orientation thereafter.  Measurements, decoded candidates, and exact
validation are unchanged, so the adaptive coupling of
\cref{thm:certified-adaptive-coded-bank-reuse} is preserved.

This closes the anchor-right-hand-side count, not the graph-local work bound.
Every rental still pays
\cref{eq:source-oriented-coded-bank-work}; after a purchase, every use still
pays the stored-response reads and multiplication in
\cref{eq:certified-harmonic-build-apply-split}.  These are the two remaining
implementation charges.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If the threshold is never reached, the rule rents
$R_\ell^{\rm src}<p_\ell$ right-hand sides.  Otherwise it rents fewer than
$p_\ell$ right-hand sides and then buys exactly $p_\ell$ target right-hand
sides.  In the latter case
$R_\ell^{\rm src}\geq p_\ell$, so both cases prove
\cref{eq:online-orientation-rhs-count}.  Summing over levels and using
$\sum_\ell p_\ell=P_{\rm epoch}$ gives
\cref{eq:epoch-orientation-rhs-count}.  Exactness is
\cref{eq:bidirectional-harmonic-identity}; the rent-or-buy decision depends
only on declared dimensions, never on bank contents.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Slack-normalized partial-gate packing]
\label{thm:slack-normalized-partial-gate-packing}
Let
$S_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_J$ be a monotone nested exact-face trace,
put $B_j:=S_{j+1}\setminus S_j$, and let
$z^j:=x[S_j]$.  Fix an exterior set $W\subseteq V\setminus S_J$ and define
its normalized exact demand at stage $j$ by
\begin{equation*}
 r_v^j:=\frac{c_v-Q_{vS_j}z^j_{S_j}}{\sqrt{d_v}},
 \qquad v\in W.
\end{equation*}
Fix a gate $g\in\mathbb R$ and a finite-band width $m>0$.  Retain separately
the checkpoint band $\{v:r_v^0>g-m\}$, and on the remaining labels put
\begin{equation*}
 W_-:=\{v\in W:r_v^0\leq g-m\},
 \qquad s_v:=g-r_v^0\geq m.
\end{equation*}

For each $j$, let $E_{B_j}$ embed $\mathbb R^{B_j}$ into
$\mathbb R^{S_{j+1}}$, and define
\begin{align*}
 H_j&:=E_{B_j}^TQ_{S_{j+1}S_{j+1}}^{-1}E_{B_j},\\
 u_j&:=E_{B_j}^TQ_{S_{j+1}S_{j+1}}
               (z^{j+1}-z^j)_{S_{j+1}},\\
 R_j&:=-D_{W_-}^{-1/2}Q_{W_-,S_{j+1}}
       Q_{S_{j+1}S_{j+1}}^{-1}E_{B_j}H_j^{-1/2},\\
 a_j&:=H_j^{1/2}u_j.
\end{align*}
Horizontally concatenate the $R_j$, vertically stack the $a_j$, and put
\begin{equation}
 R:=[R_0\ \cdots\ R_{J-1}],
 \qquad
 a:=(a_0^T,\ldots,a_{J-1}^T)^T,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E:=\|a\|_2^2
 =\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\|z^{j+1}-z^j\|_Q^2,
 \label{eq:slack-packed-response-factor}
\end{equation}
and $b:=\sum_j|B_j|$.  With
$D_s:=\operatorname{diag}(s_v:v\in W_-)$, the slack-normalized response
matrix
\begin{equation*}
 X:=D_s^{-1}R
\end{equation*}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|X\|_F^2\leq\frac{b}{m^2}.
 \label{eq:slack-packed-mass}
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal E>0$, every far label that crosses the gate belongs to one heavy
row set:
\begin{equation}
 \{v\in W_-:r_v^J>g\}
 \subseteq
 \left\{v\in W_-:\|X_{v,:}\|_2^2>\frac1{\mathcal E}\right\}.
 \label{eq:slack-crossing-heavy-containment}
\end{equation}
Moreover its charged degree volume obeys
\begin{equation}
 \vol\{v\in W_-:r_v^J>g\}
 \leq\frac{b\mathcal E}{m^2}.
 \label{eq:slack-crossing-volume}
\end{equation}

When $\mathcal E>0$, consequently applying
\cref{thm:coded-bucket-row-locator} to $X$ with
\begin{equation*}
 \theta:=\mathcal E^{-1},
 \qquad
 K:=\max\{1,b\mathcal E/m^2\}
\end{equation*}
produces, with its declared failure probability, a candidate superset of
every far-to-violating crossing using
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  K\log^2N\log(N/\delta)
 \right)
 \label{eq:slack-packed-target-rows}
\end{equation}
target rows and
$O(K\log N\log(N/\delta))$ candidates.  The checkpoint transition band is
not claimed empty or free; its maintenance, every target-row application,
and exact candidate validation remain charged separately.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{eq:dual-frontier-support},
$Q_{S_{j+1}S_{j+1}}(z^{j+1}-z^j)_{S_{j+1}}$ is supported on $B_j$.
Hence
\begin{equation*}
 (z^{j+1}-z^j)_{S_{j+1}}
 =Q_{S_{j+1}S_{j+1}}^{-1}E_{B_j}u_j,
 \qquad
 \|a_j\|_2^2=\|z^{j+1}-z^j\|_Q^2.
\end{equation*}
The exterior demand increments therefore factor as
\begin{equation}
 (r^J-r^0)_{W_-}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}R_ja_j
 =Ra.
 \label{eq:slack-packed-demand-factor}
\end{equation}
Monotonicity makes every coordinate of this vector nonnegative.

The squared norm of row $v$ of $R_j$ is
$(\chi_v^{B_j})^2/d_v$ in the notation of
\cref{lem:source-aware-leverage-packing}.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \|X\|_F^2
 =\sum_{v\in W_-}\frac1{s_v^2d_v}
   \sum_j(\chi_v^{B_j})^2
 \leq\frac1{m^2}\sum_j|B_j|,
\end{equation*}
which proves \cref{eq:slack-packed-mass}.

If $r_v^J>g$, then
$(Ra)_v>s_v$.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation*}
 1<\frac{|(Ra)_v|^2}{s_v^2}
 \leq\|X_{v,:}\|_2^2\|a\|_2^2,
\end{equation*}
proving \cref{eq:slack-crossing-heavy-containment}.  The same inequality and
$s_v\geq m$ imply
\begin{equation*}
 d_vm^2
 <\mathcal E\sum_j(\chi_v^{B_j})^2.
\end{equation*}
Sum over crossing labels and apply
\cref{eq:source-aware-leverage-sum} separately to every $j$ to obtain
\cref{eq:slack-crossing-volume}.  Finally,
\cref{eq:slack-packed-mass} is precisely the mass cap
$\|X\|_F^2\leq K\theta$; the coded-locator bounds follow directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Realized directional slack bank]
\label{cor:realized-directional-slack-bank}
Under the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:slack-normalized-partial-gate-packing}, define the single realized
normalized-change column
\begin{equation}
 y:=D_s^{-1}(r^J-r^0)_{W_-}=Xa.
 \label{eq:realized-slack-direction}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \|y\|_2^2\leq\frac{b\mathcal E}{m^2},
 \qquad
 \{v\in W_-:r_v^J>g\}\subseteq\{v:y_v>1\}.
 \label{eq:realized-slack-mass-crossing}
\end{equation}
Consequently the coded locator applied to the one-column matrix $y$, with
$\theta=1$ and
\begin{equation*}
 K:=\max\{1,b\mathcal E/m^2\},
\end{equation*}
contains every far crossing with its declared probability and uses
$\widetilde O(1+b\mathcal E/m^2)$ target rows and candidates.

This state is causal and append-only for a fixed target bank and fixed
checkpoint slacks.  At prefix $t$, put
\begin{equation*}
 y^{[t]}:=D_s^{-1}(r^t-r^0)_{W_-}.
\end{equation*}
Every scalar total/bit bucket measurement satisfies
\begin{equation}
 M(y^{[t+1]})=M(y^{[t]})
   +M\!\left(D_s^{-1}(r^{t+1}-r^t)_{W_-}\right).
 \label{eq:directional-bucket-append}
\end{equation}
Thus the decoder retains one scalar per bucket record and never stores or
replays an earlier correction direction.  In a harmonic implementation, the
target row again multiplies the column for $v$ by $1/s_v$, and each exact
correction supplies one new directional response update through
\cref{thm:bidirectional-harmonic-application}.  The online rent-or-buy rule
remains applicable.  Target-row installation and application, capacity
growth, checkpoint-slack refresh, transition-band state, and exact validation
remain charged.

Unlike \cref{cor:gaussian-compressed-slack-bank}, this corollary has no
Gaussian source layer: it locates crossings for the realized accumulated
correction rather than preserving correction-independent row energies for all
possible directions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{eq:slack-packed-mass,eq:slack-packed-response-factor},
\begin{equation*}
 \|y\|_2^2=\|Xa\|_2^2
 \leq\|X\|_F^2\|a\|_2^2
 \leq\frac{b\mathcal E}{m^2}.
\end{equation*}
If $r_v^J>g$, then
$y_v=(r_v^J-r_v^0)/s_v>1$, proving
\cref{eq:realized-slack-mass-crossing}.  The coded-locator conclusion follows
from \cref{thm:coded-bucket-row-locator}.  Finally, every bucket measurement
is linear in its input column, so
\cref{eq:directional-bucket-append} follows from
$y^{[t+1]}-y^{[t]}=D_s^{-1}(r^{t+1}-r^t)_{W_-}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian-compressed slack bank]
\label{cor:gaussian-compressed-slack-bank}
Under the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:slack-normalized-partial-gate-packing}, assume
$\mathcal E>0$ and let
$G\in\mathbb R^{b\times r}$ have independent standard Gaussian entries and
put
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde X:=\frac1{\sqrt r}XG.
\end{equation*}
There is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that, for
$r\geq c\log(4N/\delta)$, with probability at least $1-\delta$ a coded bank
applied to $\widetilde X$ contains every crossing in
\cref{eq:slack-crossing-heavy-containment}, uses
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(1+\frac{b\mathcal E}{m^2}\right)
\end{equation*}
target rows and candidates, and has only $r=O(\log(N/\delta))$ source
columns.  More explicitly one may take row threshold
$1/(2\mathcal E)$ and mass parameter
$K=\max\{1,3b\mathcal E/m^2\}$, with the coded-bank failure budget included
in the displayed probability.

In the harmonic realization, the target measurement matrix is the usual
hash-and-bit bank with its column for $v$ multiplied by $1/s_v$; the existing
transposed harmonic identity applies without change.  The Chebyshev source
whitening in \cref{thm:chebyshev-frontier-whitening} supplies the corresponding
Gaussian response columns up to its explicit constant sandwich.  Those
constant factors only rescale the declared threshold and capacity.  This
corollary bounds measurement dimension, not harmonic construction or
application work.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Standard Gaussian norm concentration and a union bound over at most $N$ rows
give, simultaneously,
\begin{equation*}
 \tfrac12\|X_{v,:}\|_2^2
 \leq\|\widetilde X_{v,:}\|_2^2
 \leq\tfrac32\|X_{v,:}\|_2^2
 \qquad(v\in W_-)
\end{equation*}
with failure probability at most $\delta/2$.  On this event every crossing
row has sampled energy greater than $1/(2\mathcal E)$, while
\begin{equation*}
 \|\widetilde X\|_F^2
 \leq\tfrac32\|X\|_F^2
 \leq\frac{3b}{2m^2}.
\end{equation*}
Apply \cref{thm:coded-bucket-row-locator} with the remaining failure budget
$\delta/2$.  The harmonic statement is linear substitution of the diagonal
slack scaling into
\cref{eq:transposed-harmonic-sketch,eq:transposed-response-sketch}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Append-only bucket-norm accumulation]
\label{lem:append-only-bucket-norm-accumulation}
Fix one hash-and-bit target bank and fixed row weights, and let source blocks
$X^{(1)},X^{(2)},\ldots$ arrive as new column blocks.  Put
$X^{[t]}:=[X^{(1)}\ \cdots\ X^{(t)}]$.  The exact decoder of
\cref{thm:coded-bucket-row-locator} for every prefix $X^{[t]}$ can be
maintained without retaining or replaying any earlier source column.

For every bucket/bit record, retain the two scalars
\begin{equation*}
 A_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}:=\|M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 C_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}
 :=\|M_{s,b,0}^{[t]}-M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}\|_2^2.
\end{equation*}
On arrival of block $t+1$, add the squared norms of its two corresponding
bucket vectors.  This uses $O(p)$ retained scalar cells and
$O(pr_{t+1})$ transient arithmetic after the new $p\times r_{t+1}$
measurements have been supplied.  At any prefix, comparing
$A_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}$ and $C_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}$ gives exactly the batched bit
test \cref{eq:coded-locator-bit-test}.

Thus fresh causal source blocks do not themselves force source-history
storage or replay.  The claim requires a fixed bank and fixed slack weights.
Allocating a larger bank after old blocks have been discarded, changing an
old row weight, constructing its harmonic representation, and supplying each
new measurement block are not free and are not covered by this lemma.  A new
label whose past row is identically zero may be installed prospectively
without source replay, but its response-state update remains charged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every bucket record, column concatenation gives
\begin{align*}
 \|M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t+1]}\|_2^2
 &=\|M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}\|_2^2
   +\|M_{s,b,\ell}^{(t+1)}\|_2^2,\\
 \|M_{s,b,0}^{[t+1]}-M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t+1]}\|_2^2
 &=\|M_{s,b,0}^{[t]}-M_{s,b,\ell}^{[t]}\|_2^2\\
 &\quad+
   \|M_{s,b,0}^{(t+1)}-M_{s,b,\ell}^{(t+1)}\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
The summands occupy disjoint source coordinates, so there are no cross terms.
Induction proves that the retained scalars are the exact prefix norms used by
the decoder.  The work and storage bounds follow by counting the $p$ records
and the new source width.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Three-label slack reweighting obstructs norm-only partial
refresh]
\label{prop:three-label-norm-only-reweight-obstruction}
Consider one fixed repetition and one bucket of the hash-and-bit bank in
\cref{thm:coded-bucket-row-locator}.  Three colliding labels
$v_0,v_1,v_2$ have binary identifiers $00,01,10$ and realized signs $+1$.
Before a checkpoint-slack change, give every row weight one and let the old
source prefix have one scalar column.  The two possible row histories
\begin{equation}
 X=(-4,1,1)^T,
 \qquad
 X'=(-2,-1,-1)^T
 \label{eq:three-label-reweight-histories}
\end{equation}
produce exactly the same complete append-only bucket-norm state from
\cref{lem:append-only-bucket-norm-accumulation}:
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf N(X)=\mathsf N(X')
 :=(A_1,C_1,A_2,C_2)=(1,9,1,9).
 \label{eq:three-label-old-norm-state}
\end{equation}
Here bit one isolates $v_1$ and has complement $v_0+v_2$, while bit two
isolates $v_2$ and has complement $v_0+v_1$.

Now refresh the positive inverse-slack row weights from $(1,1,1)$ to
\begin{equation}
 w^+=(1,3,2),
 \qquad
 s^+=(1,1/3,1/2),
 \label{eq:three-label-new-slack-weights}
\end{equation}
where $s^+$ is the corresponding new slack vector.  In the first bit test,
the reweighted bit-bucket norm squared is $9$ for both histories, whereas the
reweighted complement norm squared is
\begin{equation}
 \|X_{v_0}+2X_{v_2}\|_2^2=4,
 \qquad
 \|X'_{v_0}+2X'_{v_2}\|_2^2=16.
 \label{eq:three-label-reweight-flip}
\end{equation}
Thus \cref{eq:coded-locator-bit-test} decodes that bit as one for $X$ and
zero for $X'$.  No deterministic function of the retained state
\cref{eq:three-label-old-norm-state}, the fixed bank metadata, and the new
weights can refresh the exact decoder for both histories.

This obstruction does not rely on negative row entries.  With two source
columns, the nonnegative histories
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&2\\1&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
have common old state $(1,5,1,5)$, while the same new weights make the first
comparison $9>8$ in the first history and $9<10$ in the second.

Three colliding labels are minimal for this exact state obstruction.  With at
most two labels, injective identifiers have a separating bit, whose two sides
give the two individual squared row norms.  Whenever another bit groups the
two rows, its old grouped norm also gives their inner product; when no bit
groups them, that inner product is not needed.  Hence every reweighted bit and
complement norm is reconstructible for one or two labels.

Name the attempted no-replay interface \textsc{NormOnlyWeightRefresh}.  It
stages one version/capacity header and the three positive weights, validates
the header, identifiers, weight positivity, and fixed-bank identity, and is
forbidden to read an old source cell, scan the anchor cut, apply a response,
or retain any prechange state beyond \cref{eq:three-label-old-norm-state}.
Universal exactness therefore forces it to reject before persistent mutation.
One such valid-but-unrefreshable request has the complete vector
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &(C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj},R_{\rm int},C_{\rm pre},C_{\rm ctl},C_{\rm rec},
 C_{\rm resp},M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit})\\
 &=\bigl(0,0,1,0,\Theta(1),0,0,\Theta(1),\Theta(1),0,1\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:three-label-reweight-rejection-eleven-vector}
\end{equation}
The control coordinate charges the capacity/weight header, all three weight
reads, and validation; the sole emission is an explicit rejection record.
Repeated rejected attempts add this vector attempt by attempt.

For comparison, \textsc{ReplayThreeWeightRefresh} is an exact accepted
micro-implementation when the three old row values in each of the two
nonnegative source columns above are available as six stored response cells.
It reads all six cells, forms the four reweighted two-cell bucket/complement
vectors, overwrites the four retained norms, performs the exact bit tests,
and emits one refreshed decoder record.  Charging the twelve numerical writes
(eight measurement cells and four norms), its complete vector is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &(C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj},R_{\rm int},C_{\rm pre},C_{\rm ctl},C_{\rm rec},
 C_{\rm resp},M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit})\\
 &=\bigl(0,0,1,0,\Theta(1),0,6,\Theta(1),\Theta(1),12,1\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:three-label-reweight-replay-eleven-vector}
\end{equation}
If those six old cells are not stored, a source-oriented reconstruction must
instead add its actual anchor-cut scan and anchor-response application to
$C_{\rm resp}$ (and any newly exposed adjacency to $C_{\rm adj}$ and
$R_{\rm adj}$); neither cost is hidden in
\cref{eq:three-label-reweight-replay-eleven-vector}.

This is an exact-real, one-bucket, named-state STOP.  It proves neither that
every weight refresh costs six response reads nor that arbitrary bank
reweighting needs quadratic state.  Retaining a richer Gram sketch, replaying
old source blocks, rebuilding from the exposed cut, using a collision-free
bank, changing the decoder, or proving a different support-safe trace all
remain possible.  It also says nothing about template mutation, an RPPR/KKT
chronology, terminal PPR/RPPR output, finite precision, coefficient bits, or
word-RAM complexity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $X$, both isolated entries equal one and both complements equal $-3$;
for $X'$, both isolated entries equal $-1$ and both complements again equal
$-3$.  Squaring proves \cref{eq:three-label-old-norm-state}.  Under
$w^+$, the first isolated entry has magnitude three in either history, while
the two complements are $-2$ and $-4$, proving
\cref{eq:three-label-reweight-flip} and the indistinguishability claim.  The
two nonnegative matrix products are direct and give the displayed $8$ and
$10$ complement norms.

For minimality, absorb the known hash signs into the at-most-two row vectors.
An injective binary encoding supplies a bit separating them, so the retained
norms on that bit are their individual squared norms.  If a second bit groups
them on one side, the retained squared norm of the sum determines twice their
inner product after subtracting the two individual norms.  Positive diagonal
reweighting then evaluates every grouped norm by the polarization identity.
If no bit groups them, every measured side is empty or a singleton and
reweighting is immediate.  This also covers a one-label bucket.

Finally, a deterministic no-replay interface receives identical retained
inputs on the two histories but owes opposite exact first-bit replies, so it
cannot accept both.  The two resource vectors follow by counting the named
validation/rejection interface and the explicit six-read, twelve-write replay
schedule coordinate by coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three-label pivot-Gram refresh]
\label{thm:three-label-pivot-gram-refresh}
Fix one repetition and one bucket containing exactly the three sign-absorbed
rows $z_0,z_1,z_2\in\mathbb R^r$ with identifiers $00,01,10$.  Retain the
canonical unweighted five-scalar state
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S
 :=(P_0,A_1,C_1,A_2,C_2)
 :=\bigl(
 \|z_0\|_2^2,
 \|z_1\|_2^2,
 \|z_0+z_2\|_2^2,
 \|z_2\|_2^2,
 \|z_0+z_1\|_2^2
 \bigr).
 \label{eq:three-label-pivot-gram-state}
\end{equation}
Then the two needed pivot Gram entries are
\begin{equation}
 g_{02}:=\langle z_0,z_2\rangle
 =\frac{C_1-P_0-A_2}{2},
 \qquad
 g_{01}:=\langle z_0,z_1\rangle
 =\frac{C_2-P_0-A_1}{2}.
 \label{eq:three-label-pivot-recovery}
\end{equation}
Consequently every positive weight triple $w=(w_0,w_1,w_2)$ has exact
refreshed decoder norms
\begin{align}
 A_1(w)&=w_1^2A_1,
 &C_1(w)&=w_0^2P_0+w_2^2A_2+2w_0w_2g_{02},
 \notag\\
 A_2(w)&=w_2^2A_2,
 &C_2(w)&=w_0^2P_0+w_1^2A_1+2w_0w_1g_{01}.
 \label{eq:three-label-pivot-refresh}
\end{align}

The state in \cref{eq:three-label-pivot-gram-state} remains canonical and
unweighted.  In particular, after any refresh at current weights $w$, let a
new source block of width $r_{\rm new}$ have sign-absorbed unweighted rows
$\widehat z_0,\widehat z_1,\widehat z_2$.  The ordinary total and two bit
measurements arrive as
\begin{equation}
 M_1=w_1\widehat z_1,
 \qquad
 M_2=w_2\widehat z_2,
 \qquad
 M_0=w_0\widehat z_0+w_1\widehat z_1+w_2\widehat z_2.
 \label{eq:three-label-weighted-append-measurements}
\end{equation}
Before changing the canonical state, recover
\begin{equation}
 \widehat z_1=M_1/w_1,
 \qquad
 \widehat z_2=M_2/w_2,
 \qquad
 \widehat z_0=(M_0-M_1-M_2)/w_0,
 \label{eq:three-label-unweighted-append-recovery}
\end{equation}
and add the five increments defined by
\cref{eq:three-label-pivot-gram-state} with $z_i$ replaced by
$\widehat z_i$.  Thus arbitrary interleavings of source-block appends and
positive weight refreshes are exact; no old source block is replayed.
Supplying the three measurements remains charged to the underlying harmonic
or response implementation.

Name the refresh operation
\begin{equation*}
 \textsc{ThreeLabelPivotGramRefresh}.
\end{equation*}
It validates one fixed-bucket/version header and three positive weights, evaluates
\cref{eq:three-label-pivot-recovery,eq:three-label-pivot-refresh}, materializes
the four refreshed decoder norms, and emits one decoder record.  The canonical
state uses one additional persistent scalar compared with the four-norm state
in \cref{prop:three-label-norm-only-reweight-obstruction}.  Its complete
per-refresh vector is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &(C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj},R_{\rm int},C_{\rm pre},C_{\rm ctl},C_{\rm rec},
 C_{\rm resp},M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit})\\
 &=\bigl(0,0,1,0,\Theta(1),0,0,\Theta(1),\Theta(1),4,1\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:three-label-pivot-refresh-eleven-vector}
\end{equation}
Repeated refreshes add this vector request by request; source-block append
arithmetic and measurement supply are separate from it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding $C_1$ and $C_2$ in
\cref{eq:three-label-pivot-gram-state} gives
\cref{eq:three-label-pivot-recovery}.  In the first bit, $z_1$ is alone and
$z_0,z_2$ share the complementary side; in the second bit, $z_2$ is alone and
$z_0,z_1$ share the complementary side.  Expanding the corresponding weighted
squared norms proves \cref{eq:three-label-pivot-refresh}.

Positivity of the current weights makes
\cref{eq:three-label-unweighted-append-recovery} exact.  Column concatenation
adds squared norms and inner products across disjoint old and new source
coordinates, so the five recovered increments produce exactly the canonical
state of the concatenated unweighted rows.  Induction over an arbitrary
append/refresh sequence proves repeated exactness.  Counting the one extra
canonical scalar, four materialized decoder norms, validation workspace, and
one emitted record gives
\cref{eq:three-label-pivot-refresh-eleven-vector}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Four-label obstruction to one-pivot refresh]
\label{prop:four-label-one-pivot-refresh-obstruction}
The five-scalar state of
\cref{thm:three-label-pivot-gram-refresh} is not a universal state after a
fourth collision.  Give the codes $00,01,10,11$ realized signs $+1$ and unit
old weights.  The scalar histories
\begin{equation}
 X=(-6,-5,-5,4)^T,
 \qquad
 X'=(-6,-5,-5,6)^T
 \label{eq:four-label-pivot-histories}
\end{equation}
have the common pivot $P_0=36$ and common four decoder norms
\begin{equation}
 (A_1,C_1,A_2,C_2)=(1,121,1,121).
 \label{eq:four-label-pivot-common-state}
\end{equation}
After the positive refresh $w=(1,1,1,3)$, the first-bit isolated-side norm is
$49$ for $X$ and $169$ for $X'$, while its complement norm remains $121$.
Hence the exact first-bit comparison is $49<121$ in the first history and
$169>121$ in the second.  No deterministic refresh from this named
five-scalar state can be exact on both histories.

This is a sharp STOP for extending the one-pivot three-label state unchanged
to four labels.  It is not a lower bound on richer Gram state, nonlinear
encodings into unrestricted real cells, source replay, or another decoder.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For either history, the first bit has sides $z_1+z_3=\mp1$ and
$z_0+z_2=-11$; the second has sides $z_2+z_3=\mp1$ and
$z_0+z_1=-11$.  This proves
\cref{eq:four-label-pivot-common-state}.  Reweighting the first isolated side
gives $-5+3\cdot4=7$ and $-5+3\cdot6=13$, while the complement is unchanged.
Squaring proves the two opposite comparisons and the indistinguishability
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Co-side Gram refresh]
\label{thm:co-side-gram-refresh}
Fix one bucket containing $k$ sign-absorbed rows
$z_1,\ldots,z_k\in\mathbb R^r$ with distinct codes in $\{0,1\}^L$.  Call an
unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ \emph{co-side} when the two codes agree in at least
one bit, and let $c$ be the number of pairs whose codes are bitwise
complements.  Retain the canonical state
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf G_{\rm side}
 :=\{G_{ii}:1\leq i\leq k\}
 \mathbin{\cup}
 \{G_{ij}:i<j,\ \{i,j\}\text{ is co-side}\},
 \qquad
 G_{ij}:=\langle z_i,z_j\rangle.
 \label{eq:co-side-gram-state}
\end{equation}
For every positive weight vector $w\in\mathbb R_{>0}^k$, every bit
$\ell$, and side $a\in\{0,1\}$, its exact weighted decoder norm is
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\sum_{i:\operatorname{bit}_\ell(i)=a}w_i z_i\right\|_2^2
 =\sum_{i:\operatorname{bit}_\ell(i)=a}w_i^2G_{ii}
 +2\!\sum_{\substack{i<j:\operatorname{bit}_\ell(i)=
                     \operatorname{bit}_\ell(j)=a}}
       w_iw_jG_{ij}.
 \label{eq:co-side-gram-refresh}
\end{equation}
Thus the named \textsc{CoSideGramRefresh} state is sufficient for arbitrary
repeated positive reweights.  Complementarity is a matching on the distinct
codes, so $c\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and its exact dimension is
\begin{equation}
 d_{\rm side}=k+\binom{k}{2}-c=\Theta(k^2).
 \label{eq:co-side-gram-dimension}
\end{equation}

The dimension in \cref{eq:co-side-gram-dimension} is also necessary only in
the following explicit model: the retained cells are linear statistics of
the Gram matrix, $r\geq k$, and from them one must reconstruct every exact
side norm in \cref{eq:co-side-gram-refresh} for all positive weights on an
open set.  No unrestricted real-cell information lower bound is claimed.

Maintenance is not free.  If a new width-$r_{\rm new}$ block supplies every
individual unweighted row $\widehat z_i$, then updating
\cref{eq:co-side-gram-state} costs
\begin{equation*}
 \Theta(d_{\rm side}r_{\rm new})
 =\Theta((k^2-c)r_{\rm new})
\end{equation*}
inner-product arithmetic and $\Theta(k^2)$ persistent scalar cells.  When
$k>L+1$, the ordinary total-plus-$L$-bit measurement block has fewer vector
rows than labels and does not in general determine these individual rows or
all of their co-side Gram increments.  The implementation must therefore pay
for richer measurements or per-label reads, together with their response,
materialization, and validation costs.  This theorem proves no generic
product-scale graph-work bound, RPPR/KKT chronology, terminal solver result,
finite-precision guarantee, or word/bit complexity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two distinct binary codes fail to share a bit side exactly when they differ
in every bit, that is, when they are bitwise complements.  Every code has at
most one complement, so the omitted pairs form a matching of size $c$.
Expanding each squared side sum gives
\cref{eq:co-side-gram-refresh}; every off-diagonal term appearing there is
co-side, and no complementary-pair entry can appear.  Counting the retained
diagonals and all noncomplementary pairs proves
\cref{eq:co-side-gram-dimension} and sufficiency.

For the qualified necessity statement, positive-definite $k\times k$ Gram
matrices form an open subset of the symmetric matrices when $r\geq k$.
Viewed as a polynomial in the positive weights, a side norm has coefficient
$G_{ii}$ on $w_i^2$ and coefficient $2G_{ij}$ on $w_iw_j$ exactly when
$i,j$ share that side.  Exact reconstruction on an open weight set therefore
recovers all $d_{\rm side}$ displayed Gram coordinate functionals.  Any
linear Gram-statistic state from which they are recovered must have them in
its row span and hence have dimension at least $d_{\rm side}$.

For an appended block, each retained entry increases by the corresponding
width-$r_{\rm new}$ inner product, proving the stated update count.  Finally,
the ordinary coded measurements are only $L+1$ linear combinations of the
$k$ row vectors.  If $k>L+1$, their coefficient map has a nontrivial kernel,
so those measurements do not identify the individual rows and, in general,
do not identify every required Gram increment.
\end{proof}

Together,
\cref{thm:slack-normalized-partial-gate-packing,cor:realized-directional-slack-bank,cor:gaussian-compressed-slack-bank,lem:append-only-bucket-norm-accumulation,prop:three-label-norm-only-reweight-obstruction,thm:three-label-pivot-gram-refresh,prop:four-label-one-pivot-refresh-obstruction,thm:co-side-gram-refresh}
close the information-level parts of an epoch-internal partial query.
Checkpoint slack normalization handles heterogeneous far-label margins with
one heavy-row threshold.  For the realized gate, one accumulated directional
column suffices and its scalar bucket measurements update linearly with no
source history.  If correction-independent row energies are needed instead,
Gaussian compression reduces the concatenated batch source dimension to
logarithmic size and a fixed coded bank retains exact prefix decoder norms
without storing old source columns.  That fixed-weight state has an exact
boundary: a three-label collision cannot be refreshed after a genuine slack
reweight from its retained norms alone.  One additional pivot norm repairs
that exact three-label bucket across arbitrary repeated positive refreshes,
provided weighted append measurements are first converted back to canonical
unweighted rows.  Four labels already obstruct the same one-pivot state.  A
general exact repair retains precisely the co-side Gram entries, but this is
quadratic bucket state and its append maintenance needs richer measurements
or per-label reads once the occupancy exceeds $L+1$.  A correct scalable
implementation must exploit sparser collision structure, replay old
response/source cells geometrically, rebuild from the cut, use a different
bank/decoder, or reject.  These results do not yet
bound installation or application of the slack-weighted harmonic target rows,
growth beyond a bank's declared capacity, a paid strategy for checkpoint
slack-weight changes, maintenance of the transition band, or exact
validation.  Those are now the entire response-side implementation ledger for
this route.
