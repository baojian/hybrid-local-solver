\section{Falsifiable conclusion and next proof step}
\label{sec:terminal-modal-conclusion}

Exact orthogonality and the even-mode band remove spatial and temporal
cancellation.  The finite-$q$ correction theorem proves the proper-prefix
chronology, so the optimum formula gives
\eqref{eq:terminal-modal-source-transform} unconditionally for $m\ge64$,
while the ideal-sign lemma separates the packet from its correction.  The
position and velocity targets are proved on the growing band in
\cref{lem:terminal-modal-position-profile,lem:terminal-modal-velocity-profile}.
The correlated stopped ledger proves the early binomial-window half-retention
and hence the literal nonlinear regime for $qk<3/50$.  The folded-source
completion proves every interior static two-step row uniformly from $m=64$;
the five moving-frontier rows have strictly positive joint first-order
limits, and the finite causal-transfer ledger gives the explicit common
cutoff $m\ge4096$.  Their combination proves the static $21q^3/80$ target in
that range.  The late position lower bound then gives raw positivity and the
unclipped safe envelope for every time in the continued named recurrence.
For all sufficiently large $m$, exact modal anti-cancellation separately
yields the terminal certificate block through
\eqref{eq:terminal-modal-horizon}, and hence the unconditional asymptotic bound
\eqref{eq:terminal-modal-log-unconditional}.

That logarithmic block belongs to the prescribed one-sided certificate, not
to semantic accuracy.  The same positive propagators and the all-time
unclipped envelope prove \eqref{eq:terminal-modal-semantic-stop} at
$k=\lceil15/(4q)\rceil$ for every $m\ge4096$.  Returning at the earlier of
that theorem time and the prescribed certificate uses $O(q^{-2})$ charged
work on this family.  The measured first crossing near $qk=3.43$ is
consistent with, but not used by, this more conservative exact bound.

The result is deliberately narrow: it proves a lower block only for the
named exact-real recurrence.  It does not cover other implementations, face
schedules, local accelerated algorithms, or nonlocal response primitives,
and the cutoff is deliberately conservative; no claim is made for every
$64\le m<4096$.  In particular, neither the certificate delay nor the
semantic upper bound is an eleven-resource class theorem.
