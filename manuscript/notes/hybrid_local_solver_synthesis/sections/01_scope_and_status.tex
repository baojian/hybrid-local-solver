\section{Scope, provenance, and claim status}
\label{sec:scope}

This document is a rigorous synthesis rather than a verbatim transcript.  It
retains the substantive ideas developed in the discussion, removes duplicated
or informal passages, corrects statements that were later found to be false,
and makes every remaining proof obligation explicit.  The source results are
the locally evolving set framework of \citet{zhou2024iterative}, the AESP
framework of \citet{huang2025accelerated}, and the recent analysis of
classical acceleration for \(\ell_1\)-regularized PageRank by
\citet{fountoulakis2026complexity}.  Catalyst supplies the outer inexact
accelerated proximal-point machinery \citep{lin2018catalyst}.

The note uses one scoped PageRank normalization in order to state and prove
results.  This normalization is not asserted to be the repository-wide
canonical convention.  The active project documentation still treats the
residual convention as an open decision; consequently, any migration into the
main manuscript, solver code, or experiments must update the mathematical
conventions, the residual decision record, and the tests in the same change.

\subsection{Central research question}

The target problem is to compute an \(\eps\)-accurate personalized PageRank
vector using degree-weighted local work.  Classical monotone local methods
have worst-case work of order
\[
    \frac{1}{\alpha\eps},
\]
whereas global accelerated methods replace the condition-number dependence
\(1/\alpha\) by \(1/\sqrt{\alpha}\) but may lose locality.  The desired local
accelerated scale is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \softO\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}\eps}\right).
    \label{eq:target-complexity}
\end{equation}
AESP proves the accelerated outer-iteration dependence, but its current
worst-case local bound is
\(
\softO(R^2/(\sqrt{\alpha}\eps^2))
\).
The hybrid proposal exploits the experimentally favorable early AESP regime
and delegates the final high-accuracy cleanup to a direct local relaxation
method.

Two cautions are essential.  First, the objective-gap handoff proves a fast
\emph{tail}; it does not by itself bound the degree-weighted work spent by the
AESP burn-in.  Second, the recent star lower bound for classical FISTA shows
that small optimal support does not prevent transient activation of a
high-degree boundary vertex \citep{fountoulakis2026complexity}.  A complete
worst-case theorem must therefore control the path of active sets, not only the
final solution.
