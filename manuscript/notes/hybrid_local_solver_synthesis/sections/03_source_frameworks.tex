\section{The two source frameworks}
\label{sec:source-frameworks}

\subsection{Locally evolving set methods}

The locally evolving set framework of \citet{zhou2024iterative} represents a
local iterative method through active sets \(S_k\) satisfying one-hop
propagation:
\begin{equation}
    S_{k+1}
    \subseteq
    S_k\cup\bigcup_{u\in S_k}N(u).
    \label{eq:one-hop-evolution}
\end{equation}
Its cost is \(\sum_k\vol(S_k)\), and its trajectory-dependent locality is
measured by the average active-set volume and an active residual ratio
\(\gamma_k\).  For the monotone methods, the paper proves a bound of the form
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\overline{\vol}(S)}{\overline\gamma}
    \leq \frac{1}{\eps}.
    \label{eq:work-one-locality}
\end{equation}
This ratio often predicts observed work much more accurately than the crude
worst-case volume bound.

For the system in \cref{eq:work-one-system}, a local SOR coordinate update is
\begin{align}
    x^+&=x+\omega\widehat r_u e_u,
    \label{eq:locsor-work-one-x}\\
    \widehat r^+
    &=\widehat r-\omega\widehat r_u e_u
      +\chi\omega\widehat r_u W e_u.
    \label{eq:locsor-work-one-r}
\end{align}
At \(\omega=1\), this is local Gauss--Seidel.  The source paper proves an
unconditional local work bound at this monotone parameter.  It also identifies
the globally optimal SOR parameter
\begin{equation}
    \omega_\star
    =\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^2},
    \label{eq:optimal-sor}
\end{equation}
but does not prove an analogous graph-uniform local accelerated bound, because
over-relaxation creates signed residuals and destroys the monotonicity used by
the local work argument.

The same framework localizes gradient descent and two classical accelerated
recurrences.  \LOCGD processes an active set in parallel and retains a
monotone weighted residual.  \LOCCH and \LOCHB localize both residual and
momentum coordinates.  Their residual dynamics become nonhomogeneous
second-order recurrences, where the omitted coordinates act as a forcing term.
The source accelerated theorem is conditional on a geometric-mean bound for
these localization penalties; it is not a graph-uniform unconditional
\(\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eps))\) theorem.

\paragraph{Empirical active-set shape.}
The motivating observation is that accelerated locally evolving set methods
often produce a bell-shaped active volume: the active set is small at the
beginning, largest in a middle regime, and small again near termination.  This
is an empirical pattern rather than a universal theorem.  It suggests that a
solver should exploit acceleration while the active set is cheap and avoid
paying for its expensive middle or late regime when a monotone tail solver can
finish more economically.

\subsection{AESP: acceleration between local processes}

AESP moves momentum outside the local inner recurrence.  At outer stage
\(t\), it approximately minimizes
\begin{equation}
    h_t(z)
    :=f(z)+\frac{\eta}{2}\norm{z-y^{(t-1)}}_2^2,
    \label{eq:aesp-prox-objective}
\end{equation}
returns an inexact solution \(x^{(t)}\), and extrapolates
\begin{equation}
    y^{(t)}
    =x^{(t)}
     +\beta\bigl(x^{(t)}-x^{(t-1)}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \beta
    =\frac{\sqrt{\alpha+\eta}-\sqrt\alpha}
           {\sqrt{\alpha+\eta}+\sqrt\alpha}.
    \label{eq:aesp-momentum}
\end{equation}
Thus each inner solver is a complete monotone local process, while acceleration
acts only between processes.

The Hessian of \(h_t\) is \(Q+\eta I\), with condition number
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_{\rm in}
    =\frac{1+\eta}{\alpha+\eta}.
    \label{eq:inner-condition}
\end{equation}
For the AESP choice
\begin{equation}
    \eta=1-2\alpha,
    \label{eq:aesp-eta}
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_{\rm in}=2.
    \label{eq:inner-condition-two}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every inner shifted system has a PageRank-type damping parameter
of \(1/2\).  This constant conditioning explains why the inner local processes
can make rapid initial progress even when the original \(\alpha\) is small.

Let
\begin{equation}
    q_t^{(k)}:=D^{1/2}\grad h_t(z_t^{(k)}),
    \qquad
    S_t^{(k)}
    :=\left\{u:\frac{\abs{q_{t,u}^{(k)}}}{d_u}\geq\eps_t\right\}.
    \label{eq:aesp-inner-active}
\end{equation}
For LOCGD, the source analysis proves
\begin{equation}
    \norm{q_t^{(k+1)}}_1
    \leq
    \bigl(1-\tau\gamma_t^{(k)}\bigr)
    \norm{q_t^{(k)}}_1,
    \qquad
    \tau
    :=\frac{2(\alpha+\eta)}{1+\alpha+2\eta}.
    \label{eq:aesp-inner-contraction}
\end{equation}
Under \cref{eq:aesp-eta}, \(\tau=2/3\).  The same per-coordinate mechanism
underlies LOCAPPR.  Crucially, the proof uses absolute values and therefore
allows signed inner gradients generated by the Catalyst momentum point.

The local stopping threshold is chosen to certify an inner objective gap
\(h_t(z)-h_t^\star\leq\phi_t\).  With
\begin{equation}
    C_{t,0}:=\norm{D^{1/2}\grad h_t(z_t^{(0)})}_1,
    \label{eq:inner-initial-mass}
\end{equation}
the source sufficient threshold is
\begin{equation}
    \eps_t
    =\max\left\{
        \sqrt{\frac{(\alpha+\eta)\phi_t}{m}},
        \frac{2(\alpha+\eta)\phi_t}{C_{t,0}}
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:aesp-inner-tolerance}
\end{equation}
This follows from the mixed-norm estimate
\begin{equation}
    h_t(z)-h_t^\star
    \leq
    \frac{
       \norm{D^{-1/2}\grad h_t(z)}_\infty
       \norm{D^{1/2}\grad h_t(z)}_1
    }{2(\alpha+\eta)}.
    \label{eq:mixed-norm-certificate}
\end{equation}

Define the stage locality factor
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_t
    :=\frac{\overline{\vol}(S_t)}{\gamma_t}.
    \label{eq:lambda-stage}
\end{equation}
The source theorem gives
\begin{equation}
    \Work_t^{\rm in}
    \leq
    \min\left\{
        \frac{\Lambda_t}{\tau}
        \log\frac{C_{t,0}}{C_{t,K_t}},
        \frac{C_{t,0}-C_{t,K_t}}{\tau\eps_t}
    \right\},
    \qquad
    \Lambda_t
    \leq\min\left\{\frac{C_{t,0}}{\eps_t},2m\right\}.
    \label{eq:aesp-stage-work}
\end{equation}

For \(q=\alpha/(1-\alpha)\) and
\begin{equation}
    \rho_A:=0.9\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}},
    \label{eq:aesp-rho}
\end{equation}
the AESP objective estimate specializes to
\begin{equation}
    f(x^{(t)})-f(x^\star)
    \leq
    U_t
    :=\frac{200(1-\alpha^2)}{\alpha}
      (1-\rho_A)^{t+1}.
    \label{eq:aesp-objective-bound}
\end{equation}
Therefore the outer stage count is
\(\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)\).  Combining this with
\cref{eq:aesp-stage-work}, the paper proves
\begin{equation}
    \Work_{\AESP}
    \leq
    \min\left\{
       \softO\!\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt\alpha}\right),
       \softO\!\left(\frac{R^2}{\sqrt\alpha\eps^2}\right)
    \right\},
    \label{eq:aesp-current-bound}
\end{equation}
where \(R\) controls the largest initial weighted inner-gradient mass across
outer stages.

\paragraph{The \(1/\eps^2\) bottleneck.}
The final PPR error is obtained through a function-gap target of order
\(\alpha\eps^2\).  The mixed-norm certificate in
\cref{eq:mixed-norm-certificate} then turns this into an inner coordinate
threshold that can be of order \(\eps^2\), producing the local
\(1/\eps^2\) work term.  Catalyst itself is not the sole cause of the square;
the conservative conversion from objective gap to a local coordinatewise
certificate is the immediate bottleneck.

\paragraph{Figure 4 observation.}
The large-graph experiments of \citet{huang2025accelerated} show that
AESP-LOCAPPR is the most operation-efficient AESP variant, due to online
per-coordinate reuse, and that the shifted inner systems yield a markedly
steeper error reduction in the early stage than APPR, optimized APPR, or
LocGD.  The hybrid design below treats this observation as a testable empirical
hypothesis, not as a premise of the proof.
