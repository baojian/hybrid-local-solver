\begin{abstract}
This note converts a sequence of research discussions into a self-contained,
claim-audited technical document.  It reviews the locally evolving set
framework and the Accelerated Evolving Set Process (AESP), formalizes a
proof-oriented hybrid that uses AESP as an early-stage accelerator and
Gauss--Seidel local SOR as a tail solver, and separates unconditional
convergence statements from conditional accelerated work bounds.  The main
rigorous conclusions are: (i) the AESP-to-LocSOR hybrid converges from every
finite handoff point; (ii) an objective-gap handoff gives a tail work bound
\(\mathcal O(1/(\sqrt{\alpha}\epsppr))\); (iii) direct black-box balancing yields
a graph-structure-free, run-dependent
\(\mathcal O(R/(\alpha^{3/4}\epsppr))\) inner-plus-tail bound; and (iv) the full
end-to-end bound \(\softO(1/(\sqrt{\alpha}\epsppr))\) follows whenever the early
AESP locality factor is \(\mathcal O(1/\epsppr)\).  We correct a previously
misstated weighted-\(\ell_1\) SOR range, derive an exact safeguard for
over-relaxation, and explain how confinement and KKT-slack machinery suggest a
route to the missing AESP locality lemma.

The framework also extends naturally to \(\ell_1\)-regularized PageRank.  We
prove that its unit-step proximal map is a \((1-\alpha)\) contraction in
weighted \(\ell_\infty\) and \(\ell_1\) norms, and that the fixed-point
residual divided by \(\alpha\) certifies solution error.  Consequently, every
finite composite Catalyst handoff followed by momentum-free ISTA converges;
the remaining issue is a graph-independent local-work bound from an arbitrary
accelerated warm start.  The final part turns the research problem into an
objectively verifiable Terminal-Bench Science task whose official target is to
improve a frozen AESP baseline on a hidden suite, while retaining broader
baseline comparisons for task-author calibration.
\end{abstract}
