\subsection{Thick equitable shells also collapse to one dimension}
\label{subsec:equitable-shells}

Let $G$ be connected, fix a root $o$, and write
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_i:=\{v:\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=i\},
 \qquad
 m_i:=|\mathcal L_i|,
 \qquad 0\leq i\leq R.
 \label{eq:equitable-shells}
\end{equation}
Call the rooted graph \emph{root-distance equitable} if, for every
$v\in\mathcal L_i$, the three counts
\begin{equation}
 \nu_i^-:=|N(v)\cap\mathcal L_{i-1}|,
 \qquad
 \nu_i^0:=|N(v)\cap\mathcal L_i|,
 \qquad
 \nu_i^+:=|N(v)\cap\mathcal L_{i+1}|
 \label{eq:equitable-neighbor-counts}
\end{equation}
depend only on $i$; shells outside $[0,R]$ are empty.  Put
$\bar d_i:=\nu_i^-+\nu_i^0+\nu_i^+$.  Double-counting consecutive-shell
edges gives
\begin{equation}
 m_i\nu_i^+=m_{i+1}\nu_{i+1}^-.
 \label{eq:equitable-edge-balance}
\end{equation}
This promise allows large and internally complicated shells.  It holds, for
example, for cliques and complete bipartite graphs rooted at any vertex, and
for hypercubes, Hamming graphs, Johnson graphs, and every distance-regular
graph.

Use the standard adjacency-list access model: querying the degree of an
exposed vertex label costs $O(1)$, while enumerating its adjacency list costs
$d_v$.  The following is a promise-class result; recognizing global
equitability is not charged to the solver.

\begin{theorem}[Output-linear exact RPPR on root-distance-equitable graphs]
\label{thm:equitable-shell-one-pass}
Suppose $G$ is root-distance equitable and the RPPR seed is $o$.  Let
$e_i:=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal L_i}/\sqrt{m_i}$ and let $\widehat Q$ be the
restriction of $Q$ to their span.  This matrix is symmetric tridiagonal, with
\begin{align}
 \widehat Q_{ii}
 &=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
   -\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_i^0}{\bar d_i},
 \label{eq:equitable-quotient-diagonal}\\
 \widehat Q_{i,i+1}
 &=-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
 \sqrt{\frac{\nu_i^+\nu_{i+1}^-}
 {\bar d_i\bar d_{i+1}}}.
 \label{eq:equitable-quotient-offdiagonal}
\end{align}
The quotient demand is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat c_0
 =\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\bar d_0}}
  -\alpha\rho\sqrt{\bar d_0},
 \qquad
 \widehat c_i=-\alpha\rho\sqrt{m_i\bar d_i}
 \quad(1\leq i\leq R).
 \label{eq:equitable-quotient-demand}
\end{equation}
For $i\geq1$, put $\gamma_i:=-\widehat Q_{i,i-1}>0$ and form
\begin{align}
 \delta_0&:=\widehat Q_{00},
 &\eta_0&:=\widehat c_0,
 \notag\\
 \delta_i&:=\widehat Q_{ii}-\frac{\gamma_i^2}{\delta_{i-1}},
 &\eta_i&:=\widehat c_i+
   \frac{\gamma_i\eta_{i-1}}{\delta_{i-1}}
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq R).
 \label{eq:equitable-forward-schur}
\end{align}
If $\eta_0\leq0$, the optimum is zero.  Otherwise let $J$ be the index
immediately before the first nonpositive $\eta_i$, or $J=R$ if all messages
are positive.  Then
\begin{equation}
 S^\star(\rho)=\bigcup_{i=0}^J\mathcal L_i.
 \label{eq:equitable-exact-support}
\end{equation}
Its exact coordinates are recovered from
\begin{equation}
 y_J=\frac{\eta_J}{\delta_J},
 \qquad
 y_i=\frac{\eta_i+\gamma_{i+1}y_{i+1}}{\delta_i}
 \quad(i=J-1,\ldots,0),
 \qquad
 x_v=\frac{y_i}{\sqrt{m_i}}
 \quad(v\in\mathcal L_i).
 \label{eq:equitable-backsolve}
\end{equation}
A lazy shell scan discovers the quotient coefficients, solves the problem,
and certifies the full KKT system in
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl(1+\vol(S^\star(\rho))\bigr)
 \label{eq:equitable-output-linear-work}
\end{equation}
adjacency and arithmetic work, with no support, radius, or confinement oracle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal U$ be the subspace of vectors constant on every shell and let
$P$ be orthogonal averaging onto $\mathcal U$.  Equitability and constant
degree $\bar d_i$ within each shell show directly that normalized adjacency,
and hence $Q$, maps $\mathcal U$ into itself.  Since $Q$ is symmetric,
$\mathcal U^\perp$ is invariant as well, so $PQ=QP$.  The shifted demand
$c_\rho$ lies in $\mathcal U$.  For every feasible $x\geq0$, also $Px\geq0$,
and, with $z:=(I-P)x$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\rho(x)=\Phi_\rho(Px)+\frac12z^\top Qz.
 \label{eq:equitable-orthogonal-splitting}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness therefore puts the unique optimum in $\mathcal U$.
Its nonempty support is connected and contains $o$; because it is a union of
whole shells, it must be a shell prefix.

Taking the matrix entries in the orthonormal basis $(e_i)_{i=0}^R$ gives
\cref{eq:equitable-quotient-diagonal}.  Consecutive-shell edge balance
\cref{eq:equitable-edge-balance} gives
\begin{equation*}
 e_i^\top D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}e_{i+1}
 =\sqrt{\frac{\nu_i^+\nu_{i+1}^-}
 {\bar d_i\bar d_{i+1}}},
\end{equation*}
which proves
\cref{eq:equitable-quotient-offdiagonal}; nonconsecutive entries vanish.
Projecting $c_\rho$ gives
\cref{eq:equitable-quotient-demand}.  Thus $\widehat Q$ is a symmetric
positive-definite tridiagonal Stieltjes matrix, all pivots in
\cref{eq:equitable-forward-schur} are positive, and Gaussian elimination
gives the forward recurrence and
\cref{eq:equitable-backsolve}.

All messages through $J$ are positive, so reverse substitution gives
$y_i>0$ for $0\leq i\leq J$.  If $J<R$, applying one more formal elimination
step to the prefix solution gives
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{J+1}=\widehat c_{J+1}+\gamma_{J+1}y_J\leq0.
 \label{eq:equitable-message-is-kkt}
\end{equation}
The negative of this quantity is the aggregated shifted KKT slack on the
first inactive shell.  Equitability makes the coordinate slack identical at
every vertex of that shell, so its sign is coordinatewise valid.  Vertices
beyond it have no edge to the support and have negative shifted demand.
Thus the prefix solve satisfies the full KKT system.  If every message is
positive, there is no inactive boundary.  The zero case follows directly
from $\widehat c_0\leq0$ and the negative demands away from the seed.

It remains to justify locality.  Once $\mathcal L_i$ is certified active,
scan its adjacency lists exactly once.  The scan enumerates the distinct
labels in $\mathcal L_{i+1}$, hence $m_{i+1}$, and counts their incident edges
from $\mathcal L_i$, hence the promised common value $\nu_{i+1}^-$.  Degree
queries give $\bar d_{i+1}$, while the same scan gives $\nu_i^+$.  Therefore
$\gamma_{i+1}$, $\widehat c_{i+1}$, and $\eta_{i+1}$ are known before any
adjacency list in $\mathcal L_{i+1}$ is scanned.  Only when this message is
positive does the algorithm scan the new shell to obtain $\nu_{i+1}^0$ and
$\nu_{i+1}^+$, form its pivot, and continue.  Consequently every active
adjacency list is scanned once and no inactive-boundary list is scanned.
The boundary labels and their degree queries are charged to edges in the
last active scan.  The reverse scalar solve and output write are linear in
the active shells and vertices.  This proves
\cref{eq:equitable-output-linear-work}.
\end{proof}

The preceding theorem was stated as a promise result only to keep its formula
short.  Its structural hypothesis can in fact be audited online, so using the
fast path never requires trusting a global symmetry oracle.  For the current
shell prefix
$U_i:=\bigcup_{h=0}^i\mathcal L_h$, let $x^{[i]}$ denote its restricted
Dirichlet solution and let $\xi_i^{[i]}$ be its common coordinate value on
$\mathcal L_i$.  Once the scalar checks have succeeded through shell $i$,
the boundary violation at $v\in\mathcal L_{i+1}$ is explicitly
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_v^{[i]}
 :=c_{\rho,v}-(Qx^{[i]})_v
 =c_{\rho,v}
  +\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{d_v\bar d_i}}
   |N(v)\cap\mathcal L_i|\,\xi_i^{[i]}.
 \label{eq:audited-shell-boundary-violation}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[A certifying shell-quotient fast path on every graph]
\label{thm:certifying-shell-fast-path}
There is an exact single-seed RPPR algorithm on an arbitrary graph with the
following behavior.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item It follows the scalar recurrence
 \cref{eq:equitable-forward-schur} only while scans certify that every active
 shell has common degree and common neighbor counts to the preceding, current,
 and next shells.
 \item Before admitting the next shell, it evaluates
 $\zeta_v^{[i]}$ for every exposed boundary label using only the last active
 shell scan and degree queries.  If all values are nonpositive, it returns the
 exact optimum.  If the positive set is not the entire shell, or a uniformity
 check fails, it hands that exact positive set and the current safe prefix to
 the condition-free boundary gate of
 \cref{thm:condition-free-graph}.
 \item Every fast-path admission contains only true-support vertices.  Hence
 the fallback is exact, and a failed audit returns a concrete pair of labels
 with different local signatures or different violation signs.
\end{enumerate}
If the algorithm terminates without fallback, its work is
$O(1+\vol(S^\star(\rho)))$.  This happens on every root-distance-equitable
graph, but requires equitability only on the scanned active prefix; the graph
beyond the first inactive boundary may be arbitrary.  Thus the global promise
and its recognition cost are removed from the correctness claim.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scanning $\mathcal L_i$ reveals, for every vertex in that shell, its degree
and its three neighbor counts, and it enumerates every label in
$\mathcal L_{i+1}$.  Counting appearances of each boundary label gives
$|N(v)\cap\mathcal L_i|$ without scanning that label's adjacency list; one
degree query then gives every quantity in
\cref{eq:audited-shell-boundary-violation}.  Hashing the resulting signatures
either certifies uniformity or returns two explicit witnesses in time linear
in the scan.

Inductively, suppose all audits through $i$ succeeded.  The invariant-subspace
and Gaussian-elimination proof of
\cref{thm:equitable-shell-one-pass}, applied only to $Q_{U_iU_i}$, shows that
the scalar point is exactly $x^{[i]}$ and is positive.  The set
\begin{equation*}
 B_i=\{v\in\mathcal L_{i+1}:\zeta_v^{[i]}>0\}
\end{equation*}
is therefore precisely the violating batch of the exact boundary gate.
\Cref{thm:condition-free-graph} gives
$B_i\subseteq S^\star(\rho)$.  If it is empty, the active equalities hold on
$U_i$, every boundary inequality holds by the displayed test, and every more
distant nonseed vertex has zero coupling to $U_i$ and strictly negative
shifted demand.  These are the full KKT conditions.

If $B_i$ is a proper nonempty subset, the one-scalar shell recurrence has
encountered a genuine partial-shell event, and the exact gate continues from
the same batch.  If $B_i=\mathcal L_{i+1}$, the Schur-complement argument in
\cref{thm:condition-free-graph} proves that the restricted minimizer on
$U_i\cup B_i$ is positive.  The algorithm may therefore scan the admitted
shell.  A successful audit supplies the next quotient diagonal and continues
the induction; a failed audit starts the general gate from this still-safe
region.  Thus no failed symmetry guess can affect correctness.

On a no-fallback execution, every active adjacency list is scanned once, and
the inactive boundary contributes only labels, incident-edge counts, degree
queries, and the coordinatewise KKT test.  All are charged to the last active
scan, proving the stated work.  Global root-distance equitability makes every
audit succeed, while the proof itself used no information beyond the scanned
prefix and its boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The persistent-support product lower bound is oracle-specific]
\label{cor:spider-oracle-separation}
On the path-bundle instance used for the repository's persistent-support
lower bound, the algorithm in \cref{thm:spider-one-pass} has
\begin{equation}
 \Work=\Theta(1/\rho).
 \label{eq:spider-direct-work}
\end{equation}
The same instance costs
$\Omega(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ under the persistent-support one-hop payment
rule.  Hence that product is not a lower bound for local methods that charge
actual frontier scans and retain directed Schur messages.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The source support bound gives
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$.  Conversely, the lower-bound construction
proves that the first
$L=\Theta(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ vertices of each of
$B=\Theta(\sqrt\alpha/\rho)$ arms are active.  Their volume is
$\Omega(BL)=\Omega(1/\rho)$.  Apply \cref{thm:spider-one-pass}.  The larger
persistent-support charge comes from summing the entire resident volume over
the $L$ exposure rounds; the Schur-message algorithm scans only the new
frontier and makes one reverse pass.
\end{proof}

This separation changes the interpretation of ``matching the lower bound.''
For literal persistent-support algorithms, the product scale remains tight.
For elimination-aware local algorithms, the symmetric spider is no longer a
hard instance; a future universal lower bound must obstruct compressed
messages, not merely one-hop propagation.
