\subsection{Finite-band intervals and energy-packed response changes}
\label{subsec:finite-band-packing}

Exact zero signs are unnecessary for an approximate output guarantee.  More
strongly, a response implementation need not maintain the same additive
accuracy at every boundary label.  Define the normalized shifted demand
\begin{equation}
 \bar e_{\rho,v}(x)
 :=\frac{c_{\rho,v}-(Qx)_v}{\sqrt{d_v}}
 =-\frac{\kappa_{\rho,v}(x)}{\sqrt{d_v}}.
 \label{eq:normalized-shifted-demand}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Margin-adaptive finite-band gate]
\label{thm:margin-adaptive-finite-gate}
Fix $\eta>0$.  Starting from the zero or singleton initialization in
\cref{thm:condition-free-graph}, maintain an exact positive Dirichlet point
$x^U$ on the current face $U$.  Suppose a response procedure supplies
certified intervals
\begin{equation}
 L_v\leq \bar e_{\rho,v}(x^U)\leq U_v,
 \qquad v\in\partial U.
 \label{eq:adaptive-demand-interval}
\end{equation}
At a fixed face, apply the following exhaustive rule:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item if
 $B:=\{v\in\partial U:L_v>\eta/2\}$ is nonempty, admit $B$ and compute the
 exact Dirichlet point on $U\cup B$;
 \item if $B$ is empty and $U_v\leq\eta$ for every boundary label, stop;
 \item otherwise refine any intervals that prevent either certificate,
 without changing the face.
\end{enumerate}
Assume only that the refinement procedure eventually produces (i) or (ii) at
each fixed face.  Then every admitted face is contained in
$S^\star(\rho)$, all Dirichlet coordinates remain positive, and the terminal
face $T$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x^T\leq x^\star(\rho),
 \qquad
 \left\|D^{-1/2}\bigl(x^\star(\rho)-x^T\bigr)\right\|_\infty
 \leq\frac\eta\alpha.
 \label{eq:adaptive-finite-gate-error}
\end{equation}
Every admitted batch additionally has the exact objective shock
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\rho(x^U)-\Phi_\rho(x^{U\cup B})
 \geq\frac{\eta^2}{8}\vol(B).
 \label{eq:adaptive-finite-gate-shock}
\end{equation}
Thus interval widths may be arbitrarily coarse away from the transition band;
uniform pointwise $\eta/4$ estimates are sufficient but not necessary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The zero and singleton cases are handled exactly as in
\cref{thm:condition-free-graph}.  Suppose inductively that
$U\subseteq S^\star$ and $0\leq x^U\leq x^\star$.  If
$v\notin S^\star$, Stieltjes signs and the optimum KKT inequality give
\begin{equation*}
 \bar e_{\rho,v}(x^U)
 \leq \bar e_{\rho,v}(x^\star)\leq0.
\end{equation*}
An admitted label instead has exact normalized demand larger than
$\eta/2$, so $B\subseteq S^\star$.  Its Schur right-hand side
$q_B:=c_{\rho,B}-Q_{BU}x^U$ obeys
$q_B>(\eta/2)D_B^{1/2}\one$.  Inverse positivity of the block Schur
complement gives a strictly positive new block correction; back-substitution
is nonnegative.  Comparison with the full optimum gives
$x^U\leq x^{U\cup B}\leq x^\star$, closing the induction.

At termination, the active demands vanish and every boundary demand is at
most $\eta\sqrt{d_v}$; all more distant nonseed demands are negative.  Put
$h:=x^\star-x^T\geq0$ and
$M:=\|D^{-1/2}h\|_\infty$.  If $M>0$, choose a maximizing coordinate $i$.
Because $x_i^\star>0$, the optimum demand vanishes there, and the maximum
principle using $QD^{1/2}\one=\alpha D^{1/2}\one$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha M\sqrt{d_i}
 \leq(Qh)_i
 =c_{\rho,i}-(Qx^T)_i
 \leq\eta\sqrt{d_i}.
\end{equation*}
This proves \cref{eq:adaptive-finite-gate-error}.

For an admitted batch, completing the square after Schur elimination gives
\begin{equation*}
 \Phi_\rho(x^U)-\Phi_\rho(x^{U\cup B})
 =\frac12q_B^T\mathcal S_B^{-1}q_B.
\end{equation*}
Since $\mathcal S_B\preceq I$ and
$q_B>(\eta/2)D_B^{1/2}\one$, the right side is at least
$\eta^2\vol(B)/8$, proving
\cref{eq:adaptive-finite-gate-shock}.
\end{proof}

The next lemma controls what a response reporter must update.  It applies to
an arbitrary exterior set, not only to the immediate graph boundary.

\begin{lemma}[Exterior response is an energy contraction]
\label{lem:response-energy-packing}
Let $U\subseteq T\subseteq S^\star(\rho)$ be two positive exact Dirichlet
faces, extend $x^U$ by zero on $T\setminus U$, and let
$W\subseteq V\setminus T$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 y_W
 :=\bigl(c_{\rho,W}-Q_{WT}x^T_T\bigr)
   -\bigl(c_{\rho,W}-Q_{WT}x^U_T\bigr).
 \label{eq:exterior-response-change}
\end{equation}
Then $y_W\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \|y_W\|_2^2
 \leq\|x^T-x^U\|_{Q_{TT}}^2
 =2\bigl(\Phi_\rho(x^U)-\Phi_\rho(x^T)\bigr).
 \label{eq:response-energy-contraction}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for arbitrary margins $m_v>0$ and
\begin{equation*}
 H(m):=\left\{
  v\in W:\frac{y_v}{\sqrt{d_v}}\geq m_v
 \right\},
\end{equation*}
one has
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{v\in H(m)}d_vm_v^2
 \leq2\bigl(\Phi_\rho(x^U)-\Phi_\rho(x^T)\bigr).
 \label{eq:weighted-response-packing}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $m_v=\theta$ then
\begin{equation}
 \vol(H(\theta))
 \leq\frac{2}{\theta^2}
 \bigl(\Phi_\rho(x^U)-\Phi_\rho(x^T)\bigr).
 \label{eq:uniform-response-packing}
\end{equation}
For disjoint nested expansion epochs, the right sides telescope.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\delta:=x^T_T-x^U_T\geq0$.  Stieltjes signs give
$y_W=-Q_{WT}\delta\geq0$.  The principal matrix on $T\cup W$ is positive
definite, so its Schur complement gives
\begin{equation*}
 Q_{WT}Q_{TT}^{-1}Q_{TW}\preceq Q_{WW}\preceq I.
\end{equation*}
Hence the operator
$R:=Q_{WT}Q_{TT}^{-1/2}$ has $\|R\|_2\leq1$, and
\begin{equation*}
 \|y_W\|_2
 =\|R Q_{TT}^{1/2}\delta\|_2
 \leq\|\delta\|_{Q_{TT}}.
\end{equation*}
Because $x^T$ is stationary on $T$, completing the square gives the equality
in \cref{eq:response-energy-contraction}.  For every $v\in H(m)$,
$y_v^2\geq d_vm_v^2$; summing and applying
\cref{eq:response-energy-contraction} proves
\cref{eq:weighted-response-packing,eq:uniform-response-packing}.  Objective
drops telescope over disjoint nested index intervals.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform response leverage bound]
\label{cor:uniform-response-leverage}
For a face $T$ and exterior label $v$, define
\begin{equation*}
 \chi_v^2:=Q_{vT}Q_{TT}^{-1}Q_{Tv}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\chi_v^2\leq Q_{vv}\leq1$.  Consequently, if $x^T$ is the exact face
solution and
$\|\widetilde x_T-x^T_T\|_{Q_{TT}}\leq\delta$, then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\left|Q_{vT}(\widetilde x_T-x^T_T)\right|}{\sqrt{d_v}}
 \leq\delta.
 \label{eq:uniform-response-leverage-error}
\end{equation}
Thus no coordinate-specific leverage estimate is required to turn an energy
certificate into a valid queried boundary interval.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take the singleton exterior set $W=\{v\}$ in the Schur-complement inequality
used in \cref{lem:response-energy-packing}.  Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$Q_{TT}$ energy norm then gives the displayed error bound, using $d_v\geq1$.
\end{proof}

The universal leverage bound is deliberately source-oblivious.  A correction
created by a new batch has a smaller subspace, and its complete sensitivity
ledger has an exact scalar interpretation.

\begin{theorem}[Source-aware leverage is terminal Schur-diagonal loss]
\label{thm:source-aware-schur-diagonal-loss}
Let $A\subsetneq T=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ and
$W\subseteq V\setminus T$.  For $U\subseteq V$, use
\begin{equation*}
 Q/U:=Q_{\bar U\bar U}
       -Q_{\bar U U}Q_{UU}^{-1}Q_{U\bar U},
 \qquad \bar U:=V\setminus U.
\end{equation*}
Write $L^A:=Q/A$ and $L^T:=Q/T$ for these terminal Schur complements, let
$E_B$ embed $\mathbb R^B$ into $\mathbb R^T$, and put
\begin{equation*}
 H_B:=E_B^TQ_{TT}^{-1}E_B.
\end{equation*}
For $v\in W$, define the source-aware squared leverage
\begin{equation}
 (\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2
 :=Q_{vT}Q_{TT}^{-1}E_BH_B^{-1}
      E_B^TQ_{TT}^{-1}Q_{Tv}.
 \label{eq:source-aware-schur-leverage}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 (\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2
 =L^A_{vB}(L^A_{BB})^{-1}L^A_{Bv}
 =L^A_{vv}-L^T_{vv},
 \label{eq:source-aware-diagonal-identity}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{v\in W}(\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2\leq |B|.
 \label{eq:source-aware-rank-budget}
\end{equation}
Every source correction $\Delta=Q_{TT}^{-1}E_Bh$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 |Q_{vT}\Delta|
 \leq\chi_v^{B\mid A}\|\Delta\|_{Q_{TT}}.
 \label{eq:source-aware-response-interval}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every group $C\subseteq W$,
\begin{equation}
 Z_{B\mid A}(C)
 :=\sum_{v\in C}\frac{(\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2}{d_v}
 =\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
  D_C^{-1}\bigl(L^A_{CC}-L^T_{CC}\bigr)
 \right].
 \label{eq:source-aware-group-diagonal-loss}
\end{equation}

If $S_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_J$ and
$B_j:=S_{j+1}\setminus S_j$, then every $v\notin S_k$ has the temporal
budget
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\chi_v^{B_j\mid S_j})^2
 =(Q/S_0)_{vv}-(Q/S_k)_{vv}
 \leq Q_{vv}-\alpha
 =\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.
 \label{eq:source-aware-temporal-budget}
\end{equation}
Thus source-aware sensitivity is a monotone scalar resource that telescopes
per exterior label, independently of the number of previous batches.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order $T$ as $(A,B)$ and put $K_B:=L^A_{BB}$.  Block inversion gives
\begin{equation*}
 H_B=K_B^{-1},
 \qquad
 Q_{vT}Q_{TT}^{-1}E_B=L^A_{vB}K_B^{-1}.
\end{equation*}
Substitution proves the first equality in
\cref{eq:source-aware-diagonal-identity}.  Schur associativity gives
\begin{equation*}
 L^T_{WW}
 =L^A_{WW}-L^A_{WB}K_B^{-1}L^A_{BW},
\end{equation*}
which proves the diagonal equality and
\cref{eq:source-aware-group-diagonal-loss}.

For the rank budget, define
\begin{equation*}
 P_B:=Q_{TT}^{-1/2}E_BH_B^{-1}E_B^TQ_{TT}^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 R:=Q_{WT}Q_{TT}^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
The first matrix is an orthogonal projector of rank $|B|$.  The Schur
inequality in \cref{lem:response-energy-packing} gives $RR^T\preceq I$, so
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{v\in W}(\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2
 =\operatorname{tr}(RP_BR^T)
 \leq\operatorname{tr}(P_B)=|B|.
\end{equation*}
Writing $w_v:=E_B^TQ_{TT}^{-1}Q_{Tv}$ and applying Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$H_B$ inner product proves
\cref{eq:source-aware-response-interval}.

Finally apply \cref{eq:source-aware-diagonal-identity} at consecutive faces
and telescope.  The variational characterization gives
$Q/S_k\succeq\alpha I$, while $(Q/S_0)_{vv}\leq Q_{vv}$.  Since
$Q_{vv}=(1+\alpha)/2$, this proves
\cref{eq:source-aware-temporal-budget}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Additive-error hierarchical locator]
\label{cor:additive-schur-diagonal-locator}
Under \cref{thm:source-aware-schur-diagonal-loss}, let a rooted partition
hierarchy on $W$ have arity at most $b$ and depth $L$.  Fix a source
correction $\Delta=Q_{TT}^{-1}E_Bh$, its energy
$E:=\|\Delta\|_{Q_{TT}}>0$, and a common normalized interval margin $m>0$.
Put $\tau:=m^2/E^2$.  Suppose a group query returns a certified one-sided
estimate
\begin{equation}
 Z_{B\mid A}(C)\leq\widehat Z(C)
 \leq\gamma Z_{B\mid A}(C)+\beta\tau,
 \qquad 0\leq\beta<1.
 \label{eq:additive-schur-group-oracle}
\end{equation}
Starting at the root, discard $C$ when $\widehat Z(C)<\tau$ and otherwise
visit its children; test the exact predicate at every reached leaf.  This
returns precisely
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal A_m
 :=\left\{v\in W:
   \frac{\chi_v^{B\mid A}E}{\sqrt{d_v}}\geq m
 \right\}
\end{equation*}
using at most
\begin{equation}
 1+bL\left(
  1+\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}|B|\frac{E^2}{m^2}
 \right)
 \label{eq:additive-schur-locator-work}
\end{equation}
group queries.  Its candidate output satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \vol(\mathcal A_m)\leq |B|\frac{E^2}{m^2}.
 \label{eq:additive-schur-locator-output}
\end{equation}
Thus negligible Schur-diagonal losses need only scale-matched additive
accuracy, not a multiplicative estimate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $v\in\mathcal A_m$, every ancestor $C$ satisfies
$Z_{B\mid A}(C)\geq\tau$ and cannot be discarded.  Conversely the exact leaf
test removes every false candidate.  A retained node has
$Z_{B\mid A}(C)\geq(1-\beta)\tau/\gamma$.  Retained nodes at a fixed depth
are disjoint, and \cref{eq:source-aware-rank-budget} gives
$Z_{B\mid A}(W)\leq|B|$ because $d_v\geq1$.  This proves
\cref{eq:additive-schur-locator-work}.  Every candidate satisfies
$d_vm^2\leq(\chi_v^{B\mid A})^2E^2$; summing and reusing the rank budget proves
\cref{eq:additive-schur-locator-output}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accelerated all-boundary diagonal-loss checkpoint]
\label{thm:chebyshev-diagonal-loss-checkpoint}
Let $S\subseteq V$ be a nonempty exposed face and
$W:=\{v\notin S:Q_{vS}\neq0\}$ its exposed boundary.  For $v\in W$, define
the cumulative normalized terminal-Schur diagonal loss
\begin{equation}
 \ell_v(S)
 :=\frac{Q_{vS}Q_{SS}^{-1}Q_{Sv}}{d_v}
 =\frac{Q_{vv}-(Q/S)_{vv}}{d_v}.
 \label{eq:checkpoint-diagonal-loss}
\end{equation}
Fix $\zeta,\xi,\delta\in(0,1/2)$ and put
$M:=\max\{1,|W|\}$.  There is a degree
\begin{equation}
 m=O\!\left(
  \frac1{\sqrt\alpha}
  \log\frac1{\alpha\zeta}
 \right)
 \label{eq:inverse-square-root-degree}
\end{equation}
polynomial $p_m$ such that the following procedure uses
\begin{equation}
 r=O\!\left(\xi^{-2}\log\frac{M}{\delta}\right)
 \label{eq:checkpoint-sketch-count}
\end{equation}
independent standard Gaussian vectors $g_t\in\mathbb R^S$:
\begin{equation}
 z_t:=p_m(Q_{SS})g_t,
 \qquad
 \overline\ell_v
 :=\frac1r\sum_{t=1}^r
 \frac{(Q_{vS}z_t)^2}{d_v},
 \qquad
 \widehat\ell_v
 :=\frac{\overline\ell_v}
 {(1-\xi)(1-\zeta)^2}.
 \label{eq:checkpoint-estimator}
\end{equation}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $v\in W$,
\begin{equation}
 \ell_v(S)\leq\widehat\ell_v
 \leq
 \Gamma_{\zeta,\xi}\ell_v(S),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\zeta,\xi}
 :=\frac{(1+\xi)(1+\zeta)^2}
 {(1-\xi)(1-\zeta)^2}.
 \label{eq:checkpoint-one-sided-guarantee}
\end{equation}
Consequently every hierarchy group $C\subseteq W$ has the simultaneous
one-sided estimate
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{v\in C}\ell_v(S)
 \leq\sum_{v\in C}\widehat\ell_v
 \leq\Gamma_{\zeta,\xi}
       \sum_{v\in C}\ell_v(S).
 \label{eq:checkpoint-group-guarantee}
\end{equation}
All estimates can be constructed from the adjacency lists of $S$ and the
degrees of their incident boundary labels in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(r(m+1)\vol(S)\right)
 =\widetilde O_{\zeta,\xi,\delta}
   \!\left(\frac{\vol(S)}{\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \label{eq:checkpoint-construction-work}
\end{equation}
work and $O(r(|S|+|W|))$ storage.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the ``Static all-boundary checkpoint at accelerated cost'' theorem
proved in the companion response-preconditioned hybrid note, under the
notational substitutions $h_v(S)=\ell_v(S)$ and $W_S=W$.  Its proof gives
the displayed Chebyshev degree, Gaussian concentration guarantee, and a
charged-volume construction bound.  Here every exposed vertex has positive
degree, so
$\operatorname{cvol}(S)=|S|+\vol(S)\leq2\vol(S)$; the same row-scan argument
therefore proves \cref{eq:checkpoint-construction-work}.  We retain the local
statement so later ladder arguments can cite its exact notation, while proof
ownership remains with the response-preconditioned note.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Geometric checkpoints already meet the product scale]
\label{cor:geometric-diagonal-loss-checkpoints}
Let $S_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_K$ be checkpoint faces satisfying
\begin{equation*}
 \vol(S_{i+1})\geq(1+\gamma)\vol(S_i),
 \qquad \gamma>0.
\end{equation*}
Run \cref{thm:chebyshev-diagonal-loss-checkpoint} independently at every
checkpoint with failure budgets $\delta_i>0$ satisfying
$\sum_i\delta_i\leq\delta$.  Then all checkpoint guarantees hold with
probability at least $1-\delta$, and for fixed $\zeta,\xi,\gamma$ their
total construction work is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{\vol(S_K)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:geometric-checkpoint-total-work}
\end{equation}
Thus a full exposed-boundary snapshot at every geometric anchor is already
affordable at the accelerated product scale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A union bound gives simultaneous correctness.  The geometric growth implies
$\sum_i\vol(S_i)\leq(1+1/\gamma)\vol(S_K)$; applying
\cref{eq:checkpoint-construction-work} and absorbing the logarithmic failure
budgets proves \cref{eq:geometric-checkpoint-total-work}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Frontier whitening needs no exact square root]
\label{prop:spectral-frontier-chebyshev-whitening}
Let $A\subsetneq A\mathbin{\dot\cup}F=:T$ and define the frontier Schur
complement and its lifted response by
\begin{equation*}
 K_F:=Q_{FF}-Q_{FA}Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{AF},
 \qquad
 (\mathcal U_Fy)_A:=-Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{AF}y,
 \qquad
 (\mathcal U_Fy)_F:=y.
\end{equation*}
For an exterior group $C\subseteq V\setminus T$, put
\begin{equation}
 R_C:=D_C^{-1/2}Q_{CV}\mathcal U_F,
 \qquad
 Z_{F\mid A}(C):=\norm{R_CK_F^{-1/2}}_F^2.
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-group-mass}
\end{equation}
Then $\alpha I\preceq K_F\preceq I$.  Suppose an SPD representation
$\widetilde K_F$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 (1-\upsilon)K_F\preceq\widetilde K_F
 \preceq(1+\upsilon)K_F,
 \qquad 0\leq\upsilon<1.
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-spectral-approximation}
\end{equation}
For every $\zeta\in(0,1/2)$, a polynomial $p_q$ of degree
\begin{equation}
 q=O_\upsilon\!\left(
  \frac1{\sqrt\alpha}\log\frac1{\alpha\zeta}
 \right)
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-polynomial-degree}
\end{equation}
gives, simultaneously for every exterior group,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(1-\zeta)^2}{1+\upsilon}Z_{F\mid A}(C)
 \leq\norm{R_Cp_q(\widetilde K_F)}_F^2
 \leq
 \frac{(1+\zeta)^2}{1-\upsilon}Z_{F\mid A}(C).
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-group-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Thus logarithmically many Gaussian source vectors
$p_q(\widetilde K_F)g_t$ replace the exact vectors $K_F^{-1/2}g_t$ in every
group-norm sketch at a total source-preparation cost
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O_{\zeta,\upsilon,\delta}\!\left(
  \frac{T_{\rm mv}}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right),
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-source-work}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm mv}$ is the cost of one multiplication by
$\widetilde K_F$.  In the exact case, one multiplication
\begin{equation}
 K_Fy=Q_{FF}y-Q_{FA}Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{AF}y
 \label{eq:frontier-whitening-exact-matvec}
\end{equation}
uses one charged anchor-response application plus
$O(\vol(F))$ frontier-incidence work.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Schur variational identity gives
\begin{equation*}
 y^TK_Fy
 =\min_x
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}^{\!T}
 Q_{TT}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
 \geq\alpha\norm{y}_2^2,
\end{equation*}
while $K_F\preceq Q_{FF}\preceq I$.  Hence
$\operatorname{spec}(\widetilde K_F)\subseteq
[(1-\upsilon)\alpha,1+\upsilon]$.  Chebyshev approximation of
$x^{-1/2}$ on this interval yields \cref{eq:frontier-whitening-polynomial-degree}
and
\begin{equation*}
 (1-\zeta)^2\widetilde K_F^{-1}
 \preceq p_q(\widetilde K_F)^2
 \preceq(1+\zeta)^2\widetilde K_F^{-1}.
\end{equation*}
Inverting \cref{eq:frontier-whitening-spectral-approximation}, taking a
congruence by $R_C$, and tracing proves
\cref{eq:frontier-whitening-group-sandwich}.  A Chebyshev recurrence takes
$q$ matrix-vector products per Gaussian source; the usual
$O(\log(M/\delta))$ sources for $M$ simultaneous group tests are absorbed in
\cref{eq:frontier-whitening-source-work}.  Finally,
\cref{eq:frontier-whitening-exact-matvec} is the definition of the Schur
complement.  Its two sparse products are formed from the rows of $F$, and its
middle operation is one application of the stored anchor response.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positive exact-rung square-root ladder]
\label{prop:positive-resolvent-rung-ladder}
Use the anchor, frontier, face, lift, and frontier Schur complement of
\cref{prop:spectral-frontier-chebyshev-whitening}.  Fix
$\eta\in(0,1/8)$, let
\begin{equation*}
 t_0:=\eta\sqrt\alpha,
 \qquad t_{j+1}:=(1+\eta)t_j,
 \qquad J:=\min\{j:t_j\geq\eta^{-1}\},
\end{equation*}
and, for $0\leq j<J$, define
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_j:=t_{j+1}^2,
 \qquad
 \omega_j:=\frac2\pi(t_{j+1}-t_j),
 \qquad
 r_\eta(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{\omega_j}{x+\sigma_j}.
 \label{eq:positive-rung-rational}
\end{equation}
Then the ladder has
\begin{equation*}
 J=O\!\left(
  \eta^{-1}\left(\log\frac1\alpha+\log\frac1\eta\right)
 \right)
\end{equation*}
rungs and, uniformly on $[\alpha,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 (1-4\eta)x^{-1/2}\leq r_\eta(x)\leq x^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:positive-rung-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Its two work-mass sums satisfy
\begin{align}
 \sum_j\frac{\omega_j}{\alpha+\sigma_j}
 &\leq\frac1{\sqrt\alpha},
 \label{eq:positive-rung-work-mass}\\
 \sum_j\omega_j\frac{1+\sigma_j}{\alpha+\sigma_j}
 &\leq\frac1{\sqrt\alpha}
  +\frac{2(1+\eta)}{\pi\eta}.
 \label{eq:positive-rung-coordinate-mass}
\end{align}

Let $E_F$ embed the frontier into $T=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}F$ and put
\begin{equation*}
 M_j:=Q_{TT}+\sigma_jE_FE_F^T.
\end{equation*}
Every rung is a sparse shifted face system and obeys the exact block identity
\begin{equation}
 M_j^{-1}E_F
 =\mathcal U_F(K_F+\sigma_jI)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal U_Fr_\eta(K_F)
 =\sum_j\omega_jM_j^{-1}E_F.
 \label{eq:positive-rung-block-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently every exterior group mass obtained from
$\mathcal U_Fr_\eta(K_F)$ lies between $(1-4\eta)^2$ times and one times the
exact $K_F^{-1/2}$ group mass.

This ladder also has a monotone delayed-push realization.  Put
$s_F:=(\sqrt{d_u})_{u\in F}$.  If $g\geq0$, residual coordinate settlement of
all shifted Schur systems
\begin{equation*}
 (K_F+\sigma_jI)y_j=\omega_jg
\end{equation*}
at the trigger $p_{j,u}/\sqrt{d_u}\geq\tau$, charging $d_u$ for coordinate
$u$, uses at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\langle s_F,g\rangle}{\tau}
 \left(
  \frac1{\sqrt\alpha}+\frac{2(1+\eta)}{\pi\eta}
 \right)
 \label{eq:positive-rung-coordinate-work}
\end{equation}
Schur-coordinate work.  Signed sources are split into their positive and
negative parts.

For the sparse face realization, let $x_j\geq0$ be any partial state with
nonnegative debt
\begin{equation*}
 d_j:=\omega_jE_Fg-M_jx_j\geq0.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_Fr_\eta(K_F)g
 =\sum_jx_j+\sum_jM_j^{-1}d_j
 \label{eq:positive-rung-debt-invariant}
\end{equation}
exactly.  A local settlement touches only one sparse graph row and its cut
incidences.  If $d_j=(d_{j,A},d_{j,F})$, exact anchor cleanup replaces the
frontier debt by
\begin{equation}
 d_{j,F}-Q_{FA}Q_{AA}^{-1}d_{j,A}\geq0,
 \label{eq:positive-rung-anchor-cleanup}
\end{equation}
which is precisely the residual of the shifted Schur system.  Thus the
$1/\sqrt\alpha$ inverse-square-root scale admits positive exact rungs and
delayed nonnegative debt; no signed over-relaxation or precomputed target
harmonic row is algebraically necessary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity
\begin{equation*}
 x^{-1/2}=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{x+t^2}
\end{equation*}
is a positive resolvent representation.  \Cref{eq:positive-rung-rational}
is its right-endpoint rule on the geometric
grid $[t_0,t_J]$.  It is a lower bound.  On one grid interval the integrand
changes by at most $(1+\eta)^2$; hence quadrature loses at most $2\eta$
relatively.  The omitted lower and upper tails each lose at most
$2\eta/\pi$ relatively.  This proves
\cref{eq:positive-rung-sandwich}.  Evaluating the same lower sum at
$x=\alpha$ proves
\cref{eq:positive-rung-work-mass}.  Also
$\sum_j\omega_j=2(t_J-t_0)/\pi\leq2(1+\eta)/(\pi\eta)$, which proves
\cref{eq:positive-rung-coordinate-mass}.

The Schur complement of $M_j$ onto $F$ is
$K_F+\sigma_jI$; block inversion proves
\cref{eq:positive-rung-block-identity}.  Functional calculus in
\cref{eq:positive-rung-sandwich}, followed by squaring, congruence, and a
trace, proves the group-mass statement.

Since $Q\sqrt d=\alpha\sqrt d$, principal restriction and inverse positivity
give $K_Fs_F\geq\alpha s_F$ coordinatewise.  Also $K_F$ is Stieltjes and
$(K_F)_{uu}\leq1$.  The residual potential
$\langle s_F,p_j\rangle$ therefore decreases by at least
\begin{equation*}
 \tau d_u\frac{\alpha+\sigma_j}{1+\sigma_j}
\end{equation*}
when $u$ is settled, while its initial value is
$\omega_j\langle s_F,g\rangle$.  Summing over the rungs and applying
\cref{eq:positive-rung-coordinate-mass} proves
\cref{eq:positive-rung-coordinate-work}.

Finally $M_jx_j+d_j=\omega_jE_Fg$.  Apply $M_j^{-1}$, sum, and use
\cref{eq:positive-rung-block-identity} to obtain
\cref{eq:positive-rung-debt-invariant}.  Stieltjes signs preserve
nonnegativity and make each coordinate settlement row-local.  Adding
$Q_{AA}^{-1}d_{j,A}$ to the anchor state zeros its debt and changes the
frontier residual by $-Q_{FA}Q_{AA}^{-1}d_{j,A}$, proving
\cref{eq:positive-rung-anchor-cleanup} and the shifted Schur interpretation.
\end{proof}

The checkpoint theorem deliberately estimates the cumulative loss from the
original matrix to the present face.  Subtracting two noisy snapshots does
not give relative control of a tiny intervening loss.  Therefore the theorem
closes the Monte Carlo work bound for full boundary initialization and
geometric anchor rebuilding, but not the light-update problem between
checkpoints.  A deterministic certificate or Las Vegas validation remains
separate.  The dynamic operation is now sharper: starting from a materialized
one-sided snapshot, maintain or locate only the incremental terminal-Schur
diagonal loss created by the current frontier, with additive accuracy at the
pruning scale of \cref{cor:additive-schur-diagonal-locator}.
By \cref{prop:spectral-frontier-chebyshev-whitening}, this locator does not
need an exact frontier inverse square root: an exact charged anchor response,
or any constant-factor spectral frontier representation, prepares all source
probes at the accelerated scale.  Alternatively,
\cref{prop:positive-resolvent-rung-ladder} replaces both the square root and
the target-row algebra by positive sparse shifted propagation with exact
delayed debt.  What it does not yet prove is that settling those graph debts
inherits the Schur-coordinate work bound without repeatedly scanning the old
anchor.  The companion response note now removes the literal-settlement
obstruction at a fixed face: it sums every outstanding debt fragment first,
uses signed Chebyshev semi-iteration only inside the flush, and converts its
energy certificate into simultaneous one-sided exterior intervals.  One such
flush costs
$\widetilde O(\operatorname{cvol}(T)/\sqrt\alpha)$ graph work, and one flush
per geometrically growing face has the final-volume product bound.  The
companion's singleton-path proposition rules out relying on those flushes
alone for the literal exact gate.  The open condition is therefore an
output-sensitive partial flush after light events, or a separately proved
support-safe trace, rather than fixed-face propagation itself.
The companion response note closes that target operation when the normalized
anchor--boundary cut has small rank: a rank-$s$ cut needs only $s$ harmonic
basis vectors.  It also gives a linear-volume comb whose cut has full rank and
whose rank-deficient energy error is bounded below independently of the comb
length for fixed $\alpha<1$.  Thus a universal low-rank bank is not
the missing arbitrary-core theorem; high-rank nonequitable cores require
on-demand sparsification, sparse recovery, or a different implicit response.

The packing lemma does not itself locate the labels in $H(m)$.  It proves
that their output volume, rather than the full boundary size, is the correct
charge.  The remaining large-core primitive is therefore precise: maintain a
certified aggregate Schur-diagonal-loss oracle, equivalently a heavy-change
reporter for the Schur response, rekeying a label only when its accumulated
normalized change consumes its current interval margin.
Far-below-threshold labels may retain coarse one-sided intervals.  This target
is strictly weaker than refreshing every demand or maintaining every exact
zero crossing.

\begin{corollary}[The coarse-regularization product target is unconditional]
\label{cor:coarse-tree-target}
If $\rho\geq\sqrt\alpha$, the condition-free forest gate obeys
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{\rm graph\text{-}gate}
 =O(1/\rho^2)
 =O\!\left(\frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:coarse-tree-target}
\end{equation}
Thus the elementary repeated-solve gate itself reaches the product scale in
the coarse regime.  In the fine regime, the lazy-response solver
\cref{thm:lazy-tree-response} supplies the sharper unconditional bound.
\end{corollary}
