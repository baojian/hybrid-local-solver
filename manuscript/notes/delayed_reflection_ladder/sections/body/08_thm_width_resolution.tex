\subsection{Sparse cyclic regions via elimination width}

The forest proof extends with an explicit and measurable price for fill.
For a symmetric elimination ordering of $Q_{SS}$, let $w$ be the maximum
number of later neighbors of any pivot in the filled graph.

\begin{theorem}[Width-sensitive exact debt resolution]
\label{thm:width-resolution}
Given an elimination ordering of width $w$, the block correction and boundary
update on $S$ can be computed in
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl(\vol(S)+w^2|S|\bigr)
 =O\bigl((w+1)^2\vol(S)\bigr)
 \label{eq:width-resolution-work}
\end{equation}
arithmetic and adjacency-list work, with $O(\vol(S)+w|S|)$ storage.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a pivot with $k$ later filled neighbors, the symmetric Schur update is a
rank-one update of a $k\times k$ block and costs $O(k^2)$ arithmetic.  The
definition of the ordering gives $k\leq w$, so all factorization updates cost
$O(w^2|S|)$.  Forward and backward substitution cost $O(w|S|)$.  Matrix
assembly and the boundary residual update each require one adjacency-list
scan of $S$, costing $O(\vol(S))$.  The stored factors have at most
$O(w|S|)$ off-diagonal entries.  Finally $|S|\leq\vol(S)$.
\end{proof}

Thus cycles do not force a return to repeated row cleanup.  They enter the
bound through local elimination width.  Forests have $w=1$; cactus-like and
small-cycle-core regions are promising precisely when they admit a uniformly
small-width ordering.
