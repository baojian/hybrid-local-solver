\subsection{The alpha-scaled regularization path has a geometric work ledger}

The residual ladder in \cref{sec:ladder} counts a fixed volume once per rung
and therefore carries a dynamic-range logarithm.  The RPPR path has a sharper
ledger because its certified volume grows geometrically with decreasing
regularization.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-rung continuation on supplied supports]
\label{thm:support-path-ledger}
Fix $s\in\{1,2\}$ and a path
\begin{equation}
 1\geq\rho_0>\rho_1>\cdots>\rho_K>0,
 \qquad
 \rho_{k+1}=\lambda_\alpha^s\rho_k.
 \label{eq:regularization-ladder}
\end{equation}
Suppose each exact support $S_k:=S^\star(\rho_k)$ and a width-$w$
elimination ordering of $G[S_k]$ are supplied.  For a single seed, solving
every shifted Dirichlet system from scratch and checking every boundary KKT
certificate costs
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{0:K}
 =O\!\left(
  \frac{(w+1)^2}{\rho_K(1-\lambda_\alpha^s)}
 \right)
 =O\!\left(
  \frac{(w+1)^2}{\rho_K\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:support-path-work}
\end{equation}
For forest supports, the factor $(w+1)^2$ is an absolute constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Support monotonicity gives $S_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq S_K$, although only
the volume bound is needed.  By \cref{thm:width-resolution} and
$\vol(S_k)\leq1/\rho_k$, the solve and boundary check at rung $k$ cost
$O((w+1)^2/\rho_k)$.  Reverse the geometric sum from the last rung:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^K\frac1{\rho_k}
 =\frac1{\rho_K}\sum_{j=0}^K\lambda_\alpha^{sj}
 \leq\frac1{\rho_K(1-\lambda_\alpha^s)}.
 \label{eq:regularization-volume-sum}
\end{equation}
The constant-time single-seed checks contribute $O(K)$, which is dominated
by the displayed bound when $\rho_0\leq1$.  Finally,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{1-\lambda_\alpha}
 =\frac{1+\sqrt\alpha}{2\sqrt\alpha},
 \qquad
 \frac1{1-\lambda_\alpha^2}
 =\frac{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^2}{4\sqrt\alpha},
 \label{eq:path-ledger-factor}
\end{equation}
which proves the final order for both allowed values of $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[End-to-end target reduced to support discovery]
\label{cor:discovery-reduction}
If, at each rung of \cref{eq:regularization-ladder}, an online procedure
discovers $S_k$ and a width-$w$ ordering using
$O((w+1)^2\vol(S_k))$ additional work, then the complete continuation method
has
\begin{equation}
 \Work
 =O\!\left(\frac{(w+1)^2}{\rho_K\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:end-to-end-support-target}
\end{equation}
On forest instances this matches the repository's
$1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha)$ persistent-support product scale, up to constants,
under a different, explicitly elimination-aware algorithm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Add the assumed discovery cost to the exact-solve cost rung by rung and reuse
the geometric sum \cref{eq:regularization-volume-sum}.
\end{proof}

The support-oracle qualifier is essential.  This theorem does not contradict
the repository's persistent-support one-hop lower bound: it is a direct
elimination result after the free boundary has been supplied, whereas that
lower bound charges the history needed to expose the boundary.  The theorem
therefore localizes the remaining difficulty to \emph{discovery}, not the
linear algebra of terminal cleanup.
