\title{Alpha-Scaled Exact Rungs and Delayed Reflection Debt:\\

\begin{abstract}
The measured R-LSOR ladder alternates optimally over-relaxed spreading rungs
with exact $\omega=1$ rungs and substantially reduces charged work on its
24-cell benchmark.  We isolate the algebra behind that observation and ask
what would be needed for a graph-uniform accelerated local-work theorem.  If
$\lambda_\alpha=(1-\sqrt\alpha)/(1+\sqrt\alpha)$, then optimal SOR has
$\omega_\star=1+\lambda_\alpha^2$: a push leaves signed reflection
$-\lambda_\alpha^2r_u$.  Combining this identity with a geometric threshold
ladder shows that no-current-rung reactivation requires
$b\leq\lambda_\alpha^{-2}$, while no-next-rung reactivation requires
$b\leq\lambda_\alpha^{-1}$.  Both bases are $1+\Theta(\sqrt\alpha)$, so the
ladder contains
$\Theta(\log R/\sqrt\alpha)$ rungs over dynamic range $R$, exactly the
accelerated iteration scale.  Fixed bases such as two or three therefore
cannot support an asymptotic proof as $\alpha\downarrow0$.

We also give an exact split-residual invariant that tags the reflection as
delayed debt without changing the PageRank residual.  Two obstructions remain.
An immediate over-relaxed/exact pair collapses algebraically to one ordinary
Gauss--Seidel push, so the delay is load-bearing.  More seriously, on the
interior of the long-spider path, an optimal-SOR push sends a backward packet
of magnitude $\lambda_\alpha|r_u|$, larger than its self-debt
$\lambda_\alpha^2|r_u|$.  Suppressing self-reactivation alone therefore does
not prevent the triangular traversal.

The principal new result removes the algebraic part of that obstruction.
For any discovered region $S$, one restricted block correction annihilates
the residual on all of $S$ exactly.  If $G[S]$ is a forest, leaf elimination
computes the correction and its boundary residual in $O(\vol(S))$ work,
regardless of how many signed backward packets were deferred.  More generally,
a supplied elimination ordering of width $w$ gives
$O((w+1)^2\vol(S))$ work.  Thus the spider's triangular cleanup is not
intrinsic: it is caused by resolving a tree through repeated row pushes.
The block correction also erases all within-$S$ relaxation history: with the
outside iterate fixed, it returns the unique restricted Dirichlet solution.
Thus over-relaxation can help only by discovering a better region sooner, not
by improving the terminal point on a fixed region.
For endpoint paths we close that discovery problem completely.  A forward
Schur recurrence stops at the first nonpositive transformed demand, which is
exactly the boundary KKT condition; one reverse pass returns the solution in
$O(\vol(S^\star))$ work.  A radial version gives the same result on the
symmetric path-bundle spider used by the repository's lower bound.
On an arbitrary rooted tree, each child response is continuous, monotone, and
piecewise affine, is zero below an explicit one-edge threshold, and is obtained
by inverting a strictly increasing Schur map.  A lazy breakpoint iterator
therefore follows only realized activation events.  A graph-universal
Chebyshev argument bounds the exact support radius by
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$; charging each event through its active ancestors
gives a condition-free exact tree solver with
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ degree work and no support, radius, or
confinement oracle.
On arbitrary graphs, fixing the seed value exposes one monotone
piecewise-affine active-set homotopy with at most one event per nonseed vertex,
so cyclicity does not obstruct exact support discovery.  For graphs whose
biconnected articulation blocks have size at most $q$, lazy block responses
give the condition-free bound
$\widetilde O(q^3/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$; this includes bounded cyclic gadgets
glued through articulation vertices.  The block identifiers need not be
supplied.  When an activation merges previously exposed blocks, every newly
usable edge is incident on a zero-valued activating vertex, so the old
response prefix remains exact and only the future suffix is rebuilt.  This
causal merge rule recovers the same product scale from ordinary degree and
adjacency access.
Large blocks are not automatically hard.  Reflection symmetry reduces an
arbitrarily long cycle to a tridiagonal radial system, giving an output-linear
exact solver.  The same conclusion holds with arbitrarily thick shells on
every root-distance-equitable graph: orthogonal shell averaging reduces the
entire obstacle problem to a tridiagonal quotient, and a lazy shell scan
discovers and solves the exact support in output-linear work.  This includes
cliques, complete bipartite graphs, hypercubes, and all distance-regular
graphs.  The shell fast path is locally certifying on every graph: it either
returns the exact solution or exposes the first partial-shell or nonuniform
event and safely transfers to the general gate.  More broadly, several
inequivalent thick cells may occupy one distance layer.  If they admit an
equitable tree quotient, the lazy tree-response solver applies to the quotient
and gives the exact product bound
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$, even when the original graph has
unbounded treewidth and large biconnected cores.  No cell identifiers are
needed: online rooted color refinement groups indistinguishable boundary
cells into a tied event, scans that bundle only after activation, and forks
its future response when newly exposed incidences distinguish the cells.
The refinement and response forks have the same product-scale amortization.
Independently, if the exact
support contains at most
$\chi_\star$ vertices in any root-distance shell, the condition-free boundary
gate with width-$w$ elimination costs
$\widetilde O((w+1)^2\chi_\star/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$.  Thus constant-width
cycles and strips meet the product scale even though their biconnected blocks
can be arbitrarily large.
For approximate output, uniform point estimates on the whole boundary are
also stronger than necessary.  A margin-adaptive gate needs only certified
one-sided intervals: admit when a lower endpoint is above the safe threshold,
stop when every upper endpoint is below the error threshold, and refine only
the unresolved transition band.  The change of all exterior demands under a
nested exact expansion is an $\ell_2$ contraction of the primal energy
correction.  Consequently only energy-proportional boundary volume can move
by a prescribed normalized margin, and every scalar response leverage is at
most one.  Source-aware squared leverage is exactly terminal Schur-diagonal
loss; it telescopes to at most $(1-\alpha)/2$ per still-exterior label.  Its
hierarchical locator needs only one-sided additive accuracy at the current
margin scale.  A randomized Chebyshev inverse-square-root sketch now
constructs all
exposed boundary diagonal losses at a fixed face in
$\widetilde O(\vol(S)/\sqrt\alpha)$ work, and geometrically spaced full
checkpoints have the same final-volume product bound.  This reduces the
general cyclic target further to incremental diagonal-loss reporting between
checkpoints for the energy-packed interval crossings.  The associated
frontier source probes no longer require an exact Schur inverse square root:
Chebyshev whitening accepts any constant-factor spectral frontier and costs
only $\widetilde O(T_{\rm mv}/\sqrt\alpha)$.  More directly, a positive
geometric resolvent ladder realizes the inverse square root through shifted
exact rungs whose total monotone settlement mass is $O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$.
Every partial rung has an exact nonnegative delayed-anchor debt.  The companion
response note now aggregates every fixed-face debt before a signed Chebyshev
flush, returning certified one-sided exterior intervals in
$\widetilde O(\operatorname{cvol}(T)/\sqrt\alpha)$ graph work.  The remaining
graph operation on high-rank cores is an output-sensitive partial flush or a
different safe trace: the companion note also proves that a literal path gate
can be forced through singleton faces whose volume-growth ratios tend to one,
so pure geometric sleep is not sufficient.
The exact gate also dominates one unit proximal-gradient step in objective
value.  Consequently its objective gap contracts by the unconditional factor
$1-\alpha$ per nonempty batch, giving an exact margin-sensitive termination
bound.  This is sharp at the level relevant to acceleration: on the
three-vertex endpoint path, the first-batch gap ratio tends to
$(1-\alpha)^2/(1+6\alpha+\alpha^2)=1-8\alpha+O(\alpha^2)$ as
$\rho\downarrow0$.  Thus no graph-uniform
$1-\Theta(\sqrt\alpha)$ batch contraction is available; an accelerated
general proof must use finite support discovery or compressed Schur events,
not objective decrease alone.  We record the exact block-Schur formula for a
tied activation batch, reducing that remaining implementation problem to
signed block-column updates of inactive slacks.
We also give an exact RPPR support-oracle terminal theorem: on a forest
support, the shifted Dirichlet solve returns the RPPR optimum and the
unregularized residual certificate in $O(1/\rho)$ work.  What remains is
compressed event or batch maintenance inside a large biconnected core that
has neither bounded articulation blocks, an equitable tree quotient, nor
thin radial support.  Along the RPPR path
$\rho_{k+1}=\lambda_\alpha^s\rho_k$, the support volumes form a geometric
ledger: exact re-solving of all supplied width-$w$ supports costs
$O((w+1)^2/(\rho_{\rm final}\sqrt\alpha))$, with no extra dynamic-range
logarithm.  If support discovery has the same output-sensitive cost, this
matches the intended product scale.  More generally, under an explicit
threshold-progress hypothesis, the residual ladder has work
$\widetilde O(V/\sqrt\alpha)$, specializing to the intended
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ or
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ scale when an appropriate volume bound
$V$ is available.  No universal local-oracle optimality claim is made.
\end{abstract}
