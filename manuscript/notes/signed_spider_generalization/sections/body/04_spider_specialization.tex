\section{Spiders: what is proved, what is stronger, and what remains}
\label{sec:spider-specialization}

Let $G_{B,N}$ have a hub $o$ and $B$ internally disjoint arms of length $N$.
For a prefix depth $1\leq L<N$, let $U_L$ contain the hub and the first $L$
vertices of every arm; degrees are the ambient degrees of $G_{B,N}$.  This is
the first nontrivial family after a star: the quotient dimension grows with
$L$, the support can propagate along long arms, but the graph remains
bipartite and has a scalar articulation at the hub.

\subsection{Exact prefix spectrum and the SOR consequence}

The proof-owning \texttt{volume\_gated\_acceleration} note establishes
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{U_L}=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2(L+1)}\right)
 \label{eq:spider-prefix-cross-singular}
\end{equation}
for the largest singular value in \eqref{eq:bipartite-cross-block}.  Combining
that proved spectrum with \Cref{thm:fixed-bipartite-sor} gives the following
new SOR specialization.

\begin{corollary}[Optimal SOR on an exposed spider prefix]
\label{cor:spider-prefix-sor}
On $U_L$, put
\begin{equation}
 t_L:=\sqrt{
  1-c_\alpha^2
  \cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2(L+1)}\right)
 }.
 \label{eq:spider-prefix-t}
\end{equation}
Then the optimal red--black SOR factor is
$\zeta_L=(1-t_L)/(1+t_L)$, and
\begin{equation}
 t_L=\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{\alpha+L^{-2}}\right).
 \label{eq:spider-prefix-t-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Since
$\operatorname{vol}(U_L)=B(2L+1)$, a known-face semantic solve to tolerance
$\tau$ costs
\begin{equation}
 W_{B,L}=O\!\left(
  \frac{B(2L+1)}{\sqrt{\alpha+L^{-2}}}
  \log\frac{2}{\tau\sqrt{\alpha+L^{-2}}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:spider-prefix-sor-work}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \eqref{eq:spider-prefix-cross-singular} into
\eqref{eq:fixed-face-optimal-sor}.  Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 t_L^2=(1-c_\alpha^2)
 +c_\alpha^2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2(L+1)}\right).
\end{equation*}
Here $1-c_\alpha^2=4\alpha/(1+\alpha)^2$ and the sine term is
$\Theta(L^{-2})$, proving \eqref{eq:spider-prefix-t-asymptotic}.  Every
selected arm vertex has ambient degree two because $L<N$, while the hub has
degree $B$, giving $B+2BL=B(2L+1)$ and then
\eqref{eq:spider-prefix-sor-work}.
\end{proof}

This formula has a useful interpretation:
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{t_L}
 =\Theta\!\left(\min\{L,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\right).
 \label{eq:spider-two-regimes}
\end{equation}
Before the arm reaches the diffusion length $\alpha^{-1/2}$, its length is
the condition scale; afterward, teleportation caps the condition number and
the square-root PageRank scale takes over.  The spider does not create an
$\alpha^{-2}$ condition number.  Its difficulty is repeated volume and
support evolution, not worse fixed-face conditioning.

For the complete finite spider rather than a proper Dirichlet prefix,
$\sigma_U=1$ because every connected graph's normalized adjacency has the
degree eigenvector.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 t_{\rm full}=\frac{2\sqrt\alpha}{1+\alpha},
 \qquad
 W_{B,N}=O\!\left(
  \frac{BN}{\sqrt\alpha}
  \log\frac{2}{\tau\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:full-spider-sor-work}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact radial PPR profile}

For a hub seed, symmetry makes the degree-normalized PPR value
\begin{equation}
 y_j:=\frac{\pi_v}{d_v}
 \qquad (v\text{ at depth }j)
 \label{eq:spider-radial-y}
\end{equation}
constant on each level.  Put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\alpha:=\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:spider-lambda}
\end{equation}
Solving the one-dimensional recurrence gives
\begin{align}
 y_0&=\frac{\sqrt\alpha}{B}
       \frac{1+\lambda_\alpha^{2N}}
            {1-\lambda_\alpha^{2N}},
 \label{eq:spider-center-profile}\\
 y_j&=y_0
 \frac{\lambda_\alpha^j+\lambda_\alpha^{2N-j}}
      {1+\lambda_\alpha^{2N}},
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq N.
 \label{eq:spider-arm-profile}
\end{align}
In particular, $0<y_j\leq y_0$.

\begin{proof}[Derivation]
The mass PageRank equation divided by degree is
\begin{equation*}
 y_v=\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}\frac{s_v}{d_v}
 +c_\alpha\frac1{d_v}\sum_{u\sim v}y_u.
\end{equation*}
Away from the hub this is
$y_j=(c_\alpha/2)(y_{j-1}+y_{j+1})$, whose characteristic roots are
$\lambda_\alpha$ and $\lambda_\alpha^{-1}$.  The endpoint condition
$y_N=c_\alpha y_{N-1}$ selects a reflected profile proportional to
$\lambda_\alpha^j+\lambda_\alpha^{2N-j}$.  The hub condition
$y_0=2\alpha/((1+\alpha)B)+c_\alpha y_1$ yields
\eqref{eq:spider-center-profile}.  Finally,
$\lambda^j+\lambda^{2N-j}\leq1+\lambda^{2N}$ for $0<\lambda<1$.
\end{proof}

Equations \eqref{eq:spider-center-profile}--\eqref{eq:spider-arm-profile}
identify a natural output-scale family.  Take
\begin{equation}
 N=\Theta(\alpha^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 B=\Theta(\sqrt\alpha/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}).
 \label{eq:spider-output-scale-family}
\end{equation}
in the nontrivial regime
$\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}=O(\sqrt\alpha)$, with integer rounding.
Then $\max_j y_j=\Theta(\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$ and
$BN=\Theta(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$.  A constant-factor reduction of the
zero-start semantic error in $O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ sweeps would therefore give
the desired log-free work
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}}\right).
 \label{eq:spider-desired-log-free}
\end{equation}

The fixed-face two-norm theorem proves the same expression with a logarithm,
but it does not prove the constant-factor semantic statement because the
two-norm conversion can lose a factor depending on the number of arms.  The
following maximum-norm wave argument removes that loss.

\subsection{Exact log-free semantic damping for plain SOR}

Let $x^{(k)}$ be the zero-start iterate after $k$ complete red--black sweeps,
where every even-depth shell is relaxed first and every odd-depth shell
second.  On the complete spider use the graph-global parameter
\begin{equation}
 \omega_\alpha=1+\lambda_\alpha^2,
 \qquad
 \zeta_\alpha:=\lambda_\alpha^2.
 \label{eq:radial-sor-parameters}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Log-free semantic damping on every finite symmetric spider]
\label{thm:radial-semantic-damping}
For every $0<\alpha<1$, every number of arms $B\geq1$, every arm length
$N\geq1$, and every integer $k\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 \max_v\frac{|x_v^{(k)}-x_v^0|}{\sqrt{d_v}}
 &\leq
 \zeta_\alpha^k
 \left(1+2k(1-\zeta_\alpha)\right)
 \max_v\frac{x_v^0}{\sqrt{d_v}}
 \label{eq:radial-semantic-exact-envelope}\\
 &\leq
 8(1+k\sqrt\alpha)e^{-4k\sqrt\alpha}
 \max_v\frac{x_v^0}{\sqrt{d_v}}.
 \label{eq:radial-semantic-exponential-envelope}
\end{align}
The constants are independent of both $B$ and $N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\begin{equation*}
 e_v^{(k)}:=
 \frac{x_v^0-x_v^{(k)}}{\sqrt{d_v}}.
\end{equation*}
Dividing the homogeneous coordinate-SOR error equation by $\sqrt{d_v}$
gives the semantic update
\begin{equation}
 e_v^+=(1-\omega_\alpha)e_v
 +\frac{\omega_\alpha c_\alpha}{d_v}
   \sum_{u\sim v}e_u
 =-\zeta_\alpha e_v
 +\frac{2\lambda_\alpha}{d_v}
   \sum_{u\sim v}e_u.
 \label{eq:radial-semantic-coordinate-update}
\end{equation}
Hub seeding and equal arm lengths preserve radial symmetry.  Denote the
common depth-$j$ value by $e_j^{(k)}$.

Reflect the depth sequence evenly at $0$ and $N$ and then extend it with
period $2N$.  This extension turns the endpoint averages into ordinary
nearest-neighbor averages on a cycle.  For indices modulo $N$, put
\begin{equation*}
 E_i^{(k)}:=e_{2i}^{(k)},
 \qquad
 O_i^{(k)}:=e_{2i+1}^{(k)},
\end{equation*}
and let $L$ be the cyclic shift on these $N$ cells.  Equation
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-coordinate-update} becomes
\begin{align}
 E^{(k+1)}
 &=-\zeta_\alpha E^{(k)}
   +\lambda_\alpha(I+L^{-1})O^{(k)},
 \label{eq:radial-even-update}\\
 O^{(k+1)}
 &=-\zeta_\alpha O^{(k)}
   +\lambda_\alpha(I+L)E^{(k+1)}.
 \label{eq:radial-odd-update}
\end{align}
Eliminating the opposite color shows that either
$Z^{(k)}=E^{(k)}$ or $Z^{(k)}=O^{(k)}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Z^{(k+1)}
 =2\zeta_\alpha MZ^{(k)}
  -\zeta_\alpha^2Z^{(k-1)},
 \qquad
 M:=\frac{L+L^{-1}}2.
 \label{eq:radial-chebyshev-recurrence}
\end{equation}
After dividing by $\zeta_\alpha^k$, the recurrence is the Chebyshev wave
equation.  For $k\geq1$ its exact solution is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{Z^{(k)}}{\zeta_\alpha^k}
 =T_k(M)Z^{(0)}
 +U_{k-1}(M)
   \left(\frac{Z^{(1)}}{\zeta_\alpha}-MZ^{(0)}\right).
 \label{eq:radial-chebyshev-solution}
\end{equation}
Because $M=(L+L^{-1})/2$,
\begin{align}
 T_k(M)&=\frac{L^k+L^{-k}}2,
 \label{eq:radial-chebyshev-first-kind}\\
 U_{k-1}(M)&=
 \sum_{q=0}^{k-1}L^{k-1-2q}.
 \label{eq:radial-chebyshev-second-kind}
\end{align}
Every shift is an isometry in maximum norm.  Therefore the two operator norms
in \eqref{eq:radial-chebyshev-first-kind}--%
\eqref{eq:radial-chebyshev-second-kind} are at most $1$ and $k$,
respectively.  This is the dimension-free step that an Euclidean spectral
bound misses.

It remains to bound the two initial wave mismatches.  At zero start,
$e_j^{(0)}=y_j$, so \eqref{eq:spider-arm-profile} gives
\begin{equation}
 \|E^{(0)}\|_\infty=y_0,
 \qquad
 \|O^{(0)}\|_\infty\leq y_0.
 \label{eq:radial-initial-color-norms}
\end{equation}
The source lies on the even color.  The odd PPR equations give
\begin{equation*}
 O^{(0)}=\frac{c_\alpha}{2}(I+L)E^{(0)}.
\end{equation*}
Using $c_\alpha/\lambda_\alpha=2/(1+\zeta_\alpha)$ in
\eqref{eq:radial-even-update} yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac{E^{(1)}}{\zeta_\alpha}-ME^{(0)}
 =s_\alpha(I+M)E^{(0)},
 \qquad
 s_\alpha:=\frac{1-\zeta_\alpha}{1+\zeta_\alpha}.
 \label{eq:radial-even-wave-mismatch}
\end{equation}
Hence its maximum norm is at most
$2(1-\zeta_\alpha)y_0$.

For the odd color, direct substitution of
\eqref{eq:radial-even-update} into
\eqref{eq:radial-odd-update} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{O^{(1)}}{\zeta_\alpha}-MO^{(0)}
 =(M-\zeta_\alpha I)O^{(0)}.
 \label{eq:radial-odd-wave-mismatch}
\end{equation}
The explicit profile is decreasing from either reflected endpoint and, for
$0\leq j\leq N-2$, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq y_j-y_{j+2}
 =\frac{(1-\zeta_\alpha)y_0}
        {1+\lambda_\alpha^{2N}}
   \left(
    \lambda_\alpha^j
    -\lambda_\alpha^{2N-j-2}
   \right)
 \leq(1-\zeta_\alpha)y_0.
 \label{eq:radial-profile-two-step-difference}
\end{equation}
The reflected boundary differences obey the same bound.  Consequently,
$\|(M-I)O^{(0)}\|_\infty\leq
(1-\zeta_\alpha)y_0$, and
\eqref{eq:radial-odd-wave-mismatch} also has norm at most
$2(1-\zeta_\alpha)y_0$.

Applying \eqref{eq:radial-chebyshev-solution} to both colors proves
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-exact-envelope}.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 1-\zeta_\alpha
 =\frac{4\sqrt\alpha}{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^2}
 \leq4\sqrt\alpha,
 \qquad
 \zeta_\alpha^k=\lambda_\alpha^{2k}
 \leq e^{-4k\sqrt\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Since $1+8z\leq8(1+z)$ for $z\geq0$, the exponential envelope follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The source-first sweep order is necessary]
\label{prop:radial-sweep-order-witness}
The sharp envelope
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-exact-envelope} does not hold unchanged when the
source is on the second color and the original even--odd sweep order is kept.
Already on the two-vertex path, take $\alpha=1/9$, start from zero, put the
source on the odd vertex, and relax the even vertex first.  After one full
sweep the maximum semantic error has fallen by only $4/5$, whereas the
right-hand-side factor in
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-exact-envelope} is $5/8$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $\lambda_\alpha=1/2$ and $\zeta_\alpha=1/4$.  The exact semantic PPR
error at zero start is $(4/9,5/9)$, ordered as even then odd.  The two
coordinate updates from
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-coordinate-update} give
\begin{equation*}
 e_{\rm even}^{(1)}
 =-\frac14\frac49+\frac59=\frac49,
 \qquad
 e_{\rm odd}^{(1)}
 =-\frac14\frac59+\frac49=\frac{11}{36}.
\end{equation*}
Thus the maximum-error ratio is $(4/9)/(5/9)=4/5$.  In contrast, the claimed
one-sweep factor would be
$\zeta_\alpha(1+2(1-\zeta_\alpha))=5/8$.  Swapping the two colors puts the
source first and restores the theorem by symmetry.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Log-free output-scale plain SOR]
\label{cor:radial-output-scale-sor}
On the family \eqref{eq:spider-output-scale-family}, choose the hidden
constants so that the zero-start semantic error is a fixed constant multiple
of $\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}$.  A predetermined
$k=O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ full-sweep count reaches semantic error
$\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}$ in
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm radial\text{-}SOR}
 =O\!\left(
  \frac1{\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}}
 \right)
 \label{eq:radial-output-scale-sor-work}
\end{equation}
charged degree work, including every sweep and explicit output.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:radial-semantic-damping} reduces any fixed initial-to-target
ratio in $O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ sweeps.  A full spider sweep costs its degree
volume $2BN=\Theta(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$, and the output has
$1+BN=O(1/\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$ coordinates.
\end{proof}

This closes the former plain-SOR radial conjecture, but only for a complete
equal-arm spider, a hub seed, zero start, full red--black sweeps, and the
predetermined semantic schedule, with the hub color relaxed first.
Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-sweep-order-witness} shows that the order clause
cannot simply be deleted.  Unequal arms, nonradial seeds, support discovery,
and changing faces are not consequences of the theorem.

\subsection{A graph-uniform maximum-norm extension is impossible}

The fixed-face spectral theorem remains valid on every bipartite graph, but
its Euclidean bound cannot be converted into a graph-independent relative
maximum-semantic contraction.  The obstruction is finite propagation across
a degree-skewed sequence of two-step channels.

For an integer $n\geq1$, define a finite simple connected bipartite graph
$G_n=R\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ as follows.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 R=\{r_0,\ldots,r_n\},
 \qquad
 B=\mathbin{\dot\bigcup}_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i,
 \qquad
 |B_i|=w_i:=3^{n-1-i}.
\end{equation*}
Every vertex in $B_i$ is adjacent to exactly $r_i$ and $r_{i+1}$, and there
are no other edges.  Seed $r_0$, start SOR from zero on the entire graph,
relax $R$ first, and use the graph-global parameter
$\omega_\alpha=1+\zeta_\alpha$ from
\eqref{eq:radial-sor-parameters}.

\begin{theorem}[No graph-uniform relative semantic SOR envelope]
\label{thm:global-semantic-sor-stop}
Let $0<\alpha<1$, and let $x^{(k)}$ be the iterate on $G_n$ after $k$ complete
source-first red--black sweeps.  Then, for every $0\leq k\leq n$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|x^{(k)}-x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
      {\|x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
 \geq c_\alpha^{8n},
 \qquad
 c_\alpha=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.
 \label{eq:global-semantic-sor-stop-lower}
\end{equation}
Consequently there is no finite graph-independent constant $C$ for which
every finite connected bipartite graph, single seed on the first color, and
integer $k\geq0$ obey
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|x^{(k)}-x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
      {\|x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
 \leq C(1+k\sqrt\alpha)e^{-4k\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:global-semantic-sor-envelope-refuted}
\end{equation}
More strongly, for every fixed $T>0$ there is no graph-uniform fixed-factor
semantic reduction guaranteed at any time through $T/\sqrt\alpha$ full
sweeps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $y=D^{-1/2}x^0$.  In semantic coordinates the PPR equations have the form
\begin{equation}
 y_R=c_\alpha Ay_B+h e_{r_0},
 \qquad
 y_B=c_\alpha Cy_R,
 \qquad h>0,
 \label{eq:funnel-semantic-ppr}
\end{equation}
where $A:R\leftarrow B$ and $C:B\leftarrow R$ are row-stochastic neighbor
averages.  Thus, with $K=AC$ and $r=c_\alpha^2$,
\begin{equation}
 y_R=h(I-rK)^{-1}e_{r_0}.
 \label{eq:funnel-even-green}
\end{equation}
The semantic maximum principle puts the maximum of $y$ at $r_0$: at every
other vertex $y_v=c_\alpha(P_{\rm row}y)_v$ is strictly below the maximum of
its neighbors.

The two-step chain $K$ is a lazy birth--death chain on
$r_0,\ldots,r_n$.  Its endpoint rows split equally between staying and the
unique inward neighbor, while every internal row satisfies
\begin{equation}
 K(i,i)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 K(i,i-1)=\frac38,
 \qquad
 K(i,i+1)=\frac18.
 \label{eq:funnel-two-step-chain}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a first step chooses one of the $w_{i-1}+w_i$ incident channel
vertices and the second step returns to either endpoint with probability
$1/2$; here $w_{i-1}=3w_i$.

Let $\tau_0$ be the first hitting time of $r_0$ for $K$ started at $r_n$.
At every positive internal state the expected one-step decrease in the index
is $3/8-1/8=1/4$, and at $r_n$ it is $1/2$.  The additive drift bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_{r_n}\tau_0\leq4n.
 \label{eq:funnel-hitting-time}
\end{equation}
The discounted Green identity and the strong Markov property give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{y_{r_n}}{y_{r_0}}
 =\frac{[(I-rK)^{-1}]_{n0}}{[(I-rK)^{-1}]_{00}}
 =\mathbb E_{r_n}r^{\tau_0}.
 \label{eq:funnel-discounted-hitting}
\end{equation}
Since $t\mapsto r^t$ is convex and decreasing, Jensen's inequality and
\eqref{eq:funnel-hitting-time} imply
\begin{equation}
 \frac{y_{r_n}}{y_{r_0}}
 \geq r^{\mathbb E\tau_0}
 \geq r^{4n}=c_\alpha^{8n}.
 \label{eq:funnel-green-ratio-lower}
\end{equation}

It remains to connect this static ratio to literal SOR.  Let
$e^{(k)}=D^{-1/2}(x^0-x^{(k)})$.  At zero start $e^{(0)}=y$.  The semantic
coordinate update is
\eqref{eq:radial-semantic-coordinate-update}, and
$2\lambda_\alpha=(1+\zeta_\alpha)c_\alpha$.  Therefore an updated nonseed
coordinate remains exactly equal to $y_v$ whenever its current value and all
neighbor values still equal the corresponding entries of $y$.  Only the seed
can change in the first $R$ block.  Induction over the two color blocks shows
that after $k\geq1$ sweeps the changed coordinates lie within graph distance
$2k-1$ of $r_0$.  Since $r_n$ has distance $2n$, it is unchanged for every
$k\leq n$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\|e^{(k)}\|_\infty}{\|e^{(0)}\|_\infty}
 \geq\frac{y_{r_n}}{y_{r_0}}
 \geq c_\alpha^{8n},
\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{eq:global-semantic-sor-stop-lower}.

For the two impossibility statements, fix $T>0$ and set
$\alpha_n=(T/n)^2$ for integers $n>T$.  Then
$n=T/\sqrt{\alpha_n}$ and
\begin{equation*}
 c_{\alpha_n}^{8n}
 =\left(\frac{1-T^2/n^2}{1+T^2/n^2}\right)^{8n}
 \longrightarrow1.
\end{equation*}
Thus no fixed reduction occurs through that entire horizon.  Given a proposed
$C$, choose $T$ so that $C(1+T)e^{-4T}<1/2$ and then take $n$ large enough
that the left side at $k=n$ exceeds $1/2$.  This contradicts
\eqref{eq:global-semantic-sor-envelope-refuted}.
\end{proof}

The relative stop also has a narrowly scoped absolute-work consequence for
this literal full-sweep primitive.  It uses the fact that the seed scale is
not smaller than inverse graph volume.

\begin{corollary}[Complete-face plain-SOR product-work stop]
\label{cor:global-semantic-sor-work-stop}
There is no graph-independent constant $C_W$ such that zero-start,
source-color-first plain SOR with the graph-global $\omega_\alpha$, output
only after complete red--black sweeps, always reaches
\begin{equation*}
 \|x^{(k)}-x^0\|_{D,\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}
\end{equation*}
on every finite connected bipartite graph in charged degree work at most
$C_W/(\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm ppr})$.  This remains false when the
entire graph and its bipartition are supplied for free.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $G_n$, put $d_i=d_{r_i}$ and let $H$ be the $R$-by-$B$ incidence matrix.
Then
\begin{equation*}
 K=D_R^{-1}H D_B^{-1}H^\top,
 \qquad
 D_R^{1/2}KD_R^{-1/2}
 =D_R^{-1/2}H D_B^{-1}H^\top D_R^{-1/2}\succeq0.
\end{equation*}
Thus $K$ is a reversible positive-semidefinite Markov chain, with stationary
law
\begin{equation*}
 \pi_i=\frac{d_i}{\sum_{j=0}^n d_j},
 \qquad
 d_0=3^{n-1},
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^n d_j=|E(G_n)|=3^n-1.
\end{equation*}
Write $G_r(0,0)=[(I-rK)^{-1}]_{00}$.  The stationary eigenvector's
contribution to the reversible spectral expansion, positivity of all the
remaining resolvent terms, and the elementary return-probability upper bound
give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\pi_0}{1-r}\leq G_r(0,0)\leq\frac1{1-r}.
 \label{eq:funnel-green-diagonal-bounds}
\end{equation}
Here the exact single-seed source in
\eqref{eq:funnel-semantic-ppr} is
$h=(1-c_\alpha)/d_0$.  Combining
\eqref{eq:funnel-even-green} and
\eqref{eq:funnel-green-diagonal-bounds} yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{(1+c_\alpha)(3^n-1)}
 \leq y_{r_0}
 \leq\frac1{(1+c_\alpha)3^{n-1}}.
 \label{eq:funnel-seed-output-scale}
\end{equation}
In particular, since
$\operatorname{vol}(G_n)=2(3^n-1)$,
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{vol}(G_n)y_{r_0}\geq\frac2{1+c_\alpha}>1.
\end{equation*}

Fix $T>0$, take $\alpha_n=(T/n)^2$ as in the theorem, and set
$\varepsilon_n=y_{r_0}/2$.  For all sufficiently large $n$, the semantic
error at every completed sweep through $n$ is strictly larger than
$\varepsilon_n$.  Those $n$ sweeps alone cost
$n\operatorname{vol}(G_n)$.  With $q_n=\sqrt{\alpha_n}=T/n$,
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{n\operatorname{vol}(G_n)}{1/(q_n\varepsilon_n)}
 =\frac{T\operatorname{vol}(G_n)y_{r_0}}2
 \geq\frac{T}{1+c_{\alpha_n}}>\frac T2.
\end{equation*}
Since $T$ is arbitrary, no constant $C_W$ can give the claimed product-work
bound.
\end{proof}

The same family separates harmless scalar adaptation from genuinely nonlocal
response information.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive block-SOR and source-polynomial propagation stop]
\label{thm:adaptive-polynomial-propagation-stop}
On the supplied funnel $G_n$, the following statements hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item Start from zero and perform complete red--black sweeps, always updating
      the source color $R$ before $B$.  At every visit to every coordinate,
      the relaxation $\omega_{k,u}$ may be any finite real scalar chosen from
      the entire graph, exact eigenvalue advice, randomness, and all previous
      scalar or iterate history.  Provided the only state write is the literal
      local SOR row update, after $k$ complete sweeps
      \begin{equation}
       \operatorname{supp}(x_R^{(k)})\subseteq\{r_0,\ldots,r_{k-1}\},
       \qquad
       \operatorname{supp}(x_B^{(k)})
       \subseteq\bigcup_{i=0}^{k-1}B_i.
       \label{eq:adaptive-sor-light-cone}
      \end{equation}
      Hence $x_{r_n}^{(k)}=0$ and
      \begin{equation}
       \frac{\|x^{(k)}-x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
            {\|x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
       \ge c_\alpha^{8n}
       \qquad(0\le k\le n).
       \label{eq:adaptive-sor-stop}
      \end{equation}
      This includes face-optimal, per-sweep, per-coordinate, and safeguarded
      scalar relaxation schedules.  Convergence-valid SOR schedules normally
      restrict $\omega_{k,u}$ to $(0,2)$; the propagation statement itself
      needs only that every scalar be finite.

\item Let $b$ be the single-seed normalized right-hand side and let $p_d$ be
      any scalar polynomial of degree at most $d$.  Then
      \begin{equation}
       \operatorname{supp}(p_d(Q)b)
       \subseteq \operatorname{Ball}_{G_n}(r_0,d).
       \label{eq:source-polynomial-light-cone}
      \end{equation}
      Consequently, for every $d\le2n-1$,
      \begin{equation}
       \frac{\|p_d(Q)b-x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
            {\|x^0\|_{D,\infty}}
       \ge c_\alpha^{8n}.
       \label{eq:source-polynomial-stop}
      \end{equation}
      The same conclusion holds pathwise for any zero-start recurrence whose
      vectors are formed from scalar linear combinations and at most $d$
      successive sparse $Q$-matvec dependency layers.  Its scalar
      coefficients may depend adaptively on exact eigenvalues or global inner
      products.  Thus the statement covers standard Chebyshev
      semi-iteration, unpreconditioned CG/Krylov, heavy ball, and
      variable-coefficient Richardson in their complete-face forms.
\end{enumerate}

For every fixed $T>0$, taking respectively
$\alpha_n=(T/n)^2$ in part~(i) or
$\alpha_n=(T/(2n-1))^2$ in part~(ii) makes the lower bounds tend to one.
Thus neither class has a graph-uniform fixed semantic reduction through
$T/\sqrt{\alpha_n}$ complete block sweeps or local matvec layers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the semantic iterate as $z=D^{-1/2}x$.  One literal coordinate update
has the affine form
\begin{equation}
 z_u^+=(1-\omega_{k,u})z_u
 +\omega_{k,u}\left(c_\alpha(P_{\rm row}z)_u
 +h\mathbf1_{\{u=r_0\}}\right).
 \label{eq:adaptive-semantic-row-update}
\end{equation}
Starting from zero, a new nonzero coordinate can therefore appear only at
the seed or next to the old support; the numerical value of the scalar
$\omega_{k,u}$ is irrelevant.  During an $R$ block there are no same-color
edges, so visit order and repeated visits within that block cannot carry
information through a second layer.  Inductively, the $k$th $R$ block can
first reach $r_{k-1}$ and the following $B$ block can first reach $B_{k-1}$.
This proves \eqref{eq:adaptive-sor-light-cone}.  Since $r_n$ is at distance
$2n$, it remains zero through sweep $n$.  The exact solution has semantic
maximum at $r_0$, while
$y_{r_n}/y_{r_0}\ge c_\alpha^{8n}$ by
\eqref{eq:funnel-green-ratio-lower}; hence
\eqref{eq:adaptive-sor-stop}.

For part~(ii), $Q$ has the graph sparsity pattern plus its diagonal and $b$ is
supported only at $r_0$.  Induction on $j$ gives
$\operatorname{supp}(Q^jb)\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}(r_0,j)$, proving
\eqref{eq:source-polynomial-light-cone}.  Scalar combinations, including
adaptive three-term coefficients, do not enlarge the union of these
supports.  If $d\le2n-1$, the polynomial iterate is zero at $r_n$, so the
same static Green ratio proves \eqref{eq:source-polynomial-stop}.

For either scaling of $\alpha_n$,
$c_{\alpha_n}^{8n}\to1$, exactly as in
\Cref{thm:global-semantic-sor-stop}.  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The block-order hypothesis cannot be replaced by an arbitrary notion of one
``sweep.''  With $\omega=1$, the single Gauss--Seidel permutation
\begin{equation}
 r_0,\ B_0,\ r_1,\ B_1,\ldots,\ r_{n-1},\ B_{n-1},\ r_n
 \label{eq:interleaved-funnel-order}
\end{equation}
visits every coordinate once and changes $r_n$: after the seed update, strict
positivity propagates along each next local average.  What
\Cref{thm:adaptive-polynomial-propagation-stop} counts is color-block depth
or sparse-matvec dependency depth, not an order-free word ``sweep.''

\begin{corollary}[Scalar-adaptive and polynomial complete-face work stop]
\label{cor:adaptive-polynomial-work-stop}
No graph-independent constant gives
$O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}))$ charged work on every supplied
bipartite face for either of the following zero-start primitives:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
 \item literal source-first complete-face red--black SOR with arbitrary
       adaptive scalar relaxations; or
 \item a source-Krylov/Chebyshev recurrence in which every new polynomial
       dependency layer is evaluated by a complete sparse $Q$-matvec.
\end{enumerate}
Spectral preprocessing is allowed here only through scalar advice, and all of
its work and scalar reductions are charged in addition to these passes.
Preprocessing that writes a nonlocal vector state belongs to the separately
excluded response class below.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For part~(a), use the proof of
\Cref{cor:global-semantic-sor-work-stop}; its finite-propagation step is now
uniform over the scalar schedule by
\Cref{thm:adaptive-polynomial-propagation-stop}(i).

For part~(b), take $\alpha_n=(T/(2n-1))^2$ and
$\varepsilon_n=y_{r_0}/2$.  For all sufficiently large $n$, every degree at
most $2n-1$ still has error above $\varepsilon_n$.  Under the stated
complete-face primitive, one sparse $Q$-matvec is charged as one
degree-volume scan (diagonal vector work only adds), so these dependency
layers cost at least $(2n-1)\operatorname{vol}(G_n)$ up to an immaterial
endpoint convention.  Since $q_n=\sqrt{\alpha_n}=T/(2n-1)$,
\[
 \frac{(2n-1)\operatorname{vol}(G_n)}{1/(q_n\varepsilon_n)}
 =\frac{T\operatorname{vol}(G_n)y_{r_0}}2>\frac T2.
\]
Arbitrary $T$ rules out a uniform constant.  Spectral estimation only adds
work.
\end{proof}

The scope boundary is algebraic, not rhetorical.  Exact eigenvalues used
only as scalar coefficients do not break finite propagation.  A dense
eigenvector transform, a nonzero global warm start, an inverse, Schur, or
Green response, or a preconditioner with nonlocal columns can.  For example,
$Q^{-1}$ makes one preconditioned Richardson step exact, and the funnel
response
\[
 y_R=h(I-c_\alpha^2K)^{-1}e_{r_0},\qquad y_B=c_\alpha Cy_R
\]
fills every layer immediately.  More generally, a preconditioner with graph
propagation radius $\ell$ expands one $BQ$ dependency layer by at most
$\ell+1$; a dense response has no finite-radius conclusion.  The theorem is
therefore not a lower bound on response composition, quotient solves, or
other nonlocal primitives.

The scope is deliberately algorithm-specific.  The theorem and corollary do
not contradict the Euclidean fixed-face spectral theorem or its supplied-face
logarithmic solve, whose conversion to maximum semantic norm can depend on
graph geometry.  They do not lower-bound other algorithms, local discovery,
or response composition; indeed the graph is supplied for free above.  The
adaptive extension refutes only graph-uniform fixed reduction and target
product work for the named complete-face scalar block-SOR and local-polynomial
classes.  The
symmetric-spider theorem and response-based tree algorithms remain intact.

\subsection{How the radial two-rung theorem differs}

The proof-owning \texttt{two\_rung\_direct\_theory} note gives a different
accelerated radial algorithm.  On a symmetric spider with $R$ arms, it groups
every depth shell into one commuting block, uses optimal SOR while the
normalized residual is above a coarse threshold, and finishes with unit
relaxation.  If
$B_{\rm edge}>1$ denotes the source theorem's admissibility constant, the
imported theorem assumes
\begin{equation*}
 1<B_{\rm rung}<B_{\rm edge},
 \qquad
 B_{\rm rung}R\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}<1,
\end{equation*}
and gives
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm radial\text{-}two\text{-}rung}
 =O\!\left(
  \mathcal V_{\rm exp}
  \left[1+
   \frac{\log(1/(B_{\rm rung}R\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}))}
        {\sqrt\alpha}
  \right]
 \right)
 \label{eq:imported-radial-two-rung}
\end{equation}
when the arms are not yet exhausted.  Under the same two parameter
hypotheses, a second imported theorem handles finite arms: if the wave reaches
the far leaves, it certifies the closed pendant paths, Schur-deflates them,
solves the remaining center scalar, and reverse-recovers all arms within the
same asymptotic bound.

This imported result and
\Cref{thm:radial-semantic-damping} should not be merged.  The imported method
uses the note-scoped residual gate
$\tau=\alpha\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}$, a two-rung schedule, refreshed radial
selection, and possibly exact Schur deflation; it can explore only the needed
prefix.  The new theorem instead scans the complete spider every sweep and
uses a predetermined semantic count.  Both are accelerated, but their
locality and stopping contracts differ.

\subsection{A stronger end-to-end spider algorithm already exists}

For RPPR, forcing SOR to perform support discovery is unnecessary on a
spider.  The proof-owning \texttt{propagate\_settle\_framework} note gives an
exact kinetic response algorithm for a rooted spider with arbitrary arm
lengths.  Eliminating each active arm prefix leaves an arrowhead system.  Its
hub value is one monotone scalar, and every next arm event has a stable
threshold.  A heap reports each event once and reverse substitution recovers
the exact solution.

The imported charged bound is
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm kinetic}
 =O\!\left(\operatorname{cvol}(S^\rho)
            \log(2+B)\right),
 \label{eq:kinetic-spider-import}
\end{equation}
where $S^\rho=\operatorname{supp}(x^\rho)$ and
$\operatorname{cvol}(S)=\sum_{v\in S}(1+d_v)$.  Since
$\operatorname{vol}(S^\rho)\leq1/\rho$ and
$\operatorname{cvol}(S^\rho)\leq2/\rho$ for a nonempty support, choosing
$\rho=\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}$ and applying
\Cref{thm:rppr-ppr-bias} gives
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm kinetic}
 =O\!\left(
  \frac{\log(2+B)}{\varepsilon_{\rm ppr}}
 \right)
 \label{eq:kinetic-spider-ppr-work}
\end{equation}
for semantic PPR output in exact arithmetic.
If $S^\rho$ is empty, the source algorithm instead returns the zero vector
after its constant-work initial test.

This is stronger than \eqref{eq:spider-desired-log-free} in its
$\alpha$-dependence.  It is not evidence that the SOR analysis is wrong; it
uses a stronger persistent-response primitive that exploits the spider's
one-articulation geometry.  The algorithmic lesson is to use the response
solver on spiders and use SOR as a benchmark or a numerical tail, rather than
insist that one primitive be universal.
