\section{What the ranking theorem already proves}
\label{sec:direct-fixed-region}

Fix a coordinate region $S$ and hold $x_{S^c}$ constant. Let $x^\dagger$
be the unique minimizer over that affine subspace, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(x):=f(x)-f(x^\dagger),
 \qquad
 C_S:=\sum_{u\in S}(1+d_u),
 \label{eq:direct-fixed-region-quantities}
\end{equation}
and rank only coordinates in $S$.

\begin{theorem}[Refreshed charge-aware contraction]
\label{thm:direct-refreshed-contraction}
Suppose every update refreshes the residual and chooses a maximizer of
$p_u(r)$ over all $u\in S$. If
$W_K:=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}c_{u_j}$, then a fixed-$\omega$ phase satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(x^{(K)})
 \leq\Delta(x^{(0)})
 \exp\left(-\frac{\kappa_\omega\alpha W_K}{C_S}\right).
 \label{eq:direct-refreshed-contraction}
\end{equation}
If selection is instead restricted to a nonempty frontier
$\mathcal F_\theta(r)$, the first operation after each refresh obeys the
weaker one-step bound
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(x^+)
 \leq
 \left(1-\frac{\kappa_\omega\alpha c_u}{4C_S}\right)
 \Delta(x).
 \label{eq:direct-frontier-contraction}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the fixed region,
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta(x)=\frac12r_S^\mathsf TQ_{SS}^{-1}r_S
 \leq\frac{\|r_S\|_2^2}{2\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Writing $h_{\max}=\max_{i\in S}r_i^2/c_i$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 h_{\max}\geq\frac{\|r_S\|_2^2}{C_S}
 \geq\frac{2\alpha\Delta(x)}{C_S}.
\end{equation*}
By \cref{thm:direct-ranking}, all-coordinate score maximization chooses
$h_u\geq h_{\max}/2$. Combining this with
\cref{lem:direct-coordinate-decrease} yields
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta(x)-\Delta(x^+)
 =\kappa_\omega c_uh_u
 \geq\frac{\kappa_\omega\alpha c_u}{C_S}\Delta(x).
\end{equation*}
Multiplication over updates and $1-z\leq e^{-z}$ prove
\cref{eq:direct-refreshed-contraction}.

For frontier selection, an inactive $v$ has $p_v\leq\theta$, while the
selected active coordinate has $p_u>\theta/2$. Hence
$p_u\geq\frac12\max_{i\in S}p_i$ and
$h_u\geq h_{\max}/8$. Repeating the preceding calculation gives
\cref{eq:direct-frontier-contraction}.
\end{proof}

This theorem validates the ranking chassis but does not explain acceleration.
For optimal SOR, put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\alpha:=\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:direct-lambda}
\end{equation}
Then $\omega_\star=1+\lambda_\alpha^2$ and
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{\omega_\star}
 =\frac{1-\lambda_\alpha^4}{1+\alpha}
 =\frac{8\sqrt\alpha}{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^4}.
 \label{eq:direct-optimal-objective-factor}
\end{equation}
Thus a ledger that sees only monotone coordinate objective decrease assigns
the spreading pushes a factor $\Theta(\sqrt\alpha)$ less progress than exact
pushes at the same residual. Classical SOR acceleration must instead be
visible through propagation, cancellation, or a multi-step polynomial.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional finite-work fallback]
\label{prop:direct-fallback}
Consider any fixed-$\omega$ threshold phase that executes only coordinates
with $|r_u|/\sqrt{d_u}>\theta$. If its initial fixed-region objective gap is
$\Delta_0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{\rm phase}
 \leq\frac{2\Delta_0}{\kappa_\omega\theta^2}.
 \label{eq:direct-phase-fallback}
\end{equation}
Consequently, every literal two-rung run on a finite graph terminates. If
$U_{\rm sw}:=\operatorname{supp}(r^{\rm sw})$ at the switch and
$\mathcal V_{\rm sw}:=\sum_{u\in U_{\rm sw}}d_u$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{\rm exact}
 \leq\frac{B^2(1+\alpha)}{\alpha}\mathcal V_{\rm sw}.
 \label{eq:direct-exact-fallback}
\end{equation}
For a single seed $s=e_v$, the spreading phase has the explicit bound
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{\rm spread}
 \leq
 \frac{(1+\sqrt\alpha)^4}
      {8B^2d_v\alpha^{3/2}\epsppr^2}.
 \label{eq:direct-spread-fallback}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An executed coordinate satisfies
$r_u^2>\theta^2d_u\geq\theta^2c_u/2$. Its objective decrease is therefore
larger than $\kappa_\omega\theta^2c_u/2$. Sum over the phase to obtain
\cref{eq:direct-phase-fallback}.

At the switch,
\begin{equation*}
 \|r^{\rm sw}\|_2^2
 =\sum_{u\in U_{\rm sw}}d_u
   \left(\frac{|r_u^{\rm sw}|}{\sqrt{d_u}}\right)^2
 \leq B^2\tau^2\mathcal V_{\rm sw}.
\end{equation*}
Hence the remaining objective gap is at most
$B^2\tau^2\mathcal V_{\rm sw}/(2\alpha)$. Apply
\cref{eq:direct-phase-fallback} with $\omega=1$, $\theta=\tau$, and
$\kappa_1=1/(1+\alpha)$.

Finally, at zero start and a single seed,
$\Delta_0=\frac12b^\mathsf TQ^{-1}b\leq\|b\|_2^2/(2\alpha)
=\alpha/(2d_v)$. Use the spreading threshold $B\tau=B\alpha\epsppr$ and
substitute \cref{eq:direct-optimal-objective-factor} into
\cref{eq:direct-phase-fallback}.
\end{proof}

The fallback is deliberately recorded as a correctness boundary, not as the
desired result. Its $1/\alpha$ terminal factor and
$1/(\alpha^{3/2}\epsppr^2)$ spreading factor are much too large. The next
section proves on the smallest coupled graph that both losses can be artifacts
of a one-step energy ledger.
