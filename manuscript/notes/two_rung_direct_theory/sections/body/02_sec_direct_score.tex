\section{Literal update and the exact work score}
\label{sec:direct-score}

Every diagonal entry of $Q$ is
\begin{equation}
 q_0:=Q_{uu}=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}.
 \label{eq:direct-diagonal}
\end{equation}
At a current residual value $\sigma=r_u$, a relaxation-$\omega$ push is
\begin{equation}
 x^+=x+\omega\frac{\sigma}{q_0}e_u,
 \qquad
 r^+=r-\omega\frac{\sigma}{q_0}Qe_u.
 \label{eq:direct-push}
\end{equation}
The literal implementation forms the live frontier
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_\theta(r)
 :=\left\{u:\frac{|r_u|}{\sqrt{d_u}}>\theta\right\},
 \label{eq:direct-frontier}
\end{equation}
ranks its snapshot by
\begin{equation}
 p_u(r):=\frac{|r_u|\sqrt{d_u}}{1+d_u},
 \label{eq:direct-empirical-score}
\end{equation}
and takes the top
$\min\{8192,\lceil|\mathcal F_\theta|/32\rceil\}$ coordinates. Operations
are sequential and guarded against the live threshold; a coordinate made
inactive by an earlier operation in the batch is skipped without charge.

\begin{lemma}[Exact coordinate decrease]
\label{lem:direct-coordinate-decrease}
For every $0<\omega<2$, the update \cref{eq:direct-push} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 f(x)-f(x^+)
 =\kappa_\omega r_u^2,
 \qquad
 \kappa_\omega:=\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{1+\alpha}.
 \label{eq:direct-coordinate-decrease}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the exact objective decrease per charged operation is ranked by
\begin{equation}
 h_u(r):=\frac{r_u^2}{1+d_u}
 \label{eq:direct-exact-work-score}
\end{equation}
when $\omega$ is fixed within a phase.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\nabla_u f(x)=-r_u$, the one-dimensional quadratic expansion with
$\delta=\omega r_u/q_0$ gives
\[
 f(x+\delta e_u)-f(x)
 =-\delta r_u+\frac{q_0\delta^2}{2}
 =-\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{2q_0}r_u^2.
\]
Use $2q_0=1+\alpha$ and divide by $c_u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The empirical key is universally charge-aware]
\label{thm:direct-ranking}
For every coordinate of every graph without isolated vertices,
\begin{equation}
 p_u(r)^2\leq h_u(r)\leq2p_u(r)^2.
 \label{eq:direct-score-equivalence}
\end{equation}
Therefore, if $u$ maximizes $p_i(r)$ over a set of coordinates, then
\begin{equation}
 h_u(r)\geq\frac12\max_i h_i(r).
 \label{eq:direct-rank-two-approx}
\end{equation}
More generally, the $k$ coordinates with largest $p_i(r)$ have total
snapshot work score at least one half of the largest possible total
$h$-score of any $k$ coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The definitions give the exact relation
\begin{equation*}
 p_u(r)^2
 =\frac{d_u}{1+d_u}h_u(r).
\end{equation*}
Because $d_u\geq1$, the multiplier belongs to $[1/2,1)$. If $u$ maximizes
$p$, then
$h_u\geq p_u^2\geq\max_i p_i^2\geq\frac12\max_i h_i$.
For the top-$k$ statement, the selected set maximizes the sum of $p_i^2$;
apply \cref{eq:direct-score-equivalence} before and after that comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An exact alternative score]
\label{cor:direct-exact-score}
The key
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p_u(r):=\frac{|r_u|}{\sqrt{1+d_u}}
 \label{eq:direct-alternative-score}
\end{equation}
orders coordinates exactly by objective decrease per charge. The measured
key obeys
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{\sqrt2}\widehat p_u(r)
 \leq p_u(r)<\widehat p_u(r).
 \label{eq:direct-key-comparison}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The theorem is stronger than a band-restricted comparison: it uses neither
an upper residual threshold nor a sign assumption. It also identifies a
low-risk empirical ablation, namely replacing $p_u$ by $\widehat p_u$ while
leaving the two-rung schedule unchanged.

\begin{remark}[Snapshot quality is not batch quality]
\label{rem:direct-staleness}
The top-$k$ conclusion is about the residual at the moment of ranking.
Earlier sequential pushes change the live residuals of later selected
coordinates. The live guard makes a newly inactive entry free, but an entry
can remain barely active and still be charged. Thus
\cref{thm:direct-ranking} proves the quality of the first executed operation
of every refreshed batch and the static quality of the declared set; a
separate staleness amortization is required for the work of the whole literal
batch.
\end{remark}
