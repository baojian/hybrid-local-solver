\begin{abstract}
Conjugate gradients (CG) is usually treated as a global baseline for local
PageRank, yet its graph propagation from a seed-supported right-hand side is
finite. For the shared symmetric PageRank matrix, the iterate, residual, and
search direction after $k$ iterations are supported inside the $k$-hop seed
ball. Exact CG can therefore be implemented as a locally evolving set process
whose active envelope is the cumulative support of its search directions and
whose actual matrix work scans only the current direction support. Its
degree-weighted work is bounded by the sum of volumes of the visited balls.
The usual $1/\sqrt\alpha$ CG iteration scale survives under this
implementation. Orthogonality does not destroy spatial locality.

The obstruction is threshold locality. Projecting a live search direction
onto a changing residual-active set destroys $Q$-conjugacy even on a
three-coordinate path. Full reorthogonalization repairs conjugacy but makes
each new direction dense on the accumulated support, leading to the known
quadratic-space and cubic-time conjugate-directions PageRank construction.
Restarting CG after every support change is another correct repair. For the
PageRank $M$-matrix, exact principal-subsystem solutions grow coordinatewise,
and their residual is nonnegative and supported only on the boundary, so an
evolving-set outer loop has a rigorous certificate. The missing ingredient is
work: repeated principal solves can revisit every prefix. A geometric-envelope
rule closes this particular gap. After every failed solve it grows active
degree volume by a factor of two, or exhausts the seed component. The number
of restarts is then logarithmic and the sum of revisited envelope volumes is
at most three times the terminal envelope volume. This trade is not uniformly
local: a nonviolating boundary hub of arbitrary degree can be forced into the
halo by one low-degree violation.

An instrumented prototype confirms the distinction. Exact frontier-sparse CG
is certified on paths, stars, trees, spiders, and random regular graphs.
Restart-after-every-expansion is between $1.6$ and $23.4$ times more expensive
on the tested instances. Factor-two geometric envelopes reduce its work by
$10.8$ times on the path and $9.1$ times on the long spider, but still cost
$2.1$ and $2.6$ times frontier-sparse CG and overshoot the path envelope.
The promising research target is therefore exact frontier-sparse CG as a new
baseline. Geometric-envelope CG is useful only behind an explicit volume or
degree-overshoot guard. For the literal factor-two policy, a graph-uniform
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ theorem is refuted, not merely open.
\end{abstract}

The locally evolving set framework requires only that the next active set be
contained in the current set and its one-hop neighborhood
\citep{zhou2024iterative}. It does not require the active set to be selected
by residual threshold. This distinction permits exact CG to fit the framework
without changing its recurrence.

\section{Exact CG has finite graph propagation}
\label{sec:finite-propagation}

Let $C:=\supp(b)$ and recursively define closed seed balls
\begin{equation}
 B_0:=C,
 \qquad
 B_{k+1}:=B_k\cup\mathcal N(B_k).
 \label{eq:balls}
\end{equation}
Starting from $x_0=0$, exact CG uses
\begin{align}
 r_0&=b, & p_0&=r_0,\nonumber\\
 a_k&=\frac{\inner{r_k}{r_k}}{\inner{p_k}{Qp_k}},
 &x_{k+1}&=x_k+a_kp_k,\nonumber\\
 r_{k+1}&=r_k-a_kQp_k,
 &p_{k+1}&=r_{k+1}
  +\frac{\inner{r_{k+1}}{r_{k+1}}}{\inner{r_k}{r_k}}p_k.
 \label{eq:cg}
\end{align}
These are the standard exact-arithmetic recurrences
\citep{hestenes1952methods}.

\begin{theorem}[Finite propagation]
\label{thm:finite-propagation}
For every $k\geq0$ before exact CG terminates,
\begin{equation}
 \supp(x_k)\subseteq B_{k-1},
 \qquad
 \supp(r_k)\subseteq B_k,
 \qquad
 \supp(p_k)\subseteq B_k,
 \label{eq:support-inclusions}
\end{equation}
where $B_{-1}:=\emptyset$. Moreover,
$\supp(Qp_k)\subseteq B_{k+1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The base case follows from $x_0=0$ and $r_0=p_0=b$. The matrix $Q$ has
nonzeros only on the diagonal and graph edges, so applying $Q$ to a vector
supported on $B_k$ produces a vector supported on $B_{k+1}$. If the
inclusions hold at $k$, then $x_{k+1}$ is supported on $B_k$,
$r_{k+1}$ on $B_{k+1}$, and $p_{k+1}$ on the union of $B_{k+1}$ and $B_k$,
which is $B_{k+1}$. Induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locally evolving realization and work]
\label{cor:local-cg-work}
Define the cumulative direction envelope
\begin{equation}
 E_k:=\bigcup_{j=0}^k\supp(p_j).
 \label{eq:cumulative-envelope}
\end{equation}
Then $E_{k+1}\subseteq E_k\cup\mathcal N(E_k)$, so $(E_k)$ is a locally
evolving set sequence. Computing $Qp_k$ by scanning only adjacency lists of
$S_k:=\supp(p_k)$ costs exactly $\vol(S_k)$ edge operations and at most
$\vol(B_k)$. Through iteration $K$, exact frontier-sparse CG costs
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm FCG}(K)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\vol(S_k)+\vol(\supp(x_K))
 \leq\sum_{k=0}^{K}\vol(B_k),
 \label{eq:frontier-work}
\end{equation}
where the final term recomputes the verifier-owned residual.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every coordinate newly entering $\supp(p_{k+1})$ must enter through
$Qp_k$: the other terms in the recurrence are $r_k$ and $p_k$, whose supports
are already contained in $E_k$. Since $Q$ couples only equal or adjacent
vertices, the new coordinates lie in $\mathcal N(\supp(p_k))\subseteq
\mathcal N(E_k)$. This proves the evolving-set inclusion.

Only columns indexed by $S_k$ contribute to $Qp_k$, and each selected graph
column costs its degree. The support inclusions in
\cref{thm:finite-propagation} give the upper bound. The iterate support is
contained in $B_{K-1}$, so the final residual recomputation is covered by the
last ball term.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The active set generally grows]
The containment in \cref{thm:finite-propagation} is spatial locality, not a
sparsity theorem for every direction. The recurrence adds a multiple of the
previous direction, so $p_k$ is typically dense on much of $B_k$. Exact CG
therefore revisits the explored ball, unlike a queue process that can forget
inactive interior vertices.
\end{remark}

\section{Convergence and the residual certificate}
\label{sec:certificate}

The shared matrix is a nonsingular symmetric $M$-matrix and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I,
 \qquad
 Q\sqrt d=\alpha\sqrt d,
 \label{eq:q-bounds}
\end{equation}
where $\sqrt d:=D^{1/2}\one$.

\begin{lemma}[Degree-infinity residual certificate]
\label{lem:certificate}
Every $x\in\R^n$ satisfying \cref{eq:certificate} also satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(x-x^0)}_\infty\leq\epsppr.
 \label{eq:error-certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c=(1-\alpha)/(1+\alpha)$. Then
\begin{equation*}
 Q^{-1}=\frac{2}{1+\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^\infty
 c^j\left(D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\right)^j\geq0
\end{equation*}
coordinatewise. Since $Q\sqrt d=\alpha\sqrt d$, it follows that
$Q^{-1}\sqrt d=\alpha^{-1}\sqrt d$. With $e=x^0-x=Q^{-1}r(x)$,
\begin{equation*}
 |e|\leq Q^{-1}|r(x)|
 \leq\alpha\epsppr Q^{-1}\sqrt d
 =\epsppr\sqrt d.
\end{equation*}
Divide coordinatewise by $\sqrt d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A priori CG iteration scale]
\label{prop:cg-rate}
For $0<\alpha<1$, define
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\alpha:=\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Exact CG satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x_k-x^0}_Q
 \leq2\lambda_\alpha^k\norm{x_0-x^0}_Q.
 \label{eq:cg-energy-rate}
\end{equation}
For a point seed $s=e_v$, it is sufficient for \cref{eq:certificate} that
\begin{equation}
 2\lambda_\alpha^k\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{d_v}}
 \leq\alpha\epsppr\sqrt{d_{\min}},
 \qquad d_{\min}:=\min_i d_i.
 \label{eq:sufficient-k}
\end{equation}
Thus a sufficient iteration count is
$O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha)))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The standard CG energy bound uses
$\kappa(Q)\leq1/\alpha$, giving \cref{eq:cg-energy-rate}. Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{x^0}_Q^2=b^\top Q^{-1}b
 \leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\norm{b}_2^2
 =\frac{\alpha}{d_v}.
\end{equation*}
Because $Q^2\preceq Q$,
$\norm{r_k}_2=\norm{Q(x^0-x_k)}_2\leq\norm{x^0-x_k}_Q$. Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}r_k}_\infty
 \leq\frac{\norm{r_k}_2}{\sqrt{d_{\min}}},
\end{equation*}
and \cref{eq:sufficient-k} is sufficient. Finally,
$-\log\lambda_\alpha=\Theta(\sqrt\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional accelerated local work]
\label{cor:conditional-work}
If all balls through the sufficient iteration $K$ in
\cref{prop:cg-rate} satisfy $\vol(B_k)\leq V_{\rm loc}$, then
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm FCG}(K)
 =\widetilde O\left(\frac{V_{\rm loc}}{\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:conditional-work}
\end{equation}
If additionally $V_{\rm loc}=O(1/\epsppr)$, this has the desired
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ scale.
\end{corollary}

The second premise is strong. It constrains graph balls, whereas APPR-style
locality constrains coordinates whose residual exceeds a threshold. A star
or expander can make $B_k$ global before CG has certified the requested
accuracy.

\section{Why thresholded CG is not CG}
\label{sec:masking}

A direct attempted localization replaces $p_k$ by
$P_{S_k}p_k$, where $S_k$ contains only residual-active coordinates, but
retains the Fletcher--Reeves coefficient in \cref{eq:cg}. The following
minimal example refutes that route.

\begin{proposition}[Three-coordinate masking obstruction]
\label{prop:masking-obstruction}
Let
\begin{equation}
 H=\begin{bmatrix}
 2&-1&0\\
 -1&2&-1\\
 0&-1&2
 \end{bmatrix},
 \qquad b=e_1.
 \label{eq:three-path}
\end{equation}
After exact line minimization along $d_0=e_1$, the residual is
$r_1=(0,1/2,0)^\top$. If the next direction is masked to the residual-active
set $\{2\}$, then $d_1$ is proportional to $e_2$ and
\begin{equation}
 d_0^\top H d_1\neq0.
\end{equation}
Hence the masked directions are not $H$-conjugate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first step length is
$a_0=(e_1^\top e_1)/(e_1^\top He_1)=1/2$, giving the displayed residual.
But $e_1^\top He_2=-1$. The unique correction within
$\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$ that is conjugate to $e_1$ is proportional to
$e_2+\tfrac12e_1$, which reactivates the previous coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A design trilemma]
A support-changing conjugate method must give up at least one of the
following without further structure:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item exact conjugacy to all previous directions;
 \item freely changing or shrinking active sets;
 \item a short recurrence with local memory.
\end{enumerate}
Full reorthogonalization retains the first two at increasing storage cost.
Restarting retains the second and third but discards conjugacy history.
Flexible-CG theory similarly uses explicit orthogonalization or truncation
when the underlying iteration varies \citep{notay2000flexible}.
\end{remark}

\section{Full orthogonalization preserves support but densifies it}
\label{sec:full-orthogonalization}

Suppose $U_0\subset U_1\subset\cdots$ are nested coordinate sets. At step
$t$, select a coordinate vector $u_t$ supported on $U_t$ and define
\begin{equation}
 d_t=u_t-\sum_{j<t}
 \frac{\inner{u_t}{Qd_j}}{\inner{d_j}{Qd_j}}d_j.
 \label{eq:gram-schmidt}
\end{equation}
If every old $d_j$ is supported in $U_j$, then $d_t$ is supported in $U_t$.
Thus $Q$-orthogonalization cannot create coordinates outside the accumulated
set. It does, however, combine every previous direction and is generally
dense within $U_t$.

This is exactly the structural tradeoff exposed by CDPR
\citep{martinezrubio2023accelerated}. CDPR adds one negative-gradient
coordinate at a time, performs full $Q$-orthogonalization, and reaches the
exact minimizer of a nonnegative $M$-matrix quadratic. Its published bounds
are
\begin{equation}
 O\left(|S^\star|^3+|S^\star|\vol(S^\star)\right)
 \quad\text{time},
 \qquad
 O(|S^\star|^2)
 \quad\text{space}.
 \label{eq:cdpr-cost}
\end{equation}
Consequently, full orthogonalization proves that exact spatial locality is
possible, but it is not the preferred large-scale implementation.

\section{Exact evolving principal systems}
\label{sec:principal}

Restarting CG after a support change avoids invalid masked recurrences. The
outer set process has particularly clean geometry for PageRank.

For a set $U\supseteq\supp(b)$, let $x^U$ be the zero-padded principal
solution
\begin{equation}
 x^U_U:=Q_{UU}^{-1}b_U,
 \qquad
 x^U_{V\setminus U}:=0,
 \label{eq:principal-solution}
\end{equation}
and let $r^U=b-Qx^U$.

\begin{theorem}[Principal-solution locality and monotonicity]
\label{thm:principal-geometry}
For every $U\supseteq\supp(b)$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item $x^U\geq0$;
 \item $r^U_U=0$, $r^U_{\operatorname{Ext}(U)}=0$, and
       $r^U_{\partial U}\geq0$;
 \item if $U\subseteq U'$, then $x^U\leq x^{U'}\leq x^0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every principal matrix $Q_{UU}$ is a nonsingular $M$-matrix, so
$Q_{UU}^{-1}\geq0$ and $x^U_U\geq0$. The principal equation gives zero
residual on $U$. A vertex in $\operatorname{Ext}(U)$ is neither a seed nor a
neighbor of $U$, so its residual is zero. For $i\in\partial U$,
\begin{equation*}
 r^U_i=-Q_{iU}x^U_U\geq0
\end{equation*}
because the off-diagonal entries of $Q$ are nonpositive.

Pad $x^U$ into $U'$. Its residual restricted to $U'$ is nonnegative by the
same argument. Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 x^{U'}-x^U
 =Q_{U'U'}^{-1}r^U_{U'}\geq0.
\end{equation*}
The comparison with $x^0$ is the special case $U'=V$.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Exact evolving principal-system process}
\label{alg:exact-restarted}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $U_0\gets\supp(b)$
\FOR{$j=0,1,\ldots$}
  \STATE compute $x^{U_j}$ from \cref{eq:principal-solution}
  \STATE recompute $r^{U_j}=b-Qx^{U_j}$
  \IF{$\norm{D^{-1/2}r^{U_j}}_\infty\leq\alpha\epsppr$}
    \STATE return $x^{U_j}$
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $A_j\gets\{i\in\partial U_j:r^{U_j}_i>
      \alpha\epsppr\sqrt{d_i}\}$
  \STATE $U_{j+1}\gets U_j\cup A_j$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{corollary}[Certificate and finite termination]
\label{cor:principal-termination}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:exact-restarted} either returns a point satisfying
\cref{eq:error-certificate}, or adds at least one vertex. It therefore
terminates after at most $n-|\supp(b)|+1$ principal solves.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:principal-geometry}, every nonzero residual outside $U_j$ is on
the boundary and is nonnegative. If $A_j$ is empty, the residual certificate
holds; otherwise the set expands. Apply \cref{lem:certificate} at
termination.
\end{proof}

In the executable prototype, each principal system is solved approximately
by warm-started CG until its interior residual is at most one quarter of the
final threshold. The full residual is then recomputed, all violating outside
vertices are admitted in one batch, and CG is restarted. This is safe because
no direction crosses a support change, but the exact monotonicity of
\cref{thm:principal-geometry} is no longer asserted for intermediate CG
iterates.

Every $Q_{UU}$ has spectrum in $[\alpha,1]$, so an inner solve has the usual
$1/\sqrt\alpha$ iteration scale. The unresolved cost is the sum over
expanding supports:
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm RCG}
 =\sum_j\left[
   \vol(\supp(x_j))
   +\sum_{\ell=0}^{K_j-1}\vol(\supp(p_{j,\ell}))
 \right].
 \label{eq:restart-work}
\end{equation}
A path can expose only the next one or two boundary vertices, forcing many
restarts and repeated prefix scans. Correctness alone does not amortize
\cref{eq:restart-work}.

The literal policy nevertheless has a graph-uniform output-volume guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[Violation-only envelope volume]
\label{prop:violation-only-volume}
Run the exact principal-system process from $U_0=\{v\}$ and admit only
coordinates satisfying
$r^{U_j}_i>\alpha\epsppr\sqrt{d_i}$. If $U_J$ is its terminal set, then
\begin{equation}
 \vol(U_J)
 \leq d_v+\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\epsppr}.
 \label{eq:violation-volume}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $i$ be admitted when $U_j$ expands to $U_{j+1}$. By
\cref{thm:principal-geometry}, the old residual restricted to $U_{j+1}$ is
nonnegative and
\begin{equation*}
 x^{U_{j+1}}-x^{U_j}
 =Q_{U_{j+1}U_{j+1}}^{-1}r^{U_j}_{U_{j+1}}\geq0.
\end{equation*}
For every positive-definite matrix, its inverse diagonal is at least the
reciprocal of its diagonal. Since $Q_{ii}=(1+\alpha)/2$ and the inverse here
is coordinatewise nonnegative,
\begin{equation*}
 x^{U_{j+1}}_i
 \geq\left(Q_{U_{j+1}U_{j+1}}^{-1}\right)_{ii}r^{U_j}_i
 >\frac{2\alpha\epsppr}{1+\alpha}\sqrt{d_i}.
\end{equation*}
Later principal solutions only increase this coordinate. Moreover,
$x^{U_J}\leq x^0$ and
$\inner{\sqrt d}{x^0}=1$, the latter following from
$Q\sqrt d=\alpha\sqrt d$ and $\inner{\sqrt d}{b}=\alpha$.
Summing over the distinct admitted vertices gives
\begin{equation*}
 1\geq\inner{\sqrt d}{x^{U_J}}
 >\frac{2\alpha\epsppr}{1+\alpha}
   \sum_{i\in U_J\setminus\{v\}}d_i.
\end{equation*}
Adding the initial degree $d_v$ proves \cref{eq:violation-volume}.
\end{proof}

\section{Geometric envelopes amortize the restart tax}
\label{sec:geometric-envelope}

The failed literal policy suggests separating two questions: whether a
restricted solve is certified, and how far the next envelope should grow.
The factor-two rule used here is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item solve on the fixed set $U_j$ until the interior satisfies one quarter
 of the final coordinate threshold, then recompute the true full residual;
 \item admit every outside coordinate violating \cref{eq:certificate};
 \item if the new degree volume is below $2\vol(U_j)$, scan the active
 boundary and grow a breadth-first halo until the volume doubles or every
 connected component meeting $U_j$ is exhausted;
 \item restart CG on the resulting fixed envelope.
\end{enumerate}
Every individual halo admission is adjacent to the currently exposed set, so
the batched description decomposes into legal one-hop locally evolving steps.
All active-set scans and all scans of newly admitted halo vertices are charged.

The following warm-start observation retains the usual CG iteration scale.

\begin{lemma}[Nonpositive-objective warm starts]
\label{lem:warm-start}
Let $x^U$ be the principal minimizer on $U$, and let $z$ be supported on $U$
with $f(z)\leq0$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{z-x^U}_Q^2
 \leq\norm{x^U}_Q^2
 \leq\frac{\alpha}{d_v}
 \label{eq:warm-start-bound}
\end{equation}
for the point seed $s=e_v$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Completing the square on the principal system gives
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{z-x^U}_Q^2=2\bigl(f(z)-f(x^U)\bigr),
 \qquad
 f(x^U)=-\tfrac12\norm{x^U}_Q^2.
\end{equation*}
The first inequality follows from $f(z)\leq0$. Since
$Q_{UU}\succeq\alpha I$ and $\norm{b}_2^2=\alpha^2/d_v$,
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{x^U}_Q^2=b_U^\top Q_{UU}^{-1}b_U
 \leq\alpha^{-1}\norm{b}_2^2=\frac{\alpha}{d_v}.
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

Starting from zero, exact CG line minimizations never increase $f$, and
zero-padding an iterate into an expanded coordinate set does not change its
objective. Hence every warm start used by the geometric-envelope process
satisfies \cref{lem:warm-start}. In particular, a uniform sufficient inner
iteration count $K$ is determined by
\begin{equation}
 2\lambda_\alpha^K\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{d_v}}
 \leq\frac{\alpha\epsppr\sqrt{d_{\min}}}{4},
 \label{eq:geometric-inner-count}
\end{equation}
so $K=O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha)))$.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric-envelope work]
\label{thm:geometric-envelope-work}
Assume every fixed-envelope CG call reaches its stated interior certificate.
Let $U_0,\ldots,U_J$ be the solved envelopes of the factor-two process and
write $v_j:=\vol(U_j)$. Then
\begin{align}
 J&\leq1+\left\lceil\log_2\frac{v_J}{v_0}\right\rceil,
 &\sum_{j=0}^Jv_j&\leq3v_J.
 \label{eq:geometric-volume-ledger}
\end{align}
If each call uses at most $K_{\max}$ CG iterations, the implementation's
degree-weighted graph work, including verifier products and halo discovery,
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm GECG}\leq(3K_{\max}+10)v_J.
 \label{eq:geometric-work}
\end{equation}
The returned point satisfies \cref{eq:error-certificate}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every nonexhausting failed envelope at least doubles in volume. If a halo
cannot double, scanning the whole active boundary followed by breadth-first
search has exhausted every component meeting the active set. The next
principal solve has no outside residual, so there can be at most one such
nondoubling expansion and it is terminal. The restart and geometric-series
bounds in \cref{eq:geometric-volume-ledger} follow.

At envelope $U_j$, the initial warm-residual product, at most $K_{\max}$
direction products, and the verifier product cost at most
$(K_{\max}+2)v_j$. Boundary discovery scans at most $v_j$ at a failed epoch,
and breadth-first discovery scans each newly admitted vertex at most once.
Thus discovery costs at most $\sum_jv_j+v_J$, and
\begin{equation*}
 W_{\rm GECG}
 \leq(K_{\max}+3)\sum_jv_j+v_J
 \leq(3K_{\max}+10)v_J.
\end{equation*}
The interior certificate is strictly stronger than the final threshold. If
the full certificate fails, an outside violation therefore exists and the
set expands. Finite termination and \cref{lem:certificate} complete the
correctness claim.
\end{proof}

Combining \cref{eq:geometric-inner-count,eq:geometric-work} yields the
trajectory-dependent bound
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm GECG}
 =\widetilde O\left(\frac{\vol(U_J)}{\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:geometric-conditional-work}
\end{equation}
This closes restart amortization in terms of the terminal explored envelope.
It does not prove that $\vol(U_J)=O(1/\epsppr)$ on every graph; look-ahead can
overshoot a minimal certificate-supporting region.

In fact, no graph-uniform terminal-envelope bound exists for the factor-two
halo rule.

\begin{proposition}[High-degree decoy obstruction]
\label{prop:decoy-obstruction}
Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and choose $\epsppr$ such that
\begin{equation}
 0<\epsppr<\min\left\{
 \frac{1-\alpha}{3(1+\alpha)},
 \frac{(1-\alpha)^2}
 {3(1+\alpha)^2-(1-\alpha)^2}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:decoy-epsilon-range}
\end{equation}
There is a family of seed-connected graphs, indexed by an arbitrarily large
integer $M$, on which exact violation-only expansion terminates with degree
volume $5$, while factor-two halo expansion has terminal degree volume at
least $M+5$ and graph work at least $M$. Consequently, neither terminal
volume nor work admits an upper bound depending only on $\alpha$ and
$\epsppr$ for the factor-two policy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use vertices $s,a,u,h,z_1,\ldots,z_{M-1}$ and edges
\begin{equation*}
 sa,\quad au,\quad ah,\quad hz_j\ (1\leq j<M),
\end{equation*}
with seed $s$. Thus
$d_s=d_u=d_{z_j}=1$, $d_a=3$, and $d_h=M$.

For the exact principal solution on $U_0=\{s\}$, direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^{U_0}_a}{\sqrt{d_a}}
 =\alpha\frac{1-\alpha}{3(1+\alpha)}.
 \label{eq:decoy-first-residual}
\end{equation}
Hence $a$ is admitted. Its degree already grows the envelope from volume one
to volume four, so no halo is needed at this step.

On $U_1=\{s,a\}$, solving the two-by-two principal system gives
\begin{align}
 \frac{r^{U_1}_u}{\sqrt{d_u}}
 &=\alpha\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}
 {3(1+\alpha)^2-(1-\alpha)^2},
 &
 \frac{r^{U_1}_h}{\sqrt{d_h}}
 &=\frac{1}{M}\frac{r^{U_1}_u}{\sqrt{d_u}}.
 \label{eq:decoy-second-residuals}
\end{align}
By \cref{eq:decoy-epsilon-range}, $u$ violates. For all sufficiently large
$M$, $h$ does not. Violation-only expansion therefore adds just $u$.
After solving on $\{s,a,u\}$, the remaining normalized hub residual is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^{\{s,a,u\}}_h}{\sqrt{d_h}}
 =\frac{\alpha}{M}
 \frac{(1-\alpha)^2}
 {3(1+\alpha)^2-2(1-\alpha)^2},
 \label{eq:decoy-literal-terminal}
\end{equation}
which is below $\alpha\epsppr$ for all sufficiently large $M$. Thus the
violation-only process terminates at volume
$d_s+d_a+d_u=5$.

For factor-two growth, however, adding $u$ to $U_1$ raises volume only from
$4$ to $5$, short of the target $8$. The only other boundary coordinate is
the nonviolating hub $h$, so any boundary-first halo that reaches the target
must admit it. The envelope volume immediately becomes at least $M+5$ and
never decreases. At final verification the positive hub coordinate forces a
scan of its $M$ incident edges, so work is at least $M$. Since $M$ is
arbitrary, the claimed uniform bounds are impossible.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is caused by degree volume, not vertex count: the geometric
method adds only one unnecessary vertex. It also survives exact principal
solves, so tighter inner tolerances cannot repair it. A valid implementation
safeguard must reject a halo admission before its degree crosses a declared
volume budget and then revert to violation-only expansion or another
certifying method. Such a guard preserves correctness but forfeits the
unconditional geometric restart ledger whenever it fires.

\section{Executable evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

The implementation in
\texttt{src/hybrid\_solver\_codex/evolving\_cg.py} charges one unit for every
adjacency-list entry scanned by a local $Q$ product. It recomputes the true
residual before accepting either solver. The deterministic experiment
\texttt{experiments/explore\_evolving\_cg.py} uses
$\alpha=0.01$, $\epsppr=10^{-7}$, random seed $7$, and a sparse direct solve
only for the reported error diagnostic.

All three methods certified \cref{eq:certificate} on every row.
Frontier-sparse CG explored only $153$ vertices on the path but all vertices
on the other four graphs. Geometric envelopes explored $192$ path vertices.
This directly supports four conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item Orthogonality and spatial locality coexist on slow-growth graphs.
 \item Fast graph growth can make exact CG global within a few iterations.
 \item Restarting after every boundary expansion is not a competitive
 standalone method: relative to FCG it costs $22.9$ times more work on the
 path and $23.4$ times more on the long spider.
 \item Factor-two growth removes most of that restart tax: relative to the
 literal policy, work falls by $10.8$ times on the path, $9.1$ times on the
 spider, and $1.5$ times on the random regular graph. It is unchanged on the
 star and tree, whose natural boundary batches already grow geometrically.
\end{enumerate}

The geometric policy is nevertheless more expensive than FCG on every row;
on the path and spider its factors are $2.1$ and $2.6$. The result repairs a
specific repeated-prefix failure rather than establishing empirical
superiority over ordinary CG.

The separate decoy experiment fixes $\alpha=0.01$ and $\epsppr=0.25$ and uses
hub degrees $M=16,64,256,1024$, and $4096$. Every run is certified. Violation-only
CG has explored volume $5$ and work $33$ for every $M$; geometric-envelope CG
has explored volume $M+5$ and measured work exactly $4M+37$. This is an
executable scaling witness for \cref{prop:decoy-obstruction}, not the basis of
its proof.

\section{Recommended algorithmic direction}
\label{sec:direction}

The evidence supports two distinct next steps.

\paragraph{Promote frontier-sparse CG to a measured baseline.}
The current global CG baseline scans every graph vertex on every iteration.
The exact sparse recurrence should instead maintain the supports of $p_k$ and
$Qp_k$, charge \cref{eq:frontier-work}, and report ball growth. This change
does not alter CG numerically; it corrects the access model.

\paragraph{Put geometric CG behind a hard overshoot guard.}
A competitive hybrid should not solve every one-hop principal system. A
monotone local method should first discover a coarse envelope $U$ and a true
boundary residual. Restricted CG can then refine on fixed $U$. If the
boundary violates the final certificate, the factor-two rule gives a proved
restart and revisit ledger only while halo vertices fit a declared degree
budget. The decoy obstruction proves that fallback is mathematically
necessary, not merely conservative engineering.

The corresponding proof obligations are:
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=3.4cm]
 \item[Ball confinement for FCG.] Bound $\vol(B_K)$ through the certified CG
 iteration $K$, or identify graph parameters under which this is possible.
 \item[Envelope sufficiency.] Relate a coarse local residual or KKT margin to
 the probability that restricted CG triggers another boundary expansion.
 \item[Envelope overshoot.] The graph-uniform bound is refuted by
 \cref{prop:decoy-obstruction}. Future positive results must assume bounded
 degree/volume growth, use a hard cap, or avoid nonviolating halo admission.
 \item[Signed handoff.] If CG is used before a monotone local tail, prove that
 the tail accepts its signed warm-start residual under the same final
 certificate.
 \item[Finite precision.] Recompute the residual periodically and restart on
 loss of conjugacy; no recursively updated residual is accepted by the final
 verifier.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusion}

The initial concern that orthogonal directions eliminate locality is too
strong. Exact CG is already a locally evolving graph process: its support has
finite propagation, the cumulative direction envelope expands by at most one
hop, its matrix products can be charged to the current direction volume, and
it retains accelerated spectral convergence. What
orthogonality eliminates is aggressive forgetting of the explored interior.
Masking breaks conjugacy; full reorthogonalization stores dense history; and
one-hop restart pays repeated-prefix work. Geometric envelope growth removes
that last tax in terms of the terminal explored volume, while making the
remaining obstacle explicit: envelope overshoot.

The immediate deliverable is therefore frontier-sparse exact CG with honest
local work accounting and violation-only restricted CG as the support-safe
reference. The factor-two variant has a complete conditional work theorem but
an unconditional locality counterexample. The publishable conclusion is
therefore a proved tradeoff, not a missing lemma: short-recurrence CG keeps
finite propagation; masking destroys conjugacy; violation-only restarts keep
output volume but can revisit prefixes; and nonviolating geometric look-ahead
amortizes those revisits only by risking unbounded degree-volume overshoot.
Any future positive hybrid must add graph structure, an explicit budgeted
fallback, or a new way to recycle orthogonality without admitting that halo.
