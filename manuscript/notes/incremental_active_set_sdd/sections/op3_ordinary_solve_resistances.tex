\section{Resistance estimates from ordinary approximate solves}
\label{sec:op3-ordinary-solve-resistances}

\paragraph{Scope and primitive.}
The graph is supplied in full, connected, and has positive conductances.
The following construction uses ordinary right-hand-side solves from
\cref{thm:op3-paid-cycle-solve}; their errors may depend nonlinearly on
the right-hand side. It does not assume that all solves are evaluations
of one fixed symmetric operator. All arithmetic below is charged in the
exact-real word model, including fair bits, dyadic searches and allocated
words. A square root appearing in the proof is not a computed primitive.

\begin{lemma}[Paid square-root upper brackets; \statusproved]
\label{lem:op3-paid-square-root-bracket}
For $a\geq1$, using only arithmetic and comparisons, one can compute $s$
with $\sqrt a\leq s\leq h\sqrt a$, where $h=257/256$, in
$O(1+\log a)$ word work and allocated words.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Double an upper endpoint from one until its square is at least $a$.
The lower endpoint equal to half that upper endpoint lies below $\sqrt a$.
Perform eight bisections, keeping the upper endpoint on equality.
If the original upper endpoint was $U$, the final interval has width
$U/512$ and $\sqrt a\geq U/2$. Thus its upper endpoint exceeds
$\sqrt a$ by at most $\sqrt a/256$. Each comparison, arithmetic result
and temporary word is counted. The case $a=1$ also obeys this bracket.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary-solve resistance sketch; \statusproved]
\label{thm:op3-ordinary-solve-resistances}
Let $n\geq2$, $m\geq1$, $\chi_w=c_{\max}/c_{\min}$ and
$0<p_R<1$. Suppose an ordinary solver for a supplied weighted Laplacian
returns a vector satisfying relative energy-norm error $\eta$, or may
fail to do so with probability at most $\delta_s$, with a bounded cost
$T_{\rm solve}(\eta,\delta_s)$ on every branch. Shared preparation
costs are charged separately. Preparation is fixed independently of the
sketch bits, and each group uses fresh solver state and independent bits.
There is a procedure returning estimates of all original-edge effective
resistances satisfying, jointly with probability at least $1-p_R$,
\[
 \tfrac34 R_e\leq\widehat R_e\leq\tfrac54 R_e\qquad(e\in E).
\]
Put $q=512$, $g=32\lceil\log_2(4m/p_R)\rceil+1$, and use
$\eta=1/(128m)$ and $\delta_s=1/(16q)$ in every solve. Besides shared
preparation, bounded work and all allocated words are at most
\[
 O\!\left(m(1+\log\chi_w)+gq\,[m+n+T_{\rm solve}(\eta,\delta_s)]
             +mg\log(2+g)\right).
\]
The one-vertex graph returns an empty vector directly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Scan the original conductances to obtain $c_{\min}$ and form
$c'_e=c_e/c_{\min}\geq1$, with Laplacian $L'=L/c_{\min}$.
Use \cref{lem:op3-paid-square-root-bracket} to compute
$\sqrt{c'_e}\leq s_e\leq h\sqrt{c'_e}$. In each of $g$ independent
groups, generate $q$ independent sign vectors
$\bm a\in\{-1,1\}^m$. Form the zero-sum demand
$\bm b=B^T\operatorname{diag}(\bm s)\bm a$ by one edge scan, and
solve $L'\bm x=\bm b$ to the prescribed energy accuracy.
For each original edge $e=(u,v)$, accumulate
$(\widehat x_u-\widehat x_v)^2/q$. If a solver returns
\textnormal{FAIL}, use a zero vector for that whole group and stop its
remaining rows. Finally take the coordinatewise median of the $g$
group vectors and divide by $c_{\min}$.

Here is the complete error analysis. Fix a queried edge $e$ and first
use exact solves for one group. The corresponding scalar random variable
$Z_e=x_u-x_v$ is a linear combination of independent signs. If
$S_e=\mathbb E Z_e^2$ and $R'_e=c_{\min}R_e$, then
\[
 R'_e\leq S_e\leq h^2R'_e,
 \qquad \mathbb E Z_e^4\leq3S_e^2.
\]
The first inequalities follow by inserting
$C'\preceq\operatorname{diag}(\bm s)^2\preceq h^2C'$
between $B L'^+\bm b_e$ and its transpose. For a linear sign sum
$Z=\sum_j a_j z_j$, expansion gives
$\mathbb E Z^4=3(\sum_jz_j^2)^2-2\sum_jz_j^4$, proving the second.
Consequently the variance of a $q$-row mean square is at most
$2S_e^2/q$. Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\left\{\left|q^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^q Z_{e,k}^2-S_e\right|
       >S_e/8\right\}\leq\frac{128}{q}=\frac14.
\]

For every sign row, not just with high probability,
$\|L'^+\bm b\|_{L'}\leq h\sqrt m$. Indeed
$L'^{+/2}B^TC'^{1/2}$ has operator norm one on the nonconstant
subspace, and the remaining diagonal scaling has norm at most $h$.
For any energy-valid approximate solve, energy Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 |(\widehat x_u-\widehat x_v)-(x_u-x_v)|
 \leq\sqrt{R'_e}\,\|\widehat{\bm x}-\bm x\|_{L'}
 \leq\eta h\sqrt m\sqrt{R'_e}.
\]
This is a pointwise bound, even for nonlinear or right-hand-side-dependent
errors. Over $q$ rows, the normalized Euclidean error is at most
$\eta h\sqrt m\sqrt{R'_e}\leq(h/128)\sqrt{R'_e}$.
A union bound makes all $q$ solves energy-valid with probability at
least $15/16$. No independence between the good-solve event and the
sketch-concentration event is needed. On their intersection, the reverse
triangle and triangle inequalities give a group estimate $A_e$ with
\[
 \left(\frac78-\frac h{128}\right)^2R'_e
 \leq A_e\leq h^2\left(\frac{17}{16}+\frac1{128}\right)^2R'_e.
\]
Here $\sqrt{7/8}\geq7/8$ and $\sqrt{9/8}\leq17/16$.
The displayed rational lower factor is at least $3/4$ and the upper
factor at most $5/4$. Therefore this group is good for the fixed edge
with probability at least $1-1/4-1/16=11/16$.

The independent groups need not be simultaneously good for all edges.
For each fixed edge their bad indicators are independent with probabilities
at most $5/16$. Applying exponential Markov with multiplier $11/5$
bounds the probability of a bad majority by
\[
 \left(\frac{\sqrt{55}}8\right)^g
 \leq\left(\frac78\right)^{g/2}\leq e^{-g/16}.
\]
For the stated $g$, this is at most $p_R/m$. Union over the $m$
original edges proves the joint median claim. Dividing by $c_{\min}$
undoes the initial scaling. A failed row remains a bad group in this
analysis; we never condition the distribution on successful capped draws.

All original-edge scans, normalizations and brackets cost the first
term of the work bound. Each row explicitly allocates its demand,
consumes $m$ fair bits, invokes one paid solve, and scans all queried
edges. Row outputs are streamed into one group accumulator; stored group
vectors have $mg$ words. Each coordinate median is computed by the paid
merge sort with a reusable buffer from the earlier local certificate.
Its input records, comparisons, copies and temporary arrays cost
$O(g\log(2+g))$. Early group failure only decreases work. This proves
the same bound for all failure branches and all allocated words.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Supplied arbitrary-weight resistance estimates; \statusproved]
\label{cor:op3-resistance-near-linear}
With the checked Abraham--Neiman tree source and the cycle backend of
\cref{cor:op3-cycle-solve-near-linear}, the preceding estimator has
work and total allocated words
\[
 O\bigl(m[\mathcal L^6+\log\chi_w]\bigr),
 \qquad \mathcal L=\log(2+n+m+1/p_R).
\]
All resistances meet the stated error simultaneously with probability
at least $1-p_R$. This is supplied-graph work.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Aggregate parallel edges by paid sorting before tree selection; identical
endpoint pairs have the same effective resistance and their answer can
be copied back to every original record in linear work. For the source
tree, each prescribed ordinary solve has cost $O(m\mathcal L^5)$,
with $q$ constant and $g=O(\mathcal L)$. Initial normalization and
square-root brackets cost $O(m(1+\log\chi_w))$; aggregation only
increases this logarithm by $O(\log m)$ if $\chi_w$ refers to the original
records. The preceding theorem supplies all other costs. The source tree
can be fixed and its preparation reused for every right-hand side.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Audit and limitations.}
The implementation is \texttt{ordinary\_solve\_resistances.py}, with
source and backend hashes in \texttt{ORDINARY\_SOLVE\_RESISTANCES\_AUDIT.json}.
Exact validators enumerate sign moments, energy bounds, dyadic brackets,
nonlinear but admissible solver errors, median budgets and early failures.
Complete prescribed sampling runs include the actual cycle backend on
supplied trees and a triangle; a separate dense nonlinear provider is
explicitly labelled as validation. These finite checks support the proof,
not its asymptotic probability argument. Tree selection remains the named
source algorithm. The full supplied recursion still needs its final capped
composition; general local OP3 remains \statusopen.

The final audit checks 260 dyadic brackets, 68 exact second/fourth-moment
identities, 1,556 nonlinear edge-error bounds, and 15 joint-median budgets.
Five complete actual-backend fixtures use 676 prescribed groups; a separate
dense nonlinear fixture uses 225 groups. Parallel edges and relative
weights from $2^{-80}$ to $2^{80}$ appear in the moment checks, and an
actual triangle sketch has weight ratio $2^{80}$. The full run took
78.955 seconds. The subsequent core composition is now provided in
\cref{sec:op3-bounded-core-sparsifier}.
