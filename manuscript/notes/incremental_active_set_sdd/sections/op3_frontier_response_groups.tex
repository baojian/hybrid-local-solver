\section{Adaptive response groups and aggregate reconstruction}
\label{sec:op3-frontier-response-groups}

The explicit star obstruction has a low-rank origin. The following
implementation shares responses among frontier vertices with the same
original adjacency into the admitted set. It discovers this partition
locally, permits unequal original degrees within a group, and includes
threshold reporting and reconstruction in its cost.

\begin{theorem}[Paid adaptive frontier groups; \statusproved]
\label{thm:op3-frontier-response-groups}
Under the model and parameters of \cref{thm:op3-exact-frontier}, there is
a deterministic local exact-real word producer of the same original ACL
certificate, with original support volume $V<2/e$. Let $b_i$ be the maximum
number of group records in its live list during pivot $i$, counting empty
parents until that pivot retires them. Its work and total allocated words
are
\[
 O\left(\left(1+V+\sum_i(b_i+1)^2\right)\log(2+V)\right),
\]
and its live state is $O((1+V)^2)$ words. Each admitted original row is read
once, or skipped when $\gamma=0$, and each discovered degree is queried
once. The original volume guard precedes each row buffer. One final reverse
replay reconstructs all physical potentials and emits $\bar\alpha Dx$.
No supplied partition, ambient scan or numerical oracle is used. In
particular, if the realized group counts are bounded by $B$, the work is
$O((1+V)(1+B)^2\log(2+V))$, with no target-$\alpha$ dependence in this
arithmetic model. A graph-uniform small-$B$ claim is not made.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After a completed pivot, partition the known unadmitted vertices into
groups $T_t$ with equal original adjacency columns into the admitted set
$S$. Maintain a scalar $q_t$ and symmetric nonnegative entries $C_{tu}$ so
that for $j\in T_t$, $k\in T_u$, $j\ne k$,
\[
 h_j=-\lambda d_j+q_t,\qquad s_j=d_j-C_{tt},\qquad
 \operatorname{fill}(j,k)=C_{tu}.
\]
These identities follow from the Schur expressions in
\cref{thm:op3-exact-frontier}. Initially the seed is a singleton group with
$q=1$ and $C=0$; after its removal every remaining source entry is zero.
The grouping condition says nothing about individual original degrees or
unread edges between unadmitted vertices.

Each group stores its members in a binary heap ordered by original degree
and then label. Stable vertex records retain their heap positions. Thus
insertion and removal of any named member cost $O(\log(2+V))$. Some member
is eligible precisely when the minimum-degree member satisfies
$q_t>(\lambda+\kappa)d_j$. Scan the live group list and take the minimum
member of its first eligible group. This scan costs the number of groups,
not the total number of member vertices, and all failed checks are charged.

Remove eligible $i$ from its group $p$ and save
\[
 \sigma=d_i-C_{pp}>0,\qquad
 z_i=(-\lambda d_i+q_p)/\sigma>0.
\]
Expose its original row. For each previously known unadmitted original
neighbor, move that member from its old group to a child group, creating
one such child per old group in this row. A per-pivot marker and direct
parent pointer identify that child. This separates neighbors and
nonneighbors without scanning the unencountered members. A child copies
its parent's $q$ and its interactions with the current groups, including
the self entry. All newly discovered vertices form one fresh group with
previous $q=0$, $C=0$: none could have had an earlier admitted original
neighbor. Mark every child and fresh group as a neighbor group for this
pivot. Empty old groups may now leave the live list; their scalar records
remain available through saved references.

For every remaining group $t$, the effective coupling from the removed
pivot is
\[
 w_t=C_{pt}+\gamma\,\mathbf1\{t\text{ is a neighbor group of }i\}.
\]
Make one shared load update and one update for each unordered group pair,
including diagonal pairs:
\[
 q_t\gets q_t+w_tz_i,
 \qquad C_{tu}\gets C_{tu}+w_tw_u/\sigma.
\]
Each individual member then has exactly the scalar Schur update of
\cref{thm:op3-exact-frontier}. The partition remains valid after adding
$i$ to $S$, because its only new adjacency bit was used in the refinement.
Therefore all positive-pivot, original residual, support-volume and
pre-row safety arguments from that theorem apply, regardless of the new
eligible-pivot order. The heap minima certify termination on every group,
and unseen residuals are zero.

The reconstruction needs more than mutable group names. Save $z_i$, one
coefficient $w_t/\sigma$ per current group, each parent--child split, the
fresh-discovery group if present, and the pivot's old parent. Start reverse
replay with zero sums on all final groups; retired empty groups also have
zero initial sums. At the reversed pivot, group sums refer to the member
sets immediately after that forward pivot's removal and refinement, with
later admitted physical values already known. Set
\[
 x_i=z_i+\sum_t(w_t/\sigma)\,\Sigma_t.
\]
This is the individual back-substitution formula, aggregated over groups.
Undo the saved splits by adding each child sum into its old parent and
clearing the child. Clear the fresh-discovery sum: those labels were absent
from the earlier frontier and had zero coupling to its admitted set, while
their already reconstructed individual output values remain stored. Finally
add $x_i$ to its old parent's sum. The resulting aggregates describe exactly
the known unadmitted sets before this pivot. Induction backward through the
history proves every reconstruction and pays only for saved group
coefficients, splits and pivot records. It does not expand a historical
group into its member list.

To charge discovery and representation, each old-member move is paid by
one newly read original incidence. Each fresh discovery is also paid by
such an incidence. There are $O(1+V)$ heap insertions and moves in total;
geometrically grown heap arrays reserve $O(1+V)$ words even counting retired
capacities. Every newly created group except the initial seed has an
original incidence that pays for its creation, so there are at most $1+V$
historical groups. Stable vertex labels use an AVL map. A second
insertion-only AVL map indexes symmetric pairs of stable group indices,
including retained historical entries, with $O(\log(2+V))$ lookup and
update cost. Clone at most $b_i$ new groups against at most $b_i$ current
records, then make $O((1+b_i)^2)$ shared Schur operations. Live-list scans,
empty retirements, group gates and saved lifting records cost $O(1+b_i)$
per pivot. The final unsuccessful group scan costs $O(1+V)$. All map
frames, scalar temporaries, buffers and historical records are charged.
This proves the total bound. There are $O((1+V)^2)$ historical matrix
entries and at most $O((1+V)^2)$ saved group coefficients, proving the live
state bound. Individual degrees are never replaced by group-average or
induced degrees.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The star clique stays compressed; \statusproved]
\label{prop:op3-grouped-star-tail}
For the star with a path tail of length at least $8m$, $m\geq4$,
$e=1/(4m)$ and any $0<\alpha\leq1/3$, the group producer uses
$O(m\log(2+m))$ local work and total allocated words. Its support volume
is $\Theta(m)$, and its transient group count is at most two. The tail
length need not be supplied. This is a concrete representation improvement
on a graph family already covered by the earlier tree results.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The seed row puts its $m$ leaves and the first tail vertex into one fresh
group. Its first face value is the $x_0$ in
\cref{prop:op3-explicit-frontier-cubic}. Since $\gamma\geq1/2$, every leaf
passes the same strict gate proved there. The heap selects all degree-one
leaves before the degree-two tail vertex. Each leaf row only points back to
the eliminated seed, so it causes no split or fresh group. The single
shared Schur coefficient handles every leaf pivot. Thereafter the algorithm
has at most one frontier vertex on the remaining endpoint path. Each such
pivot retires its emptied group after creating at most one fresh group.
Thus $b_i\leq2$ throughout. The seed and leaves contribute $2m+1$ to
$V$, and the general volume bound gives $V<8m$; the far terminal endpoint
cannot be reached within that budget. Apply
\cref{thm:op3-frontier-response-groups}. Existing fast tree algorithms are
not new consequences of this example.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact audit and measured comparison.}
\path{frontier_groups.py} implements the producer, degree heaps, paid
partition refinement, shared matrix and reverse aggregates. The full audit
passes 7,311 original ACL outputs, 49,915 final residual rows and 12,986
prefix Schur/AVL/heap/partition checks. There are 5,700 independent checks
of reverse coordinates and restored membership aggregates. Dense obstacles,
face solves, inverse columns and historical membership snapshots are
validators only. The audit includes 708 initial and 768 final exact gate
ties, 594 actual partition splits, 655 old-member moves, arbitrary signed
large labels, tiny teleportation through $2^{-1024}$ and three private
huge hubs whose outside rows are not read.

For $m=64$, the same star-and-tail parameter case has admitted volume 131.
The explicit reference records 45,760 fill updates and 17,313,546 charged
units; the grouped producer records 66 shared matrix updates and 66,793
charged units. These are the implementations' operation ledgers, not wall
time speedups. The grouped $m=256$ case has volume 515, 258 shared updates
and 309,100 charged units. Graphs, parameters, hashes and all counters are
in \path{FRONTIER_GROUPS_AUDIT.json}. General response diversity and OP3
remain \statusopen; the next tests concern a structural type bound and a
branching obstruction to this particular group-order rule.
