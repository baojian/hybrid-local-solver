\section{A proper-support potential bound and a small-teleportation reduction}
\label{sec:op3-small-alpha-floor}

\begin{lemma}[Unit-source superlevels and a proper-support cap; \statusproved]
\label{lem:op3-superlevel-potential-cap}
On a finite connected simple unweighted graph with at least two vertices,
let $0<\gamma<1$, $M_\gamma=D-\gamma A$, and suppose
$x\geq0$ has original residual $r=e_v-M_\gamma x\geq0$.
Every nonempty strict positive superlevel set $H_t=\{i:x_i>t\}$,
$t\geq0$, is connected and contains the seed. Moreover
\[
 (1-\gamma)\sum_{i\in H_t}d_ix_i
 +\gamma\sum_{\substack{i\in H_t,\ j\notin H_t\\i\sim j}}
 (x_i-x_j)
 =\boldsymbol{1}_{v\in H_t}-\sum_{i\in H_t}r_i\leq1.
\]
In particular $\gamma|x_i-x_j|\leq1$ on every edge. If
$S=\operatorname{supp}x$ is a proper subset of the graph, then
\[
 \|x\|_\infty\leq |S|/\gamma.
\]
For the exact positive-$\lambda$ obstacle, proper support gives
$\|u\|_\infty<1/(\lambda\gamma)$.
An upper bound on the residual is unnecessary for this lemma.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Sum $M_\gamma x=e_v-r$ over $H_t$, canceling internal edge
differences. This gives the displayed identity. The grounding and every
outward boundary difference are nonnegative. The same identity applies
to a connected component of $H_t$, because its neighbors in any other
component of $H_t$ are absent. If that component did not contain the
seed, its right side would be nonpositive, whereas its grounding term
is strictly positive. Thus every component contains the seed, proving
both assertions about $H_t$.

For an edge with unequal endpoints, choose $t$ strictly between its
two nonnegative values. The corresponding nonnegative boundary sum
contains $\gamma|x_i-x_j|$, which is therefore at most one. Equal
endpoints are immediate. If $S$ is nonempty and proper, take a simple
path from a maximizing vertex to the first vertex outside $S$.
It has at most $|S|$ edges. Telescope the endpoint values and use the
edge bound to obtain the maximum estimate. Empty support is immediate.
For the exact obstacle, its original residual is nonnegative and its
positive support has original volume below $1/\lambda$; original
degrees are at least one, so $|S|<1/\lambda$ as well.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the proper-support condition is needed.}
On the graph of one edge, take seed zero and
\[
 x=\frac{(1,\gamma)}{1-\gamma^2}.
\]
Its original residual is exactly zero and both coordinates are positive.
As $\gamma\uparrow1$ its maximum diverges, while $|S|/\gamma$ stays
bounded. Thus even exact original ACL output can have a large constant
component on full support. The superlevel and gradient statements still
hold; the path to a zero coordinate is the missing premise.

\begin{theorem}[A paid small-alpha reduction with a volume contract;
\statusconditional]
\label{thm:op3-small-alpha-floor}
Fix target $\alpha\in(0,1]$, $\epsappr\in(0,1)$, failure budget
$p\in(0,1)$ and a known $B\geq1$.
Suppose a native original-ACL producer, at supplied parameters $(a,e,q)$,
has a computable bound $\mathcal W(a,e,q,B)$ for work and total allocated
words on every execution. With probability at least $1-q$ it returns
an explicitly listed nonnegative sparse potential $x$ satisfying
\[
 0\leq e_v-M_{\gamma_a}x\leq e d,\qquad
 \operatorname{vol}_G(\operatorname{supp}x)\leq B,
 \qquad \gamma_a=(1-a)/(1+a).
\]
Its nonzero output entries have distinct labels; it may return
\textnormal{FAIL}. All discovery and preprocessing work belongs to
$\mathcal W$. Define
\[
 a_0=\frac{\epsappr}{4B+\epsappr},\qquad
 a_{\mathrm{eff}}=\max\{\alpha,a_0\}.
\]
There is a wrapper returning original ACL output at target
$(\alpha,\epsappr)$ with probability at least $1-p$. Its work and
total allocated words on every execution are at most
\[
 \mathcal W(a_{\mathrm{eff}},\epsappr/2,p/2,B)
 +(1+B)\operatorname{polylog}
 (2+B+\alpha^{-1}+\epsappr^{-1}+p^{-1}).
\]
It calls the native producer once. If a parameter change is needed and
the native support is nonempty and proper, it returns the same potential.
If that support is the whole graph, it uses one complete supplied solve
at the original target. A sparse output word bound alone does not meet
the native support-volume hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Call the native producer at $(a_{\mathrm{eff}},\epsappr/2,p/2)$.
Before any support-row scan, reject more than $B$ output entries,
invalid nonpositive entries or duplicate labels, and query the original
degrees of its positive support $S$. Reject immediately if their sum
exceeds $B$. Sorting and these checks cost
$O((1+B)\log(2+B))$. They preserve every good native output.
In particular a tiny output containing a huge-degree vertex cannot
force an expensive adjacency-list scan on a bad native execution.

If $\alpha\geq a_0$, no parameter changes and the native good output
already has target accuracy. An empty support is also independent of
$\alpha$ and can be returned. Otherwise scan the original support rows,
using a paid membership map. Finding one neighbor outside $S$ proves
that $S$ is proper. If no such neighbor exists, connectedness and
$S\ne\varnothing$ imply $S=V(G)$. The degree pass bounds the entire
scan and copied entries by $B$, even on a bad native execution.

Consider the proper branch on the native good event. Here
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}=a_0$ and
\[
 \gamma_0=\frac{2B}{2B+\epsappr},\qquad
 \frac{1-\gamma_0}{\gamma_0}=\frac{\epsappr}{2B},\qquad
 0\leq\gamma_\alpha-\gamma_0\leq1-\gamma_0.
\]
The potential lemma gives $\|x\|_\infty\leq |S|/\gamma_0
\leq B/\gamma_0$. Consequently the target residual is exactly
\[
 r_\alpha=r_0+(\gamma_\alpha-\gamma_0)Ax,
 \qquad
 0\leq r_\alpha
 \leq\left(\frac{\epsappr}{2}
       +\frac{(1-\gamma_0)B}{\gamma_0}\right)d
 =\epsappr d.
\]
This proves the same-potential transfer; the original normalization of
the emitted approximation uses $\bar\alpha D$ at the target parameter.

In the full-support branch, every original degree and adjacency list
of the graph has now been supplied, at volume at most $B$.
Apply \cref{thm:op3-supplied-envelope-work} to this entire supplied
graph at original $\alpha$, accuracy $\epsappr$ and failure budget
$p/2$. Its preparation can reuse the materialized rows. It costs the
second term in the stated bound on every execution. It requires no
assertion that the previous native vector is accurate: the supplied
graph is complete by the closure check itself. Recovered output degrees
are obtained from its paid sorted degree map, without outside queries.
Source randomness for this call is fresh. A conditional union bound
over native and fallback failures is at most $p$; the fallback is
invoked at most once. Output copies, formatting and all guards are
charged. The native cap and supplied cap include failed attempts.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation of the reduction.}
When the explicit native volume contract has $B=C/\epsappr$ for a
fixed $C>0$, the new floor is
$a_0=\epsappr^2/(4C+\epsappr^2)$.
Thus arbitrary smaller teleportation can be reduced to a native local
problem at this accuracy-squared scale, with a paid full-graph exception
only when that whole graph has volume at most $B$. This isolates a
parameter range; it does not supply the native near-linear local producer.
In particular, one cannot apply the theorem to an arbitrary sparse ACL
algorithm without first proving its original support-volume contract.

\begin{proposition}[A larger same-vector floor can fail on paths;
\statusproved]
\label{prop:op3-large-alpha-floor-obstruction}
For every integer $N\geq16$, consider the original path on
$4N^2+1$ vertices, seeded at an endpoint, and set
\[
 \epsappr=N^{-2},\quad \lambda=\epsappr/4,\quad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=1-N^{-2},\quad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{target}}=1-N^{-4}.
\]
The exact $\lambda$-obstacle at $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is an
original $\epsappr/2$ ACL potential with proper support of volume
below $4/\epsappr$. The same potential violates target
$\epsappr$ accuracy at the seed. Here the effective teleportation
is of order $\epsappr$ and the target teleportation is of order
$\epsappr^2$. This refutes this larger same-vector substitution;
it is not a lower bound on general algorithms or other transformations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Number path vertices from the seed. Set
$z_i=(N-i)^2/(4N)$ for $0\leq i<N$ and zero elsewhere.
The seed row satisfies
\[
 (M_{\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}z)_0
 =\tfrac12-\frac1{2N^2}+\frac1{4N^3}
 \leq\tfrac12<1-\lambda.
\]
For $1\leq i<N$, original degree is two and
\[
 (M_{\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}z)_i
 =-\frac1{2N}+\frac{(N-i)^2+1}{2N^3}
 \leq-\frac{N-1}{N^3}\leq-2\lambda.
\]
Thus $z$ is a positive-coordinate subsolution, so the maximum-principle
argument gives $z\leq u$, where $u$ is the exact native obstacle.
Its support is proper because its volume is below $1/\lambda=4N^2$,
whereas the entire path has volume $8N^2$.
At the seed, $u_0>0$ and the native residual equals $\lambda$.
The target seed residual therefore satisfies
\[
 (r_{\mathrm{target}})_0
 =\lambda+(N^{-2}-N^{-4})u_1
 \geq\lambda+(N^{-2}-N^{-4})\frac{(N-1)^2}{4N}
 >\epsappr.
\]
For example, the added term is at least $3/(32N)>N^{-2}$ for
$N\geq16$, using $N^{-2}-N^{-4}\geq3/(4N^2)$ and
$(N-1)^2\geq N^2/2$. The original seed degree is one.
Finally $\alpha=(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)$ gives the stated parameter
orders. All assertions use original degrees on the full path.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Audit scope.}
The exact script \texttt{small\_alpha\_floor.py} implements the paid
sparse-output volume/closure wrapper with native and full-component
callbacks. Its obstacle and truncated-Neumann vectors are explicit
validators, not a fast local producer. It checks strict-superlevel flux,
connectivity, edge gradients, proper-support maxima, target residuals,
the full-support counterexample and the private large-path subsolution.
Source/backend hashes and finite counts are in
\path{SMALL_ALPHA_FLOOR_AUDIT.json}.

The full run takes 3.110 seconds and checks 1,644 target ACL outputs,
including 152 same-vector transfers and 259 complete-component reference
fallbacks. It verifies 7,545 superlevel flux/connectivity identities,
15,341 edge gradients and 1,071 proper-support maxima on 2,741 potentials.
Six private-path witnesses check 1,002 positive subsolution rows. The
volume pass rejects all three huge-degree outputs before any row, and
five malformed native outputs are rejected by explicit guards.
