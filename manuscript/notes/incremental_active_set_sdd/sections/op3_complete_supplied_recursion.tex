\section{Capped composition of the supplied near-linear recursion}

\paragraph{What changes at this checkpoint.}
The weighted constructor in \cref{cor:op3-weighted-constructor-contract}
now meets the former source hypothesis on the global numerical ranges
proved in \cref{thm:op3-simultaneous-recursive-ranges}. This section
makes the treatment of failed executions explicit and composes the

\begin{lemma}[Capping primitive work and cumulative allocations; \statusproved]
\label{lem:op3-primitive-execution-cap}
Consider an exact-real word algorithm whose input includes computable
budgets for primitive work, total allocated words, recursion depth,
number of designated source calls, and other nonnegative cumulative
counters such as node-input mass. There is a wrapper with constant
factor work overhead whose simulated payload halts with \textnormal{FAIL}
before exceeding any budget. It agrees with every execution staying within the budgets,
apart from the harmless constant factor accounting overhead. Its total
work and total allocated words, including the final rejected guard and
wrapper temporaries, are $O(1+T+A)$ when $T$ and
$A$ bound the original primitive work and total allocated words.
No complete-source-call runtime guarantee is required on a bad branch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Execute the fixed algorithm one word instruction at a time, maintaining
counters for executed work, every allocated word, the current call depth
and the cumulative designated-call count. Check and reserve the resource
increment before the instruction's effect. A bulk initialization or copy
can either be expanded into single-word steps, or have its entire known
work/allocation increment reserved before entering its bounded loop.
In particular, a request for an enormous buffer is rejected before that
buffer is created. The interpreter is applied to an imported source's
word algorithm too; checking after an opaque source call returns is not
this construction.

Every executed instruction consumes at least one work unit, including a
zero-word allocation, a failed objective guard and an empty-list step.
Releasing an array decreases live storage, but does not decrease the
cumulative allocation counter. Call entry checks depth and the designated
call budget before allocating its frame; returning releases the frame
without refunding its allocation. Counter arithmetic and comparisons
have constant overhead per original word step or reserved word.
A first rejected reservation has constant additional cost and immediately
terminates the execution. Thus there cannot be an uncharged infinite loop
of rejected requests or zero-sized operations. Counters, wrapper frames
and termination words fit the same constant factor overhead.
An arithmetic instruction outside its domain is mapped to
\textnormal{FAIL}, charging its domain check. These checks do not alter
any mathematically valid execution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional confidence for capped adaptive calls; \statusproved]
\label{lem:op3-capped-adaptive-confidence}
Suppose at most $N$ designated calls are allowed. At each call, conditional
on every valid past execution and its supplied input, fresh randomness
makes that call satisfy its complete contract with probability at least
$1-\delta$. Then the probability of any first failed contract before
termination is at most $N\delta$. The inputs and number of calls may
be chosen adaptively. No independence of complete recursive instances
is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each position $i\leq N$, restrict to histories that reach call $i$
without a previous failed contract. Conditional on such a history, the
probability that this call is the first failure is at most $\delta$.
Integrate over those histories and sum over the at most $N$ positions.
These first-failure events are disjoint, so their total is at most
$N\delta$. Stopping early only removes histories.

The conditional assumption matters. For example, let a shared seed be a
uniform label $U\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}$. An oracle can expose $U$ in its
first output while declaring failure on an input exactly equal to $U$.
For any fixed input the failure probability is $1/s$, but choosing the
second input to be the exposed label forces failure under the same seed.
With fresh independent randomness the conditional failure is again $1/s$.
The actual constructor uses fresh independent fair bits for each call.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A complete source-backed supplied VWF recursion; \statusproved]
\label{thm:op3-complete-supplied-recursion}
Consider a supplied connected graph VWF instance on $n\geq2$ vertices with finite attained
minimum, $\Phi(0)\leq0$, and total input size $M_0=m+S$, where $S$
counts all scalar pieces and includes one per vertex. Let
$B_0\geq-\Phi^*$ be computable, and let $Z\geq2$ bound the initial
quantities in \cref{thm:op3-simultaneous-recursive-ranges}, including
$\eta^{-1}$, for requested $\eta\in(0,1]$ and $p\in(0,1)$.
Set
\[
 L=\left\lceil\log_2(2+M_0+Z+p^{-1})\right\rceil.
\]
There are universal computable constants $C,d$ and a supplied algorithm
returning \textnormal{FAIL} or a feasible vector $x$. Every execution
uses at most $CM_0L^d$ exact-real word work and total allocated words,
and with probability at least $1-p$ it returns a vector satisfying
\[
 \Phi(x)\leq\tfrac12\Phi^*+\eta.
\]
The algorithm uses the checked arbitrary-positive-weight low-stretch-tree
source, the proved weighted constructor, persistent forest elimination,
global compression, the mixed additive schedule, and the bounded-size
dense forward-piece base policy. No polynomial weight-ratio assumption
in the current core size is imposed. This is an existence/construction
and work theorem in the stated word model, not a claim that the entire
source-backed recursion is implemented by the finite audits.
For one vertex, the same conclusion follows by scanning its scalar
pieces for the first derivative zero or nonnegative lower-end derivative;
this exact $O(S)$ base is used before any graph edge-range expression.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the recursion of \cref{thm:op3-conditional-supplied-recursion},
with $K=A L^{12}$ for a sufficiently large universal $A$. In a nonbase
call choose
\[
 j=\min\{n,\lceil C_2mL^2/K\rceil\},
\]
and use \cref{cor:op3-weighted-constructor-contract}, with failure budget
$\delta=p/(4M_0)$, followed by the proved spectral edge floor.
The $j=n$ branch uses the whole-graph sparsifier. On a valid constructor
execution, adjusted quality is at most $K$ and the core has
$O(jL)$ edges. These conclusions use global $L$:
$\log(2+n+m+1/\delta)=O(L)$ for every capped input below.

Here is a noncircular good-execution induction. Impose a per-node size
cap $M\leq M_0$, a depth cap $D_0=\lceil\log_2 M_0\rceil+1$,
and a total node-input-mass cap $2M_0$. Analyze any prefix up to the
first putative cap violation, on which all constructor contracts hold.
Every constructed graph then has at most $O(M_0L)$ records and quality
at most $K$. Choose the parameter $P$ of the simultaneous range theorem
to bound these quantities. Since $\log P=O(L)$ and $D_0=O(L)$,
that theorem bounds every numerical logarithm, including the actual
input weight ratio, by $O(L^2)$ along this prefix.
Consequently every compression grid has $O(L^2)$ bins per vertex,
and the new constructor costs
\[
 O\bigl(m[L^6+\log(c_{\max}/c_{\min})]\bigr)=O(mL^6).
\]
This is exactly the cost hypothesis formerly missing from the conditional
recurrence, without assuming the ratio is polynomial in the shrinking $n$.

The size calculation in that recurrence now applies on the prefix:
\[
 M'=O(ML^4/K),\qquad
 \#\text{ children}=O(\sqrt K L),\qquad
 \sum_{\text{children}}M'\leq M/2.
\]
Choose $A$ large enough to make the last constant at most one half for
all $L\geq1$. Each child's positive integral size is at most $M/2$,
so no path can reach the depth cap while continuing recursion.
Summing mass by levels gives total node mass at most $2M_0$; each
individual node has size at most $M_0$. Thus none of these caps can be
the first violation. This proves the size/range induction for the whole
good execution. Since every node has positive size, there are at most
$2M_0$ constructor calls as well; base nodes need none.

For completeness, all costs in the good-execution recurrence have fixed
polynomial exponents. Nonrecursive source construction costs $O(mL^6)$.
Persistent forest passes, explicit exports, compression, residual copies,
objective guards, canonical shifts, recoveries and source-vector updates
have their previously proved word bounds, repeated at most
$O(\sqrt K L)$ times per node. At a base node $n\leq K$, the exact
dense policy costs $O(m+(S+1)n^4)$, so at most
$M\operatorname{poly}(L)$ even when the number of scalar pieces is large.
The numerical policy uses fixed scheduled counts and explicitly charged
piece traversals; it does not search for an unknown positive optimum gap.
Summing over node mass at most $2M_0$ gives a computable fixed bound
$T_0=C_3M_0L^{d_0}$ for work and every allocated word. Its constants can
be chosen from the finitely many preceding bounds and the checked source
algorithms. No assertion that $d_0$ equals the source paper's exponent
eight is made.

Apply \cref{lem:op3-primitive-execution-cap} using this good-run bound,
the preceding mass/depth caps, and designated-call cap $2M_0$.
Use constant slack to cover all checking overhead. Input validation,
failed guards, copied histories and all failed source attempts remain
inside the counter. The complete algorithm halts before any bad execution
can exceed its fixed word-work/allocation budget. On the joint event
that all constructor contracts hold, none of the caps is reached and
it coincides with the proved mixed recursion, whose correctness follows
from \cref{cor:op3-mixed-recursive-budgets} and
\cref{thm:op3-mixed-proximal-accuracy}.

Finally, \cref{lem:op3-capped-adaptive-confidence} bounds the probability
of a first constructor failure by
$2M_0\cdot p/(4M_0)=p/2\leq p$. Each constructor's complete confidence
already includes its resistance estimator, matrix sampling and bit caps;
those probabilities are not counted a second time. The remaining half
is unused slack. This proves both the returned mixed contract and the
bounded cost on every execution. Sparse output recovery and the final
feasibility check are charged before return.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Supplied absolute objective accuracy; \statusproved]
\label{cor:op3-complete-supplied-absolute}
For a supplied instance as above and absolute target $t\in(0,1]$, enlarge
$Z$ to include $t^{-1}$. The same model admits a supplied algorithm
returning \textnormal{FAIL} or a feasible vector, and with probability
at least $1-p$ the returned vector has objective gap at most $t$.
Its cost is $M_0\operatorname{poly}(L)$ word work and total
allocated words. Original ACL accuracy follows by using
\cref{lem:op3-capped-grounding-vwf,lem:op3-diffusion-energy-scale}
with $\delta=\epsappr/8$ and $t=\bar\alpha\delta^2/2$.
The separable case $\alpha=1$ is handled directly by $u=e_v/d_v$;
for $0<\alpha<1$ the positive edge weight is $\gamma$.
Paying to obtain or enlarge a suitable supplied vertex set remains a
separate obligation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use guarded residual refinement with $\beta=2$ and per-call additive
error $t/4$. After $J$ calls the original gap is at most
$2^{-J}B_0+t/2$. Choose a known
$J=O(1+\log(2+B_0/t))$ making this at most $t$; if $B_0=0$, return
zero directly. Canonical objective guards keep all accepted states in
the original nonpositive sublevel. The existing residual range bounds
therefore bound all translated instances by the same original energy
scale, rather than multiplying a fresh gap bound over iterations.
Their numerical logarithms remain polynomial in $L$.
Allocate $p/J$ to each invocation of the complete supplied theorem and
apply its work cap. Fresh source randomness and a conditional union
bound give total failure at most $p$; $\log J=O(L)$ and the additional
factor $J=O(L)$ are absorbed by the stated polynomial. Residual copies,
objective comparisons and final output are explicitly included.
For the physical specialization, boxing preserves the absolute objective
gap by \cref{lem:op3-capped-grounding-vwf}. The chosen $t$ and
\cref{lem:op3-diffusion-energy-scale} imply coordinate error at most
$\delta$. The downward-clipping proof of
\cref{thm:op3-diffusion-accuracy-bridge} then gives the original ACL
residual. Boxing, clipping, input scans and output entries are charged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Audit scope and the remaining conjecture.}
The exact resource/confidence model audit is
\texttt{supplied\_recursion\_caps.py}. Its durable source hash is in
\path{SUPPLIED_RECURSION_CAPS_AUDIT.json}. It checks rejected
reservations before payload execution, zero-size loops, cumulative
allocations despite releases, invalid requests, nested mass/depth/call
bounds, and adaptive conditional probability trees. A reused-seed example
shows why a per-fixed-input failure statement is insufficient on its own.
These checks audit the cap semantics and composition algebra, not a full
implementation of the asymptotic recursion or imported tree algorithm.
The preceding numerical and constructor audits remain their separate
finite evidence. General OP3, the significant-envelope finder and a
rank-independent geometric event producer remain \statusopen.

The full model audit checks 12,374 transaction prefixes and 39,807 accepted
or rejected requests, including 9,818 halted adversarial traces. It rejects
three enormous allocations before their payloads, caps four zero-size
loops, and charges three allocate/release loops cumulatively. It also
checks 252 adaptive conditional union bounds on 48,891 probability-tree
nodes, 40 mass/depth/call trees and four reused-seed counterexamples.
These finite checks took 1.284 seconds and do not measure full-solver work.
