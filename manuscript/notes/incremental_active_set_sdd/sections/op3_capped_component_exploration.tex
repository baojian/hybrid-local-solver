\section{A paid small-component gate}
\label{sec:op3-capped-component-exploration}

\begin{theorem}[Complete component or original-volume witness;
\statusproved]
\label{thm:op3-capped-component-gate}
Given the original graph, seed and $B\geq1$, a deterministic local
algorithm either returns the complete original graph with volume at
most $B$, or certifies $\operatorname{vol}(G)>B$. Its work and total
allocated words are $O((1+B)\log(2+B))$, and live graph state is
$O(1+B)$. Every discovered degree is queried once, and every started
original row is queried once. Even charging a partially consumed row
in full, the total started-row degree is at most $B$. No ambient size,
global label list or uncharged dictionary is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Keep stable vertex records in an AVL search tree. Each record holds
its label, queried original degree, tree links and height, queue link,
cached row and completion flag. Insert the seed and maintain the sum
$V_{m known}$ of degrees of all distinct discovered records. Whenever
this exceeds $B$, immediately return the distinct-degree witness.
It is a lower bound on the original graph volume, regardless of the
degrees of undiscovered vertices.

Otherwise remove one record from the intrusive FIFO, charge its full
degree and allocate a row buffer of exactly that size. Before this
operation, the set of all started rows, including the new one, is a
subset of the known records and has degree sum at most
$V_{m known}\leq B$. Read neighbors one at a time. A new label
creates one stable record, queries one original degree and enters the
queue. Store its pointer in the current buffer. If its degree pushes
$V_{m known}$ over budget, stop before starting another row or
allocating that new vertex's adjacency buffer. Thus even a huge outside
degree costs one reply and one constant-size record; the already
started row is fully charged despite a possible partial stop.

If the queue empties, all neighbors of all discovered records have
been discovered and all their rows are complete. This nonempty closed
set is the whole graph by connectedness, and its original volume is
$V_{m known}\leq B$. Conversely, on a graph of volume at most $B$
the degree test never rejects, so the finite exploration reaches this
complete outcome. The partial result on a larger graph is only a
volume witness; it is not asserted to be a significant-potential envelope.

Before rejection there are at most $\lfloor B\rfloor$ known records,
and the rejecting degree query creates at most one extra record.
There are at most $B$ charged row entries and at most $B+1$ degree
queries. AVL insertion or lookup uses $O(\log(2+B))$ comparisons,
pointer reads and writes. For completeness, the implemented rotations
preserve binary-search order and balance $|h_L-h_R|\leq1$; the minimum
node count at height $h$ satisfies
$N_h\geq1+N_{h-1}+N_{h-2}$, so height is $O(\log(2+B))$.
Each traversed ancestor requires only constant height and rotation work.
Records stay at fixed addresses, so queue and adjacency pointers remain
valid under tree rotations. The final in-order scan allocates exactly
one output array and a stack of the recorded tree height, in $O(1+B)$
work. Records, row buffers and queue links use $O(1+B)$ live words;
including transient call frames conservatively bounds total allocated
words by the stated work. All comparisons, partial-row charges, output
records and failed-budget branches are included.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Small whole graphs already have the desired ACL scale;
\statusproved]
\label{cor:op3-small-whole-graph}
For $B=4/\epsappr$, the complete branch can return original target
ACL output in
$\epsappr^{-1}\operatorname{polylog}(2+\epsappr^{-1}+p^{-1})$
work and total allocated words, with failure probability at most $p$.
No target-teleportation dependence remains in the exact-real word model.
Consequently the remaining algorithmic ACL question may be restricted
to graphs of original volume greater than $4/\epsappr$, after paying
this gate. This restriction does not provide nested-system reuse on
the larger graphs.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On the complete branch, the known whole graph is a valid supplied
envelope for every target obstacle and has volume at most $4/\epsappr$.
Apply \cref{thm:op3-alpha-independent-supplied-envelope}, reading
the already cached rows through the paid label map. Its source-backed
numerical construction retains its explicit confidence and primitive
caps. The gate is deterministic, so it spends no failure probability.
On the other branch the degree witness proves the stated size restriction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implemented gate audit.}
\path{capped_component_exploration.py} implements the AVL map, rotations,
intrusive queue, precharged rows and distinct-degree witness. The full
run checks 4,086 component outcomes, comprising 1,653 complete graphs
and 2,433 large-volume witnesses, in 1.114 seconds. It checks 26,660
one-time degree queries and 53,743 charged degree units, including
partially read terminal rows. Cases include a 4,096-vertex path,
permuted labels of large magnitude, private paths with $2^{1024}$
vertices and private hubs of degree $2^{1024}$ whose rows are never
read. Source and backend hashes are recorded in
\path{CAPPED_COMPONENT_EXPLORATION_AUDIT.json}.
The gate is implemented; the corollary's fast numerical solver retains
the earlier supplied-source interface.
