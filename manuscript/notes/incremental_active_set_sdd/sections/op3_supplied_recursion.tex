\section[Conditional supplied recursion]{Confidence accounting and a conditional supplied near-linear recursion}
\label{sec:op3-supplied-recursion}

\paragraph{What is and is not imported.}
CPW's printed Lemma 4.4 assumes polynomial edge-weight ratio. Its
weighted forest/routing construction is described in Sections 5.1--5.6.
The underlying low-stretch-tree source explicitly handles arbitrary
positive weights \citep[Sections 6--7]{abraham2019petal}; the general
spectral-sparsification source uses fixed solver precision
\citep[Sections 3.4 and 4, Theorem 4.2]{koutis2015sparsification}.
These facts motivate the contract below, but do not silently change the
statement of CPW's theorem. We keep the complete positive-weight
constructor contract explicit and label the full solver result
\statusconditional. The numerical lemmas proved earlier do not depend
on that source-extension decision. The later
\cref{cor:op3-weighted-constructor-contract,thm:op3-complete-supplied-recursion}
now discharge the constructor and capped-execution obligations on the
tracked global ranges. The conditional theorem below is retained as a
reusable contract statement.

\begin{proposition}[Confidence from a supplied resistance estimator; \statusproved]
\label{prop:op3-spectral-confidence-wrapper}
Suppose a supplied procedure on a connected positive-weight graph
returns all effective resistances within relative error $1/4$, jointly
with probability at least $3/4$, at cost $T_R$. Independent calls use
fresh randomness. For any $p\in(0,1)$, there is a wrapper producing a
graph $S$ with
\[
 G\preceq S\preceq2G
\]
with probability at least $1-p$, using $O(n\log(n/p))$ sampled edge
records. Its extra work besides $O(\log(1/p))$ estimator calls is
\[
 O\bigl(mk\log(2+k)+n\log(n/p)\log(2+m)\bigr),
 \qquad k=O(1+\log(1/p)).
\]
Every estimate vector, median buffer, sample and output is charged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take an odd $k\geq8\lceil\log_2(2/p)\rceil$ independent complete
estimate vectors and use coordinatewise medians. If a majority of
vectors are good, every median is good. For the number of bad vectors,
the exponential Markov bound with multiplier three gives majority-failure
probability at most $(\sqrt3/2)^k\leq e^{-k/8}\leq p/2$.
Scale each median by twice its original edge weight, obtaining scores
$s_e$ satisfying
\[
c_eR_e\leq s_e\leq4c_eR_e,
 \qquad t=\sum_es_e\leq4(n-1).
\]
Abort if any score is nonpositive or if $t>4(n-1)$, charging the
complete check. This never happens on the good median event and caps
the sampling work even on an erroneous estimator output.
Sample edges independently with probabilities $p_e=s_e/t$. On the
$(n-1)$-dimensional space orthogonal to the constants, the proof matrices
\[
 Y_e=(c_e/p_e)G^{+/2}b_eb_e^TG^{+/2}
\]
have expectation identity and operator norm $c_eR_e/p_e\leq t$.
These square roots are proof notation, not computed objects.
The matrix Chernoff inequalities
\citep[Corollary 5.2]{tropp2012tails} imply for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\frac1q\sum_{j=1}^qY_{e_j}
       \notin[(1-\varepsilon)I,(1+\varepsilon)I]\right\}
 \leq2(n-1)e^{-\varepsilon^2q/(3t)}.
\]
For clarity, the scalar exponents follow from
$\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)\geq\varepsilon^2/2$
and $(1+\varepsilon)\log(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon^2/3$.
Choose $\varepsilon=1/3$ and
$q\geq27t\lceil\log_2(4n/p)\rceil$. The sampling failure is at
most $p/2$. Scaling the sampled Laplacian by $3/2$ gives the claimed
spectral interval; union with the median event proves the confidence.
Sorting each median and binary-search sampling give the displayed work.
Repeated sampled edges may be aggregated by paid multiplicity updates.
The audit's exact rational CDF sampler is a reference realization; no
constant-time integer bit operations are asserted.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Explicit constructor hypothesis.}
Fix a global logarithmic parameter $L$. For every supplied connected
positive-weight graph with $m$ edges and every integer $10\leq j<n$,
assume an available constructor returns a $j$-tree $Q$ with
\[
 G\preceq Q\preceq C_0mL^2G/j,
 \qquad m_{\rm core}\leq C_1jL,
\]
at cost $O(mL^c)$ for a fixed universal $c$. The guarantee must hold
for the actual input weights, with requested failure probability
$p/(4M_0)$; its logarithm is included in $L$. The rooted forest,
core records and all allocations are part of its output/cost contract.
For $j=n$, use a whole-graph constant-factor sparsifier instead.
The preceding confidence wrapper addresses the core-sampling probability;
the weighted forest/decomposition/routing assumptions still require their
separate source reconciliation. This hypothesis does not grant free
access to an unknown neighborhood.

\begin{theorem}[A supplied VWF recursion under that contract; \statusconditional]
\label{thm:op3-conditional-supplied-recursion}
Consider a supplied connected graph VWF instance with finite minimum,
$\Phi(0)\leq0$, total input size $M_0=m+S$, and requested
$\eta\in(0,1]$, $p\in(0,1)$. Here $S$ counts all scalar pieces,
including one per vertex. Let a computable $B_0\geq-\Phi^*$ be given,
and let $Z\geq2$ bound the initial quantities in
\cref{thm:op3-simultaneous-recursive-ranges}, including $\eta^{-1}$.
Set $L=\lceil\log_2(2+M_0+Z+p^{-1})\rceil$.
Under the explicit constructor contract, a supplied algorithm returns
a $(2,\eta)$ output in $M_0\operatorname{poly}(L)$ exact-real word
work and total allocated words, with probability at least $1-p$.
It uses the proved persistent forest, global compression and mixed
accuracy schedule, and may use the proved dense forward-piece policy
only at an explicitly bounded base size. The logarithmic exponent is
not claimed to be CPW's exponent eight.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use $K=A L^{12}$ for a sufficiently large universal $A$, and stop
recursing when $n\leq K$. In a nonbase case choose
\[
 j=\min\{n,\lceil C_2mL^2/K\rceil\},
\]
where $C_2$ absorbs the constructor quality and the factor-two spectral
floor. Since $n>K$ and $m\geq n-1$, the nontrivial choice is at least
ten after adjusting universal constants. If the minimum equals $n$,
whole-graph sparsification gives quality at most four after the floor.
In either case adjusted quality is at most $K$ and
$j=O(mL^2/K)$. Every inner exact forest pass is followed by the
mixed refinement/compression schedule, with
$\eta_{\rm child}=\eta/(2^{34}K)$.

Here is a simultaneous induction on the input sizes and ranges.
Every constructed graph has at most $O(M_0L)$ edge records, and
all qualities are at most $K$. A path whose successive input sizes
halve has depth $O(\log M_0)$. Thus the preceding range theorem bounds
every compression grid by $O(L^2)$ bins per vertex. Consequently a child
has
\[
 m' = O(mL^3/K),\qquad S'=O(mL^4/K),\qquad
 M'=O(ML^4/K),
\]
where $M=m+S$ is the parent input size. The base and induction step
may equivalently use a depth cap $\lceil\log_2 M_0\rceil+1$;
the size estimate shows the cap is never reached on a valid execution.

One accelerated invocation makes $O(\sqrt K)$ inner calls, each with
$O(\log K)\leq O(L)$ refinement calls. The sum of child sizes is
therefore at most
\[
 O(\sqrt K L)\,O(ML^4/K)
 =O(ML^5/\sqrt K)\leq M/2
\]
for a sufficiently large $A$. This also justifies the path-depth and
range induction. Full construction, normalization, exact forest passes,
exports, residual copies, bin scans, objective guards, canonical shifts,
recoveries, source updates and outputs cost $M\operatorname{poly}(L)$
outside the child calls. Old versions and failed calls remain included.

At a base case build the dense graph matrix once in $O(m+n^2)$ work.
The forward-piece policy makes at most $S+1$ bound QPs; each has at most
$n$ strict face expansions and at most $O(n^3)$ arithmetic per dense
principal solve. Hence its work is $O(m+(S+1)n^4)$, which is
$M\operatorname{poly}(L)$ because $n\leq K$. This is not a
constant-time base case when many scalar pieces remain. All newly
allocated words are bounded by the same charged operation count.

The sum of input sizes over the complete recursion tree is at most
$2M_0$, so summing the nonrecursive costs proves the work bound.
The number of constructor calls is at most $2M_0$. Allocate
$p/(4M_0)$ to each call, using fresh randomness on its adaptively supplied
input; the conditional union bound is at most $p/2$. A corresponding
call/work cap may abort failed-source executions. On the event that all
constructor contracts hold, no cap is reached. Correctness then follows
by induction from \cref{cor:op3-mixed-recursive-budgets} and the mixed
accelerated theorem. The complete constructor confidence already includes
its source estimator; the remaining failure budget is unused slack.
The asserted work is fully supplied
graph work throughout.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Absolute accuracy and original ACL.}
A computable $B_0$ is available from the canonical input-gap proposition:
add $-\Phi(0)$ to its gap upper bound. Guarded $\beta=2$ refinement
with per-call additive error $t/4$ reaches absolute gap $t$ after
$O(1+\log(2+B_0/t))$ calls. Residual range bounds keep their initial
numerical logarithms controlled. Split the total failure budget over
that known number of outer calls. Thus the same conditional supplied
near-linear conclusion holds up to another logarithmic factor.
For original capped diffusion, the existing boxing and gap-to-residual
bridge then supplies ACL accuracy. Paying for a suitable supplied vertex
set, and for any repeated enlargements, remains a separate obligation.
No support-local or arbitrary-graph OP3 theorem is inferred here.
