\section[Explicit proximal accuracy]{Explicit proximal accuracy and certified restarts on a supplied graph}
\label{sec:op3-capped-proximal-budget}

\paragraph{Status and imported ingredient.}
The parameter policy and restart reduction below are \statusproved{}
drafts for a supplied relative-oracle interface. The accelerated convergence
inequality is a \statussource{} ingredient: Algorithm 9 and Theorem 8.3 of
Chen--Peng--Wang, PDF p.\ 44 \citep{chen2021diffusion}. The normalized
proximal instance is their Claim 8.19, PDF pp.\ 50--51. These pages and
the numerical-range use in Claim 8.21, p.\ 52, were visually checked.
No fast implementation of the inner oracle or preconditioner construction
is imported by the following reduction.

Use the original capped objective $\Phi=E_{\rm cap}$ from
\cref{lem:op3-original-capped-coercivity}. Put
$\mathcal G=\gamma L$, $h(x)=\sum_iq_i(x_i)+I_{x\geq0}$ and
$\Phi(x)=x^\top\mathcal Gx/2+h(x)$. Thus $\Phi(0)=0$.
Assume $\epsappr d_v<1$ and a supplied graph Laplacian $\mathcal H$
obeying $\mathcal G\preceq\mathcal H\preceq\kappa\mathcal G$,
$\kappa\geq1$. Let $m$ be the original edge count, $m_H$ the supplied
preconditioner edge count, $C_0=1/(2\bar\alpha)$, and $\Phi^*=\min\Phi$.
The capped and original quadratic minima agree and $-\Phi^*\leq C_0$.

For a possibly infeasible center $x$, its model and normalized oracle
objective are
\begin{align}
 \Phi_x(y)&=\Phi(y)+\tfrac12(y-x)^\top
              (\mathcal H-\mathcal G)(y-x),\label{eq:op3-capped-prox-model}\\
 E_x(y)&=\Phi_x(y)-\Phi_x(0)
 =\tfrac12y^\top\mathcal Hy+
 \sum_i\bigl[q_i(y_i)+((\mathcal G-\mathcal H)x)_iy_i\bigr].
 \label{eq:op3-capped-prox-normalization}
\end{align}
Each new vertex function has the same two pieces, domain and zero value
at zero. Individual final slopes need not remain positive. Nevertheless
$\Phi_x\geq\Phi$ and the original coercivity imply existence of a
finite model minimum on $y\geq0$.

\begin{theorem}[An explicit tolerance for one original-capped run;
 \statusproved]
\label{thm:op3-explicit-capped-proximal-budget}
Let $a\geq0$ satisfy $\Phi(a)\leq0$ and let a known
$0<\underline\Delta\leq\Delta:=\Phi(a)-\Phi^*$ be supplied.
Choose the least power of two $T$ with $T^2\geq256\kappa$, and set
\begin{equation}
 R=\frac{\kappa}{\bar\alpha\lambda},\qquad
 e_0=\frac{\underline\Delta}{2^{30}\kappa^4},\qquad
 B=\frac92\kappa mR^2+C_0,\qquad \delta_{\rm rel}=e_0/B.
 \label{eq:op3-explicit-capped-proximal-policy}
\end{equation}
Run source Algorithm 9 from $y_0=z_0=a$ for $T$ iterations. At every
center $x$, obtain a feasible relative-oracle output satisfying
\begin{equation}
 E_x(q)\leq\frac{E_x^*}{1+\delta_{\rm rel}}.
 \label{eq:op3-capped-inner-relative-contract}
\end{equation}
Then every model error is at most $e_0$, every feasible iterate lies in
$[0,R]^n$, every center lies in $[-R,2R]^n$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(y_T)-\Phi^*\leq\Delta/32.
 \label{eq:op3-capped-run-contraction}
\end{equation}
All tolerances are determined before the calls they justify. Apart from
the oracle work, a run costs $O(T(n+m+m_H)+\log(2+\kappa))$ word work
and $O(Tn+\log(2+\kappa))$ newly allocated words. Supplied edges are
read-only input; every new iterate and normalized vertex record is charged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Convexity of $h$ and optimality of an optimum $u$ give
$\Phi(a)-\Phi(u)\geq\|a-u\|_{\mathcal G}^2/2$, and hence
$\|a-u\|_{\mathcal H}^2\leq2\kappa\Delta$.
The source convergence bound for a prefix $k\geq2$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(y_k)-\Phi^*\leq\frac2{(k+1)^2}
 \left(\|a-u\|_{\mathcal H}
       +3\sum_{j=1}^k j\sqrt{2e_j}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:op3-source-inexact-apg-bound}
\end{equation}
No Euclidean strong-convexity estimate is substituted for this Laplacian
seminorm. One may also obtain the seminorm version by applying the convex
smooth bound with $\mathcal H+\nu I$: for any fixed finite run, its same
oracle outputs have new errors at most
$e_j+\nu\|q_j-x_j\|_2^2/2$. Let $\nu\downarrow0$ in that bound.

The chosen integer satisfies $16\sqrt\kappa\leq T<32\sqrt\kappa$.
Since $\sum_{j=1}^Tj\leq528\kappa$ and
$e_0\leq\Delta/(2^{30}\kappa^4)$,
\[
 3\sum_{j=1}^T j\sqrt{2e_0}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{8\kappa}
 \leq\frac18\sqrt{\kappa\Delta}.
\]
For example the first inequality follows from
$1584\sqrt2<4096$. Thus any already justified prefix with
$e_j\leq e_0$ obeys
$\Phi(y_k)-\Phi^*\leq8\kappa\Delta/(k+1)^2$ for $k\geq2$.
At the first step $x_1=a$, and the model optimum is no greater than
$\Phi_a(a)=\Phi(a)$, so its error bound gives
$\Phi(y_1)-\Phi^*\leq\Delta+e_0\leq2\kappa\Delta$.
All such feasible iterates therefore have gap at most $2\kappa\Delta$.
Because $\Delta\leq-\Phi^*\leq C_0$, original coercivity then implies
\[
 \lambda\sum_i(y_k)_i
 \leq\Phi(y_k)+C_0
 \leq(2\kappa-1)(-\Phi^*)+C_0\leq2\kappa C_0.
\]
This is exactly the stated radius $R$; the initial point has the same bound.

It remains to justify each error before using this consequence. Write
$A_k=(k+1)/2$. The source updates imply, for $k\geq1$,
\[
 z_k=A_ky_k-(A_k-1)y_{k-1},\qquad
 x_{k+1}=y_k+\frac{k-1}{k+2}(y_k-y_{k-1}).
\]
Consequently previously bounded feasible iterates give the next center
in $[-R,2R]^n$; $x_1=a$ does also. Each original edge difference is at
most $3R$, and its weight $\gamma\leq1$, so
$\|x\|_{\mathcal H}^2\leq9\kappa mR^2$.

Put $e_x=\Phi_x(q)-\min\Phi_x$. Rearranging the normalized relative
contract, as in source equation (14), gives
\[
 0\leq e_x\leq\delta_{\rm rel}(\Phi_x(0)-\Phi_x(q))
 \leq\delta_{\rm rel}\bigl(\tfrac12\|x\|_{\mathcal H}^2+C_0\bigr)
 \leq e_0.
\]
Here $\Phi_x(q)\geq\Phi(q)\geq\Phi^*$, independently of the size
of the new output. This establishes the next error using only old bounds,
and the prefix estimate establishes the next feasible radius. Induction
therefore closes without a circular range assumption. Applying the prefix
estimate at $T$ gives $8\kappa\Delta/T^2\leq\Delta/32$.

Repeated doubling determines $T$ with no real-square-root oracle.
The two supplied sparse products and the vector updates cost
$O(n+m+m_H)$ per iteration. Linear shifts create constant-size records
for each of the two-piece vertex functions. Only constantly many current
vectors are needed, but every newly allocated vector across all iterations
is included in the stated total. The oracle's internal work, failed
attempts, retained state and output allocations are separate costs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A certified restart driver for original ACL accuracy;
 \statusproved]
\label{cor:op3-capped-certified-restart-driver}
Let $t=\bar\alpha(\epsappr/8)^2/2$ and
$\Delta_{\min}=\bar\alpha t/(1+\gamma)$. Start with $a=0$.
Compute the original quadratic certificate $C(a)$ from
\cref{thm:op3-local-gap-certificate}. If $C(a)\leq t$, return its
downward-clipped original ACL output. Otherwise run
\cref{thm:op3-explicit-capped-proximal-budget} with
$\underline\Delta=\Delta_{\min}$, box the result at $1/\bar\alpha$,
and use it as the next $a$. All runs use the same original capped
objective and supplied $\mathcal H$.

The driver terminates after at most
$J=\min\{j\geq0:C_0/32^j\leq\Delta_{\min}\}$ accelerated runs,
returning an original ACL certificate. In particular
$J=O(1+\log(1/\alpha)+\log(1/\epsappr))$, and
\[
 \log(1/\delta_{\rm rel})
 =O\bigl(1+\log(2+m)+\log(2+\kappa)
            +\log(1/\alpha)+\log(1/\epsappr)\bigr).
\]
If $W_s$ is all work of inner call $s$, the implemented certificate interface
gives total supplied-graph work
\[
 O\bigl(J\sqrt\kappa(n+m+m_H)
       +(J+1)(n+m)\log(2+n)\bigr)+\sum_s W_s.
\]
The corresponding driver allocations are $O(J\sqrt\kappa n+(J+1)(n+m))$,
in addition to input storage and every inner call's allocated state.
These expressions retain the entire inner-oracle cost and do not prove OP3.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every current $a$ lies in the cap box, so its capped and original quadratic
energies agree. A failed certificate and its gap sandwich imply
$\Phi(a)-\Phi^*>\bar\alpha t/(1+\gamma)=\Delta_{\min}$.
Boxing a run's output cannot increase its capped energy and transfers that
bound to the original quadratic. Thus each failed guard is followed by
a factor-$32$ gap contraction, and $\Phi(a)\leq0$ is preserved.
When the gap is at most $\Delta_{\min}$, the other side of the sandwich
forces $C(a)\leq t$. This proves the run bound and original ACL guarantee.
The parameter logarithms follow directly by substituting the displayed
positive scales into \eqref{eq:op3-explicit-capped-proximal-policy}.
There are at most $J+1$ certificate scans, including every failed guard,
$JT$ inner calls, and $J$ full boxing passes. The local certificate's
allocated records and reusable sort buffer are linear in scanned volume;
its comparisons and writes cost that volume times its label logarithm.
Bounding that volume by the supplied $n+m$ gives the stated accounting.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What is still missing.}
The outer restart policy avoids shifted residual domains at this level.
Its inner normalized objectives still have new linear terms and possibly
negative individual final slopes. A fast recursive solver for all these
calls, with compatible compression and error budgets, remains
\statusopen. So do a paid local construction of the input graph and a
bound on cumulative work when that graph changes. The original certificate
protects acceptance, but the finite-run bound assumes the inner relative
contracts; an inaccurate provider cannot be assigned the theorem's work
guarantee merely because a later certificate rejects its output.

\paragraph{Exact audit scope.}
\texttt{capped\_proximal\_budget.py} audits normalized model identities,
the explicit relative-to-absolute bound, original feasible sublevels,
infeasible centers, source extrapolation indices and final contraction.
Its dense capped piece enumeration includes vertices beyond the cap and
negative individual proximal final slopes. The driver audit
\texttt{capped\_restart\_driver.py} uses the implemented original local
certificate for every guard and final output, including tiny-tolerance
multiple-run cases. The inner candidates are exact reference optima plus
controlled perturbations; all reference solves and perturbation searches
are validators, not a fast inner oracle. Durable audit records give the
final complete counts, parameter profiles and source hashes.
