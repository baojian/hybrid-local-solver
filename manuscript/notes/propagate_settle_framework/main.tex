\begin{abstract}
Three independently useful empirical policies in this repository share one
shape: an aggressive local phase transports residual across a frontier, and a
later unit-relaxation phase removes the signed leftovers near the terminal
gate.  We formalize that shape as \emph{propagate--settle}.  For a fixed load
$\ell$ and region $U$, exact settlement is the restricted Dirichlet map
that holds $x_{U^c}$ fixed and zeros $(\ell-Qx)_U$.  This map is idempotent;
settlements on nested regions absorb one another; and, most importantly, it
erases every update trajectory supported inside $U$.  Hence two algorithms
that discover the same nested regions have identical exactly settled
iterates, regardless of their relaxation factors, rankings, or signed packet
histories.  Over-relaxation can improve an exactly settled method only by
changing region discovery or the work needed to obtain it.

The same algebra gives an exact certificate for approximate delivery:
if the remaining interior residual is $e_U$, its objective defect above exact
settlement is $e_U^TQ_{UU}^{-1}e_U/2$.  This makes repeated $\omega=1$
coordinate cleanup a measurable inexact settlement rather than an informal
``exact rung.''

For work, define charged volume
$\operatorname{cvol}(U)=\sum_{u\in U}(1+d_u)$ and the settled-volume revisit
factor
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 :=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^K\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)}
          {\operatorname{cvol}(U_K)}.
\]
If every propagation epoch scans its region $O(1)$ times and exact settlement
uses a width-$w$ ordering, total work is
$O((w+1)^2\mathfrak R_{\rm set}\operatorname{cvol}(U_K))$.
Applied to condition-free RPPR boundary expansion, this sharpens the earlier
batch-count ledger to
$O((w+1)^2\mathfrak R_{\rm set}/\rho)$.  Therefore the accelerated product
scale needs only
$\mathfrak R_{\rm set}=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$, not an accelerated bound
on the raw number of batches.  This is the theoretical counterpart of the
adaptive frontier solver's measured work-per-touched-support ratio and is a
strictly weaker target in thick cores where many early regions may be small.
That weaker target is nevertheless not automatic for the literal exact gate.
A four-vertex tailed triangle already has two activation batches in the same
root-distance shell.  More generally, a width-two fan core can force any
prescribed number $L$ of singleton batches among vertices all at distance one,
with $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}>(L+1)/5$.  Quantifying the construction gives a
logarithmic revisit lower bound in $1/\rho$.  Thus a log-free gate theorem is
false even at constant elimination width and support radius.  This does not
refute the polylogarithmic target above; it proves that its hidden dynamic-
range factor, or incremental Schur maintenance that avoids fresh settlement,
is necessary.

The obstruction is to repeated materialization, not to exact discovery
itself.  Cumulative settled volume has an exact activation-age dual: a vertex
is charged once for every settlement after it enters.  A block-Schur update
instead represents the old-coordinate correction lazily and updates every
remaining violation demand through one signed Schur block-column.  On the
tailed fan, retaining only the two-coordinate separator
$\{o,v_j\}$ reproduces the same first $L$ singleton batches and their exact
restricted solution in $O(m+L)$ work, versus $\Omega(mL)$ for fresh
settlement.  More generally, a charged online activation trace with frontal
size $\zeta$ and at most $\nu$ exact boundary-demand signatures admits a lazy
implementation in
$O((1+\zeta^2+\nu\zeta)\operatorname{cvol}(S)+\mathcal T_{\rm sep})$ work.
Exact signatures are not necessary: a kinetic threshold theorem gives an
$O(\operatorname{cvol}(S)\log R)$ exact solver on rooted spiders even when all
$R$ frontier responses differ, and certified response trees replace equality
by rigorous margin intervals.  Conversely, a constant-degree expander family
with full RPPR support forces $\zeta,\nu=\Omega(n)$ in every flat presentation.
Thus the structural condition cannot be removed graph-uniformly inside this
framework.  More sharply, expanders force quadratic storage for explicit
original-basis dense fronts, while sparse cyclic Stieltjes systems admit
arbitrarily light singleton cascades that force quadratic updates in eager
demand arrays.  Stable bounded block--cut presentations nevertheless give an
exact product-scale hierarchy for polylogarithmic block size.  The companion
\texttt{delayed\_reflection\_ladder} note removes the stable-metadata
assumption: activation at a zero coordinate preserves the consumed response
prefix, so an online block merge rebuilds only future pieces and retains the
same product scale under ordinary adjacency access.  A cutoff at
$\zeta=\alpha^{-1/4}$ pays the accelerated dense-algebra budget exactly.
Per-event shock-only continuation is itself refuted; nevertheless, all
numerical repair over an accuracy epoch collapses to one final accelerated
solve when responses are carried separately.  Arbitrary rooted trees meet
the product bound with a different exact trace.  For approximate output, a
finite-resolution theorem replaces exact zero signs by an $\alpha\tau$ KKT
band and reduces the product bound to certified band-response maintenance.
Uniform point estimates are unnecessary: certified one-sided intervals
suffice, and exact exterior response changes are $\ell_2$ contractions of the
primal energy shock.  Thus only energy-packed interval crossings need to be
located.  One tempting cyclic stress test does not reach that reporter
question: on the notched double sun, apart from the at-most-one pair containing
the seed, every frontier petal and its matching report leaf are exact
boundary-demand twins.  A canonical all-violations RPPR gate therefore admits
each such pair together, so the prescribed petal-only structural epoch is not
a single-seed RPPR trace.  A minimal edge-only degree asymmetrization also
fails.  Pairing the report leaves raises their degree from one to two and gives
a full-rank anchor--report cut, but exact KKT balance implies that any gate
batch which still excludes every report can add a frontier petal only at the
single seed anchor.  Thus the construction has varying potential attachment
directions but no legal linear report-free epoch.
Inserting the smallest two-edge report stem does not create that epoch.  The
degree-one petal has exact anchor threshold $2\alpha\rho/\vartheta$, while
the internal degree-two relay has threshold $4\alpha\rho/\vartheta$.
Therefore every nonseed relay is preceded or co-admitted by its petal in every
report-free canonical batch.  Once all relays are active, at most the one
petal paired with a positive relay seed can remain.  This is a chronology
obstruction before reporter algebra; reversing the local thresholds or
activating the relays from another backbone is necessary.
Putting the petals on their own cycle does reverse the isolated thresholds:
a relay can cross at $4\alpha\rho/\vartheta$ while a degree-three petal with
no active cycle neighbor waits until $6\alpha\rho/\vartheta$.  The full
canonical chronology still fails.  At a report-quiet face containing every
relay, the relay and report equations uniformly cap the neighboring anchor
coordinates.  If a nonseed anchor's petal were still inactive, its exact
anchor balance would require the sum of its two anchor-cycle neighbors to be
strictly larger than the sum of those two caps.  Hence all petals except
possibly the single anchor- or relay-seed site have already entered.  The
local window therefore supplies no later linear petal epoch and no sequence
on which varying-response reporter algebra can begin.
Feeding the relays from a second cycle does not repair the chronology.  In the
smallest double-cycle feed, a backbone cycle is matched sitewise to both the
anchor cycle and the report relays.  For every seed on the backbone and every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\rho>0$, a report-free canonical prefix contains no
petal at all.  The proof is a maximum principle for two normalized exact-face
coordinates: report quietness caps each active relay, which caps the maximum
active anchor below the strict petal threshold; the same cap makes every new
anchor admission find its relay already active or co-admit it in the same
all-violations batch.  Thus the first petal batch, if one exists, already
contains a report.  This is specific to backbone seeds and canonical
all-violations batching.  An anchor seed does give an exact report-free first
petal in a nonempty parameter range, but the complete canonical chronology
admits at most the three petals at the seed and its two neighboring sites
before the first report; either a report enters earlier or the next petal
layer co-admits the seed site's report.  The independent feed therefore also
stops before response algebra for that report-free target.  The last plausible
core orbit, a relay seed, is even shorter: depending on two exact thresholds,
its singleton face either terminates or its first propagating all-violations
batch contains the incident report, possibly together with the backbone
vertex.  This exhausts the backbone-, anchor-, and relay-seed candidates and
retires this double-cycle graph family for the prescribed long report-free
later-petal witness, without ruling out other cyclic witnesses or analyzing
any post-report trace.
Exact light response and its strictly weaker finite-resolution version remain
open on nonequitable cyclic cores.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact settlement on a fixed region}
\label{sec:settlement}

Fix a load $\ell\in\mathbb R^V$ and define the quadratic
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_\ell(x):=\frac12x^TQx-\ell^Tx,
 \qquad
 e_\ell(x):=\ell-Qx.
 \label{eq:load-quadratic}
\end{equation}
For $U\subseteq V$, let $\iota_U$ denote zero extension from
$\mathbb R^U$ to $\mathbb R^V$.

\begin{definition}[Dirichlet settlement]
\label{def:dirichlet-settlement}
The exact settlement of $x$ on $U$ is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_U^\ell(x)
 :=x+\iota_UQ_{UU}^{-1}e_\ell(x)_U.
 \label{eq:dirichlet-settlement}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, it is the unique minimizer of $\Psi_\ell$ over all $z$ with
$z_{U^c}=x_{U^c}$.
\end{definition}

The definition is always valid because $Q\succeq\alpha I$ makes every
principal block positive definite.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical form and exact energy drop]
\label{lem:canonical-settlement}
For every $x$ and $U$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_U^\ell(x)_{U^c}&=x_{U^c},
 \label{eq:settlement-outside}\\
 \mathcal E_U^\ell(x)_U
 &=Q_{UU}^{-1}\bigl(\ell_U-Q_{UU^c}x_{U^c}\bigr),
 \label{eq:settlement-canonical}\\
 e_\ell(\mathcal E_U^\ell(x))_U&=0,
 \label{eq:settlement-residual-zero}\\
 \Psi_\ell(x)-\Psi_\ell(\mathcal E_U^\ell(x))
 &=\frac12 e_\ell(x)_U^TQ_{UU}^{-1}e_\ell(x)_U.
 \label{eq:settlement-energy-drop}
\end{align}
In particular, the settled point depends on $x$ only through the outside
coordinates $x_{U^c}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $z_U=Q_{UU}^{-1}e_\ell(x)_U$.  The definition changes no outside
coordinate, and
\[
 x_U+z_U
 =x_U+Q_{UU}^{-1}
   (\ell_U-Q_{UU}x_U-Q_{UU^c}x_{U^c}),
\]
which proves \cref{eq:settlement-canonical}.  Multiplying that identity by
$Q_{UU}$ proves \cref{eq:settlement-residual-zero}.  Finally, the exact
quadratic expansion along $\iota_Uz_U$ gives
\[
 \Psi_\ell(x+\iota_Uz_U)-\Psi_\ell(x)
 =-e_\ell(x)_U^Tz_U+\frac12z_U^TQ_{UU}z_U,
\]
which is minus the right side of \cref{eq:settlement-energy-drop}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Settlement absorption law]
\label{thm:settlement-absorption}
For every fixed load $\ell$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item $\mathcal E_U^\ell\mathcal E_U^\ell=\mathcal E_U^\ell$.
 \item If $U\subseteq T$, then
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal E_T^\ell\mathcal E_U^\ell
  =\mathcal E_U^\ell\mathcal E_T^\ell
  =\mathcal E_T^\ell.
  \label{eq:nested-absorption}
 \end{equation}
 \item If $y-x$ is supported in $U$, then
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal E_U^\ell(y)=\mathcal E_U^\ell(x).
  \label{eq:trajectory-erasure}
 \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After one settlement the residual on $U$ is zero, so applying the same map
again changes nothing.  If $U\subseteq T$, settling $U$ changes no coordinate
outside $T$.  The canonical formula \cref{eq:settlement-canonical} says that
settlement on $T$ depends only on those unchanged outside coordinates, which
proves $\mathcal E_T^\ell\mathcal E_U^\ell=\mathcal E_T^\ell$.
Conversely, the $T$-settled point has zero residual throughout $T$, hence
also on $U$, so the later $U$-settlement does nothing.  The last statement is
the same canonical-form argument: $x$ and $y$ agree on $U^c$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the precise sense in which settlement forgets propagation.
The propagation may be over-relaxed, ranked, asynchronous, or represented as
signed directed packets.  If all its coordinate changes remain inside the
region that is subsequently settled, none of those choices changes the
settled point.

\begin{corollary}[Immediate and block cleanup are one phenomenon]
\label{cor:cleanup-unification}
An over-relaxed coordinate push at $u$ followed immediately by exact
settlement on $\{u\}$ equals one unit-relaxation push.  More generally, every
finite sequence of coordinate updates supported in $U$, followed by exact
settlement on $U$, equals direct settlement of the pre-sequence iterate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{eq:trajectory-erasure} with $U=\{u\}$ or with the full update
support $U$.
\end{proof}

\section{Inexact delivery has an exact defect certificate}
\label{sec:inexact}

The empirical ``exact rung'' performs repeated $\omega=1$ row operations; it
does not call a block solver.  The following identity states exactly how
close such a phase is to mathematical settlement.

\begin{theorem}[Residual-to-settlement defect identity]
\label{thm:inexact-settlement}
Let $y$ be any point and $\bar y:=\mathcal E_U^\ell(y)$.  Put
$e_U:=e_\ell(y)_U$.  Then
\begin{align}
 \bar y_U-y_U&=Q_{UU}^{-1}e_U,
 \label{eq:inexact-point-defect}\\
 \Psi_\ell(y)-\Psi_\ell(\bar y)
 &=\frac12e_U^TQ_{UU}^{-1}e_U
 \leq\frac1{2\alpha}\|e_U\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:inexact-objective-defect}
\end{align}
If
$|e_u|/\sqrt{d_u}\leq\eta$ for every $u\in U$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_\ell(y)-\Psi_\ell(\bar y)
 \leq\frac{\eta^2\vol(U)}{2\alpha}.
 \label{eq:degree-gate-settlement-defect}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first two displays are
\cref{eq:dirichlet-settlement,eq:settlement-energy-drop}.  Since
$Q_{UU}\succeq\alpha I$,
$Q_{UU}^{-1}\preceq\alpha^{-1}I$.  The coordinatewise hypothesis gives
$\|e_U\|_2^2\leq\eta^2\sum_{u\in U}d_u$.
\end{proof}

Thus a delivery rung supplies two independent objects: a terminal residual
certificate for the verifier and a quantitative settlement defect inside its
touched region.  The first does not automatically imply the second with a
small graph-independent total error unless the touched volume is also
controlled; \cref{eq:degree-gate-settlement-defect} exposes that dependence.

\section{The propagate--settle quotient principle}
\label{sec:quotient}

Let
\begin{equation}
 U_0\subseteq U_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_K
 \label{eq:nested-regions}
\end{equation}
be regions discovered online.  Starting from $x^{-1}$, a general exact
propagate--settle process forms an arbitrary provisional point $y^k$ with
\begin{equation}
 \supp(y^k-x^{k-1})\subseteq U_k,
 \qquad
 x^k:=\mathcal E_{U_k}^\ell(y^k).
 \label{eq:propagate-settle-epoch}
\end{equation}
The provisional update may include many coordinate pushes and any residual
split used only for scheduling.

\begin{theorem}[Region-sequence quotient]
\label{thm:region-sequence-quotient}
Every process satisfying
\cref{eq:nested-regions,eq:propagate-settle-epoch} obeys
\begin{equation}
 x^k=\mathcal E_{U_k}^\ell(x^{-1})
 \qquad(0\leq k\leq K).
 \label{eq:canonical-settled-trajectory}
\end{equation}
Consequently, two exact propagate--settle algorithms with the same initial
outside state and the same region sequence have identical settled iterates
and identical post-settlement boundary residuals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct on $k$.  The update from $x^{k-1}$ to $y^k$ is supported in $U_k$, so
trajectory erasure gives
$x^k=\mathcal E_{U_k}^\ell(x^{k-1})$.  For $k=0$, $x^{-1}$ and $x^0$ agree
outside $U_0$, giving the display.  For $k>0$, the induction hypothesis and
nested absorption give
\[
 x^k
 =\mathcal E_{U_k}^\ell
  \mathcal E_{U_{k-1}}^\ell(x^{-1})
 =\mathcal E_{U_k}^\ell(x^{-1}).
\]
The boundary residual is a deterministic function of the settled point.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Where acceleration can enter]
\label{cor:discovery-only-acceleration}
For an exactly settled algorithm, changing $\omega$, ranking, batching, or
the fresh/debt representation while preserving the same discovered regions
cannot improve the settled objective values or terminal point.  A genuine
gain must change at least one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item which regions are discovered;
 \item how soon they are discovered;
 \item the charged work of discovering them; or
 \item the accuracy and work of an inexact settlement.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

This corollary explains both the empirical success and the theoretical
limit of two rungs.  The over-relaxed phase is useful only because it changes
the frontier seen before delivery.  If delivery were placed immediately
after every spread update on the same coordinate or fixed region, the
absorption law would reduce the method to ordinary exact updates.

\section{A cumulative-volume work theorem}
\label{sec:work}

The raw number of epochs is too coarse.  A hundred tiny early regions may
cost less than two full-support passes.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}(U_{0:K})
 :=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^K\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)}
          {\operatorname{cvol}(U_K)}.
 \label{eq:settled-revisit-factor}
\end{equation}
It always lies in $[1,K+1]$, but may be much smaller than $K+1$.

\begin{theorem}[Propagate--settle work ledger]
\label{thm:propagate-settle-work}
Suppose epoch $k$ satisfies the following measured conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item propagation, ranking, and boundary inspection cost at most
 $C_{\rm p}\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)$;
 \item exact settlement and its boundary update cost at most
 $C_{\rm s}(w_k+1)^2\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)$.
\end{enumerate}
Writing $w:=\max_kw_k$, total charged work obeys
\begin{align}
 \Work_{0:K}
 &\leq
 \bigl(C_{\rm p}+C_{\rm s}(w+1)^2\bigr)
 \sum_{k=0}^K\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)
 \notag\\
 &=O\!\left(
  (w+1)^2\mathfrak R_{\rm set}
  \operatorname{cvol}(U_K)
 \right).
 \label{eq:propagate-settle-work}
\end{align}
This is a trajectory theorem: no bound on $K$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Add the two stated costs over all epochs and factor out their maxima.  The
second line is the definition of $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two useful revisit regimes]
\label{cor:revisit-regimes}
The ledger has the following immediate consequences.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item If there are at most $K+1$ settlements, then
 $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}\leq K+1$.  In particular an alpha-scaled ladder with
 $K=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ reaches the accelerated revisit scale.
 \item If for some $0<\gamma<1$ the nested volumes satisfy
 \begin{equation}
  \operatorname{cvol}(U_k)
  \leq\gamma^{K-k}\operatorname{cvol}(U_K),
  \label{eq:geometric-volume-growth}
 \end{equation}
 then $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}$, independent of $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first claim uses monotonicity of charged volume.  The second sums a
geometric series.
\end{proof}

The second regime is why batch count is not the right remaining conjecture.
Many activation events are harmless if they occur while the settled region is
still small.  Conversely, repeatedly revisiting a nearly final region is
expensive even if the event count is only moderately large.

\section{Condition-free RPPR gate with the sharper ledger}
\label{sec:rppr-gate}

For the single-seed RPPR problem, assume the seed is $o$.  If
$(\ell_\rho)_o\leq0$, zero satisfies the KKT conditions.  Otherwise start
$U_0=\{o\}$.  At stage $k$, compute the shifted Dirichlet settlement
\begin{equation}
 Q_{U_kU_k}x^k_{U_k}=(\ell_\rho)_{U_k},
 \qquad x^k_{U_k^c}=0,
 \label{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement}
\end{equation}
and add every boundary violation
\begin{equation}
 B_k:=\left\{
  v\in\partial U_k:
  b_v-(Qx^k)_v>\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}
 \right\},
 \qquad U_{k+1}:=U_k\cup B_k.
 \label{eq:boundary-violation-gate}
\end{equation}
Stop when $B_k$ is empty.

\begin{theorem}[Safe exact boundary expansion]
\label{thm:safe-boundary-expansion}
Every nonempty batch in \cref{eq:boundary-violation-gate} contains only
vertices of $S^\star(\rho)$.  The restricted minimizers are coordinatewise
nondecreasing and bounded above by $x^\star(\rho)$.  The process terminates
at $x^\star(\rho)$ after finitely many batches.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first prove inductively that $U_k\subseteq S^\star$,
$x^k_{U_k}>0$, and $0\leq x^k\leq x^\star$.  At
$U_0=\{o\}$, the inequality $(\ell_\rho)_o>0$ makes $x_o^0>0$; it also
forces $o\in S^\star$, since an inactive root would have optimum KKT gradient
at most $-(\ell_\rho)_o<0$.  Suppose the claims hold at stage $k$ and
write $B=B_k$.  The Schur complement of $Q_{U_kU_k}$ in
$Q_{U_k\cup B,U_k\cup B}$ is a positive-definite Stieltjes matrix.  The
right-hand side obtained after eliminating $U_k$ is
\[
 (\ell_\rho)_B-Q_{BU_k}x^k_{U_k}>0
\]
coordinatewise by the definition of $B$.  Inverse positivity therefore makes
every new coordinate strictly positive, and back-substitution can only
increase the old coordinates.  Thus $x^{k+1}\geq x^k\geq0$.

Since $U_k\subseteq S^\star$ by the induction proved below, the active optimum
equations and $Q_{U_k,U_k^c}\leq0$ give
\[
 Q_{U_kU_k}x^\star_{U_k}
 =(\ell_\rho)_{U_k}-Q_{U_k,U_k^c}x^\star_{U_k^c}
 \geq(\ell_\rho)_{U_k}.
\]
Multiplication by $Q_{U_kU_k}^{-1}\geq0$ proves
$x^k\leq x^\star$ on $U_k$, and both sides are nonnegative outside it.

Now consider a violating $v\notin U_k$.  If $x_v^\star=0$, then, using
nonpositive off-diagonal entries and $x^k\leq x^\star$,
\[
 (Qx^\star-\ell_\rho)_v
 \leq (Qx^k-\ell_\rho)_v<0,
\]
contradicting the inactive KKT inequality at $x^\star$.  Hence every added
vertex belongs to the true support.

If no boundary vertex violates, the active KKT equalities hold on $U_k$ by
\cref{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement}, and the inactive inequalities hold on
its boundary: the upper residual bound is the stopping test, while
$b_v-(Qx^k)_v\geq0$ follows from $b_v=0$, $Q_{vU_k}\leq0$, and
$x^k_{U_k}>0$.  Every nonseed coordinate outside
$U_k\cup\partial U_k$ has no edge to $U_k$, value zero, and load
$(\ell_\rho)_v=-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}<0$, so its inactive KKT inequality also
holds.  The seed already lies in $U_k$.  Thus the global KKT conditions hold
and uniqueness gives $x^k=x^\star$.  Otherwise at least one new true-support
vertex is added, so finiteness follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Revisit-volume RPPR bound]
\label{thm:rppr-revisit-bound}
Let $U_0,\ldots,U_K=S^\star(\rho)$ be the realized regions of exact boundary
expansion.  If each restricted settlement and boundary update is computed
with a supplied width-$w$ elimination ordering, then
\begin{align}
 \Work_{\rm gate}
 &=O\!\left((w+1)^2
   \sum_{k=0}^K\vol(U_k)\right)
 \notag\\
 &=O\!\left(
  \frac{(w+1)^2\mathfrak R_{\rm set}}{\rho}
 \right).
 \label{eq:rppr-revisit-work}
\end{align}
Consequently the sufficient target for the intended product scale is
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 =\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha),
 \label{eq:accelerated-revisit-target}
\end{equation}
which is strictly weaker than requiring only
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ nonempty batches.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A width-$w$ factorization, solve, and boundary update on $U_k$ costs
$O((w+1)^2\vol(U_k))$.  Sum this actual stage cost rather than replacing each
volume by the final one.  By
\cref{eq:charged-volume-equivalence}, the charged-volume and degree-volume
versions of $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$ differ only by an absolute factor.  The
source support bound $\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$ gives the second line.
Substituting \cref{eq:accelerated-revisit-target} proves the product scale.
Since $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}\leq K+1$, a batch-count bound implies the revisit
bound, while the converse need not hold when early volumes are small.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not assume thin radial support and does not claim that
\cref{eq:accelerated-revisit-target} is already graph-universal.  It replaces
the open raw batch-count conjecture by a weaker amortized statement that is
closer to what the empirical algorithms measure.

\section{A same-shell cascade obstruction}
\label{sec:fan-obstruction}

The first tempting route to \cref{eq:accelerated-revisit-target} is to charge
one gate batch to one new root-distance shell.  That statement is false on the
smallest nontrivial cyclic example.

\begin{proposition}[A tailed triangle activates twice in one shell]
\label{prop:tailed-triangle}
Let the vertices be $o,u,v,z$, with edges
\begin{equation}
 \{o,u\},\quad\{o,v\},\quad\{u,v\},\quad\{v,z\},
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-edges}
\end{equation}
and seed $o$.  At
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\frac3{20},
 \qquad
 \rho=\frac1{10},
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-parameters}
\end{equation}
the exact boundary gate first admits $u$ and then admits $v$, although
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{dist}(o,u)=\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=1.
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-same-shell}
\end{equation}
Hence activation time is not bounded by root distance.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The degrees in the order $(o,u,v,z)$ are $(2,2,3,1)$.  Write
$g(x):=Qx-\ell_\rho$, so a boundary coordinate violates exactly when its
slack is negative.  On $U_0=\{o\}$, direct settlement gives
\begin{equation}
 x_o^0=\frac{24}{115\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-first-value}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the two boundary slacks gives
\begin{equation}
 g_u(x^0)=-\frac{33\sqrt2}{4600}<0,
 \qquad
 g_v(x^0)=\frac{\sqrt3}{4600}>0.
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-first-slacks}
\end{equation}
Thus $B_0=\{u\}$.  Settlement on $U_1=\{o,u\}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 x_o^1=\frac{334\sqrt2}{3045},
 \qquad
 x_u^1=\frac{44\sqrt2}{3045},
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-second-values}
\end{equation}
and now
\begin{equation}
 g_v(x^1)=-\frac{21\sqrt3}{8120}<0.
 \label{eq:tailed-triangle-second-slack}
\end{equation}
Therefore $B_1=\{v\}$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

The phenomenon is not bounded by a constant.  Define the tailed fan
$\mathcal F_m$ on
\begin{equation}
 V_m:=\{o,v_1,\ldots,v_m,z\}
 \label{eq:fan-vertices}
\end{equation}
by joining $o$ to every $v_i$, joining $v_i$ to $v_{i+1}$ for
$1\leq i<m$, and joining $v_m$ to $z$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 d_o=m,\qquad d_{v_1}=2,\qquad
 d_{v_i}=3\ (2\leq i\leq m),\qquad d_z=1.
 \label{eq:fan-degrees}
\end{equation}
Every $v_i$ lies in the first root-distance shell.  The pendant $z$ removes
the second degree-two endpoint that would otherwise start a symmetric cascade.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrarily long same-shell fan cascade]
\label{thm:fan-cascade}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  For every integer $L\geq1$, there exist
$m>L$ and $\rho>0$ such that the exact boundary gate on $\mathcal F_m$ has
the first $L$ nonempty batches
\begin{equation}
 B_0=\{v_1\},\quad B_1=\{v_2\},\quad\ldots,\quad
 B_{L-1}=\{v_L\}.
 \label{eq:fan-singleton-batches}
\end{equation}
Every intermediate region has elimination width at most two, the entire graph
has root radius two, and the realized revisit factor satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak R_{\rm set}>\frac{L+1}{5}.
 \label{eq:fan-revisit-lower}
\end{equation}
Consequently, no bound on the literal gate's revisit factor can depend on
$\alpha$ alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the scaling
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\frac{\vartheta}{m},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_0:=\frac{1-\alpha}{4+2\alpha},
 \label{eq:fan-critical-scaling}
\end{equation}
and consider a fixed prefix length while $m\to\infty$.  Write
$x_o=X/\sqrt m$ and $x_{v_i}=Y_i/m$.  The settled root equation converges to
\begin{equation}
 X=\frac{2\alpha(1-\vartheta)}{1+\alpha}.
 \label{eq:fan-limit-root}
\end{equation}
For a root neighbor of degree $d$, the scaled shifted demand after the root
contribution is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_d(\vartheta)
 :=\alpha\left[
  \frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\vartheta)}{(1+\alpha)\sqrt d}
  -\vartheta\sqrt d
 \right].
 \label{eq:fan-limit-demand}
\end{equation}
The critical value in \cref{eq:fan-critical-scaling} is chosen so that
\begin{equation}
 \psi_3(\vartheta_0)=0,
 \qquad
 \psi_2(\vartheta_0)
 =\frac{\alpha\vartheta_0}{\sqrt2}>0.
 \label{eq:fan-critical-demands}
\end{equation}

Suppose the active fan prefix is $v_1,\ldots,v_j$.  In the same limit, its
scaled coordinates solve a tridiagonal Stieltjes system $T_jY=p$, with
diagonal
\begin{equation}
 a:=\frac{1+\alpha}{2},
 \label{eq:fan-core-diagonal}
\end{equation}
first off-diagonal magnitude
\begin{equation}
 h_1:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt6},
 \label{eq:fan-first-coupling}
\end{equation}
all later off-diagonal magnitudes
\begin{equation}
 h:=\frac{1-\alpha}{6},
 \label{eq:fan-core-coupling}
\end{equation}
and right-hand side
\begin{equation}
 p=\bigl(\psi_2(\vartheta),
          \psi_3(\vartheta),\ldots,
          \psi_3(\vartheta)\bigr)^T.
 \label{eq:fan-core-load}
\end{equation}
At $\vartheta=\vartheta_0$, this is a positive multiple of $e_1$.
The irreducible Stieltjes inverse $T_j^{-1}$ is strictly positive, so the
last coordinate $Y_j^{(j)}$ is positive for every fixed $j$.

Here $\psi_d$ is the scaled violation demand
$m(\ell_\rho-Qx)$ before contributions from active path neighbors.  At the
critical value, a remote inactive degree-three vertex therefore has limiting
violation demand zero, whereas the next path vertex has limiting violation
demand
\begin{equation}
 \begin{cases}
  h_1Y_1^{(1)},&j=1,\\
  hY_j^{(j)},&j\geq2,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:fan-next-margin}
\end{equation}
which is strictly positive.  There are only finitely many margins for
$j<L$.  By continuity, one may choose
$\vartheta>\vartheta_0$ sufficiently close that
\begin{equation}
 \psi_3(\vartheta)<0,
 \qquad
 \psi_3(\vartheta)+h_jY_j^{(j)}(\vartheta)>0
 \quad(1\leq j<L),
 \label{eq:fan-separated-signs}
\end{equation}
where $h_j=h_1$ for $j=1$ and $h_j=h$ otherwise; also
$\psi_2(\vartheta)>0$.  Thus the degree-two endpoint wants to enter, every
remote degree-three coordinate does not, and precisely the next path vertex
wants to enter.

For every fixed $j$, the finite-$m$ scaled restricted system converges
entrywise to the displayed root equation and $T_j$ system.  All inequalities
in \cref{eq:fan-separated-signs} are strict, so one sufficiently large
$m>L$ preserves them simultaneously for $j=0,\ldots,L-1$.  Induction then
gives \cref{eq:fan-singleton-batches}.

Eliminating $z,v_m,v_{m-1},\ldots,v_1$ leaves at most the root and one path
neighbor after every pivot, so the fan and every active prefix have width at
most two.  All $v_i$ have root distance one and $z$ has distance two.
Finally, every region $U_0,\ldots,U_L$ contains the root and hence has
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{cvol}(U_j)\geq m+1.
\end{equation*}
The final support is contained in $V_m$, while
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{cvol}(V_m)
 =|V_m|+2|E_m|=(m+2)+4m=5m+2.
\end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 \geq\frac{(L+1)(m+1)}{5m+2}
 >\frac{L+1}{5}.
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A logarithmic dynamic-range loss is intrinsic]
\label{cor:fan-logarithmic-loss}
For every fixed $\alpha\in(0,1)$, the construction in
\cref{thm:fan-cascade} can be chosen so that
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 =\Omega_\alpha\!\left(\log\frac1\rho\right).
 \label{eq:fan-log-revisit}
\end{equation}
Hence a fresh-refactor-and-rescan implementation of the exact gate can require
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_\alpha\!\left(
  \frac1\rho\log\frac1\rho
 \right)
 \label{eq:fan-log-work}
\end{equation}
degree work even on width-two, radius-two instances.  The statement does not
refute a $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ theorem; it proves that a hidden
dynamic-range logarithm or an incremental settlement interface is necessary.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At $\vartheta_0$, forward elimination of
$T_jY=\psi_2(\vartheta_0)e_1$ has positive pivots at most $a$.
Consequently the endpoint margin in
\cref{eq:fan-next-margin} is bounded below by
\begin{equation}
 C_\alpha\left(\frac h a\right)^j
 \label{eq:fan-margin-decay}
\end{equation}
for a constant $C_\alpha>0$.  Choose
$\vartheta-\vartheta_0$ as a sufficiently small constant multiple of the
minimum margin through $L$.  The $T_j$ are uniformly strictly diagonally
dominant, so their inverse norms and the $\vartheta$-derivatives of all the
displayed demands are bounded in terms of $\alpha$ alone.  The finite-$m$
scaled root feedback and coefficient perturbation for a prefix of length at
most $L$ are $O_\alpha(L/m)$.  It is therefore sufficient to take
\begin{equation}
 m=O_\alpha\!\left(
  L\left(\frac a h\right)^L
 \right).
 \label{eq:fan-size-choice}
\end{equation}
Since $\rho=\vartheta/m$ and $\vartheta$ remains bounded away from zero,
\cref{eq:fan-size-choice} gives
$\log(1/\rho)=O_\alpha(L)$.  Combine this with
\cref{eq:fan-revisit-lower}.

For the work statement, each fresh stage scans or refactors a region
containing the degree-$m$ root and therefore costs $\Omega(m)$.  The chosen
$\rho$ satisfies $m=\Theta_\alpha(1/\rho)$ up to the harmless variation of
$\vartheta$, so the first $L$ stages give \cref{eq:fan-log-work}.
\end{proof}

The fan separates three statements that should not be conflated.  Support
radius can be constant while gate time is unbounded; constant elimination
width does not help if the same region is freshly settled after every event;
and the polylogarithmic target remains viable precisely because the cascade
margin decays geometrically.  This points away from a log-free batch theorem
and toward either a margin-aware amortization or retained Schur state.

\section{Activation age and lazy Schur settlement}
\label{sec:lazy-schur}

The revisit factor admits a dual expression that makes the fan obstruction
transparent.  For a nested trace
$U_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_K$, define the activation time
\begin{equation}
 \tau(v):=\min\{k:v\in U_k\}
 \qquad(v\in U_K).
 \label{eq:activation-time}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Activation-age identity]
\label{prop:activation-age}
Every nested trace satisfies
\begin{align}
 \sum_{k=0}^K\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)
 &=
 \sum_{v\in U_K}(1+d_v)\bigl(K-\tau(v)+1\bigr),
 \label{eq:activation-age-sum}\\
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 &=
 \sum_{v\in U_K}
 \frac{1+d_v}{\operatorname{cvol}(U_K)}
 \bigl(K-\tau(v)+1\bigr).
 \label{eq:activation-age-mean}
\end{align}
Thus $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$ is the final-charge-weighted mean number of
fresh settlements paid after activation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A fixed $v\in U_K$ appears in exactly the regions
$U_{\tau(v)},\ldots,U_K$.  Exchange the order of summation in
$\sum_k\sum_{v\in U_k}(1+d_v)$ and divide by
$\operatorname{cvol}(U_K)$.
\end{proof}

For the fan, the root has activation time zero and charge $m+1$, a constant
fraction of the full graph charge.  Its long lifetime alone yields
\cref{eq:fan-revisit-lower}.  An incremental method should therefore avoid
charging persistent coordinates merely because a new coordinate entered.
The exact algebraic interface is the following block update.

\begin{lemma}[Lazy block-Schur settlement]
\label{lem:lazy-block-schur}
Let $x$ be the shifted Dirichlet solution on $U$, with $x_{U^c}=0$, and let
$B\subseteq U^c$ be a nonempty block.  Put
\begin{equation}
 A:=Q_{UU},
 \qquad E:=Q_{UB},
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_B:=Q_{BB}-E^TA^{-1}E,
 \qquad
 q_B:=e_\ell(x)_B.
 \label{eq:lazy-schur-data}
\end{equation}
The exact shifted Dirichlet solution $x^+$ on $U\cup B$ is
\begin{align}
 x_B^+&=\mathcal S_B^{-1}q_B,
 \label{eq:lazy-schur-new-block}\\
 x_U^+&=x_U-A^{-1}Ex_B^+.
 \label{eq:lazy-schur-old-block}
\end{align}
For every remaining inactive block
$C\subseteq V\setminus(U\cup B)$,
\begin{align}
 e_\ell(x^+)_C
 &=e_\ell(x)_C-\mathcal S_{CB}x_B^+,
 \label{eq:lazy-schur-demand-update}\\
 \mathcal S_{CB}
 &:=Q_{CB}-Q_{CU}A^{-1}E\leq0.
 \label{eq:lazy-schur-cross-block}
\end{align}
Moreover, $\mathcal S_B$ is Stieltjes,
$\mathcal S_B^{-1}\geq0$, and $-A^{-1}E\geq0$.  In particular, if
$q_B>0$, then the new block is positive, the old coordinates only increase,
and every remaining violation demand can only increase.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The enlarged active equations are
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{bmatrix}A&E\\E^T&Q_{BB}\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}x_U^+\\x_B^+\end{bmatrix}
 =
 \begin{bmatrix}\ell_U\\\ell_B\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The first row and $Ax_U=\ell_U$ give
\cref{eq:lazy-schur-old-block}.  Substitution in the second row gives
\cref{eq:lazy-schur-new-block}.  For an inactive block $C$,
\begin{align*}
 e_\ell(x^+)_C
 &=\ell_C-Q_{CU}x_U^+-Q_{CB}x_B^+\\
 &=e_\ell(x)_C-
   \bigl(Q_{CB}-Q_{CU}A^{-1}E\bigr)x_B^+,
\end{align*}
which is \cref{eq:lazy-schur-demand-update}.  Principal Schur complements of
a Stieltjes matrix are Stieltjes.  Their off-diagonal blocks are
nonpositive, and inverse positivity gives all remaining signs.  Since the
diagonal of $\mathcal S_B^{-1}$ is positive, $q_B>0$ also gives
$x_B^+>0$ coordinatewise.
\end{proof}

The dense response $-A^{-1}E$ need not be written into every old coordinate.
It may be stored as an elimination response, while
\cref{eq:lazy-schur-demand-update} updates only the boundary event
representation.  Old coordinates are recovered by one reverse substitution
after the final event.  This changes the implementation question from
\emph{how many old regions were settled?} to \emph{how compactly can the
signed Schur block-columns be represented and queried?}

\begin{theorem}[The fan cascade has an output-linear exact Schur trace]
\label{thm:fan-lazy-trace}
Use the parameters of \cref{thm:fan-cascade}, and consider its first $L$
singleton batches.  There is an exact implementation that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item produces the identical batches
 $B_0=\{v_1\},\ldots,B_{L-1}=\{v_L\}$;
 \item represents every intermediate shifted Dirichlet solution exactly; and
 \item recovers $x^L$ on $U_L=\{o,v_1,\ldots,v_L\}$ exactly
\end{enumerate}
using $O(m+L)=O(\operatorname{cvol}(U_L))$ degree work and arithmetic.
In contrast, materializing and rescanning every fresh settlement through
$U_L$ costs $\Omega(mL)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scan the degree-$m$ root list once.  Its degree metadata identifies the unique
degree-two neighbor $v_1$; thereafter one scan of each admitted $v_j$
identifies the next path neighbor $v_{j+1}$.  At stage $j\geq1$, retain the
separator
\begin{equation}
 Z_j:=\{o,v_j\},
 \qquad
 I_j:=U_j\setminus Z_j,
 \label{eq:fan-separator}
\end{equation}
and the exact reduced system obtained after eliminating $I_j$:
\begin{align}
 \widehat Q_j
 &:=
 Q_{Z_jZ_j}
 -Q_{Z_jI_j}Q_{I_jI_j}^{-1}Q_{I_jZ_j},
 \label{eq:fan-reduced-matrix}\\
 \widehat\ell_j
 &:=
 (\ell_\rho)_{Z_j}
 -Q_{Z_jI_j}Q_{I_jI_j}^{-1}(\ell_\rho)_{I_j}.
 \label{eq:fan-reduced-load}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 x^j_{Z_j}=\widehat Q_j^{-1}\widehat\ell_j.
 \label{eq:fan-separator-solution}
\end{equation}
For $j=0$, the retained system is the scalar root equation.

The next vertex $v_{j+1}$ is adjacent to the old active region only through
$Z_j$.  Append it to the reduced system, producing a matrix of order at most
three, and eliminate $v_j$ to obtain
$(\widehat Q_{j+1},\widehat\ell_{j+1})$.  Store the eliminated pivot and its
two multipliers for the final reverse pass.  Each append, elimination, and
separator solve uses $O(1)$ arithmetic, and scanning the newly exposed fan
vertex costs at most three.

It remains to show that no repeated root scan is needed to reproduce the
gate.  For a degree-$d$ root neighbor with no active path neighbor, define
\begin{equation}
 \eta_d^{(j)}
 :=
 -\alpha\rho\sqrt d
 +\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{dm}}\,x_o^j.
 \label{eq:fan-live-remote-demand}
\end{equation}
Every remote $v_i$ with $i\geq j+2$ has the identical exact violation demand
$\eta_3^{(j)}$.  The unique next path vertex has exact demand
\begin{equation}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^j)_{v_{j+1}}
 =
 \begin{cases}
  \eta_2^{(0)},&j=0,\\
  \eta_3^{(1)}+h_1x_{v_1}^1,&j=1,\\
  \eta_3^{(j)}+hx_{v_j}^j,&j\geq2,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:fan-live-next-demand}
\end{equation}
where $h_1$ and $h$ are from
\cref{eq:fan-first-coupling,eq:fan-core-coupling}.  Hence the exact gate
decision at each of the first $L$ stages uses only the two separator
coordinates and two scalar comparisons.  The strict inequalities proved in
\cref{thm:fan-cascade} therefore reproduce the same singleton batches.

The initial root scan costs $m$.  The $L$ local scans, constant-size Schur
updates, and final reverse recovery cost $O(L)$.  Since $m>L$ and
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{cvol}(U_L)=m+4L,
\end{equation*}
this is output-linear.  Fresh settlement, by contrast, charges at least the
root's $m+1$ work units at each of the $L+1$ stages, giving
$\Omega(mL)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Activation-aligned separator traces}
\label{sec:activation-aligned-traces}

The fan proof uses two properties that are more general than its symmetry.
First, already processed coordinates are separated from the live boundary by
a small retained interface.  Second, boundary vertices fall into a small
number of classes whose exact violation demands agree.  The next definition
isolates precisely these two properties.

For a zero-exterior Dirichlet trace
$U_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_K$, let
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_k:=\partial U_k,
 \qquad
 B_k:=\{v\in\Gamma_k:e_\ell(x^k)_v>0\},
 \qquad
 U_{k+1}:=U_k\cup B_k,
 \label{eq:aligned-gate-trace}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{U_kU_k}x^k_{U_k}=\ell_{U_k}$ and
$x^k_{U_k^c}=0$.  Thus $B_k$ is the exact gate batch, not a proposed
surrogate.

We use a unit-cost exact-arithmetic adjacency-list model in this subsection.
Once a vertex identifier is exposed, its degree and load can be queried in
$O(1)$ work; scanning its neighbors still costs its degree.  Exact signature
keys have a canonical representation with unit-cost equality and hashing.
For normalized RPPR this can be implemented by a discrete key containing the
degree/load token and the labeled neighborhood inside $Z_k$.  Thus the theorem
below is an exact-real-RAM statement, not a finite-bit complexity theorem.

\begin{definition}[Online activation-aligned separator presentation]
\label{def:activation-aligned-presentation}
An exact trace \cref{eq:aligned-gate-trace} has a charged online
$(\zeta,\nu)$ activation-aligned separator presentation if it is equipped
with a uniform transcript-measurable update algorithm which, from the graph
exposed through $U_k$, maintains a partition
\begin{equation}
 U_k=P_k\mathbin{\dot\cup}Z_k
 \label{eq:processed-separator-partition}
\end{equation}
with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item Initially $P_0=\varnothing$, and after admitting $B_k$ the next
 separator is chosen online so that
 \begin{equation}
  Z_{k+1}\subseteq Z_k\cup B_k,
  \qquad
  P_{k+1}=P_k\cup\bigl((Z_k\cup B_k)\setminus Z_{k+1}\bigr).
  \label{eq:aligned-retirement}
 \end{equation}
 \item The retained separator shields the current frontier from every
 retired coordinate:
 \begin{equation}
  Q_{\Gamma_kP_k}=0.
  \label{eq:frontier-separation}
 \end{equation}
 \item The live frontal system is bounded:
 \begin{equation}
  |Z_k\cup B_k|\leq\zeta,
  \label{eq:aligned-frontal-size}
 \end{equation}
 where $B_K:=\varnothing$ at termination.
 \item At stage $k$, the frontier equivalence relation
 \begin{equation}
  v\sim_k w
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \ell_v=\ell_w
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  Q_{vZ_k}=Q_{wZ_k}
  \label{eq:boundary-signature}
 \end{equation}
 has at most $\nu$ classes.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\mathcal T_{\rm sep}$ be the total work of producing and updating these
separator choices which is not already charged to admitted adjacency scans or
to the dense Schur operations below.  Nonuniform advice and knowledge of the
terminal support are not included in the presentation.
\end{definition}

The last condition is an exact response condition, not merely a graph-type
label.  Degrees, normalized edge weights, and the shifted RPPR load are all
already encoded in $(\ell_v,Q_{vZ_k})$.  Equitability or a global graph
automorphism is sufficient for repeated signatures but is not required.

\begin{lemma}[Exact signature-table maintenance]
\label{lem:exact-signature-table}
Under \cref{def:activation-aligned-presentation}, the exact frontier classes,
their member lists, and one representative per class can be maintained in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
   \zeta\operatorname{cvol}(S)+(K+1)\nu\zeta
 \right)
 \label{eq:signature-table-work}
\end{equation}
work, excluding $\mathcal T_{\rm sep}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every frontier vertex is first exposed by an edge scanned from an admitted
vertex.  Consequently the number of distinct frontier vertices ever exposed
is at most $\vol(S)$, and its degree and load metadata are charged once at
exposure.  Store one canonical key and a member list for each current class.

When a vertex of $B_k$ becomes a new coordinate of $Z_{k+1}$, only frontier
vertices incident to that coordinate can split.  Those incidences are exactly
the edges revealed while scanning $B_k$; inserting a labeled coordinate into
a key costs $O(\zeta)$, so all such splits cost
$O(\zeta\operatorname{cvol}(S))$.  If a coordinate retires, separation
\cref{eq:frontier-separation} says that its entry is zero for every surviving
frontier vertex.  It can therefore be deleted once from each of the at most
$\nu$ class keys, after which equal class lists are spliced, without touching
their members individually.  Rekeying representatives and exact gate queries
over all $K+1$ stages cost $O((K+1)\nu\zeta)$.  This proves
\cref{eq:signature-table-work}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact work from separator and signature complexity]
\label{thm:aligned-separator-signature}
Suppose an exact boundary trace ending at $S:=U_K$ has an online
$(\zeta,\nu)$ activation-aligned separator presentation.  Then the identical
batches $B_0,\ldots,B_{K-1}$, every exact gate decision, and the final
Dirichlet solution on $S$ can be generated with
\begin{equation}
 \Work_{\rm aligned}
 =O\!\left(
  (1+\zeta^2+\nu\zeta)\operatorname{cvol}(S)
  +\mathcal T_{\rm sep}
 \right)
 \label{eq:aligned-separator-work}
\end{equation}
degree work and exact arithmetic.  The bound contains no settled-volume
revisit factor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At stage $k$, eliminate the retired block $P_k$ but do not recover its
coordinates.  Retain only
\begin{align}
 \widehat Q_k
 &:={Q}_{Z_kZ_k}
   -Q_{Z_kP_k}Q_{P_kP_k}^{-1}Q_{P_kZ_k},
 \label{eq:aligned-reduced-matrix}\\
 \widehat\ell_k
 &:=\ell_{Z_k}
   -Q_{Z_kP_k}Q_{P_kP_k}^{-1}\ell_{P_k}.
 \label{eq:aligned-reduced-load}
\end{align}
For an empty $P_k$, these displays mean
$\widehat Q_k=Q_{Z_kZ_k}$ and
$\widehat\ell_k=\ell_{Z_k}$.  Schur-complement exactness gives
\begin{equation}
 x^k_{Z_k}=\widehat Q_k^{-1}\widehat\ell_k.
 \label{eq:aligned-separator-solution}
\end{equation}

Now let $v\in\Gamma_k$.  By
\cref{eq:frontier-separation} and the zero exterior condition,
\begin{equation}
 e_\ell(x^k)_v
 =\ell_v-Q_{vZ_k}x^k_{Z_k}.
 \label{eq:signature-demand}
\end{equation}
Consequently every vertex in one class of
\cref{eq:boundary-signature} has exactly the same violation demand.  Solving
\cref{eq:aligned-separator-solution} and testing one representative of each
class therefore recovers $B_k$ exactly; enumerating the positive classes
costs only the number of vertices actually admitted.

Because $B_k\subseteq\Gamma_k$, separation also gives
$Q_{P_kB_k}=0$.  Append the new batch to the retained system:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \widehat Q_k&Q_{Z_kB_k}\\
  Q_{B_kZ_k}&Q_{B_kB_k}
 \end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}x_{Z_k}\\x_{B_k}\end{bmatrix}
 =
 \begin{bmatrix}\widehat\ell_k\\\ell_{B_k}\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:aligned-frontal-system}
\end{equation}
Eliminating
$(Z_k\cup B_k)\setminus Z_{k+1}$ from this system produces exactly
$(\widehat Q_{k+1},\widehat\ell_{k+1})$.  Store its pivots and multipliers
rather than expanding the solution on $P_{k+1}$.  Induction proves both the
exactness of every gate decision and the reduced system.  At termination,
one reverse pass through the stored eliminations recovers the exact solution
on all of $S$.

The dense algebra can be charged per admitted or retired coordinate rather
than cubically per stage.  Maintain both $\widehat Q_k$ and its inverse.  If
$\zeta$ is taken to be the realized maximum frontal size, initializing
$\widehat Q_0^{-1}$ costs
$O(|Z_0|^3)\leq O(\zeta^2|S|)$.  If
$b_k:=|B_k|$, block inversion of \cref{eq:aligned-frontal-system} first forms
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal T_k
 :=Q_{B_kB_k}
   -Q_{B_kZ_k}\widehat Q_k^{-1}Q_{Z_kB_k}
\end{equation*}
and then uses the standard $2$-by-$2$ inverse formula.  This costs
$O(\zeta^2b_k)$ because $b_k\leq\zeta$.  Let
$R_k:=(Z_k\cup B_k)\setminus Z_{k+1}$ and $r_k:=|R_k|$.
The inverse of the next Schur complement is the $Z_{k+1}$ principal block of
the frontal inverse.  Its matrix and reduced load are obtained by eliminating
$R_k$ directly from the frontal system in $O(\zeta^2r_k)$ arithmetic.  Thus
both $\widehat Q_{k+1}$ and its inverse remain available.

The batches are disjoint, and the retired sets are disjoint because $P_k$ is
monotone, so
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_k b_k\leq|S|,
 \qquad
 \sum_k r_k\leq|S|.
\end{equation*}
Multiplying $\widehat Q_k^{-1}\widehat\ell_k$ for all $K+1$ gate tests costs
$O((K+1)\zeta^2)$, and reverse recovery costs $O(\zeta^2|S|)$.  Every
nonterminal batch is nonempty, so
$K\leq|S|\leq\operatorname{cvol}(S)$.  Combining these dense charges with
the scans, \cref{lem:exact-signature-table}, and the charged separator updates
proves
\cref{eq:aligned-separator-work} (with $\zeta\geq1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[RPPR response-complexity criterion]
\label{cor:rppr-response-complexity}
If the exact RPPR boundary trace has an online $(\zeta,\nu)$
activation-aligned separator presentation, then it can be solved exactly in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  \frac{1+\zeta^2+\nu\zeta}{\rho}+\mathcal T_{\rm sep}
 \right)
 \label{eq:rppr-response-complexity}
\end{equation}
work.  In particular, bounded $\zeta$ and $\nu$ together with
$\mathcal T_{\rm sep}=O(1/\rho)$ give output-linear $O(1/\rho)$ work, while
\begin{equation}
 1+\zeta^2+\nu\zeta
 =\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)
 \label{eq:response-product-criterion}
\end{equation}
is sufficient for the intended
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ product scale, provided also that
$\mathcal T_{\rm sep}=\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:safe-boundary-expansion}, the terminal set is
$S^\star(\rho)$.  Apply \cref{thm:aligned-separator-signature} and the source
support bound $\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$, together with
\cref{eq:charged-volume-equivalence}.
\end{proof}

The fan prefix satisfies the definition with $\zeta=3$ and $\nu=2$: retain
$\{o,v_j\}$, distinguish only the next path vertex from the remote root
neighbors, and admit one vertex.  Thus
\cref{thm:fan-lazy-trace} is the smallest nontrivial instance of
\cref{thm:aligned-separator-signature}.  More broadly, the theorem covers
activation paths through bounded articulation blocks and nonequitable graphs
whose current boundary has only a few exact response rows.  It does not
cover an arbitrary thick core: there $\zeta$, $\nu$, or both may grow with
the boundary.  The open problem has therefore become quantitative---bound
the product in \cref{eq:response-product-criterion}, or replace exact
signatures by a certified approximate representation.

\subsection{Kinetic thresholds beyond repeated signatures}
\label{sec:kinetic-thresholds}

Exact equality of response rows is sufficient but not necessary.  A second
mechanism is that arbitrarily many distinct boundary demands may cross zero
in one stable total order.

\begin{theorem}[Kinetic scalar gate]
\label{thm:kinetic-scalar-gate}
Suppose an exact incremental Schur representation of a zero-exterior gate
trace has the following data.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item At stage $k$ it computes an exact scalar $t_k$, with
 $t_{k+1}\geq t_k$.
 \item Every current frontier vertex has an exact key
 $(a_v,\theta_v)$, where $a_v>0$, such that
 \begin{equation}
  e_\ell(x^k)_v=a_v(t_k-\theta_v).
  \label{eq:kinetic-affine-demand}
 \end{equation}
 \item A key stays fixed while its vertex remains on the frontier, except
 for explicitly reported rekey events completed before the next query.
\end{enumerate}
Let $M$ be the number of distinct frontier vertices ever exposed, $H$ the
number of rekeys, $A$ the work of the Schur updates and final recovery other
than key handling, and $R$ the work of producing the keys and rekeys.  Then
the identical exact batches and final solution can be generated in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(A+R+(M+H)\log(2+M+H)\right)
 \label{eq:kinetic-gate-work}
\end{equation}
work, using $O(M+H)$ total heap records and at most $O(M)$ live records.  A
monotone bucket queue removes the
logarithm when the keys are supplied in total order or in an order-preserving
integer universe of $O(M+H)$ buckets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Keep a minimum heap ordered by $\theta_v$ and use generation counters for
rekeys.  At stage $k$, discard stale records and extract precisely the valid
records with $\theta_v<t_k$.  By \cref{eq:kinetic-affine-demand}, these and
only these vertices have positive exact demand; equality stays inactive, as
required by the strict gate.  Insert newly exposed vertices and completed
rekeys before the next query.  Monotonicity of $t_k$ means that an extracted
live key never returns.  Every insertion, stale discard, and live extraction
is charged to one exposure or rekey, proving the work and storage bounds.
Induction on the stages gives the identical batches, and the assumed exact
Schur representation gives the final solution.
\end{proof}

The scalar heap is the first member of a low-dimensional geometric family.
The next statement isolates the data-structure obligation instead of hiding
it in the algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Low-dimensional kinetic halfspace gate]
\label{thm:kinetic-halfspace-gate}
Suppose an exact incremental trace has a state $t_k\in\mathbb R^r$ and every
live frontier row has the stable form
\begin{equation}
 e_\ell(x^k)_v=b_v+a_v^Tt_k,
 \qquad a_v\geq0,
 \label{eq:kinetic-halfspace-demand}
\end{equation}
between explicitly reported rekeys.  Let $M,H,K$ denote exposures, rekeys,
and nonempty batches, and let $A$ be the remaining algebra and recovery work.
If $\mathcal T_j(N)$ is the cost of an exact dynamic extreme-point update or
query for points in $\mathbb R^j$, then the identical batches are reported in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  A+(M+H+|S|+r(K+1))
  \max_{1\leq j\leq r}\mathcal T_j(M+H)
 \right)
 \label{eq:kinetic-halfspace-work}
\end{equation}
work.  In particular, a fixed retained separator $Z$ verifies
\cref{eq:kinetic-halfspace-demand} with $t_k=x^k_Z$,
$a_v=-Q_{vZ}\geq0$, and no rekeys for surviving frontier vertices.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rows with $a_v=0$ are constant.  Otherwise let $j$ be the largest index with
$a_{v,j}>0$ and store
\begin{equation}
 p_v:=\left(
  \frac{a_{v,1}}{a_{v,j}},\ldots,
  \frac{a_{v,j-1}}{a_{v,j}},
  \frac{b_v}{a_{v,j}}
 \right)\in\mathbb R^j.
 \label{eq:kinetic-lifted-point}
\end{equation}
For $q_j(t):=(t_1,\ldots,t_{j-1},1)$,
\begin{equation}
 e_\ell(x^k)_v>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p_v^Tq_j(t_k)>-t_{k,j}.
 \label{eq:kinetic-extreme-query}
\end{equation}
Maintain one dynamic extreme-point structure for each stratum $j$.  Repeatedly
query its maximum in direction $q_j(t_k)$, deleting and reporting a maximizer
while \cref{eq:kinetic-extreme-query} holds.  One failed query certifies that
the whole stratum is inactive.  There is one successful query per admitted
vertex, at most $r$ failed queries per stage, one insertion per exposure, and
one delete--insert pair per rekey, proving \cref{eq:kinetic-halfspace-work}.
For a fixed separator, safe nested Stieltjes settlement makes $x^k_Z$
coordinatewise nondecreasing, and separation makes every surviving row
$(\ell_v,-Q_{vZ})$ stable.
\end{proof}

The following nontrivial graph class verifies all hypotheses with no rekeys
and no bound on the number of distinct demands.

\begin{theorem}[Exact kinetic solver on a rooted spider]
\label{thm:rooted-spider-kinetic}
Let $Q$ be SPD Stieltjes and let its off-diagonal graph consist of a hub $o$
and $R$ internally disjoint path arms
$(v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,m_i})$.  Start the exact gate at $U_0=\{o\}$, so every
active region is the hub plus a prefix of each arm.  For any fixed load
$\ell$, the exact batches and terminal Dirichlet solution can be generated
online in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\operatorname{cvol}(S)\log(2+R)\right)
 \label{eq:spider-work}
\end{equation}
exact arithmetic, comparisons, and degree work.  Under the bucket condition
of \cref{thm:kinetic-scalar-gate}, the bound is
$O(\operatorname{cvol}(S))$.  All effective boundary demands may be distinct.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Eliminate every active arm prefix while retaining the hub and the single
current inactive tip $w_i$ of each unfinished arm.  The exact reduced system
is arrowhead:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix}h&c^T\\c&\operatorname{diag}(d_i)\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}x_o\\x_w\end{bmatrix}
 =\begin{bmatrix}g\\\gamma\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad h,d_i>0,\quad c_i<0.
 \label{eq:spider-arrowhead}
\end{equation}
The inactive tips are held at zero, so the hub state and exact tip demands are
\begin{equation}
 t=\frac gh,
 \qquad
 \eta_i=\gamma_i-c_it=(-c_i)(t-\theta_i),
 \qquad
 \theta_i=\frac{\gamma_i}{c_i}.
 \label{eq:spider-threshold}
\end{equation}

Let $B$ be the exact positive-tip batch.  If an admitted $w_i$ has successor
$u$, write $q_i=Q_{w_i u}<0$, $D_i=Q_{uu}$, and $L_i=\ell_u$.  Ordinary
scalar Schur complementation gives
\begin{align}
 h^+&=h-\sum_{i\in B}\frac{c_i^2}{d_i},
 &g^+&=g-\sum_{i\in B}\frac{c_i\gamma_i}{d_i},
 \label{eq:spider-hub-update}\\
 d_i^+&=D_i-\frac{q_i^2}{d_i},
 &\gamma_i^+&=L_i-\frac{q_i\gamma_i}{d_i},
 &c_i^+&=-\frac{c_iq_i}{d_i}<0.
 \label{eq:spider-tip-update}
\end{align}
For a terminal tip only \cref{eq:spider-hub-update} is needed.  Other arms'
tip triples do not change, while SPD and Stieltjes signs persist.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 t^+-t
 =\frac{\sum_{i\in B}(-c_i)\eta_i/d_i}{h^+}>0
 \label{eq:spider-strict-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
at every nonterminal gate step.  Thus the stable thresholds in
\cref{eq:spider-threshold} and a minimum heap report exactly the strict gate
events.  There are no rekeys: activating another arm changes only $(h,g)$;
when a tip activates, its record is removed and its successor is inserted
once with the new triple from \cref{eq:spider-tip-update}.

For reverse recovery, save for every eliminated tip the equation
\begin{equation}
 x_{w_i}=\frac{\gamma_i-c_ix_o-q_ix_u}{d_i},
 \label{eq:spider-recovery}
\end{equation}
with the last term absent at an arm endpoint.  At termination $x_o=g/h$, and
reverse substitution recovers every active prefix exactly.  The root scan
costs $R$, each admitted arm vertex has constant arm degree, and each exposed
tip is inserted and extracted at most once from a heap of size at most $R$.
The number of such records is $O(\operatorname{cvol}(S))$, proving
\cref{eq:spider-work}.
\end{proof}

This theorem strictly extends repeated signatures: a spider may have
$\nu=R$ and $K=\Theta(|S|)$, so representative testing can cost
$\Theta(RK)$ while the kinetic gate remains nearly output-linear.  Its sharp
limitation is also informative.  With a separator of dimension at least two,
gate events become moving halfspace queries rather than scalar thresholds;
\cref{thm:kinetic-halfspace-gate} handles a stable low-dimensional state, but
if one update changes all affine rows, the rekey count can be
$\Theta(|\Gamma|K)$.  Stability is a real structural condition: if persistent
frontier demands change by columns $a^{(k)}\lambda_k$ and admit a fixed
$q$-dimensional representation $b+At_k$, then every response column lies in
$\operatorname{col}(A)$ and hence
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{rank}[a^{(0)},\ldots,a^{(K-1)}]\leq q.
 \label{eq:kinetic-response-rank}
\end{equation}
Even a sparse path core can have full response rank: attach candidate $b_j$
and persistent probe $c_j$ to each path vertex $p_j$.  After earlier
candidates are eliminated, the response to $b_j$ is a positive multiple of
$\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)A_j^{-1}e_j$.  For sufficiently small candidate
couplings, the matrix of these columns converges after column scaling to
$\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)A^{-1}$ and is nonsingular.  Thus no fixed
$q<m$ stable kinetic state represents all $m$ updates, despite linear graph
volume.  The singleton order can be realized by choosing each candidate load
inductively: every earlier activation strictly increases every later demand,
so the interval that is nonpositive before the preceding event and positive
after it is nonempty; sufficiently negative probe loads keep all probes
inactive.  This is a representation obstruction for general fixed loads, not
an unconditional algorithmic lower bound or an RPPR-specific construction.

\subsection{Certified response trees}
\label{sec:certified-response-trees}

A complementary extension groups response rows that are not equal but are
certifiably too far from zero for their differences to matter.  The following
unit envelope makes the certificate local.  Let $x^0:=Q^{-1}b$ denote the
unregularized single-seed PageRank point.

\begin{lemma}[Unit envelope for RPPR gate states]
\label{lem:rppr-unit-envelope}
Every exact restricted gate state $x^U$ on a safe region
$U\subseteq S^\star(\rho)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x^U\leq x^\star(\rho)\leq x^0,
 \qquad
 \|x^U\|_2\leq1.
 \label{eq:rppr-unit-envelope}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first two inequalities are \cref{thm:safe-boundary-expansion}.  On an
active optimum coordinate, $(Qx^\star)_v=b_v-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}\leq b_v$;
on an inactive nonseed coordinate, Stieltjes signs and $x_v^\star=0$ give
$(Qx^\star)_v\leq0=b_v$.  Hence $Qx^\star\leq b$, and inverse positivity
gives $x^\star\leq Q^{-1}b=x^0$.  Finally
$\lambda_{\min}(Q)\geq\alpha$ and
$\|b\|_2=\alpha\|D^{-1/2}e_o\|_2\leq\alpha$, so
$\|x^0\|_2\leq1$.
\end{proof}

At stage $k$, suppose a certified factorization of the live response is
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\Gamma_kZ_k}=Y_kR_k+E_k,
 \qquad R_kR_k^T=I_{r_k},
 \qquad \|(E_k)_{v,:}\|_2\leq\varepsilon_v.
 \label{eq:certified-response-factor}
\end{equation}
A node $C$ of a response tree stores a center
$(\bar\ell_C,\bar y_C)$ and radii
\begin{equation}
 |\ell_v-\bar\ell_C|\leq a_C,
 \quad \|(Y_k)_{v,:}-\bar y_C\|_2\leq b_C,
 \quad \varepsilon_v\leq\varepsilon_C
 \qquad(v\in C).
 \label{eq:response-cluster-radii}
\end{equation}
For $t_k:=R_kx^k_{Z_k}$, put
\begin{equation}
 m_C:=\bar\ell_C-\bar y_C^Tt_k,
 \qquad \delta_C:=a_C+b_C+\varepsilon_C.
 \label{eq:response-cluster-interval}
\end{equation}
By \cref{lem:rppr-unit-envelope}, every exact demand in $C$ lies in
$[m_C-\delta_C,m_C+\delta_C]$.  Therefore a whole node is accepted when its
lower endpoint is positive, rejected when its upper endpoint is nonpositive,
and otherwise refined, eventually to an exact singleton row.

\begin{theorem}[Certified low-rank response-tree gate]
\label{thm:certified-response-tree}
Suppose a safe exact RPPR trace has a charged activation-aligned separator
presentation of frontal size $\zeta$ and the certified structures
\cref{eq:certified-response-factor,eq:response-cluster-radii}.  Let
$\mathcal V_k$ be the response-tree nodes visited at stage $k$, let
$\mathcal L_k$ be the ambiguous vertices resolved by exact singleton rows,
and put $r:=\max_kr_k$.  If $\mathcal C_{\rm maint}$ is the total work of
maintaining the factors and trees, and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\rm leaf}
 :=\sum_k\sum_{v\in\mathcal L_k}
   \bigl(1+|N(v)\cap Z_k|\bigr),
 \label{eq:response-leaf-charge}
\end{equation}
then the identical exact batches and final solution are generated in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  (1+\zeta^2)\operatorname{cvol}(S)
  +r\sum_k|\mathcal V_k|
  +\mathcal C_{\rm leaf}+\mathcal C_{\rm maint}
  +\mathcal T_{\rm sep}
 \right)
 \label{eq:certified-response-tree-work}
\end{equation}
work.  In particular, it reaches the accelerated product scale if the four
response-maintenance terms after the first are collectively
$\widetilde O(\operatorname{cvol}(S)/\sqrt\alpha)$ and
$\zeta^2=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $v\in C$, subtract the center expression in
\cref{eq:response-cluster-interval} from
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^k)_v=\ell_v-(Y_k)_{v,:}t_k-(E_k)_{v,:}x^k_{Z_k}$.
The triangle inequality, $\|t_k\|_2\leq\|x^k\|_2\leq1$, and
\cref{eq:rppr-unit-envelope} give the claimed interval.  Hence accepted,
rejected, and exact singleton leaves classify every frontier vertex exactly,
including the strict zero convention.  Induction therefore reproduces the
exact batches.  A visited node costs $O(r)$, exact leaves cost
\cref{eq:response-leaf-charge}, the factor/tree changes cost
$\mathcal C_{\rm maint}$, and the separator/Schur algebra is exactly the
charge in \cref{thm:aligned-separator-signature} without its signature term.
Reverse recovery gives the exact terminal solution.
\end{proof}

One global numerical-rank hypothesis cannot make this theorem universal.
For a matched interface of size $q$ in a $D$-regular graph,
$Q_{\Gamma Z}=-cI_q$ with $c=(1-\alpha)/(2D)$.  Every rank-$r$ matrix $M$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|cI_q-M\|_F^2\geq(q-r)c^2.
 \label{eq:matched-interface-rank-obstruction}
\end{equation}
Thus uniformly small relative row error forces $r=\Omega(q)$.  A successful
general response tree must exploit sparse near-field structure, hierarchical
low rank, margins, or another monotonicity principle rather than one global
factor.

\subsection{A graph-uniform obstruction to flat response complexity}
\label{sec:expander-response-obstruction}

The condition on $(\zeta,\nu)$ cannot be removed from the flat separator
theorem.  The obstruction already occurs on constant-degree expanders whose
RPPR optimum has full support.

\begin{proposition}[Expander obstruction]
\label{prop:expander-response-obstruction}
Fix constants $d\geq3$, $\gamma>0$, and $c>0$.  Let $(G_n,o_n)$ be connected
$d$-regular graphs such that, for $P_n:=A_n/d$,
$\lambda_2(P_n)\leq1-\gamma$.  Put $\alpha_n:=c/\log n$.  There is a constant
$\kappa=\kappa(c,\gamma)>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$, the
single-seed RPPR problem with
\begin{equation}
 \rho_n:=\frac{\kappa}{2dn}=\Theta(1/n)
 \label{eq:expander-rho}
\end{equation}
has $S^\star(\rho_n)=V_n$.  Moreover every exact boundary trace from
$\{o_n\}$ to $V_n$ and every activation-aligned separator presentation of
that trace satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \zeta\geq\min\{1/3,\gamma/6\}n,
 \qquad
 \nu\geq\min\{1/(3d),\gamma/(6d)\}n.
 \label{eq:expander-response-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently \cref{eq:response-product-criterion} is not graph-uniform.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $W_n:=(I+P_n)/2$.  In the shared normalization,
\begin{equation}
 Q_n=I-(1-\alpha_n)W_n,
 \qquad
 \pi_n:=\alpha_nQ_n^{-1}e_{o_n}
 =\alpha_n\sum_{t\geq0}(1-\alpha_n)^tW_n^te_{o_n}.
 \label{eq:expander-pagerank-series}
\end{equation}
Every nonconstant eigenvalue of $W_n$ lies in $[0,1-\gamma/2]$.  Set
\begin{equation}
 T_n:=\left\lceil
 \frac{\log(2n)}{-\log(1-\gamma/2)}
 \right\rceil.
 \label{eq:expander-mixing-time}
\end{equation}
Spectral decomposition gives
$|(W_n^t)_{vo_n}-1/n|\leq(1-\gamma/2)^t$, so
$(W_n^t)_{vo_n}\geq1/(2n)$ for $t\geq T_n$.  Since
$T_n\leq C_\gamma\log n$ and $\alpha_n\leq1/2$ for large $n$,
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{n,v}
 \geq\frac{(1-\alpha_n)^{T_n}}{2n}
 \geq\frac{e^{-2\alpha_nT_n}}{2n}
 \geq\frac{e^{-2cC_\gamma}}{2n}.
 \label{eq:expander-pagerank-floor}
\end{equation}
Take $\kappa=e^{-2cC_\gamma}/2$.  Since $Q_n\one=\alpha_n\one$, the
all-active shifted point is
\begin{equation}
 Q_n^{-1}\ell_{\rho_n}
 =\frac1{\sqrt d}\bigl(\pi_n-\rho_nd\one\bigr)>0.
 \label{eq:expander-full-support}
\end{equation}
It satisfies every active KKT equality; strict convexity therefore makes it
the RPPR optimum and $S^\star(\rho_n)=V_n$.

Consider a trace $U_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_K=V_n$.  If some
$|B_k|\geq n/3$, then $\zeta\geq n/3$.  Otherwise let $j$ be the first index
with $|U_j|\geq n/3$; then $|U_j|<2n/3$.  For
$f=\mathbf1_{U_j}-(|U_j|/n)\one$, the spectral gap gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|E(U_j,V_n\setminus U_j)|}{d}
 =f^T(I-P_n)f
 \geq\gamma\|f\|_2^2
 \geq\frac{\gamma n}{6}.
 \label{eq:expander-cut}
\end{equation}
Separation forbids any cut edge from having its active endpoint in $P_j$.
All cut edges are therefore incident on $Z_j$, so
$|Z_j|\geq\gamma n/6$, proving the $\zeta$ bound.

At every stage, a nonseed frontier vertex has
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{\rho_n,v}=-\alpha_n\rho_n\sqrt d,
 \qquad
 Q_{vZ_k}=-\frac{1-\alpha_n}{2d}
          \mathbf1_{N(v)\cap Z_k}.
 \label{eq:expander-signature}
\end{equation}
The active-neighbor set is nonempty and, by separation, lies in $Z_k$.
Vertices in one signature class have the same neighbor subset; choosing one
$z$ in that subset shows the class is contained in $N(z)$ and has size at
most $d$.  A batch of size $n/3$ therefore forces
$\nu\geq n/(3d)$.  In the balanced-cut case,
$|\Gamma_j|\geq|E(U_j,V_n\setminus U_j)|/d\geq\gamma n/6$, so
$\nu\geq\gamma n/(6d)$.  This proves
\cref{eq:expander-response-lower-bound}.
\end{proof}

This is a framework obstruction, not an algorithmic lower bound.  It rules
out graph-uniform removal of the flat separator/signature hypothesis, but it
does not rule out hierarchical, approximate, or Krylov continuation methods.

\subsection{The exact cutoff and the remaining universal lemma}
\label{sec:exact-cutoff}

The obstruction identifies where a hybrid proof must change representations.
Set
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_0:=\lceil\alpha^{-1/4}\rceil.
 \label{eq:separator-cutoff}
\end{equation}
Before the first stage whose live frontal size exceeds $\zeta_0$, the cumulative
dense Schur algebra is at most
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\zeta_0^2\operatorname{cvol}(S)\right)
 =O\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{cvol}(S)}{\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:cutoff-dense-work}
\end{equation}
If response queries and
presentation updates are also stopped when their measured charge reaches
this budget, the algorithm reaches the overflow with an exact safe face
state.  This is a rigorous reduction, but not yet a continuation theorem.

For the precise missing statement, write
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_U:=\min\left\{
   \frac12x^TQx-\ell_\rho^Tx:x\geq0,
   \ \operatorname{supp}(x)\subseteq U
 \right\},
 \qquad
 \Delta(U,B):=\Phi_U-\Phi_{U\cup B}\geq0.
 \label{eq:face-shock}
\end{equation}
For an exact positive gate batch, Schur complementation gives
$\Delta(U,B)=q_B^T\mathcal S_B^{-1}q_B/2$.  It is tempting to require a
separate repair after every event with old-face work proportional to
$\log(1+\Delta/E)$.  The next two results show that this target is false.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit RPPR output obstruction]
\label{thm:shock-output-obstruction}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  For every sufficiently large $m$ there is a tree and
$\rho=\Theta(1/m)$ whose exact RPPR gate batches begin with a singleton $B$
and then a batch of size $m$, while
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(\{o\},B)=\Theta(1/m^2).
 \label{eq:tiny-shock-large-output}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $E=\Theta(1/m)$, no explicit-batch algorithm can report
the subsequent gate event in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  \operatorname{cvol}(B)
  +\frac{\operatorname{cvol}(\{o\}\cup B)}{\sqrt\alpha}
   \log\!\left(1+\frac{\Delta(\{o\},B)}E\right)
 \right)=O(1)
 \label{eq:false-shock-output-bound}
\end{equation}
work.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $a=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $c=(1-\alpha)/2$.  The seed root $o$ has one leaf
$b$ and $m$ neighbors $c_i$, each of which has its own pendant $z_i$.  Thus
$D:=d_o=m+1$, $d_b=d_{z_i}=1$, and $d_{c_i}=2$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 H:=c+2a=1+a,
 \quad g:=\frac c{\sqrt D},
 \quad \sigma:=a-\frac{g^2}{a},
 \quad \lambda:=\frac{g^2}{a\sigma},
 \quad \rho_0:=\frac c{DH},
 \label{eq:shock-tree-parameters}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 q_0:=\frac{\alpha c(1-1/H)}{aD},
 \qquad
 \delta:=\frac{a\lambda q_0}{2\alpha(H+\lambda)},
 \qquad
 \rho:=\rho_0+\delta.
 \label{eq:shock-tree-rho}
\end{equation}
At the exact root solve, the violation of $b$ and the common scaled violation
of the $c_i$ are
\begin{equation}
 q_b=\frac\alpha a\left(\frac cD-\rho\right)>0,
 \qquad
 r_c:=\sqrt2e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{c_i}
 =\frac\alpha a\left(\frac cD-H\rho\right)<0.
 \label{eq:shock-tree-first-gate}
\end{equation}
Hence the first batch is $\{b\}$.  Eliminating $b$ increases every $r_c$ by
$\lambda q_b$, and the choice of $\delta$ gives
\begin{equation}
 r_c+\lambda q_b=\frac{\lambda q_0}{2}>0.
 \label{eq:shock-tree-second-gate}
\end{equation}
Thus the next batch is $\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}$.  Since
$\lambda=\Theta(1/D)$ and $q_b=\Theta(1/D)$,
$\Delta(\{o\},\{b\})=q_b^2/(2\sigma)=\Theta(D^{-2})$.
Meanwhile $\operatorname{cvol}(\{b\})=2$ and
$\operatorname{cvol}(\{o,b\})=\Theta(D)$.  Taking $E=D^{-1}$ makes the
right-hand side of \cref{eq:false-shock-output-bound} constant, but writing
the next batch requires $m=\Theta(D)$ word outputs.
\end{proof}

Even a compressed batch handle does not rescue a generic full-sweep repair.

\begin{theorem}[No per-event shock bound for full-sweep repair]
\label{thm:no-full-sweep-shock-repair}
Let $A$ have order at least two, and let an enlarged restricted Hessian be
\begin{equation}
 M=\begin{bmatrix}A&g\\g^T&c\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad A\succeq\alpha I,\quad g\leq0,\quad
 \sigma:=c-g^TA^{-1}g\in(0,c).
 \label{eq:full-sweep-enlargement}
\end{equation}
There are a strictly positive old optimum $x^U$, a feasible point
$y=x^U+e$ of old-face error $E$ with $g^Te=0$, and an arbitrarily small
positive new-coordinate demand $q$, such
that optimizing the new coordinate alone leaves enlarged-face error strictly
larger than $E$.  Hence accelerated gradient, Chebyshev, or CG repairs whose
first nontrivial old-face operation is a full matrix--vector product must pay
$\Omega(\operatorname{cvol}(U))$ even as $\Delta/E\to0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a nonzero $e\in\ker(g^T)$ and scale it so that
$e^TAe/2=E$.  Choose $x^U$ positive enough that $x^U+e\geq0$, set the old
load to $Ax^U$, and choose the new load so its demand at $x^U$ is $q>0$.
The exact enlargement gain is $\Delta=q^2/(2\sigma)$.  The demand at $y$ is
still $q$, so optimizing only the new coordinate decreases the objective by
$q^2/(2c)=(\sigma/c)\Delta$.  The enlarged error therefore remains
\begin{equation}
 E+\left(1-\frac\sigma c\right)\Delta>E.
 \label{eq:full-sweep-residual-error}
\end{equation}
Some old coordinate must change.  The stated full-sweep methods cannot make
that change before one old-face matrix--vector product.  Bounded-degree path
or cycle Stieltjes blocks with one pendant new vertex realize
\cref{eq:full-sweep-enlargement}; taking
$\Delta/E=1/\operatorname{cvol}(U)^2$ makes the proposed logarithmic budget
$O(1)$ while that first sweep costs $\Omega(\operatorname{cvol}(U))$.
\end{proof}

The second missing resource is exact sign information.  If $x^T$ is the
exact point on a safe face and $z$ has objective error at most $E$, then
\begin{equation}
 \|z-x^T\|_{Q_{TT}}\leq\sqrt{2E},
 \qquad
 |e_{\ell_\rho}(z)_v-e_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)_v|<\sqrt{2E}
 \label{eq:gate-margin-from-error}
\end{equation}
for every boundary vertex $v$.  Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz in the $Q_{TT}$ norm
and the positive scalar Schur complement give
$Q_{vT}Q_{TT}^{-1}Q_{Tv}<Q_{vv}\leq1$.  Thus an approximate point certifies
exact gate signs only under a margin larger than $\sqrt{2E}$, or with a
separately charged exact response oracle.

The correct remaining target must therefore be aggregate, explicitly charge
new output and response queries, and allow small shocks to accumulate before
an old-face sweep.

Conditional on exhaustively charging exact response maintenance, the
numerical part of this target is completely amortized.  To avoid hiding work
in an ``oracle,'' define $\mathcal C_{\rm resp}$ to include exactly one state
initialization or conversion at the cutoff, all preprocessing, every exact
sign query and response update on old or newly admitted coordinates, and
every old-face read or arithmetic operation before the final repair.  The
first adjacency-list scan of a newly admitted vertex and its explicit label
output are excluded and charged separately.  The response state contains no
advice depending on $S^\star$ or on future batches.

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate repair given an exact response oracle]
\label{thm:aggregate-repair-oracle}
Let $E>0$, let $U_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_J=:T$ be safe faces,
$B_j:=U_{j+1}\setminus U_j$, and
$\Delta_j:=\Phi_{U_j}-\Phi_{U_{j+1}}$.  Let $y\geq0$ be supported on $U_0$
and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \frac12y^TQy-\ell_\rho^Ty-\Phi_{U_0}\leq E.
 \label{eq:aggregate-initial-error}
\end{equation}
Suppose an online exact-response oracle reports every $B_j$ and certifies
termination with exhaustive charge $\mathcal C_{\rm resp}$ as defined above,
while each admitted adjacency list is otherwise scanned once.  Suppose also
that a fixed-face routine on $T$ satisfies
$G_k\leq C_A\exp(-c_{\rm ctr}k\sqrt\alpha)G_0$ for absolute constants
$C_A\geq1$ and $c_{\rm ctr}>0$ after $k$ full iterations, each costing
$O(\!\operatorname{cvol}(T))$.  Then the segment, including explicit output
and one final repair to error $E$, costs
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  \sum_{j<J}\operatorname{cvol}(B_j)+\mathcal C_{\rm resp}
  +\frac{\operatorname{cvol}(T)}{\sqrt\alpha}\left[
    1+\log\!\left(1+\frac{\sum_{j<J}\Delta_j}{E}\right)
  \right]
 \right).
 \label{eq:aggregate-oracle-work}
\end{equation}
If $\sum_{j<J}\Delta_j\leq C_0E$, its numerical-repair term is
$O_{C_0}(\operatorname{cvol}(T)/\sqrt\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Do not numerically repair between response events; zero-extend $y$ as the face
grows.  Its objective value does not change, whereas the restricted optimum
telescopes:
\begin{equation}
 \frac12y^TQy-\ell_\rho^Ty-\Phi_T
 \leq E+\Phi_{U_0}-\Phi_T
 =E+\sum_{j<J}\Delta_j.
 \label{eq:aggregate-gap-telescope}
\end{equation}
One final repair therefore needs at most
\begin{equation}
 \left\lceil
  \frac1{c_{\rm ctr}\sqrt\alpha}
  \log\!\left(C_A\left[1+\frac{\sum_{j<J}\Delta_j}{E}\right]\right)
 \right\rceil
 \label{eq:aggregate-final-iterations}
\end{equation}
full iterations.  Its constant logarithm gives the displayed
$\operatorname{cvol}(T)/\sqrt\alpha$ term.  All earlier response operations
belong to $\mathcal C_{\rm resp}$, while the $1$ in
$\operatorname{cvol}(B_j)$ pays each explicit label and first adjacency
scan.  The final $\operatorname{cvol}(T)/\sqrt\alpha$ term also covers
materializing the final vector and its boundary certificate.
\end{proof}

This proves that repeated numerical repair is not the remaining obstacle.
When exact response certificates are maintained independently, heavy shocks
also bound how often a numerical repair is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate continuation for heavy-shock groups]
\label{thm:heavy-shock-continuation}
Let $E>0$ and let
$U_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_H=:T$ be the materialized safe faces of one
accuracy epoch, with $B_h:=U_{h+1}\setminus U_h$ and
$\Delta_h:=\Phi_{U_h}-\Phi_{U_{h+1}}$.  Suppose the epoch begins with
objective error at most $E$ on $U_0$, and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{h<H}\Delta_h\leq C_{\rm sh}E,
 \qquad
 \Delta_h\geq\theta E\quad(h<H-1),
 \label{eq:heavy-shock-groups}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\rm sh}$ and $\theta>0$ are fixed.  Suppose an independently
maintained exact-response state reports every group and certifies all
nonmembership signs using
$O(\operatorname{cvol}(U_h)+\operatorname{cvol}(B_h))$ work for group $h$,
including all internal response events, and after each group a fixed-face
routine with the contraction in \cref{thm:aggregate-repair-oracle} restores
error at most $E$.  Then the epoch work is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  \left(\frac{C_{\rm sh}}\theta+1\right)
  \frac{\operatorname{cvol}(T)}{\sqrt\alpha}
  +\sum_{h<H}\operatorname{cvol}(B_h)
 \right).
 \label{eq:heavy-shock-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The shock floor and telescoping give
$H-1\leq C_{\rm sh}/\theta$.  Immediately after group $h$, zero-padding the
old point gives error at most $E+\Delta_h$.  The fixed-face contraction restores
error $E$ in $O_{C_A,c_{\rm ctr},C_{\rm sh}}(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ iterations.
Every intermediate face has charged volume at most
$\operatorname{cvol}(T)$, so summing those iterations and the assumed exact
response charge per group proves \cref{eq:heavy-shock-work}.  The new batches
are disjoint.
\end{proof}

For a natural exact batch with demand $q_B$ and Schur complement
$\mathcal S_B$, the bounds
$\alpha I\preceq\mathcal S_B\preceq I$ imply
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\|q_B\|_2^2
 \leq\Delta(U,B)
 \leq\frac1{2\alpha}\|q_B\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:shock-demand-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Thus, once an exact response mechanism supplies the natural batches,
\cref{thm:heavy-shock-continuation}'s repair-frequency hypothesis holds if
every nonfinal batch satisfies $\|q_B\|_2^2\geq2\theta E$.  The remaining
\emph{numerical-repair} difficulty is confined to light batches; exact
response certification remains necessary for both heavy and light batches.

\begin{problem}[Graph-uniform light-shock response]
\label{prob:light-shock-response}
Within an accuracy epoch, maintain the exact gate while leaving the numerical
iterate unchanged across consecutive batches with $\Delta_j<\theta E$.
Either terminate or group them until their cumulative shock reaches
$\theta E$, with exhaustive total response charge
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\rm resp}
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right)
 \label{eq:light-shock-response-target}
\end{equation}
over all epochs, in addition to explicit output and one scan of each newly
admitted adjacency list.  No terminal-support or future-batch advice is
allowed.
\end{problem}

\begin{lemma}[Graph-universal RPPR support radius]
\label{lem:aggregate-support-radius}
For a finite graph with one seed, put
$R_\star:=\max_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)$, with $R_\star=0$ for
empty support or support containing only the root.  Then
\begin{equation}
 R_\star
 \leq1+
 \frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}
 {\log((1+\sqrt\alpha)/(1-\sqrt\alpha))}
 \leq1+
 \frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}{2\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:aggregate-tree-radius}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum of $Q$ lies in $[\alpha,1]$.  Put
$\lambda_\alpha:=(1-\sqrt\alpha)/(1+\sqrt\alpha)$.  The degree-$k$
Chebyshev approximation can be written explicitly by setting
\begin{equation*}
 q_{k+1}(x):=
 \frac{T_{k+1}((1+\alpha-2x)/(1-\alpha))}
      {T_{k+1}((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha))},
 \qquad p_k(x):=\frac{1-q_{k+1}(x)}x.
\end{equation*}
Because $q_{k+1}(0)=1$, $p_k$ is a polynomial of degree $k$; the extremal
bound for $T_{k+1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \|Q^{-1}-p_k(Q)\|_2
 \leq\frac2\alpha\lambda_\alpha^{k+1}.
 \label{eq:aggregate-chebyshev-inverse}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=\ell\geq1$, sparsity makes
$(p_{\ell-1}(Q))_{vo}=0$.  For the unregularized point $x^0:=Q^{-1}b$,
$b_o=\alpha/\sqrt{d_o}$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x_v^\star\leq x_v^0\leq2\lambda_\alpha^\ell.
 \label{eq:aggregate-green-decay}
\end{equation}
Choose a deepest active $v$ when $R_\star\geq1$.  Its active KKT equality,
nonnegativity, and the fact that every neighbor has depth at least
$R_\star-1$ imply
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}
 <\sum_{u\in\mathcal N(v)\cap S^\star}
   \frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{d_ud_v}}x_u^\star
 \leq(1-\alpha)\sqrt{d_v}\lambda_\alpha^{R_\star-1},
 \label{eq:aggregate-deepest-demand}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{u\in\mathcal N(v)}d_u^{-1/2}\leq d_v$.  Taking logarithms gives
the first inequality in \cref{eq:aggregate-tree-radius}; the second uses
$\log((1+t)/(1-t))\geq2t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact aggregate response solver on a rooted tree]
\label{thm:aggregate-tree-response}
Let the RPPR graph be a finite tree rooted at its single seed, let
$\Delta_\star:=\max(\{d_u:u\in S^\star\}\cup\{0\})$, and define $R_\star$ as
in \cref{lem:aggregate-support-radius}.  Exact lazy subtree-response iterators
explicitly emit every active label, perform no old-face sweep during
discovery, and use one final forest resolution.  Their charges satisfy
\begin{align}
 \mathcal C_{\rm resp}
 &\leq O\!\left(
  \log(2+\Delta_\star)\left[
   \operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)
   +\sum_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
  \right]
 \right),
 \label{eq:tree-aggregate-response}\\
 \Work
 &\leq O\!\left(
  1+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)+\mathcal C_{\rm resp}
 \right)
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{1+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:tree-aggregate-work}
\end{align}
Thus arbitrary rooted trees bypass the literal gate in
\cref{prob:light-shock-response} with a condition-free exact
response-generated support solver at the same aggregate budget.  On rooted
spiders, \cref{thm:rooted-spider-kinetic} additionally reproduces the literal
exact gate batches.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a nonroot $u$ with parent $p$, put $\beta_{pu}:=-Q_{pu}>0$.  Condition on
the parent value $t$, let $z^{(u)}(t)$ be the exact nonnegative minimizer on
the subtree rooted at $u$, and let
$\mu_u(t):=\beta_{pu}z_u^{(u)}(t)$.  If $\mathcal C(u)$ is the child set, the
inverse response of the scalar root coordinate $z=z_u^{(u)}$ is
\begin{equation}
 H_u(z):=\frac{
  Q_{uu}z-\ell_{\rho,u}-\sum_{w\in\mathcal C(u)}\mu_w(z)
 }{\beta_{pu}}.
 \label{eq:aggregate-tree-inverse}
\end{equation}
On each affine piece, its numerator slope is the scalar Schur complement
after eliminating the active descendants, hence
$H_u'(z)\geq\alpha/\beta_{pu}>0$.  Therefore $H_u$ is invertible on its active
part.  Its breakpoints are contained in the merged child breakpoints plus
$u$'s activation, with ties merged; branching creates no new breakpoints.

A lazy iterator stores the current affine piece and one next event per child.
Advancing a consumed event labeled $v$ through a proper support ancestor $u$
costs $O(\log(1+d_u))$, once on every root-path edge.  Initial child records
and the final unconsumed lookahead records cost
$O(\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)\log(2+\Delta_\star))$: an inactive child is
represented by its edge and degree without scanning its adjacency list, and
any lookahead propagated upward follows the route of its active parent.
This proves \cref{eq:tree-aggregate-response}; each active adjacency list and
emitted label is charged once.

At the root, if $-\ell_{\rho,o}\geq0$, zero satisfies the root and every
nonseed KKT inequality and is returned in $O(1)$ work.  Otherwise the root
performs the same merge on
$Q_{oo}z-\ell_{\rho,o}-\sum_{w\in\mathcal C(o)}\mu_w(z)$ and stops when its
unique zero precedes the next event.  A label is consumed exactly when its
coordinate is positive there, with equality left inactive.  Thus the
consumed labels are precisely $S^\star$.  One top-down forest solve and one
boundary KKT check recover and certify the exact solution in
$O(\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star))$ work.

For nonempty support,
$\sum_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
\leq R_\star\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)$ and
$\log(2+\Delta_\star)\leq\log(2+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star))$.
Combining these bounds with \cref{lem:aggregate-support-radius} proves
\cref{eq:tree-aggregate-work}; the empty-support case was handled separately.
\end{proof}

The tree proof suggests the correct higher-rank sufficient structure, but it
requires an explicit routing contract.

\begin{lemma}[Hierarchical response-route charge]
\label{lem:hierarchical-response-charge}
Suppose an online, locally constructed exact response hierarchy persists
through an epoch.  Every emitted support label $v$ creates one event that
visits each node of a route $P(v)$ once; tied blocks are charged per emitted
label, and there are no unlabeled breakpoints except one live lookahead per
route.  At a hierarchy node $a$, let $r_a$ be the port dimension, let $N_a$
be the maximum number of live response rows, and suppose one event update or
lookahead query costs
\begin{equation}
 c_a=O\!\left(
  r_a^2+\mathcal T_{r_a}(N_a)+\log(1+\deg_{\mathcal H}(a))
 \right).
 \label{eq:hierarchical-node-charge}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal C_{\rm build}$ include all initial factorizations, in particular
any $O(r_a^3)$ port setup; let $\mathcal C_{\rm rekey}$ include only rekeys
not already charged in $c_a$; and let $\mathcal C_{\rm recover}$ include
final recovery and certification.  If strict-zero events remain inactive,
then the exact trace generated by the hierarchy has
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\rm resp}
 \leq
 \mathcal C_{\rm build}+\mathcal C_{\rm recover}
 +\mathcal C_{\rm rekey}
 +\sum_{v\in S^\star}\sum_{a\in P(v)}c_a.
 \label{eq:hierarchical-response-charge}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Charge construction and recovery to their named terms.  Persistent state
prevents rebuilding between events.  The routing contract charges every
successful event update once to $(v,a)$; the one live lookahead on a route is
charged to that route's closest emitted predecessor.  Tied events are charged
per label, and all remaining row changes are precisely the separately counted
rekeys.  Summation gives \cref{eq:hierarchical-response-charge}.
\end{proof}

If the right-hand side of \cref{eq:hierarchical-response-charge} is
$\widetilde O((1+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star))/\sqrt\alpha)$, the hierarchy
meets the aggregate response budget for the exact safe trace it generates.
It solves the literal \cref{prob:light-shock-response} only when its events are
also proved to reproduce those gate batches.  Tree event streams are scalar,
monotone, route once, and have no rekeys; a stable low-dimensional separator
has hierarchy depth one.  The full-response-rank construction following
\cref{eq:kinetic-response-rank} shows why one flat low-rank state cannot
establish \cref{eq:hierarchical-response-charge} on general cyclic cores.

Two further results delimit what a cyclic hierarchy may do.  First, exact
dense fronts cannot be its graph-uniform representation.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit multifrontal hierarchies fail on expanders]
\label{prop:explicit-multifrontal-expander}
On the bounded-degree expander RPPR family of
\cref{prop:expander-response-obstruction}, where
$\alpha_n=\Theta(1/\log n)$, $\rho_n=\Theta(1/n)$, and $S^\star=V$, every
original-basis multifrontal $LDL^T$ or Cholesky hierarchy that explicitly
stores every numerical nonzero of its frontal matrices or triangular factor
has build or storage charge $\Omega(n^2)$.  This exceeds the intended
$\widetilde O(n\sqrt{\log n})$ work even when the terminal support and the
best offline ordering are supplied.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be a balanced vertex separator.  If $|X|=\Omega(n)$ there is nothing
to prove.  Otherwise, by grouping components of $G-X$, choose their union
$A$ with $n/3\leq|A|\leq2n/3$.  Every edge leaving $A$ is incident on $X$;
bounded degree and edge expansion therefore force $|X|=\Omega(n)$.  Hence
the graph has treewidth $\Omega(n)$, so every scalar vertex-elimination
ordering in the original coordinate basis creates a filled clique of order
$\Omega(n)$.
The fill is numerical, not only symbolic.  Eliminating a Stieltjes pivot
updates
\begin{equation}
 q'_{ij}=q_{ij}-q_{ik}q_{kk}^{-1}q_{kj};
 \label{eq:stieltjes-strict-fill}
\end{equation}
whenever the fill path exists, both incident off-diagonals are negative, so
the new entry is strictly negative and cannot cancel.  The materialized
front or factor therefore contains $\Omega(n^2)$ stored scalars.  Since the
expander has
$\operatorname{cvol}(V)=\Theta(n)$, the claimed separation follows.
\end{proof}

The same issue appears dynamically before a whole dense front is built.

\begin{proposition}[A light cascade defeats explicit demand arrays]
\label{prop:explicit-demand-light-cascade}
There is a family of sparse cyclic SPD Stieltjes systems with an active core
of size $n$ and $n$ exterior candidates such that an exact boundary gate
activates the candidates in $n$ prescribed singleton batches with
arbitrarily small total shock.  After every event, every remaining exact
candidate demand changes.  Consequently any response implementation that
explicitly stores and updates all current scalar candidate demands performs
$\Omega(n^2)$ arithmetic operations or writes, although graph volume and
explicit output are $\Theta(n)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$ be an irreducible SPD Stieltjes matrix with
$\operatorname{nnz}(A)=O(n)$ whose graph contains a cycle.  Choose $\tau>0$
and $\gamma>\tau^2/\lambda_{\min}(A)$, and set
\begin{equation}
 M:=\begin{bmatrix}A&-\tau I\\-\tau I&\gamma I\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:cyclic-core-leaf-matrix}
\end{equation}
Then $M$ is positive definite.  After the first $n$ coordinates are active,
the
exterior Schur matrix is
\begin{equation}
 S:=\gamma I-\tau^2A^{-1}.
 \label{eq:dense-leaf-schur}
\end{equation}
Irreducibility gives $A^{-1}>0$, so every off-diagonal of $S$ is strictly
negative.  All later Schur complements remain strictly dense Stieltjes by
\cref{eq:stieltjes-strict-fill}.

Work in the reduced exterior quadratic $z^TSz/2-h^Tz$.  Set
$h_1=\varepsilon_1>0$.  Inductively, suppose the first $i-1$ singleton events
have occurred, and let $z^{(k)}$ be the restricted solution on
$\{1,\ldots,k\}$.  For the as-yet unspecified $h_i$, write
$r_i^{(k)}:=h_i-S_{i,1:k}z^{(k)}$.  Strict Stieltjes monotonicity makes
\begin{equation*}
 \delta_i:=r_i^{(i-1)}-r_i^{(i-2)}>0;
\end{equation*}
the difference is independent of $h_i$.  Choose
$0<\varepsilon_i<\delta_i$ and put
\begin{equation}
 h_i=S_{i,1:i-1}z^{(i-1)}+\varepsilon_i.
 \label{eq:light-cascade-load}
\end{equation}
Then $r_i^{(i-1)}=\varepsilon_i>0$ while
$r_i^{(i-2)}<0$; monotonicity gives $r_i^{(k)}\leq0$ at every earlier stage.
Future choices do not alter earlier restricted solutions.  If $\sigma_i>0$
is the current Schur pivot, this event's shock is
$\varepsilon_i^2/(2\sigma_i)$.  Given any $\delta>0$, additionally choosing
$\varepsilon_i<\sqrt{2\sigma_i\delta/2^i}$ makes the total shock smaller
than $\delta$.

To lift the construction to the full system, choose any $\bar x>0$, set
$\ell_U=A\bar x$, and set $\ell_W=h-\tau\bar x$.  Eliminating the active
core $U$ gives exactly the reduced load $h$; after an exterior vector $z$
activates, the core solution is $\bar x+\tau A^{-1}z>0$.  Thus the core
remains active throughout.  Strict density makes event $i$ change all $n-i$
remaining demands.  An eager array that materializes each post-event demand
in a distinct scalar record must therefore perform
$\sum_{i<n}(n-i)=\Theta(n^2)$ scalar updates.  The block system has only
$O(n)$ nonzeros, and the emitted output has $n$ labels.
\end{proof}

These are representation lower bounds, not unconditional computational
lower bounds.  Lazy semiseparable state may compress the cyclic example, and
matrix-free or approximate state may bypass the expander front.  A positive
cyclic composition theorem is nevertheless available when its block
presentation is stable.

\begin{definition}[Stable exposed block--cut presentation]
\label{def:stable-block-presentation}
An online block presentation assigns every exposed edge its true
biconnected-block identifier and every exposed articulation incidence its
parent or child role in the true block--cut tree of the seed component, with
each bridge treated as a size-two block.  The tree is rooted at the seed
vertex when it is an articulation, and otherwise at its unique incident root
block.  Every nonroot vertex label has a unique owner block closest to the
root; a child block conditions on and does not own its parent articulation.
Identifiers, ownership, and roles never merge or change.  Exposing an edge
also supplies its exact normalized coefficient and the neighbor degree.  The
presentation reveals no activation time, terminal-support membership, or
unexposed adjacency.  Let $\mathcal C_{\mathcal B}$ include all work for
revealing and rooting this combinatorial metadata and every presentation-
induced rekey, but not numerical block factors or reverse-solve records.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate response with bounded stable blocks]
\label{thm:stable-bounded-block-response}
Suppose a single-seed RPPR graph has a stable exposed block--cut presentation
and the true size of every block incident on an exposed edge is at most $b$.
Put
\begin{equation*}
 N:=1+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star),
 \qquad
 h_{\mathcal B}(v):=\text{number of block nodes on the root route to $v$}.
\end{equation*}
There is an exact response-generated solver that is adjacency-local relative
to the presentation, with
\begin{equation}
 \Work=O\!\left(
  \mathcal C_{\mathcal B}+b^3N
  +(b^2+\log(2+N))
    \sum_{v\in S^\star}(1+h_{\mathcal B}(v))
 \right).
 \label{eq:stable-bounded-block-work}
\end{equation}
It scans each admitted adjacency list once, emits every support label, charges
all lookaheads, and finishes with exact block-tree recovery and a boundary
KKT certificate.  Since
$h_{\mathcal B}(v)\leq1+\operatorname{dist}(o,v)$, if
$b=\log^{O(1)}(2+N)$ and
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal B}=\widetilde O(N/\sqrt\alpha)$, then
$\Work=\widetilde O(N/\sqrt\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A directed block--cut subtree $H$ meets its parent only at one articulation
$p$.  Condition on $x_p=t$.  Since $-Q_{Hp}\geq0$, Stieltjes comparison makes
the obstacle solution on $H$ coordinatewise nondecreasing in $t$.  On every
fixed active set it is affine with nonnegative slope, and the scalar response
$-Q_{pH}z(t)$ is continuous, monotone, and piecewise affine.  Every
nontrivial breakpoint carries a newly positive vertex label; ties are grouped
and equality stays inactive.

At a block $B$, current child pieces modify only diagonal and load entries at
their articulation vertices.  These are exact Schur-complement updates, so
the local matrix remains SPD Stieltjes and every inverse-update denominator
is positive.  The active block system has order at most $b$.  A bordered
vertex insertion or one labeled child-piece change updates its inverse in
$O(b^2)$ work, with ties charged per label.  For each articulation, keep a
heap of child breakpoints in the local articulation coordinate and expose
only its minimum to the block heap.  Advancing a child changes one record in
that heap at $O(\log(2+N))$ cost.  The block heap has at most one aggregate
child record per articulation and one activation record per exposed inactive
block vertex, hence $O(b)$ records.  After an inverse update, re-pulling and
rekeying those records costs
$O(b\log b)\subseteq O(b^2+\log(2+N))$.  Exact affine pullback gives the next
parent-parameter event, so each support label advances once through each
ancestor block, with no unlabeled breakpoint proliferation.  A zero-slope
coordinate keeps an unreachable child event at infinity until a later rekey;
immediate and tied events are exhausted before the iterator advances.

At the root, merge the incident block responses and define
\begin{equation*}
 F_o(t):=Q_{oo}t-(\ell_\rho)_o-
         \sum_{B\ni o}\mu_B(t).
\end{equation*}
On every affine piece, $F_o'$ is the scalar Schur complement after eliminating
the active descendants and is at least $\alpha$.  If $F_o(0)\geq0$, zero is
the optimum.  Otherwise consume the next labeled response event whenever it
occurs strictly before the unique positive zero of the current root piece.
Stop when that zero is no later than the next event; equality remains
inactive.  Obstacle comparison then identifies the root (when
$F_o(0)<0$), together with the consumed labels, with exactly $S^\star$ and
gives the exact stopping certificate.

The stable presentation prevents block merging and response reparenting.
Local initialization and root-only or empty-support records are charged to
$N$.  A final unconsumed lookahead propagated through several blocks is
charged to the closest preceding emitted label on the same route; if its
stream has no emitted descendant, it is charged to the active articulation
that owns the stream.  This adds at most a constant copy of the displayed
route sum.  Conservative dense block initialization costs $O(b^3N)$.
Retained bordered and diagonal update records permit reverse block
substitution; one cached scan of active-to-boundary incidences then evaluates
all exact KKT inequalities.  Recovery and certification also cost
$O(b^3N)$.  Therefore the event ledger gives
\cref{eq:stable-bounded-block-work}.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{v\in S^\star}(1+h_{\mathcal B}(v))
 \leq (2+R_\star)|S^\star|\leq(2+R_\star)N,
\end{equation*}
so \cref{lem:aggregate-support-radius} proves the final claim.  This solver
generates its own safe response trace; no equivalence with the literal
boundary-gate batches is asserted.
\end{proof}

The presentation condition can now be removed for bounded blocks.  The
zero-prefix theorem in \texttt{delayed\_reflection\_ladder} observes that a
newly relevant cycle always contains a just-activated zero coordinate.  The
old response prefix is therefore unchanged; versioned iterators discard only
unconsumed lookaheads, preserve off-path child heaps through articulation
handles, and rebuild the merged at-most-$b$ front from the current checkpoint.
Charging every such rebuild and propagated rekey to the activating incidence
gives ordinary adjacency access the same
$\widetilde O(b^3/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ product scale.  Stable metadata remains
a useful abstraction for \cref{thm:stable-bounded-block-response}, but it is
not an oracle assumption for the bounded-block result.

For approximate PageRank output, however, exact zero signs are stronger than
necessary.  The following theorem replaces them by a finite normalized KKT
band.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-resolution aggregate RPPR continuation]
\label{thm:finite-resolution-continuation}
Fix $\rho,\tau_{\rm gate},\tau_{\rm sol}>0$ and put
$\eta:=\alpha\tau_{\rm gate}$.  In the single-seed problem, if
$(\ell_\rho)_o\leq0$, set $T=\varnothing$ and return zero in $O(1)$ work.
Otherwise start
$U_0=\{o\}$ and maintain a \emph{positive Dirichlet face}
\begin{equation*}
 x^U_{U^c}=0,
 \qquad Q_{UU}x^U_U=(\ell_\rho)_U,
 \qquad x^U_U>0.
\end{equation*}
Suppose an online certified response procedure returns, for every
$v\in\partial U$, an estimate of the exact mathematical demand satisfying
\begin{equation}
 |\widehat e_v-e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_v|
 \leq\frac\eta4\sqrt{d_v}.
 \label{eq:finite-response-certificate}
\end{equation}
It admits every vertex with
$\widehat e_v>3\eta\sqrt{d_v}/4$, scans each admitted list once, and stops
when no such vertex remains.  Let
$\mathcal C_{\rm resp}^{(\eta)}$ exhaustively charge its preprocessing,
certified error bounds, old-face reads, rekeys, response updates, and final
termination query, excluding the separately charged first scans and label
output.  If $T$ is the terminal face, then
\begin{align}
 T&\subseteq S^\star(\rho),
 &\operatorname{cvol}(T)&\leq\frac2\rho,
 \label{eq:finite-response-safety}\\
 \|D^{-1/2}(x^\star(\rho)-x^T)\|_\infty
 &\leq\tau_{\rm gate}.
 \label{eq:finite-response-gate-error}
\end{align}
If a final feasible fixed-face accelerated solve returns $z\geq0$ supported
on $T$ with
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_{\ell_\rho}(z)-\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)
 \leq\frac{\alpha\tau_{\rm sol}^2}{2},
 \label{eq:finite-response-solve-error}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
 \|D^{-1/2}(x^0-z)\|_\infty
 \leq\rho+\tau_{\rm gate}+\tau_{\rm sol}.
 \label{eq:finite-response-total-error}
\end{equation}
The total work is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  1+\mathcal C_{\rm resp}^{(\eta)}+\operatorname{cvol}(T)
  +\frac{\operatorname{cvol}(T)}{\sqrt\alpha}
    \left[1+\log_+\!\frac{2}{\alpha\tau_{\rm sol}^2}\right]
 \right).
 \label{eq:finite-response-work}
\end{equation}
Every admitted batch additionally has shock density
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(U,B)\geq\frac{\eta^2}{8}\vol(B).
 \label{eq:finite-response-shock-density}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $(\ell_\rho)_o\leq0$, every nonseed load is negative, so zero satisfies
the KKT conditions and is $x^\star$; \cref{eq:rppr-regularization-bias},
proved below, gives the claimed error for the returned zero vector.  Hence
assume $(\ell_\rho)_o>0$.  The initialization is then a positive Dirichlet
face, lies in $S^\star$, and satisfies $0\leq x^{U_0}\leq x^\star$.

Suppose these invariants hold at $U$.  If $v\notin S^\star$, Stieltjes signs
give
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_v\leq e_{\ell_\rho}(x^\star)_v\leq0$.
Every admitted vertex instead has exact demand larger than
$\eta\sqrt{d_v}/2>0$, so $B\subseteq S^\star$.  Its exact Schur right-hand
side satisfies $q_B>(\eta/2)D_B^{1/2}\one>0$.  Hence
$x_B^{U\cup B}=\mathcal S_B^{-1}q_B>0$, while back-substitution and
Stieltjes signs give $x_U^{U\cup B}\geq x_U^U$.  Since
$U\cup B\subseteq S^\star$, comparison with the active optimum equations
also gives $x^{U\cup B}\leq x^\star$.  This closes the induction.  Every
nonterminal batch is nonempty and contained in the finite set
$S^\star\setminus U$, so the process terminates after at most
$|S^\star\setminus U_0|$ batches.  The support-volume theorem and
\cref{eq:charged-volume-equivalence} give the volume bound.

At termination, \cref{eq:finite-response-certificate} gives
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)_v\leq\eta\sqrt{d_v}$ on the boundary.  The residual is
zero on $T$ and nonpositive outside $T\cup\partial T$.  Put
$h:=x^\star-x^T\geq0$ and let
$M:=\|D^{-1/2}h\|_\infty$.  At a maximizing coordinate $i$ with $M>0$,
$x_i^\star>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \alpha M\sqrt{d_i}
 \leq(Qh)_i=e_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)_i
 \leq\alpha\tau_{\rm gate}\sqrt{d_i}.
 \label{eq:finite-response-maximum-principle}
\end{equation}
The first inequality uses $h_j\leq M\sqrt{d_j}$, nonpositive
off-diagonals, and $QD^{1/2}\one=\alpha D^{1/2}\one$.  This proves
\cref{eq:finite-response-gate-error}.

For $g:=x^0-x^\star\geq0$, the optimum KKT inequalities give
$(Qg)_i\leq\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}$ at every coordinate and equality on the
active support.  Applying the same maximum-principle argument at a maximizing
coordinate of $D^{-1/2}g$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \|D^{-1/2}(x^0-x^\star)\|_\infty\leq\rho.
 \label{eq:rppr-regularization-bias}
\end{equation}
Strong convexity and \cref{eq:finite-response-solve-error} give
$\|z-x^T\|_2\leq\tau_{\rm sol}$, which also bounds the normalized infinity
norm because $d_i\geq1$.  The triangle inequality proves
\cref{eq:finite-response-total-error}.

The zero-start restricted objective gap is at most $1/2$: by
\cref{lem:rppr-unit-envelope}, $\|x^T\|_2\leq1$ and $Q\preceq I$.
Standard fixed-face acceleration therefore proves
\cref{eq:finite-response-work}.  Finally, every admitted exact batch demand
satisfies $q_B\geq(\eta/2)D_B^{1/2}\one$.  Since its Schur complement obeys
$\mathcal S_B\preceq I$,
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta(U,B)=\frac12q_B^T\mathcal S_B^{-1}q_B
 \geq\frac12\|q_B\|_2^2
 \geq\frac{\eta^2}{8}\vol(B),
\end{equation*}
proving \cref{eq:finite-response-shock-density}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Margin-adaptive interval continuation]
\label{cor:finite-response-intervals}
In \cref{thm:finite-resolution-continuation}, replace the point estimates by
arbitrary certified intervals
\begin{equation}
 L_v\leq
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_v}{\sqrt{d_v}}
 \leq U_v,
 \qquad v\in\partial U.
 \label{eq:finite-response-intervals}
\end{equation}
At a fixed face, admit every label with $L_v>\eta/2$; if none exists, stop
when every $U_v\leq\eta$, and otherwise refine only intervals preventing one
of these two certificates.  If refinement terminates at each fixed face,
then the safety, gate-error, and shock conclusions
\cref{eq:finite-response-safety,eq:finite-response-gate-error,eq:finite-response-shock-density}
all remain valid.  No common interval width is required away from the
transition band.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of \cref{thm:finite-resolution-continuation} uses its uniform
estimate only twice.  Admission needs the exact inequality
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_v>\eta\sqrt{d_v}/2$, which follows from the certified
lower endpoint.  Termination needs
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)_v\leq\eta\sqrt{d_v}$, which follows from the certified
upper endpoint.  The same comparison induction, maximum principle, and Schur
shock calculation therefore apply verbatim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Energy packing of finite-band response changes]
\label{lem:finite-response-energy-packing}
Let $U\subseteq T\subseteq S^\star$ be positive exact Dirichlet faces and
let $W\subseteq V\setminus T$.  Extend $x^U$ by zero on $T\setminus U$ and
put
\begin{equation*}
 y_W:=e_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)_W-e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_W.
\end{equation*}
Then $y_W\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \|y_W\|_2^2
 \leq\|x^T-x^U\|_{Q_{TT}}^2
 =2\bigl(\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)-\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)\bigr).
 \label{eq:finite-response-energy-packing}
\end{equation}
For arbitrary margins $m_v>0$, if
$H:=\{v\in W:y_v/\sqrt{d_v}\geq m_v\}$, then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{v\in H}d_vm_v^2
 \leq2\bigl(\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)-\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)\bigr).
 \label{eq:finite-response-weighted-packing}
\end{equation}
In particular, a common margin $\theta$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \vol(H)\leq
 \frac{2}{\theta^2}
 \bigl(\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)-\Psi_{\ell_\rho}(x^T)\bigr).
\end{equation*}
The note's charged volume differs only by its stated constant/additive
convention.  These bounds telescope over disjoint nested response epochs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\delta:=x^T_T-x^U_T\geq0$, Stieltjes signs give
$y_W=-Q_{WT}\delta\geq0$.  The Schur complement of $Q_{TT}$ in the principal
matrix on $T\cup W$ yields
\begin{equation*}
 Q_{WT}Q_{TT}^{-1}Q_{TW}\preceq Q_{WW}\preceq I.
\end{equation*}
Thus $Q_{WT}Q_{TT}^{-1/2}$ has spectral norm at most one and
$\|y_W\|_2\leq\|\delta\|_{Q_{TT}}$.  Completing the square around the exact
face solution $x^T$ proves the equality in
\cref{eq:finite-response-energy-packing}.  Every $v\in H$ obeys
$y_v^2\geq d_vm_v^2$, proving
\cref{eq:finite-response-weighted-packing}; objective drops telescope.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Twin petals obstruct the notched-double-sun RPPR trace]
\label{prop:notched-sun-rppr-trace-obstruction}
Fix $n\geq5$.  Let
$\mathcal A=\{u_1,\ldots,u_n\}$ induce the cycle
$u_1u_2\cdots u_nu_1$ plus the chord $u_1u_3$, and attach two degree-one
leaves $f_i,w_i$ to each $u_i$.  Write
$F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$ and $W=\{w_1,\ldots,w_n\}$.
For any single seed, any $\alpha\in(0,1)$, any $\rho>0$, and any positive
Dirichlet face $U$ containing $\mathcal A$, suppose $W\cap U=\varnothing$.
If neither $f_i$ nor $w_i$ is the seed and both are unadmitted, that is,
$f_i,w_i\notin U$, then
\begin{equation}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{f_i}
 =e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{w_i}
 =-\alpha\rho
  +\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{d_{u_i}}}\,x^U_{u_i}.
 \label{eq:notched-sun-twin-demand}
\end{equation}
Consequently the canonical all-violations gate
\cref{eq:boundary-violation-gate} admits $f_i$ if and only if it admits
$w_i$ at that face.

No choice of single seed and parameters can therefore produce canonical
faces that contain $\mathcal A$, then admit $\Theta(n)$ distinct vertices of
$F$, while maintaining $W\cap U=\varnothing$ along those faces.  More
precisely, an anchor
seed leaves all $n$ pairs tied; a seed $f_j$ can distinguish only its own pair
and is already present before the anchor face is reached; and a seed in $W$
either belongs to the initial positive face or has nonpositive shifted load,
in which case the zero solution prevents the anchor face from being reached.
Thus at most one vertex of $F$ can occur without its matching $W$ label,
independently of the
geometric-checkpoint schedule.

There is also a point-estimate consequence at one fixed common face.  Put
$\eta=\alpha\tau$ and suppose one simultaneous point-estimate gate call
returns estimates for both members of every nonseed twin pair, each satisfying
\cref{eq:finite-response-certificate} at that same $U$.  If the common exact
demand is greater than $\eta$, both estimates exceed $3\eta/4$ and both labels
are admitted; if the common demand is at most $\eta/2$, neither estimate
exceeds $3\eta/4$.  Only a common demand in $(\eta/2,\eta]$ permits that
simultaneous point-estimate call to split the pair.  This statement does not
couple arbitrary asynchronous interval refinements: such a procedure may
refine or defer the two labels at different times even though their exact
demands agree at a common face.  Finally, with
$\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$, the structural pruning threshold
$\tau_{\rm fb}:=\vartheta^5$ used for the prescribed response-interface trace
is not $\eta$ and yields no KKT-demand statement without an additional
derivation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a nonseed degree-one leaf $v$, the shifted load is
$(\ell_\rho)_v=-\alpha\rho$.  The two leaves $f_i,w_i$ have the same unique
neighbor $u_i$, the same degree, and identical rows into $U$.  Since their
current coordinates are zero,
\begin{equation*}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_v
 =-\alpha\rho-Q_{vu_i}x^U_{u_i}
 =-\alpha\rho
   +\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{d_{u_i}}}x^U_{u_i},
\end{equation*}
which proves \cref{eq:notched-sun-twin-demand}.  The exact gate includes
every boundary label of positive demand, so it cannot split a nonseed pair.

This equality applies as soon as $u_i$ is active: before that time neither
leaf can be a boundary label because $u_i$ is its only neighbor.  Hence an
exact nested canonical trace can split no pair except the one containing the
single seed.  If the seed is a report leaf with positive shifted load, the
initial face contains that seed $w_j$, so $W\cap U_0\neq\varnothing$.  If the
seed has nonpositive shifted load, all loads are nonpositive and zero is the
RPPR optimum, so no positive anchor face is
reached.  If the seed is a frontier leaf with positive shifted load, it
accounts for only one exceptional $F$ label.  These cases prove the trace
obstruction.

Finally, $d_{f_i}=d_{w_i}=1$.  If their common demand $e_i$ is greater than
$\eta$, \cref{eq:finite-response-certificate} gives
$\widehat e_{f_i},\widehat e_{w_i}>3\eta/4$.  If
$e_i\leq\eta/2$, it gives
$\widehat e_{f_i},\widehat e_{w_i}\leq3\eta/4$.
This proves the simultaneous point-estimate statement; it imposes no common
schedule on separately refined interval certificates.  The last claim is a
namespace separation: $\tau_{\rm fb}=\vartheta^5$ is a structural pruning
threshold applied to response losses, whereas $\eta$ controls the
right-hand-side demand in the finite-resolution gate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A matched-report degree lift still forbids a report-free
petal epoch]
\label{prop:matched-report-sun-gate-obstruction}
Fix an even $n\geq4$.  Let
$A=\{u_1,\ldots,u_n\}$ induce the cycle $C_n$, with indices modulo $n$.
Attach two vertices $f_i,w_i$ to each $u_i$, and add the perfect matching
on the report vertices:
\begin{equation}
 E=E(C_n)\cup\{u_if_i,u_iw_i:1\leq i\leq n\}
   \cup\{w_{2j-1}w_{2j}:1\leq j\leq n/2\}.
 \label{eq:matched-report-sun-edges}
\end{equation}
Write $F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$ and $W=\{w_1,\ldots,w_n\}$.
This is a simple unweighted graph with
\begin{equation*}
 d_{u_i}=4,\qquad d_{f_i}=1,\qquad d_{w_i}=2,
 \qquad \max_v d_v=4.
\end{equation*}
In particular, the source and report rows are no longer twins.  Moreover,
$Q_{AW}=-(\vartheta/\sqrt8)I$, where
$\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$, so the anchor--report cut has rank $n$ for every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$.

Consider the canonical exact single-seed RPPR gate
\cref{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement,eq:boundary-violation-gate} for arbitrary
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\rho>0$.  Let $U$ be any positive canonical face with
$W\cap U=\varnothing$, and let $B$ be its next all-positive boundary batch.
If $B\cap W=\varnothing$, then
\begin{equation}
 B\cap F\subseteq\{f_i:\text{the single seed is }u_i\}.
 \label{eq:matched-report-sun-only-seed-petal}
\end{equation}
Consequently every canonical prefix all of whose faces avoid $W$ contains at
most one vertex of $F$.  If a positive-load seed is $f_j$, that one vertex is
the seed already present initially; if a positive-load seed is $u_j$, only
$f_j$ can subsequently enter without a report.  Every nonpositive seed load
produces the zero solution, while a positive report seed violates the prefix
condition initially.
Thus this bounded-degree asymmetric cyclic family cannot realize
$\Theta(n)$ distinct attachment-site admissions during a report-free epoch,
despite its full-rank raw report cut.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=(1+\alpha)/2$ and
$\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$.  For a seed $s$,
\begin{equation*}
 (\ell_\rho)_s
 =\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d_s}}(1-\rho d_s).
\end{equation*}
If this quantity is nonpositive, all other shifted loads are negative, so zero
is optimal.  Hence assume it is positive, in which case $s\in U_0$.  A report
seed then already lies in $W\cap U_0$.  Thus, on a positive report-free
prefix, the seed lies in $A\cup F$ and every $w_i$ is a nonseed.

Suppose $B\cap W=\varnothing$.  If $u_j\in U$, then $w_j$ is a boundary
vertex.  Its matching partner also has current coordinate zero, and the exact
all-positive gate leaves $w_j$ out only if
\begin{equation}
 0\geq e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{w_j}
 =-\alpha\rho\sqrt2+\frac{\vartheta}{\sqrt8}x^U_{u_j}.
\end{equation}
Thus $x^U_{u_j}\leq4\alpha\rho/\vartheta$.  The same upper bound holds when
$u_j\notin U$, because its current coordinate is zero.  We have therefore
proved
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x^U_{u_j}\leq\frac{4\alpha\rho}{\vartheta}
 \qquad(1\leq j\leq n).
 \label{eq:matched-report-sun-anchor-upper}
\end{equation}

Now take $f_i\in B\cap F$.  It is not the seed, since every positive seed is
already in $U_0$, and its unique neighbor $u_i$ belongs to $U$.  Its strict
gate violation is
\begin{equation}
 0<e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{f_i}
 =-\alpha\rho+\frac{\vartheta}{2}x^U_{u_i},
 \qquad
 x^U_{u_i}>\frac{2\alpha\rho}{\vartheta}.
 \label{eq:matched-report-sun-demand-band}
\end{equation}
If $u_i$ is not the seed, its exact active equation, with the unadmitted
$f_i,w_i$ coordinates equal to zero, is
\begin{equation}
 a x^U_{u_i}
 -\frac{\vartheta}{4}
  \bigl(x^U_{u_{i-1}}+x^U_{u_{i+1}}\bigr)
 =-2\alpha\rho.
 \label{eq:matched-report-sun-anchor-balance}
\end{equation}
But \cref{eq:matched-report-sun-anchor-upper,eq:matched-report-sun-demand-band}
make the left-hand side strictly larger than
\begin{align*}
 \frac{2a\alpha\rho}{\vartheta}
 -\frac{\vartheta}{4}\frac{8\alpha\rho}{\vartheta}
 &=2\alpha\rho\left(\frac a\vartheta-1\right)
 =\frac{2\alpha^2\rho}{\vartheta}>0,
\end{align*}
contradicting the negative right-hand side of
\cref{eq:matched-report-sun-anchor-balance}.  Hence $u_i$ must be the single
seed, which proves \cref{eq:matched-report-sun-only-seed-petal}.

Apply the batch conclusion at every transition of a prefix whose new face
still avoids $W$.  A positive anchor seed supplies only its one matched petal,
a positive petal seed is already the only possible $F$ coordinate before any
batch, every nonpositive seed gives zero, and a positive report seed violates
the prefix premise.  These exhaust the vertices of
\cref{eq:matched-report-sun-edges} and prove the prefix claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The two-edge relay cannot be preloaded ahead of its petal]
\label{prop:active-relay-matched-sun-preload-obstruction}
Fix an even $n\geq4$.  Let $A=\{u_1,\ldots,u_n\}$ induce the cycle
$C_n$, attach a leaf $f_i$ and a two-edge stem $u_i r_i w_i$ at every
$u_i$, and add a perfect matching on $W=\{w_1,\ldots,w_n\}$.  Thus, with
$F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$ and $R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_n\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 E={}&E(C_n)\cup\{u_if_i,u_ir_i,r_iw_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\\
    &\cup\{w_{2j-1}w_{2j}:1\leq j\leq n/2\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:active-relay-matched-sun-edges}
\end{equation}
This is a simple unweighted graph with
\begin{equation*}
 d_{u_i}=4,\qquad d_{f_i}=1,\qquad d_{r_i}=d_{w_i}=2,
 \qquad \max_v d_v=4.
\end{equation*}
Its raw anchor--relay and relay--report blocks are
$Q_{AR}=-(\vartheta/\sqrt8)I$ and $Q_{RW}=-(\vartheta/2)I$, respectively,
and hence both have rank $n$ for every $\alpha\in(0,1)$.

Run the canonical exact single-seed RPPR gate
\cref{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement,eq:boundary-violation-gate} for arbitrary
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\rho>0$.  Let $U$ be a positive canonical face with
$W\cap U=\varnothing$, and let $B$ be its next canonical all-violations
boundary batch.
For every site whose relay $r_i$ is not the seed,
\begin{equation}
 r_i\in B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_i\in U\cup B.
 \label{eq:active-relay-petal-precedence}
\end{equation}
Thus, throughout every canonical prefix whose faces avoid $W$, each nonseed
relay is preceded or co-admitted by its source petal.  In particular, any
such face containing all of $R$ already contains at least $n-1$ vertices of
$F$; it contains all of $F$ unless the single positive seed is a relay.
After all relays are active, at most the source petal paired with that one
relay seed can still be admitted.  Consequently
\cref{eq:active-relay-matched-sun-edges} cannot have a report-free active-relay
phase followed by $\Theta(n)$ distinct source-petal admissions, for any seed,
$\alpha$, $\rho$, or canonical all-violations trace.
This does not rule out a different chronology consisting of paired
petal--relay admissions; it proves that such a chronology cannot furnish the
prescribed common face with preloaded relays and a later linear petal epoch.
For a noncanonical policy allowed to choose an arbitrary subset of current
positive violations, the calculation proves only that $f_i$ is simultaneously
eligible; such a policy may defer it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$.  At a single seed $s$ the shifted load is
\begin{equation*}
 (\ell_\rho)_s=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d_s}}(1-\rho d_s).
\end{equation*}
Thus a positive start requires respectively $\rho<1/4$, $\rho<1$,
$\rho<1/2$, and $\rho<1/2$ for seed types $A,F,R,W$.
If it is nonpositive, all other shifted loads are negative and zero is the
unique optimum.  Hence every nontrivial trace starts with a positive seed in
its initial face.  A positive seed in $W$ makes a report-free prefix
impossible from the start.

Now fix $i$ with $r_i$ not the seed and suppose $r_i\in B$.  Because
$W\cap U=\varnothing$ and the only neighbors of $r_i$ are $u_i,w_i$, the
boundary membership of $r_i$ forces $u_i\in U$.  If $f_i\in U$ there is
nothing to prove.  Otherwise $f_i$ is also a boundary vertex and is not the
seed.  Since both current coordinates are zero, their exact demands are
\begin{align}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{f_i}
  &=-\alpha\rho+\frac{\vartheta}{2}x^U_{u_i},
 \label{eq:active-relay-petal-demand}\\
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{r_i}
  &=-\alpha\rho\sqrt2+\frac{\vartheta}{\sqrt8}x^U_{u_i}.
 \label{eq:active-relay-demand}
\end{align}
The strict inequality $e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{r_i}>0$ defining $r_i\in B$
implies
\begin{equation}
 x^U_{u_i}>\frac{4\alpha\rho}{\vartheta}.
 \label{eq:active-relay-anchor-threshold}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \cref{eq:active-relay-petal-demand} gives
$e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{f_i}>\alpha\rho>0$.  The canonical gate contains every
positive boundary violation, so $f_i\in B$.  This proves
\cref{eq:active-relay-petal-precedence} for every canonical all-violations
batch, including batches of arbitrary size.

It remains only to count the seed exception.  For a positive seed in $A$ or
$F$, no relay is the seed and \cref{eq:active-relay-petal-precedence} applies
at all $n$ sites.  For a positive seed $r_j$, it applies at the other $n-1$
sites, while $f_j$ is the sole petal that may enter after its relay.  A
positive seed in $W$ was already excluded, and all nonpositive seed loads give
the zero optimum.  These are all four vertex types, cover every possibly finer
seed orbit created by the fixed matching, and complete the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 E={}&E(C_n[A])\cup E(C_n[F])
      \cup\{u_if_i,u_ir_i,r_iw_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\\
    &\cup\{w_{2j-1}w_{2j}:1\leq j\leq n/2\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-relay-edges}
\end{equation}
It is simple and unweighted, with
\begin{equation*}
 d_{u_i}=4,\qquad d_{f_i}=3,\qquad
 d_{r_i}=d_{w_i}=2,\qquad \max_v d_v=4.
\end{equation*}
The raw blocks $Q_{AF}=-(\vartheta/\sqrt{12})I$,
$Q_{AR}=-(\vartheta/\sqrt8)I$, and
$Q_{RW}=-(\vartheta/2)I$ all have rank $n$ for
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, where $\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$.

Run the exact single-seed canonical all-violations gate
\cref{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement,eq:boundary-violation-gate} for arbitrary
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\rho>0$.  Let $U$ be a positive canonical face such
that
\begin{equation*}
 W\cap U=\varnothing,\qquad R\subseteq U,
\end{equation*}
and let $B$ be its next canonical all-violations boundary batch.  If also
$B\cap W=\varnothing$, then
\begin{equation}
 F\setminus U
 \subseteq
 \{f_j:\text{the single seed is }u_j\text{ or }r_j\}.
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-at-most-seed-exception}
\end{equation}
In particular, $|F\setminus U|\leq1$.  A positive petal seed leaves no
exception because it is present initially; a positive anchor or relay seed
can leave only its paired petal; a positive report seed violates the
report-free premise initially; and every nonpositive seed load gives the zero
solution.  Thus no canonical report-free chronology can activate all relays
and then admit $\Theta(n)$ later distinct petals.  If a report enters with or
before the last relay, or in the first post-last-relay batch, the required
report-free epoch has already failed.  Otherwise the face immediately after
the last-relay batch and its next batch satisfy the two report-free premises,
so all but at most one petal entered earlier or in the last-relay batch.  The
conclusion is only a KKT chronology obstruction for the canonical
all-violations gate, not a reporter, response-rank, or work lower bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $t:=a/\vartheta>1$.  At the single seed $s$,
\begin{equation*}
 (\ell_\rho)_s
 =\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d_s}}(1-\rho d_s).
\end{equation*}
Hence positive starts require respectively
$\rho<1/4,1/3,1/2,1/2$ for seed types $A,F,R,W$.  If the seed load is
nonpositive, all shifted loads are nonpositive and zero is the unique
optimum.  A positive $W$ seed belongs to the initial face.  It remains to
consider positive seeds in $A\cup F\cup R$.

For every site define the dimensionless current coordinates
\begin{equation}
 p_i:=\frac{\vartheta x^U_{u_i}}{\alpha\rho},
 \qquad
 q_i:=\frac{\vartheta x^U_{r_i}}{\alpha\rho\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-normalized-anchor-relay}
\end{equation}
Because $r_i\in U$ and $w_i\notin U$, every $w_i$ is a boundary vertex.
Its matching partner also has current coordinate zero.  Since the canonical
batch contains every positive violation, $B\cap W=\varnothing$ is exactly the
report-quietness inequality
\begin{equation*}
 0\geq e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{w_i}
 =-\alpha\rho\sqrt2+\frac{\vartheta}{2}x^U_{r_i},
 \qquad q_i\leq2.
\end{equation*}
If $r_i$ is not the seed, its active equation, with $x^U_{w_i}=0$, gives
\begin{equation}
 a x^U_{r_i}-\frac{\vartheta}{\sqrt8}x^U_{u_i}
 =-\alpha\rho\sqrt2,
 \qquad
 p_i=4+4tq_i.
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-relay-band}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
 4<p_i\leq4+8t
 \qquad(r_i\text{ not the seed}).
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-anchor-cap}
\end{equation}
In particular, the equation itself forces $u_i\in U$.  If instead $r_j$ is
the positive seed, its seeded active equation and $q_j\leq2$ give
\begin{equation*}
 p_j=4tq_j-\frac{2(1-2\rho)}{\rho}
 \leq 8t+4-\frac2\rho<4+8t.
\end{equation*}
Consequently the upper bound in
\cref{eq:petal-cycle-anchor-cap} holds at every anchor, including the one
paired with a relay seed.

Suppose now that $f_i\notin U$ and that neither $u_i$ nor $r_i$ is the seed.
Then $u_i,r_i\in U$, $x^U_{f_i}=0$, and the nonseed anchor equation is
\begin{equation*}
 a x^U_{u_i}
 -\frac{\vartheta}{4}
  \bigl(x^U_{u_{i-1}}+x^U_{u_{i+1}}\bigr)
 -\frac{\vartheta}{\sqrt8}x^U_{r_i}
 =-2\alpha\rho.
\end{equation*}
The petal-cycle neighbors do not occur in this row, and the inactive
coordinate $x^U_{f_i}$ contributes zero.  Divide by $\alpha\rho$, substitute
$q_i=(p_i-4)/(4t)$ from
\cref{eq:petal-cycle-relay-band}, and rearrange to obtain
\begin{equation}
 p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}
 =8+4\left(t-\frac1{8t}\right)p_i+\frac2t
 >8+16t,
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-neighbor-lower}
\end{equation}
where the difference between the middle expression and $8+16t$ is exactly
$4(t-1/(8t))(p_i-4)>0$.  But
\cref{eq:petal-cycle-anchor-cap}, including its relay-seed case, gives
\begin{equation*}
 p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}\leq2(4+8t)=8+16t,
\end{equation*}
a contradiction.  Therefore an inactive $f_i$ can occur only at the site of
an anchor or relay seed.  A petal seed is already active, and the four seed
types considered above exhaust every orbit, including the finer parity
orbits induced by the fixed matching.  This proves
\cref{eq:petal-cycle-at-most-seed-exception}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Local reversal, simultaneous timing, and the full trace ledger]
\label{rem:petal-cycle-relay-trace-ledger}
When $f_i,f_{i-1},f_{i+1}$ are all inactive and $f_i$ is not the seed, its
exact demand is
\begin{equation*}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x^U)_{f_i}
 =-\alpha\rho\sqrt3
  +\frac{\vartheta}{\sqrt{12}}x^U_{u_i}.
\end{equation*}
It crosses exactly when $p_i>6$, whereas a nonseed relay with no active report
crosses when $p_i>4$.  Thus the isolated window $4<p_i\leq6$ is genuine.
Once a petal-cycle neighbor is active, its positive contribution can only
make the remaining petal cross earlier.  The proposition shows that global
anchor balance and report quietness exhaust this local window before a common
all-relay face: apart from the one seed-site exception, petals are admitted
strictly earlier than or simultaneously with the last-relay batch.  It does
not analyze an arbitrary positive-subset policy, which could defer a current
positive violation.

These are exact KKT zero-sign comparisons at regularization level $\rho$.
They are not the finite-band tolerance $\eta=\alpha\tau$, the structural
pruning threshold $\tau_{\rm fb}=\vartheta^5$, or a response-loss scale.  Use
the full vector
\begin{equation*}
 \mathbf L=
 (C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj},R_{\rm int},C_{\rm pre},C_{\rm ctl},
  C_{\rm rec},C_{\rm resp},M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit}).
\end{equation*}
For a tested face or prefix, let $\mathbf P_U$ contain every charge already
paid to expose the graph, reach and certify all earlier canonical batches,
construct the exact restricted solutions, and retain current state.  It
includes first and repeated row reads, adjacency and external interaction
rounds, preprocessing, gate/control work, recurrence or response arithmetic,
old-face reads, persistent and scratch cells, recovery/materialization, and
required output.  Additive coordinates of $\mathbf P_U$ remain additive and
memory coordinates retain the larger peak.

Here $\operatorname{cvol}(V)=15n$.  If the bounded-degree gadget rows and
degrees have not been retained, validating all $n$ local contradictions from
the input uses one nonadaptive streamed scan and incremental vector
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathbf L_{\rm fresh}
 =\bigl(\Theta(n),1,0,0,\Theta(n),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-fresh-audit-vector}
\end{equation}
If those $\Theta(n)$ records were already retained and charged in
$M_{\rm pers}(\mathbf P_U)$, the incremental vector is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathbf L_{\rm stored}
 =\bigl(0,0,0,0,\Theta(n),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:petal-cycle-stored-audit-vector}
\end{equation}
For one claimed site, replace every $n$-dependent entry by $O(1)$.  Building
a cache during validation is the fresh case, with cache writes in
$C_{\rm ctl}$ and retained cells in $M_{\rm pers}$, not free stored reuse.

An actual gate trace adds its representation-dependent numerical ledger.
Fresh incidence reads and adaptive adjacency batches belong to
$C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj}$; externally ordered requests to $R_{\rm int}$;
graph-dependent setup to $C_{\rm pre}$; gate scheduling and membership to
$C_{\rm ctl}$; iterative restricted-face work to $C_{\rm rec}$; and factor,
Schur, or other response construction, application, and query work to
$C_{\rm resp}$.  Persistent and scratch state, explicit recovery or
current-vector writes, and task-required replies remain separate in
$M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit}$.  If the task requires
batch labels, it pays $C_{\rm emit}=\Theta(|B|)$; the proposition itself
requires no intermediate vector or emitted batch.  Since the legal chronology
already fails, the raw full-rank cut blocks do not prove genuinely changing
response directions along an RPPR trace.  This is exact-real-cell accounting
with no reporter lower bound, word/bit complexity, finite-precision
robustness, or numerical-stability conclusion.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[An independent cycle feed still reports before any petal]
\label{prop:double-cycle-feed-report-obstruction}
Fix an even $n\geq4$.  Let
$\mathcal B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}$ and
$A=\{u_1,\ldots,u_n\}$ each induce a cycle $C_n$.  Add the sitewise edges
$b_iu_i$, $b_ir_i$, $u_if_i$, and $r_iw_i$, and add a perfect matching on
$W=\{w_1,\ldots,w_n\}$.  Thus, with
$R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_n\}$ and $F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 E={}&E(C_n[\mathcal B])\cup E(C_n[A])
      \cup\{b_iu_i,b_ir_i,u_if_i,r_iw_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\\
    &\cup\{w_{2j-1}w_{2j}:1\leq j\leq n/2\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-edges}
\end{equation}
This is a simple unweighted graph with
\begin{equation*}
 d_{b_i}=d_{u_i}=4,\qquad d_{r_i}=d_{w_i}=2,
 \qquad d_{f_i}=1,
\end{equation*}
and its four sitewise cut blocks all have rank $n$ for
$\alpha\in(0,1)$.

Run the exact single-seed canonical all-violations gate
\cref{eq:restricted-rppr-settlement,eq:boundary-violation-gate} with
$s=e_{b_j}$ for an arbitrary backbone site $b_j$, arbitrary
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, and arbitrary $\rho>0$.  Along every canonical prefix whose
faces and next batches avoid $W$, the faces and next batches also avoid $F$.
More precisely, at every stage $k$ reached before either set is admitted,
\begin{equation}
 W\cap(U_k\cup B_k)=\varnothing
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 F\cap(U_k\cup B_k)=\varnothing.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-report-first}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if a canonical batch first intersects $F$, that same batch
intersects $W$.  Hence the independently fed graph has no nonempty
report-free petal epoch, let alone the prescribed report-free petal/attachment
trace with linearly many distinct admissions and changing report-response
directions.  This conclusion makes no claim about the trace after $W$ enters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=(1+\alpha)/2$, $\vartheta=(1-\alpha)/2$, and
$t:=a/\vartheta>1$.  A backbone seed has positive shifted load exactly when
$\rho<1/4$.  If $\rho\geq1/4$, every shifted load is nonpositive and zero is
the unique optimum, so assume $\rho<1/4$.

Fix a canonical face before the first $F$- or $W$-admission, and write its
restricted optimum as $x$.  Define, with inactive coordinates set to zero,
\begin{equation}
 p_i:=\frac{\vartheta x_{b_i}}{\alpha\rho},\qquad
 s_i:=\frac{\vartheta x_{u_i}}{\alpha\rho},\qquad
 q_i:=\frac{\vartheta x_{r_i}}{\alpha\rho\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-normalization}
\end{equation}
We prove inductively that every active anchor $u_i$ has its relay $r_i$
active.  This is initially vacuous.  Suppose it holds at the current face and
that the next canonical batch avoids $W$.  Report quietness gives
\begin{equation}
q_i\leq2
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-report-cap}
\end{equation}
for every active relay.  Let $M:=\max_i s_i$.  Suppose for contradiction that
$M>2$ and choose $i$ with $s_i=M$.  Then $u_i$ is active and its paired relay
is active by the induction hypothesis.  Neither coordinate is
the backbone seed, and $F\cup W$ is still inactive, so their exact active
equations are
\begin{align}
 t q_i-\frac{p_i}{4}&=-1,
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-relay-equation}\\
 t s_i-\frac{p_i+s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}}4&=-2.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-anchor-equation}
\end{align}
Subtracting gives
\begin{equation}
 t(M-q_i)=\frac{s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}}4-1
 \leq\frac{M-2}{2}.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-maximum-inequality}
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:double-cycle-feed-report-cap}, the left side is at least
$t(M-2)$, contradicting $t>1$.  Therefore $M\leq2$.  The exact demand
of every inactive petal is consequently
\begin{equation}
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{f_i}}{\alpha\rho}
 =-1+\frac{s_i}{2}\leq0,
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-petal-quiet}
\end{equation}
so the next batch contains no petal.

It remains to preserve the anchor--relay induction.  Fix an inactive anchor
$u_i$.  If $r_i$ is already active, the invariant is immediate.  Suppose
instead that both $u_i$ and $r_i$ are inactive.  Their exact demands are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{u_i}}{\alpha\rho}
 =-2+\frac{p_i+s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}}4,
 \qquad
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{r_i}}{\alpha\rho\sqrt2}
 =-1+\frac{p_i}{4}.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-inactive-demands}
\end{equation}
The just-proved bound gives $s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}\leq4$.  Thus a positive anchor
demand forces $p_i>4$, which makes the inactive relay's demand positive too.
The canonical all-violations batch therefore co-admits the two vertices.  In
either case the relay is active no later than its anchor.  This closes the
induction.  Repeating it for every batch that avoids $W$ proves that such a
prefix is petal-free, and hence proves the first-batch dichotomy.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The anchor-seed counterrange has only three
report-free petals]
\label{prop:double-cycle-anchor-seed-obstruction}
Fix an even $n\geq6$ in the double-cycle graph
\cref{eq:double-cycle-feed-edges}.  Run the exact canonical all-violations
gate from the anchor seed $s=e_{u_j}$ in the nonempty range
\begin{equation*}
 0<\rho<\frac{1-\alpha}{8}.
\end{equation*}
Before the first batch that intersects $W$, at most the three sites
$f_j,f_{j-1},f_{j+1}$ can be admitted.  More sharply, if a canonical batch
first intersects $\{f_{j-2},f_{j+2}\}$, then that same batch contains $w_j$
unless a report was admitted earlier.  Consequently this anchor-seed
counterexecution has no report-free epoch with $\Omega(n)$ distinct petal
admissions.  In particular it does not rescue the double-cycle feed as the
required long varying-response witness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Relabel the seed site as $0$, put
\begin{equation*}
 t=\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}>1,
 \qquad \lambda=\frac1{2\rho}>2(t+1),
 \qquad
 y_i=\frac{\vartheta x_{f_i}}{\alpha\rho},
\end{equation*}
and use the normalized coordinates $p_i,s_i,q_i$ from
\cref{eq:double-cycle-feed-normalization}.  Inactive coordinates are zero.
At an active nonseed site the exact equations are
\begin{align}
 t p_i-\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}+s_i}{4}-\frac{q_i}{2}&=-2,
 &
 t q_i-\frac{p_i}{4}&=-1,
 \notag\\
 t s_i-\frac{s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}+p_i}{4}-\frac{y_i}{2}&=-2,
 &
 t y_i-\frac{s_i}{2}&=-1.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-active-equations}
\end{align}
The $u_0$ equation has right side $\lambda-2$ instead of $-2$.
The inactive relay, petal, and report gates are respectively
$p_i>4$, $s_i>2$, and $q_i>2$.  On each fixed face all these coordinates are
affine in $\lambda$ with nonnegative slope, by inverse positivity of the
restricted Stieltjes matrix; every coordinate below that is connected to the
seed has strictly positive slope.

The initial face is $\{u_0\}$ and $s_0=(\lambda-2)/t$.  Hence $f_0$ is in
the first batch, while the three other possible violations
$b_0,u_{-1},u_1$ have the same normalized demand $-2+s_0/4$ and are in that
batch exactly when $\lambda>8t+2$.  If only $f_0$ enters, exact settlement on
$\{u_0,f_0\}$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 s_0=\frac{2(2\lambda t-4t-1)}{(2t-1)(2t+1)}.
\end{equation*}
Thus the same triple is the entire next batch exactly when
\begin{equation}
 \lambda>\Lambda_0:=8t+2-\frac{3}{2t};
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-launch}
\end{equation}
at equality the strict gate remains closed, and for
$\lambda\leq\Lambda_0$ the trace stops after $f_0$.  Therefore every trace
that proceeds farther reaches, either in one batch or in two,
\begin{equation*}
 U_{\rm launch}=\{u_0,f_0,b_0,u_{-1},u_1\}.
\end{equation*}
This accounts for the simultaneous initial batches rather than inferring the
chronology from the first petal alone.

Reflection symmetry is preserved by every canonical batch.  At the launch
face it gives $p_0=s_{-1}=s_1=:z$, where
\begin{equation*}
 t s_0-\frac{3z}{4}-\frac{y_0}{2}=\lambda-2,
 \qquad
 t y_0-\frac{s_0}{2}=-1,
 \qquad
 t z-\frac{s_0}{4}=-2.
\end{equation*}
In particular,
\begin{equation*}
 z=\frac{2(2\lambda t-16t^2-4t+3)}{t(16t^2-7)}.
\end{equation*}
The complete set of possible launch-face violations is
\begin{equation}
 \{f_{-1},f_1\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z>2,
 \qquad
 \{b_{-1},b_1,r_0\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z>4,
 \qquad
 \{u_{-2},u_2\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z>8.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-launch-gates}
\end{equation}
All groups whose displayed strict inequality holds are admitted in the same
all-violations batch.  Later settlement can split the $b_{\pm1}$ and $r_0$
crossings, so we next check their order rather than assuming the launch-face
tie persists.

If $u_2$ becomes positive while inactive, then $f_1$ and $b_1$ are already
positive or co-positive.  The former follows from the thresholds $s_1>8$
and $s_1>2$; for the latter, an inactive $b_1$ has demand
$-2+(p_0+s_1)/4>0$.  The relay $r_0$ is also active or co-positive.  If
$f_1$ is still inactive this is immediate from the launch identity
$p_0=s_1>8$.  Otherwise, before $u_2$ enters, the only two faces with $r_0$
inactive have $b_1$ respectively inactive and active.  At the formal
threshold $s_1=8$, direct solution of
\cref{eq:double-cycle-anchor-active-equations} gives
\begin{equation}
 p_0-4=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{8t^2-3}{2t^2},&b_1\notin U,\\[5pt]
 \dfrac{4(16t^2-5)}{(4t-1)(4t+1)},&b_1\in U,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-precedence}
\end{equation}
which is strictly positive for $t>1$.  Positive face slopes then prove the
claim at every $s_1>8$.

There are no omitted earlier $r_1$ or $b_2$ faces in this comparison.  Once
$f_1,b_1$ are active, the $r_1$ threshold is $p_1=4$.  At that equality,
with $r_0$ respectively inactive and active, the exact values are
\begin{equation*}
 s_1-8=
 \frac{2(32t^3-16t^2-2t+3)}{8t^2-1},
 \qquad
 s_1-8=
 \frac{8(16t^3-8t^2-3t+2)}{16t^2-3},
\end{equation*}
both positive for $t>1$.  Thus $u_2$ is admitted no later than $r_1$;
$b_2$, whose gate before $u_2$ would require $p_1>8$, cannot precede it
either.  The same conclusions hold at the negative indices.

It remains to exhaust the faces on which $f_2$ could first violate.  The
preceding order shows that such a face contains
\begin{equation*}
 \{u_0,f_0,b_0,u_{\pm1},f_{\pm1},b_{\pm1},r_0,u_{\pm2}\}.
\end{equation*}
Before $f_{\pm2}$ or a report enters, its only optional local groups are
$b_{\pm2}$ and $r_{\pm1}$.  Write
$\iota=\mathbf 1_{\{b_{\pm2}\subseteq U\}}$ and
$\delta=\mathbf 1_{\{r_{\pm1}\subseteq U\}}$.  Solve the four corresponding
restricted systems and formally set $s_2=2$.  The resulting report margin
$\Gamma_{\iota\delta}:=(q_0-2)|_{s_2=2}$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_{00}&=\frac{(2t+1)(8t-5)}{t(16t^2-3)},\\
 \Gamma_{10}&=\frac{(2t+1)(32t^3-16t^2-4t+1)}
 {t(64t^4-8t^2-1)},\\
 \Gamma_{01}&=\frac{64t^4-8t^3-28t^2+t+3}
 {t(2t-1)(2t+1)(4t-1)(4t+1)},\\
 \Gamma_{11}&=\frac{256t^5-128t^3-8t^2+16t+1}
 {64t^4(2t-1)(2t+1)}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-gamma}
\end{equation}
All four are strictly positive.  For example, after substituting $h=t-1>0$,
the nontrivial numerator polynomials in the last three lines become
\begin{align*}
 &32h^3+80h^2+60h+13,\\
 &64h^4+248h^3+332h^2+177h+32,\\
 &256h^5+1280h^4+2432h^3+2168h^2+896h+137.
\end{align*}
The exact slope ratios $(\partial_\lambda q_0)/(\partial_\lambda s_2)$ on
the same four faces, in the order $00,10,01,11$, are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 &\frac{4(2t-1)(2t+1)}{16t^2-3},\qquad
 \frac{2(2t-1)(2t+1)(8t^2-1)}{64t^4-8t^2-1},\\
 &\frac{128t^4-48t^2+5}
 {2(2t-1)(2t+1)(4t-1)(4t+1)},\qquad
 \frac{512t^6-256t^4+40t^2-1}
 {128t^4(2t-1)(2t+1)}.
\end{split}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-slope}
\end{equation}
They too are positive for $t>1$.  Hence on every possible face
\begin{equation*}
 s_2>2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_0>2.
\end{equation*}
The $f_2$ and $w_0$ violations are therefore admitted in the same canonical
batch, unless $w_0$ entered earlier.

For completeness, no deeper active coordinate can invalidate the four-face
exhaustion.  A new $r_2$ would require $p_2>4$.  On a face with $b_2$ active,
set $p_2=4$ and flag whether $u_2$ and $r_1$ are active.  The four exact
values of $q_0-2$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{00}&=\frac{(2t+1)(64t^3-16t^2-8t+1)}{t(16t^2-3)},\\
 \Delta_{01}&=\frac{(2t+1)(64t^4-16t^3-16t^2+3t+1)}
 {t^2(16t^2-3)},\\
 \Delta_{10}&=\frac{(2t+1)(64t^3-24t^2-8t+1)}{t(24t^2-5)},\\
 \Delta_{11}&=\frac{(2t-1)(512t^5+320t^4-64t^3-64t^2-8t-1)}
 {t(192t^4-48t^2+1)}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-deep}
\end{equation}
Every denominator is positive for $t>1$, and substituting $h=t-1$ makes
every displayed numerator polynomial coefficient positive.  Positive slopes
again show that $p_2>4$ forces a report.

The first farther cycle site needs a separate antipodal check when $n=6$.
There $b_3=b_{-3}$ sees both $b_2$ and $b_{-2}$, so its inactive demand is
$-2+p_2/2$ and its strict threshold is $p_2>4$; the
$\Delta_{\iota\delta}$ calculation therefore excludes it before a report.
Likewise $u_3=u_{-3}$ has threshold $s_2>4$, but already
$s_2>2\Longrightarrow q_0>2$ by
\cref{eq:double-cycle-anchor-gamma,eq:double-cycle-anchor-slope}.  For even
$n\geq8$, each first-frontier site $b_{\pm3}$ or $u_{\pm3}$ sees only its one
active $\pm2$ neighbor.  Its respective threshold is $p_2>8$ or $s_2>8$;
the former has already crossed the relay/report threshold, and the latter has
already crossed the petal/report implication.  All still farther vertices
are excluded by the same first-frontier argument.

Thus either the canonical trace stops, a report appears before the next
petal layer, or its first $f_{\pm2}$ batch co-admits $w_0$.  The only petals
in a strictly report-free prefix are consequently
\begin{equation}
 F\cap U\subseteq\{f_0,f_{-1},f_1\},
 \qquad |F\cap U|\leq3.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-three-petal}
\end{equation}
This is $O(1)$, not $\Omega(n)$.  The required chronology fails before any
response-direction lower bound is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A relay seed reports in its first propagating batch]
\label{prop:double-cycle-relay-seed-obstruction}
Fix an even $n\geq4$ in the double-cycle graph
\cref{eq:double-cycle-feed-edges}.  For every site $j$, every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, and every $\rho>0$, run the exact canonical
all-violations gate with relay seed $s=e_{r_j}$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 t=\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha},\qquad
 \rho_W(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2(2t+1)},\qquad
 \rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2(4t+1)}.
\end{equation*}
Then $0<\rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha)<\rho_W(\alpha)<1/2$, and the
initialized face $U_0$ together with its first exact boundary batch $B_0$
has the exhaustive split below, with $U_0=B_0=\varnothing$ denoting that the
gate does not initialize:
\begin{equation}
 (U_0,B_0)=
 \begin{cases}
  (\varnothing,\varnothing),&\rho\geq1/2,\\
  (\{r_j\},\varnothing),&\rho_W(\alpha)\leq\rho<1/2,\\
  (\{r_j\},\{w_j\}),&\rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha)\leq\rho<\rho_W(\alpha),\\
  (\{r_j\},\{w_j,b_j\}),&0<\rho<\rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha).
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:double-cycle-relay-first-batch}
\end{equation}
At either equality endpoint, the corresponding zero-demand vertex is not
admitted by the strict gate: $w_j\notin B_0$ at
$\rho=\rho_W(\alpha)$, whereas
$b_j\notin B_0$ and $w_j\in B_0$ at
$\rho=\rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha)$.  Thus every relay-seeded trace that propagates
meets $W$ in its first committed batch, before any petal is admitted.  In
particular this seed orbit supplies no nonempty report-free later-petal
epoch.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relay seed has shifted load
\begin{equation*}
 (\ell_\rho)_{r_j}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt2}-\alpha\rho\sqrt2.
\end{equation*}
If $\rho\geq1/2$, it and all other shifted loads are nonpositive.  The zero
vector is therefore the unique optimum, including at equality, by strict
convexity and the KKT conditions.  Now suppose $0<\rho<1/2$ and write
$\lambda=1/(2\rho)>1$.  The only positive shifted load is at $r_j$, so
$U_0=\{r_j\}$.  Exact settlement on this singleton gives
\begin{equation}
 x_{r_j}=\frac{\alpha\rho\sqrt2(\lambda-1)}{a},
 \qquad
 q_j:=\frac{\vartheta x_{r_j}}{\alpha\rho\sqrt2}
      =\frac{\lambda-1}{t}.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-relay-seed-coordinate}
\end{equation}
Its only boundary vertices are $w_j$ and $b_j$, whose normalized exact
demands are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{w_j}}{\alpha\rho\sqrt2}
 =-1+\frac{q_j}{2},
 \qquad
 \frac{e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{b_j}}{\alpha\rho}
 =-2+\frac{q_j}{2}.
 \label{eq:double-cycle-relay-seed-demands}
\end{equation}
Consequently $w_j$ violates exactly when
$\lambda>2t+1$, equivalently $\rho<\rho_W(\alpha)$, and $b_j$ violates
exactly when $\lambda>4t+1$, equivalently
$\rho<\rho_{\mathcal B}(\alpha)$.  The strict inequalities also give the
two endpoint claims.  Since $\rho_{\mathcal B}<\rho_W$ and there are no
other boundary vertices, these are all possible simultaneous first batches
and prove \cref{eq:double-cycle-relay-first-batch}.  The calculation is
local to site $j$ and does not use the matching neighbor of $w_j$; hence it
covers every rotation and both parity classes of the fixed matching on $W$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Seed and batching scope, and the full double-cycle ledger]
\label{rem:double-cycle-feed-trace-ledger}
The backbone-seed restriction is substantive.  If the seed is instead
$u_j$ and
\begin{equation}
 0<\rho<\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-seed-counterrange}
\end{equation}
then the initial face is $\{u_j\}$, with
$x_{u_j}=\alpha(1/2-2\rho)/a$, and
\begin{equation*}
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{f_j}
 =-\alpha\rho+\frac{\vartheta}{2}x_{u_j}>0,
 \qquad
 e_{\ell_\rho}(x)_{w_i}=-\alpha\rho\sqrt2<0
 \quad(1\leq i\leq n).
\end{equation*}
Thus an anchor seed gives an exact report-free first-petal batch; no
all-seed-orbit version of \cref{prop:double-cycle-feed-report-obstruction}
follows.  The complete canonical continuation is nevertheless stopped by
\cref{prop:double-cycle-anchor-seed-obstruction}: at most the three petals at
sites $j,j-1,j+1$ precede the first report, and the first $j\pm2$ petal batch
co-admits $w_j$.  A report seed violates
report exclusion initially, a petal seed is an initial attachment rather than
a later admission.  The remaining relay-seed orbit is exhausted by
\cref{prop:double-cycle-relay-seed-obstruction}: it either has no initialized
face, terminates on its singleton face, or admits $w_j$ in its first batch,
possibly together with $b_j$, and admits no petal first.  Thus the backbone,
anchor, and relay candidate orbits all fail the prescribed long report-free
later-petal trace.  This retires this double-cycle graph family for that
witness, not all possible cyclic witnesses.  The proofs use canonical all-violations
batching.  A positive-subset policy could defer a simultaneously positive
report and is not covered.

These are exact KKT zero-sign statements at regularization level $\rho$, not
finite-band, word/bit, finite-precision, or stability claims.  Although
$Q_{\mathcal B A}=-(\vartheta/4)I$,
$Q_{\mathcal B R}=-(\vartheta/\sqrt8)I$,
$Q_{AF}=-(\vartheta/2)I$, and
$Q_{RW}=-(\vartheta/2)I$ have full rank, the failed canonical chronology
shows only that these cuts do not realize the prescribed report-free
petal/attachment trace with changing response directions.  It proves nothing
about response directions after $W$ enters and yields neither a reporter,
response-rank, nor work lower bound.

Use the shared order
\begin{equation*}
 \mathbf L=(C_{\rm adj},R_{\rm adj},R_{\rm int},C_{\rm pre},C_{\rm ctl},
 C_{\rm rec},C_{\rm resp},M_{\rm pers},M_{\rm tmp},C_{\rm mat},C_{\rm emit}).
\end{equation*}
For any tested canonical prefix, let $\mathbf P_k$ be its complete paid
eleven-vector: it includes graph exposure and repeated row reads, adjacency
and external rounds, preprocessing, all exact-face recurrence or response
work, gate/control work, retained and scratch state, recovery/materialization,
and every task-required emission through face $k$.  The graph has
$\operatorname{cvol}(V)=18n$.  If its bounded-degree rows have not been
retained, one nonadaptive streamed validation of all $n$ implications above
has incremental vector
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathbf L_{\rm fresh}
 =\bigl(\Theta(n),1,0,0,\Theta(n),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-fresh-audit-vector}
\end{equation}
If those rows were already retained and charged in
$M_{\rm pers}(\mathbf P_k)$, validation has
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathbf L_{\rm stored}
 =\bigl(0,0,0,0,\Theta(n),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:double-cycle-feed-stored-audit-vector}
\end{equation}
The total ledger is $\mathbf P_k\oplus\Delta\mathbf L$, where additive
coordinates are summed and the two memory coordinates take their larger
peak.  Constructing a cache during validation is the fresh case with its
writes in $C_{\rm ctl}$ and retained cells in $M_{\rm pers}$, not free reuse.
If batch labels are required, their writes belong to $C_{\rm emit}$; the
mathematical proposition itself requires no intermediate output.  All
numerical face solves, old-face reads, response queries, state writes, and
materialization remain in $\mathbf P_k$ and are not hidden by either
incremental validation vector.

For the fixed anchor seed in
\cref{prop:double-cycle-anchor-seed-obstruction}, let
$\mathbf P_{\rm firstW}$ be the complete paid prefix through the first report
batch, or through termination if there is no report.  The local exact
chronology and its constant-radius case tables have incremental vectors
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathbf L^{\rm anchor}_{\rm fresh}
 =\bigl(O(1),1,0,0,O(1),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr),
 \qquad
 \Delta\mathbf L^{\rm anchor}_{\rm stored}
 =\bigl(0,0,0,0,O(1),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:double-cycle-anchor-audit-vectors}
\end{equation}
Auditing every rotated anchor site instead restores the $\Theta(n)$ fresh
scan/control or stored control entries above; a streamed scratch peak remains
$O(1)$.  In either form the total is the fully charged prefix
$\mathbf P_{\rm firstW}\oplus\Delta\mathbf L^{\rm anchor}$; no face solve,
batch emission, or response work is erased by the incremental proof audit.

For the fixed relay seed in
\cref{prop:double-cycle-relay-seed-obstruction}, let
$\mathbf P^{\rm relay}_{\rm firstW}$ be the complete paid prefix through the
first report batch, or through termination or the absent initialization in
the other two cases of \cref{eq:double-cycle-relay-first-batch}.  Only the
constant-radius proof audit is incremental:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathbf L^{\rm relay}_{\rm fresh}
 =\bigl(O(1),1,0,0,O(1),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr),
 \qquad
 \Delta\mathbf L^{\rm relay}_{\rm stored}
 =\bigl(0,0,0,0,O(1),0,0,0,O(1),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:double-cycle-relay-audit-vectors}
\end{equation}
The total is
$\mathbf P^{\rm relay}_{\rm firstW}\oplus
\Delta\mathbf L^{\rm relay}$, so every exact-face solve, row read, gate
decision, state write, and task-required batch emission remains charged in
the prefix; there is no reporter or response work hidden in the local vector.
Auditing every rotated relay site restores the $\Theta(n)$ fresh scan/control
or stored control entries of the global audit, while scratch remains $O(1)$.
All of these relay statements are exact-real canonical claims only, with no
arbitrary positive-subset, post-report, reporter/work lower-bound,
finite-precision, or stability consequence.
\end{remark}

\begin{problem}[Finite-resolution cyclic response]
\label{prob:finite-resolution-response}
For $\eta=\alpha\tau$, maintain the certificates
\cref{eq:finite-response-certificate}, or the strictly weaker interval
certificates \cref{eq:finite-response-intervals}, and report the
finite-resolution gate
with
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\rm resp}^{(\eta)}
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{1+\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star(\rho))}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:finite-resolution-response-target}
\end{equation}
No exact positive-versus-nonpositive distinction is required inside the
normalized KKT band $[0,\eta]$.  By
\cref{lem:finite-response-energy-packing}, the degree volume whose cumulative
normalized response moves through a prescribed interval margin is charged to
objective shock.  The remaining operation is therefore an output-sensitive
heavy-change report, not an eager refresh of every boundary demand.  In the
certified response-tree model, the exact theorem supplies this oracle only
conditionally on bounding visited nodes, finite-accuracy factor maintenance,
leaf work, and presentation updates at the displayed scale.
\end{problem}

\Cref{prop:notched-sun-rppr-trace-obstruction} rules out the existing
notched-double-sun structural epoch as a witness for this problem: its source
and report leaves, apart from the at-most-one pair containing the seed, cross
the exact gate together in matched pairs.  The problem remains open on legal
cyclic traces whose source and report rows are genuinely asymmetric.
\Cref{prop:matched-report-sun-gate-obstruction} tests a minimal edge-only
degree lift of those rows and rules it out too.  The matching makes the raw
report cut full rank, but report nonactivation imposes an anchor-coordinate
upper bound incompatible with a nonseed petal violation.  A next candidate
must therefore attenuate the report through an additional active stem or a
different bounded-degree threshold gadget; merely raising the degree of a
direct report neighbor cannot supply the desired epoch.
\Cref{prop:active-relay-matched-sun-preload-obstruction} performs that first
stem audit and stops it one step earlier: the internal degree-two relay cannot
become active before its degree-one petal.  The canonical all-violations gate preserves
this precedence in canonical all-violations batches of arbitrary size and
under every seed placement, with
only the one relay-seed site excepted.  Thus the next graph must reverse the
petal/relay thresholds or feed the relay from an independent active backbone;
there is still no legal cyclic trace on which reporter algebra may begin.
\Cref{prop:petal-cycle-relay-first-obstruction} tests the literal threshold
reversal and rules out its intended chronology as well.  Although the
degree-three petal has the isolated threshold $6\alpha\rho/\vartheta$, a
report-quiet all-relay face already contains all but at most the petal paired
with an anchor or relay seed.  The obstruction is the coupled anchor balance,
not a failure of the local threshold window or a response-rank theorem.  An
independently active relay backbone, or a different gadget whose global
settlement avoids this cap contradiction, is therefore the next legality
test.
\Cref{prop:double-cycle-feed-report-obstruction} performs the literal
independent-backbone test and stops it more strongly for every backbone seed:
as long as the next all-violations batch remains report-free, every active
anchor stays at or below its petal threshold and every newly admitted anchor
finds its relay already active or co-admits it in the same batch.  Thus no
petal enters before a report.  The full-rank raw cuts do not realize the
prescribed report-free petal/attachment sequence with changing report
responses; nothing is claimed after $W$ enters.  The proof is not
seed-uniform---\cref{eq:double-cycle-anchor-seed-counterrange} gives an exact
anchor-seed first-petal counterexecution---but
\cref{prop:double-cycle-anchor-seed-obstruction} follows that counterexecution
to the first report and permits only three report-free petals.
\Cref{prop:double-cycle-relay-seed-obstruction} exhausts the remaining core
orbit: its exact singleton demands have thresholds
$\rho_W=1/(2(2t+1))$ and
$\rho_{\mathcal B}=1/(2(4t+1))$, so the relay face either terminates or its
first propagating batch contains $w_j$, possibly together with $b_j$.
Accordingly the present double-cycle graph family is retired for the
prescribed long report-free later-petal witness.  These results do not cover
positive-subset batching, another cyclic family, or any post-report trace.
A next candidate must therefore decouple the report cap from the anchor
maximum principle, rather than merely duplicating the anchor cycle as a relay
feed.

This problem is strictly weaker than exact light-sign maintenance.  If it is
proved, then for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, choosing
$\rho=\tau_{\rm gate}=\tau_{\rm sol}=\varepsilon/3$ in
\cref{thm:finite-resolution-continuation} gives
\begin{equation}
 \|D^{-1/2}(x^0-z)\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \Work=\widetilde O\!\left(\frac1{\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha}\right).
 \label{eq:finite-resolution-product}
\end{equation}
This is a note-scoped degree-normalized PageRank guarantee; identifying it
with the repository's final residual convention still requires the explicit
accuracy mapping excluded in \cref{sec:scope}.

A stronger local sufficient primitive queues a consecutive light group at
anchor face $U$ using one old-face response scan and work proportional to the new charged
volume.  The lazy fan and kinetic spider constructions already have this
property.  Proving it for arbitrary nonequitable thick cores is the remaining
graph-uniform question.  Together with \cref{thm:aggregate-repair-oracle}, it
gives
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O(\operatorname{cvol}(S^\star)/\sqrt\alpha)
 =\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))
 \label{eq:aggregate-product-consequence}
\end{equation}
total work.

\section{Typed propagation and debt aggregation}
\label{sec:typed-propagation}

An implementation can realize one propagation epoch with three logical
states: unseen, forward-fresh, and processed.  An arrival from processed to
unseen may expose new frontier; an arrival into a processed coordinate is
stored as backward debt; and non-tree traffic is assigned to the current
cyclic settlement block.  These labels affect scheduling, not the residual
identity.

\begin{lemma}[One-scan causal epoch]
\label{lem:one-scan-causal-epoch}
Suppose an epoch discovers $U$ and satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item each adjacency list in $U$ is scanned at most $C$ times during
 propagation;
 \item every deferred packet is added algebraically to the net residual on
 $U\cup\partial U$ rather than executed as a separate row operation; and
 \item settlement is performed once on the net interior residual.
\end{enumerate}
Then propagation costs $O(C\vol(U))$.  If $G[U]$ is a forest, propagation
plus exact settlement and boundary update cost $O((C+1)\vol(U))$; under a
width-$w$ ordering the cost is
$O((C+(w+1)^2)\vol(U))$.  The bound is independent of the number of forward
and backward causal paths contributing to a residual entry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is direct adjacency-list accounting.  Residual updates are
linear, so accumulating packets and applying their sum produces the same
right-hand side as applying them separately.  One forest elimination scans
each induced and boundary edge only constantly many times.  General sparse
elimination costs $O(\vol(U)+w^2|U|)$, which is
$O((w+1)^2\vol(U))$ because the graph has no isolated vertices.
\end{proof}

The lemma closes the \emph{accounting} half of the causal idea.  The remaining
mathematical obligation is threshold progress: the labels must discover a
region whose exact settlement either certifies the current target or exposes
the next admissible frontier without rescanning processed adjacency lists.
On trees and bounded articulation blocks, directed Schur responses provide
that mechanism.  In a thick cyclic core, the corresponding compressed
interface is still open.

\section{The next proof target}
\label{sec:next-target}

For fresh settlement, the strongest useful amortized conjecture remains
\begin{equation}
 \sum_k\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)
 \leq
 \widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)\,
 \operatorname{cvol}(S^\star(\rho)),
 \label{eq:core-revisit-conjecture}
\end{equation}
for a specified causal expansion and settlement implementation.  This is
weaker than bounding the number of activation batches and stronger than a
mere convergence statement.  It also has a direct experimental diagnostic.
The tilde is essential because \cref{cor:fan-logarithmic-loss} forces a
dynamic-range logarithm.  The incremental route is now more sharply resolved:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item \emph{Amortized revisit route.}  Allow the logarithmic same-shell
 cascade forced by the fan, but prove that every further large-region revisit
 is paid by geometric decay of a KKT margin or geometric growth of
 $\operatorname{cvol}(U_k)$, with at most
 $\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ total multiplicity.
 \item \emph{Structured incremental route.}  Use exact signatures, kinetic
 thresholds, or certified response trees according to the exposed interface.
 \Cref{thm:aligned-separator-signature,thm:rooted-spider-kinetic,thm:certified-response-tree}
 give complete work theorems for these three
 mechanisms, including presentation and reporting charges.  Stable bounded
 block--cut presentations extend the response route to cyclic compositions
 by \cref{thm:stable-bounded-block-response}; the causal zero-prefix theorem
 in \texttt{delayed\_reflection\_ladder} now supplies ordinary-adjacency
 recourse when those exposed blocks merge.
 \item \emph{Universal hybrid route.}  Run exact incremental Schur algebra up
 to the cutoff \cref{eq:separator-cutoff}; after overflow, aggregate small
 face shocks before paying for an old-face pass.  The pre-overflow phase and
 aggregate numerical repair are now proved by
 \cref{eq:cutoff-dense-work,thm:aggregate-repair-oracle}.  The remaining step
 within this exact boundary-gate route is the light-event response bound
 \cref{prob:light-shock-response}.
 \item \emph{Finite-resolution route.}  For approximate output, stop asking
 for exact signs inside an $\alpha\tau$ normalized KKT band.  The resulting
 support-safe error and work reduction is proved in
 \cref{thm:finite-resolution-continuation};
 \cref{cor:finite-response-intervals} removes uniform point estimates, and
 \cref{lem:finite-response-energy-packing} charges large interval movements
 to shock.  Its remaining response target
 \cref{prob:finite-resolution-response} is strictly weaker than the literal
 exact-gate problem.
\end{enumerate}

The expander obstruction closes one tempting but false path: no proof can
remove the structural condition merely by finding a graph-uniform small flat
separator or a bounded number of exact signatures.  The remaining proof
problem within the literal hybrid route is no longer numerical continuation:
one final accelerated repair already suffices.  It is the exact response
hierarchy needed to report light events without old-face rescans.  For the
approximate target, the weaker obligation is an output-sensitive reporter for
the energy-packed interval crossings.  These are strictly more precise than
asking for an unspecified ``better separator.''

The notched-double-sun audit adds a prior gate to that program.  Before a
structural high-rank epoch is used to motivate a dynamic reporter, its face
sequence must be realized by the single-seed RPPR KKT gate.  The existing
petal/report construction fails this test by
\cref{prop:notched-sun-rppr-trace-obstruction}; changing response
representations cannot repair an admission chronology that never occurs.
The matched-report sun in
\cref{prop:matched-report-sun-gate-obstruction} shows that the direct
degree-asymmetry repair also fails, now by exact anchor balance rather than
twin symmetry, even though its raw report cut has rank $n$.  The smallest live
modification was a bounded-degree report stem whose relay might join the
active face while the designated endpoint remained out.

That smallest two-edge stem fails, now by activation order.  At a
degree-four anchor, the degree-one source petal becomes a positive boundary
violation when the anchor exceeds $2\alpha\rho/\vartheta$, whereas the
degree-two relay requires $4\alpha\rho/\vartheta$.  Hence every nonseed relay
is preceded or co-admitted by its petal in every report-free canonical batch.
Once all relays are active, all but at most the petal paired with a positive
relay seed have already entered.  This rules out the intended linear petal
epoch before any response-direction or reporter question arises.  A next
unweighted candidate must reverse those thresholds, for example by raising
the still-exterior petal degree above two, or activate each relay from an
independent backbone.

The literal degree-three reversal also fails globally.  On the petal-cycle
repair, report quietness caps every relay-fed anchor; an inactive petal at a
nonseed anchor makes that anchor's exact equation demand a sum of its two
anchor-cycle neighbors strictly larger than the sum of their caps.  Hence a
$W$-free face containing all relays whose next canonical all-violations batch
is also $W$-free already contains at least $n-1$ petals, even though an
isolated degree-three petal does cross after its relay.  There is again no
intended report-free sequence with a later linear attachment epoch and
varying response directions.  The double-cycle construction then tests the
literal independent-feed repair and fails even earlier for every backbone
seed: report quietness forces every active anchor below its petal threshold,
and every newly admitted anchor's relay is already active or is co-admitted
no later in the same batch.  Consequently it does not realize the prescribed
report-free petal/attachment trace with changing directions; nothing is
claimed after $W$ enters.  The apparent anchor-seed escape also closes: its
first report-free petal is genuine, but the complete all-violations chronology
has at most three report-free petal sites, and the first $j\pm2$ petal batch
co-admits $w_j$.  The relay-seed escape closes at the first step: for every
site and parameter, the exact singleton face either terminates or its first
propagating batch contains the incident report, with the backbone vertex
co-admitted only below the smaller threshold.  The double-cycle family is
therefore retired for this prescribed witness.  The next graph must use a
different coupling or bounded-degree settlement gadget, and must retain
explicit seed-orbit, all-violations, and report-quiet audits.

\section{Conclusion}

The three empirical solvers do support a broader theoretical framework.
Their common invariant is not ``use $\omega_\star$ and finish with
$\omega=1$,'' but ``propagate information before settling the region that
information discovered.''  Exact settlement forms an absorption algebra on
nested regions and quotients away the entire within-region update history.
Repeated coordinate delivery has an exact residual-to-settlement defect, so
the empirical and block-exact versions can be compared without conflating
them.

The work quantity left after this quotient has two exact ledgers.  Fresh
settlement pays cumulative settled volume, whose normalized revisit factor
explains the adaptive controller's statistic.  Lazy settlement instead pays
for the evolving response interface.  The tailed triangle and fan show that
the first ledger can incur a logarithmic dynamic-range loss even at constant
radius and width.  The activation-age identity locates that loss in repeated
charges to old coordinates.  The corrected activation-aligned theorem gives
the exact charged work of flat response compression.  Kinetic thresholds show
that arbitrarily many unequal responses can still be nearly output-linear on
rooted spiders, while certified response trees give a rigorous approximate-
compression theorem with exact gate decisions.

The expander construction proves that the flat condition itself cannot be
removed: even offline, every such presentation can have
$\zeta,\nu=\Omega(n)$.  This is not a lower bound against all local solvers;
it is a proof that the representation must change.  Explicit original-basis
dense fronts and eager exact-demand arrays are also ruled out by
\cref{prop:explicit-multifrontal-expander,prop:explicit-demand-light-cascade},
while \cref{thm:stable-bounded-block-response} proves that a bounded-block
composition is sufficient.  Causal zero-prefix rebuilding removes the stable
presentation requirement under ordinary adjacency access.  The
$\alpha^{-1/4}$ cutoff changes the dense-algebra representation at exactly
the accelerated budget.  The tempting per-event shock lemma is false by
\cref{thm:shock-output-obstruction,thm:no-full-sweep-shock-repair}.  What
replaces it is sharper: \cref{thm:aggregate-repair-oracle} proves that one
final repair pays all numerical shock, and
\cref{thm:aggregate-tree-response} closes the full bound on arbitrary trees.
Within the literal exact-response hybrid route, what remains unproved is the
graph-uniform light-response hierarchy in
\cref{prob:light-shock-response}.  For approximate PageRank output,
\cref{thm:finite-resolution-continuation} shows that exact zero signs are not
needed.  Certified one-sided intervals replace uniform point estimates, and
\cref{lem:finite-response-energy-packing} bounds the unavoidable large-change
output by objective shock.  The strictly weaker band-response problem
\cref{prob:finite-resolution-response} already implies the desired
$\widetilde O(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt\alpha))$ degree-normalized guarantee.  The
notched double sun remains a valid structural representation audit, but its
twin source/report leaves prevent its prescribed petal-only epoch from serving
as the canonical RPPR trace on which to close that problem.  Pairing the
report leaves to break those twins does not fix legality: the resulting exact
report-quietness band contradicts the anchor equation at every nonseed petal
gate.  The smallest two-edge report stem also fails: its internal degree-two
relay has a higher exact anchor threshold than the degree-one petal, so it
cannot be preloaded ahead of that petal.  Giving every petal degree three by
placing the petals on a cycle does reverse that local order, but the coupled
anchor equations and report-quietness caps still imply that an all-relay face
already contains all but at most one petal.  Thus this repair also supplies no
prescribed report-free sequence with a later linear petal epoch and changing
response directions.  A separate backbone cycle does not help: for every seed
on that backbone, an anchor's relay is already active or is co-admitted no
later, and the exact relay and anchor equations imply that the first petal
batch already contains a report.  Starting instead at an anchor creates one
report-free petal batch but no long escape: at most the seed petal and its two
neighbors precede the first report, and the next layer co-admits a report.
Starting at a relay cannot supply the missing epoch either: the singleton
face terminates unless its first propagating batch already contains $w_j$.
Together these results retire this double-cycle graph family only for the
prescribed report-free later-petal/attachment trace with changing directions;
they say nothing about other cyclic families or the trace after $W$ enters.
The next falsifiable family must use a different coupling or bounded-degree
settlement gadget.
The reporter remains deliberately unanalysed until a legal trace exists.
