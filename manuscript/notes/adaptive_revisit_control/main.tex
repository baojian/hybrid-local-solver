\begin{abstract}
The measured frontier adaptive ladder stores one number after each solve:
total charged row work divided by the charge of the touched support.  We
analyze this revisit ratio as a possible controller for later local PageRank
instances.  The ratio has an exact interpretation: it is the
charge-weighted mean number of paid visits to a touched coordinate.  A finer
four-way ledger, indexed by the previous and current relaxation modes on each
revisited coordinate, gives more information.  In particular, every revisit
whose previous operation was exact Gauss--Seidel certifies intervening
neighbor-generated backflow, because the exact operation left zero residual
on that coordinate.  On a nested propagate--settle execution, the same
measured ratio factors exactly into a within-epoch work multiplier and the
settled-volume revisit factor.  This yields a causal revisit bank that
enforces a declared work-per-touched-support ceiling online.  On any
support-safe terminal RPPR trace, the realized ratio itself gives the
a posteriori bound $W\leq2\varrho_{\rm rev}/\rho$.  Thus a global bank at
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ certifies an accelerated target-scale fast path;
if the bank blocks, the spent prefix is capped before a certified fallback.
This success certificate cannot be promoted to a universal theorem for the
literal fixed-band two-rung arm.  On a one-edge graph, a support-safe handoff
can lie strictly below the spreading threshold while its exact delivery tail
takes $\Omega(1/\alpha)$ work at fixed regularization and accuracy.  Thus
that arm needs forced or quantitatively developed spreading, or an exact
incremental Schur block, to carry an accelerated worst-case theorem.

The scalar ratio is not, however, a direction certificate for relaxation.
Its denominator depends on the policy, it does not order total work, and one
historical observation cannot distinguish future task classes whose best
relaxations are opposite.  The exact coordinate-energy identity gives a
second limitation: at a fixed residual and coordinate, immediate objective
decrease per charge is uniquely maximized by unit relaxation.  Any benefit
from over-relaxation must therefore be a multi-operation transport effect,
not a myopic descent effect.

Two unconditional safeguards survive these negative results.  For the
note-scoped unregularized residual gate, any causal controller may select
relaxations from $[1,2-\delta]$.  If every paid update is live, the resulting
variable-relaxation method terminates from the zero iterate with charged work
at most
\[
 \frac{1+\alpha}
 {\delta(2-\delta)\,\alpha\epsppr^{2}}.
\]
This is a safety bound, not the desired accelerated rate.  More importantly,
a budgeted adaptive prefix can switch to exact Gauss--Seidel on the same
irreversible state with an explicit near-baseline work ceiling.  For RPPR,
exact restricted settlement supplies an even stronger canonical checkpoint:
any history-dependent policy may switch without reset, and any nonempty
subset of exact boundary violations can be admitted while preserving true
support containment and finite termination.  Combining this absorption law
with the revisit bank gives a one-state, work-capped switching theorem.
Absorption alone is not enough for best-arm work.  Already on a finite
unweighted tree, the exact single-seed RPPR boundary gate has two safe batch
orders with the same terminal point whose settled-volume revisit differs by
an arbitrarily large factor.  We isolate the missing property as a mergeable
countdown.  If every arm has a work-valued countdown decreased by its own
blocks and never increased by other arms, a work-fair scheduler on one shared
state is $K\kappa$-competitive whenever those countdowns are
$\kappa$-tight.  Irreversible activation tokens instantiate this condition:
on endpoint paths, common forward Schur records plus one reverse recovery
give a $\kappa=1$ countdown and $O(C(S^\star))\leq O(1/\rho)$ work.  A
two-vertex activation breakpoint proves that no jointly continuous
state-energy countdown can pay a fixed activation charge uniformly.

In addition, a branchable weighted portfolio of $K$ local policies satisfies
the exact oracle inequality
\[
 \operatorname{Work}_{\rm port}
 \leq \min_j \frac{\operatorname{Work}_j}{\nu_j},
\]
up to one scheduling chunk, where $\nu_j$ is policy $j$'s work share.
Uniform shares are $K$-competitive with the best arm on every instance.  A
learned predictor may use the causal revisit ledger to set the shares; mixing
it with an exploration floor preserves the worst-case guarantee without any
stationarity, graph-transfer, or identifiability assumption.  Consequently a
finite portfolio automatically inherits a
$\widetilde O(\mathsf V_{\rm loc}/\sqrt\alpha)$ local-work theorem whenever one of its arms has
one.  Simultaneous branches are not essential: a geometric restart portfolio
uses one live state and is less than $4K$-competitive up to its base work
quantum.  The unresolved mathematical task is now clean: prove an accelerated
bank for a modified arm with a genuine transport mechanism, or construct an
interference-monotone transport countdown tight for such an arm.  Revisit
feedback can safely select among theories; by itself it does not create the
missing accelerated theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{The literal feedback signal}
\label{sec:literal}

Consider one sequential run with paid row operations
$t=1,\ldots,T$.  Operation $t$ acts on $u_t$, uses relaxation
$\omega_t$, and costs $c(u_t)$.  Guarded skips have zero charge and are not
included.  Let
\begin{equation}
 S:=\{u_t:1\leq t\leq T\},
 \qquad
 C(S):=\sum_{u\in S}c(u),
 \qquad
 m_u:=\card{\{t:u_t=u\}},
 \label{eq:touched}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 W:=\sum_{t=1}^T c(u_t),
 \qquad
 \varrho_{\rm rev}:=\frac{W}{C(S)}.
 \label{eq:revisit}
\end{equation}
This is the mathematical version of the frontier artifact's stored ratio.

\begin{lemma}[Exact multiplicity identity]
\label{lem:multiplicity}
For every nonempty paid run,
\begin{equation}
 \varrho_{\rm rev}
 =\frac{\sum_{u\in S}c(u)m_u}{\sum_{u\in S}c(u)}
 =1+\frac{\sum_{u\in S}c(u)(m_u-1)}{C(S)}.
 \label{eq:multiplicity}
\end{equation}
Hence $\varrho_{\rm rev}\geq1$, with equality exactly when every touched
coordinate is paid for once.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Group the terms in $W$ by their operated coordinate.  The second equality
subtracts the first paid visit to every $u\in S$.
\end{proof}

Thus the numerator of $\varrho_{\rm rev}-1$ is an exact redundant-work
ledger relative to the realized touched set.  This is useful, but it is not
yet causal: a planned spread-then-settle revisit, a same-mode repeated sweep,
and a neighbor-reactivated coordinate all contribute identically.

\subsection{A causal four-way refinement}

Label a paid operation \emph{exact} if $\omega_t=1$ and \emph{spreading} if
$\omega_t>1$.  For every revisit of $u$, inspect the most recent earlier paid
operation on the same $u$.  For $a,b\in\{\mathrm e,\mathrm s\}$, let
$\mathsf{Rev}_{a\to b}$ be the total charge of revisits whose previous same-coordinate
mode was $a$ and whose current mode is $b$.  This ledger requires only two
bits of state per touched coordinate: whether it has been visited and the
mode of its most recent paid visit.

\begin{proposition}[Causal revisit ledger]
\label{prop:causal-ledger}
Every paid run satisfies
\begin{equation}
 W=C(S)+\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm e}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm s}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm e}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm s},
 \label{eq:causal-ledger}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \varrho_{\rm rev}
 =1+\frac{\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm e}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm s}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm e}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm s}}{C(S)}.
 \label{eq:causal-ratio}
\end{equation}
Moreover, in exact sequential arithmetic, every charged revisit counted in
$\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm e}
 +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm s}$ certifies that at
least one off-coordinate operation altered that coordinate's residual after
its previous exact operation.  In the shared graph model, such an operation
must occur at a neighbor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every paid operation is either the first paid visit to its coordinate or is
in exactly one of the four previous/current-mode classes.  The first visits
sum to $C(S)$, proving \eqref{eq:causal-ledger}.

For the causal statement, write $r=b-Q\widehat x$.  A row operation on $u$
has the form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x^+=\widehat x+\omega\frac{r_u}{Q_{uu}}e_u,
 \qquad
 r^+=r-\omega\frac{r_u}{Q_{uu}}Qe_u.
 \label{eq:row-update}
\end{equation}
If $\omega=1$, then $r_u^+=0$.  Before a later paid revisit, $r_u$ must
again cross the live guard.  Operations on a coordinate $v$ change $r_u$
only through $Q_{uv}$.  In the shared PageRank matrix, $Q_{uv}\neq0$ for
$u\neq v$ exactly when $u$ and $v$ are adjacent.  Thus some intervening
neighbor operation generated the new residual.
\end{proof}

The quantity
\begin{equation}
 B_{\rm nbr}:=
 \frac{\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm e}
       +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm s}}{C(S)}
 \label{eq:backflow-share}
\end{equation}
is therefore a certified neighbor-backflow revisit share.  By contrast,
$\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm e}
 +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm s\to\mathrm s}$ mixes neighbor
backflow with the deterministic self-reflection left by over-relaxation.  The
four-way ledger does not prove which $\omega$ is optimal, but it separates a
real causal event that the scalar ratio erases.

\section{What the scalar cannot certify}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{The denominator is policy dependent}

The touched set $S$ is produced by the policy.  Consequently
$\varrho_{\rm rev}$ is not a normalization by an instance-only quantity.

\begin{proposition}[Revisit ratio does not order work]
\label{prop:not-order}
There are valid charged-event ledgers $A$ and $B$ for which
$\varrho_{\rm rev}(A)<\varrho_{\rm rev}(B)$ but $W(A)>W(B)$, with an
arbitrarily large work ratio.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use coordinates of charge two.  Ledger $A$ touches $M$ coordinates once, so
$C(S_A)=W(A)=2M$ and $\varrho_{\rm rev}(A)=1$.  Ledger $B$ touches one
coordinate twice, so $C(S_B)=2$, $W(B)=4$, and
$\varrho_{\rm rev}(B)=2$.  For $M\geq3$, the ratio ordering is opposite the
work ordering, and $W(A)/W(B)=M/2$ is unbounded.  This is a ledger-level
counterexample; it makes no claim that every such event stream is generated
by the same SOR instance.
\end{proof}

This proposition rules out comparing policies by the scalar ratio alone.  A
repeated-task learner needs an arm-independent loss normalization, such as a
declared work cap, or it must learn total charged work directly.

\subsection{One historical scalar gives no direction}

Even if the denominator problem is repaired, one chosen policy reveals no
counterfactual cost for another policy.

\begin{proposition}[One-signal nonidentifiability]
\label{prop:nonidentify}
Fix two candidate future policies.  Over unrestricted repeated-task cost
sequences, no deterministic rule that sees the same historical revisit
record in two environments can choose a bounded-competitive future policy in
both.  For a randomized rule and every $M>1$, one of two indistinguishable
environments has expected competitive ratio at least $(M+1)/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let the complete history, including $\varrho_{\rm rev}$, be identical.  In
environment one, assign future policy costs $(1,M)$; in environment two,
assign $(M,1)$.  A deterministic rule makes the same choice in both and pays
$M$ in one of them while the best policy costs one.  If it chooses the first
policy with probability $p$, its expected costs are
$p+(1-p)M$ and $pM+(1-p)$.  Their maximum is at least their average,
$(M+1)/2$.
\end{proof}

This is a decision-theoretic result, not an SOR graph construction.  Its role
is precise: a guarantee from the historical scalar requires an additional
assumption such as stationary tasks, a relation between contexts and costs,
counterfactual probes, or a safe portfolio that runs more than one policy.
Keying memory only by graph size $n$ supplies none of these assumptions;
nonisomorphic graphs, different seeds, and different gates can share $n$.

\section{Turning revisit into an online work certificate}
\label{sec:revisit-certificate}

The scalar is not a reliable predictor of the best relaxation, but it is an
exact certificate of work already spent.  It also factors cleanly into the
two revisit mechanisms isolated by the propagate--settle framework:
repetition inside one epoch and repeated settlement of a growing region.

\subsection{Two-level revisit factorization}

Let
\[
 U_0\subseteq U_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_K
\]
be nonempty nested regions.  Suppose epoch $k$ incurs charged work $W_k$,
the union of all coordinates touched by the full run is $U_K$, and write
\begin{equation}
 \mu_k:=\frac{W_k}{C(U_k)},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 :=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^K C(U_k)}{C(U_K)}.
 \label{eq:two-level-definitions}
\end{equation}
The first quantity is the within-epoch work multiplier.  The second is the
settled-volume revisit factor proved useful in the sibling
\texttt{propagate\_settle\_framework} note.

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-level revisit factorization]
\label{thm:two-level-factorization}
Define the settled-volume-weighted mean
\begin{equation}
 \overline\mu:=
 \frac{\sum_{k=0}^K\mu_kC(U_k)}
      {\sum_{k=0}^K C(U_k)}.
 \label{eq:mean-local-multiplier}
\end{equation}
Then the final operation-level revisit ratio satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \varrho_{\rm rev}
 =\overline\mu\,\mathfrak R_{\rm set}.
 \label{eq:two-level-factorization}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $m\leq\mu_k\leq M$ for every epoch, then
\begin{equation}
 m\,\mathfrak R_{\rm set}
 \leq\varrho_{\rm rev}
 \leq M\,\mathfrak R_{\rm set}.
 \label{eq:factorization-sandwich}
\end{equation}
If every epoch scans its whole settled region at least once, one may take
$m=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The total charged work is
\[
 W=\sum_{k=0}^K W_k
  =\sum_{k=0}^K\mu_kC(U_k)
  =\overline\mu\sum_{k=0}^K C(U_k).
\]
Divide by $C(U_K)$ and use
\cref{eq:revisit,eq:two-level-definitions}.  The sandwich follows because a
weighted mean lies between its smallest and largest entries.
\end{proof}

This identity bridges the empirical and theoretical statistics.  A large
$\varrho_{\rm rev}$ can arise from large within-epoch multiplicity, large
settled-volume revisit, or both.  The causal ledger diagnoses the first
mechanism; incremental Schur state or a different expansion schedule attacks
the second.  The identity applies to nested propagate--settle executions, not
automatically to the literal frontier artifact's noncanonical SOR phases.

\subsection{The revisit bank}

The ratio admits a causal token interpretation requiring no model of the
graph dynamics.  After $t$ paid events, let $S_t$ be the touched union and
$W_t$ the charged work.  For a declared budget factor $\Gamma\geq1$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{Bank}_t(\Gamma):=\Gamma C(S_t)-W_t.
 \label{eq:revisit-bank}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact revisit-bank updates]
\label{thm:revisit-bank}
Suppose event $t$ operates coordinate $u$ at charge $c(u)$.  If this is its
first paid visit, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{Bank}_t(\Gamma)-\mathsf{Bank}_{t-1}(\Gamma)
 =(\Gamma-1)c(u).
 \label{eq:bank-first}
\end{equation}
If it is a revisit, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{Bank}_t(\Gamma)-\mathsf{Bank}_{t-1}(\Gamma)
 =-c(u).
 \label{eq:bank-revisit}
\end{equation}
Therefore a controller that admits every desired first visit but admits a
revisit only when
\begin{equation}
 c(u)\leq\mathsf{Bank}_{t-1}(\Gamma)
 \label{eq:bank-admission}
\end{equation}
maintains, at every nonempty prefix,
\begin{equation}
 W_t\leq\Gamma C(S_t),
 \qquad
 \varrho_{{\rm rev},t}\leq\Gamma.
 \label{eq:bank-invariant}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A first visit increases both $C(S_t)$ and $W_t$ by $c(u)$, proving
\cref{eq:bank-first}.  A revisit increases only $W_t$, proving
\cref{eq:bank-revisit}.  The bank starts at zero.  First visits preserve its
nonnegativity for $\Gamma\geq1$, and
\cref{eq:bank-admission} preserves it on revisits.  Rearranging a nonnegative
bank gives \cref{eq:bank-invariant}.
\end{proof}

The bank is stronger than checking the ratio after a solve: it makes a
work-per-touched-support cap enforceable online.  It is not a convergence
proof.  When a useful revisit would overdraw the bank, the controller must
settle, switch policy, increase its declared budget, or invoke a certified
fallback.  Silently dropping the operation could destroy progress.

The same identity gives a useful guarantee even before one proves that the
bank never blocks.

\begin{corollary}[A posteriori accelerated RPPR fast path]
\label{cor:rppr-fast-path}
Consider any terminal RPPR execution whose charged touched union $S$ is
contained in $S^\star(\rho)$.  No nesting or fixed relaxation schedule is
required.  Its realized charged work satisfies
\begin{equation}
 W=\varrho_{\rm rev}C(S)
 \leq \frac{2\varrho_{\rm rev}}{\rho}.
 \label{eq:rppr-fast-path}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if a global revisit bank with factor $\Gamma$ remains
nonnegative until termination, then
\begin{equation}
 W\leq\frac{2\Gamma}{\rho}.
 \label{eq:banked-rppr-fast-path}
\end{equation}
Taking $\Gamma=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ certifies the intended
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ work scale on that execution.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first equality is the definition of the measured revisit ratio.  Support
containment and the source volume bound give
\[
 C(S)\leq C(S^\star(\rho))
 \leq2\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq\frac2\rho.
\]
If the bank remains nonnegative, \eqref{eq:bank-invariant} supplies
$\varrho_{\rm rev}\leq\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

This is an a posteriori certificate with no graph-structural hypothesis
beyond support safety, not a graph-uniform success theorem.  A safe service
can declare an
accelerated cap $\Gamma_{\rm acc}$, run any support-safe mixture of the new
policies, and switch at the first proposed event that would overdraw the
global bank.  The prefix then costs at most $2\Gamma_{\rm acc}/\rho$.
Canonicalize the current certified region and include that cost in any
fallback continuation bound $C_{\rm fall}$; the complete service obeys
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm total}
 \leq \frac{2\Gamma_{\rm acc}}{\rho}+C_{\rm fall}.
 \label{eq:accelerated-or-fallback}
\end{equation}
Thus every instance either carries an a posteriori target-scale
certificate or enters a separately certified fallback after a capped local
prefix.  Proving that fallback is never invoked remains the universal
acceleration problem.

The literal measured two-rung arm does not solve that problem.  The next
theorem keeps the note's unregularized residual gate and uses RPPR only as
the certified support envelope, exactly as in
\Cref{cor:rppr-fast-path}.

\begin{theorem}[A support-safe one-edge obstruction to fixed-band two-rung]
\label{thm:two-vertex-two-rung-obstruction}
Fix a band factor $B>1$ and put
\[
 \vartheta_B:=\frac{B+1}{2}\in(1,B),
 \qquad
 \alpha_B:=\min\left\{\frac14,
                   \frac{3\log\vartheta_B}{16}\right\}.
\]
For every $0<\alpha\leq\alpha_B$, there is a one-edge, single-seed
support-safe handoff at fixed RPPR regularization $\rho=1/4$ for which the
literal two-rung schedule
\[
 [(Bg,\omega_\star),(g,1)]
\]
executes no spreading operation and needs exactly
\begin{equation}
 N_B(\alpha)
 :=\left\lceil
   \frac{\log\vartheta_B}
        {\log((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha))}
  \right\rceil
 \label{eq:two-vertex-exact-tail}
\end{equation}
exact-delivery pushes.  Its realized revisit factor satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \varrho_{\rm rev}
 \geq\frac{N_B(\alpha)}2
 \geq\frac{3\log\vartheta_B}{16\alpha}.
 \label{eq:two-vertex-bank-lower}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho$ is fixed and
$\epsppr=(1-\alpha)/[(1+\alpha)\vartheta_B]$ stays bounded away from zero.
Consequently the literal fixed-band arm cannot guarantee an
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ global bank, even on one edge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let the graph be $o-v$, with seed $o$.  Both degrees equal one, and write
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}a&-h\\-h&a\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 a:=\frac{1+\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 h:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 \zeta:=\frac{h}{a}=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.
\]
At $\rho=1/4$, the shifted RPPR load is
$\ell_\rho=(3\alpha/4,-\alpha/4)$.  Its full solution has
\[
 (Q^{-1}\ell_\rho)_v
 =\frac{\alpha(3h-a)}{4(a^2-h^2)}>0
\]
because $\alpha<1/2$.  Thus $S^\star(1/4)=\{o,v\}$.  More explicitly, the
canonical root point $y=(3\alpha/(4a),0)$ has shifted boundary violation
\[
 (\ell_\rho-Qy)_v=\frac{\alpha(3\zeta-1)}4>0,
\]
so the exact boundary gate certifies $v$ before any operation on it.

Do not settle the enlarged two-coordinate RPPR system.  Instead switch on
the same irreversible state to the note's unregularized residual
$r=b-Q\widehat x$.  One exact root update moves $y_o$ to $\alpha/a$, the
unregularized root checkpoint, and leaves
\begin{equation}
 r=(0,\alpha\zeta).
 \label{eq:two-vertex-handoff}
\end{equation}
This update and every later one touch only the certified set
$S^\star(1/4)$.

Choose
\[
 g:=\frac{\alpha\zeta}{\vartheta_B},
 \qquad
 \epsppr:=\frac g\alpha=\frac{\zeta}{\vartheta_B}.
\]
At the handoff, $\max_u|r_u|=\alpha\zeta<Bg$, so the strict live guard on
the spreading rung is empty with a nonzero margin.  In the exact rung only
one residual coordinate is nonzero at a time.  A unit row push zeros it and
transfers the factor $h/a=\zeta$ to the other endpoint.  After $t$ pushes,
the maximum residual is therefore $\alpha\zeta^{t+1}$.  The first $t$ for
which this is at most $g$ is exactly \eqref{eq:two-vertex-exact-tail}.

Each tail push has charge two and $C(S^\star)=4$, so the tail alone gives
$\varrho_{\rm rev}\geq N_B/2$.  For $\alpha\leq1/2$,
\[
 \log\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}
 =2\operatorname{arctanh}(\alpha)
 \leq\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}
 \leq\frac{8\alpha}{3}.
\]
Substitution proves \eqref{eq:two-vertex-bank-lower}.  The choice of
$\alpha_B$ also makes $N_B\geq2$; all prior gate and handoff work can only
increase the ratio.
\end{proof}

Thus the false lemma is now precise: support containment, current residual
band, and an exact delivery rung do not imply accelerated cleanup.  This
does not refute a forced or quantitatively developed $\omega_\star$ burn-in,
nor exact Schur settlement; either mechanism prevents the displayed dead
spreading phase.  In the causal ledger, the tail is entirely
exact-after-exact neighbor backflow, so merely labeling that debt also does
not make it an accelerated countdown.

For the next corollary, every paid row update, ranking scan, or boundary scan
is represented as a charged coordinate-touch event in this same ledger.  A
procedure may use a more detailed cost model, but it may not omit these
charges and still invoke the bank guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Banked propagate--settle work]
\label{cor:banked-propagate-settle}
Consider nested epochs $U_0,\ldots,U_K$.  In epoch $k$, cap all propagation,
ranking, and boundary-inspection work by running a revisit bank with factor
$\Gamma_{\rm p}$ and touched union $U_k$.  Suppose exact settlement and its
boundary update cost at most
$C_{\rm s}(w+1)^2C(U_k)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Work}_{0:K}
 \leq
 \bigl(\Gamma_{\rm p}+C_{\rm s}(w+1)^2\bigr)
 \mathfrak R_{\rm set}\,C(U_K).
 \label{eq:banked-propagate-work}
\end{equation}
For RPPR, if $U_K=S^\star(\rho)$ and
$\mathfrak R_{\rm set}=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$, this becomes
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Work}_{0:K}
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rm p}+(w+1)^2}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
 \label{eq:banked-rppr-product}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The bank gives propagation-side work at most
$\Gamma_{\rm p}C(U_k)$ in each epoch.  Add the settlement bound and sum.
Definition \eqref{eq:two-level-definitions} proves
\eqref{eq:banked-propagate-work}.  In the shared graph model,
$C(U)\leq2\vol(U)$, while
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$.  Substitute these inequalities and the
stated revisit target.
\end{proof}

This corollary removes one assumption from the abstract propagate--settle
ledger: bounded propagation multiplicity can be enforced rather than merely
postulated.  The remaining product-scale obligation is the cross-epoch
factor $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$, or an incremental state representation that
avoids paying fresh settlement volume at every epoch.

\section{Absorption-safe causal switching}
\label{sec:absorption-switching}

Exact settlement supplies a canonical checkpoint at which changing policies
requires neither cloning nor restart.  The cleanest theorem is for RPPR,
whose support gate is already compatible with the shared source model.
Define the shifted load
\begin{equation}
 \ell_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one.
 \label{eq:adaptive-shifted-load}
\end{equation}
For a nonempty region $U$, its canonical restricted point is
\begin{equation}
 x[U]_U:=Q_{UU}^{-1}(\ell_\rho)_U,
 \qquad
 x[U]_{U^c}:=0.
 \label{eq:adaptive-canonical-point}
\end{equation}
At this checkpoint, define the boundary-violation set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(U):=
 \left\{v\in\partial U:
  (\ell_\rho-Qx[U])_v>0
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:adaptive-boundary-violations}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Absorption-safe adaptive boundary gate]
\label{thm:absorption-safe-switch}
Assume a single seed $o$ and $(\ell_\rho)_o>0$.  Start with $U_0=\{o\}$.
At checkpoint $k$, a causal controller may use the entire work and revisit
history to choose any nonempty subset
\begin{equation}
 \varnothing\neq B_k\subseteq\mathcal B(U_k)
 \label{eq:adaptive-subset}
\end{equation}
when $\mathcal B(U_k)$ is nonempty, and set
$U_{k+1}:=U_k\cup B_k$.  It may change its ranking, relaxation, batching, or
proposal policy arbitrarily between checkpoints.  After every expansion,
replace the current iterate by the canonical point $x[U_{k+1}]$.

Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item every $U_k$ lies in the true support $S^\star(\rho)$;
 \item every newly added coordinate is positive at the next checkpoint;
 \item the checkpoint $x[U_k]$ depends only on $U_k$, so every earlier
 propagation and policy choice is absorbed exactly;
 \item the process terminates after at most
 $|S^\star(\rho)|-|U_0|$ expansions; and
 \item at termination, $x[U_K]=x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus policy switching at exact checkpoints is correct on one irreversible
state and needs no cross-policy state-transfer assumption.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The matrix $Q$ is a symmetric positive-definite Stieltjes matrix, so every
principal inverse and every principal Schur-complement inverse is
entrywise nonnegative.  At $U_0$, the positive seed load gives
$x[U_0]_o>0$.  It also forces $o\in S^\star(\rho)$: otherwise the inactive
KKT inequality would contradict $(\ell_\rho)_o>0$.  Suppose
$x[U_k]_{U_k}>0$ and choose
$B_k$ as in \eqref{eq:adaptive-subset}.  Block elimination gives
\begin{equation}
 x[U_{k+1}]_{B_k}
 =
 \left(
  Q_{B_kB_k}
  -Q_{B_kU_k}Q_{U_kU_k}^{-1}Q_{U_kB_k}
 \right)^{-1}
 (\ell_\rho-Qx[U_k])_{B_k}>0.
 \label{eq:adaptive-schur-positive}
\end{equation}
The old coordinates receive
$-Q_{U_kU_k}^{-1}Q_{U_kB_k}x[U_{k+1}]_{B_k}\geq0$, so positivity persists.

For support containment, assume inductively $U_k\subseteq S^\star(\rho)$.
Order comparison gives
$x[U_k]_{U_k}\leq x^\star_{U_k}$.  If
$v\in\mathcal B(U_k)$ but $x_v^\star=0$, then the Stieltjes off-diagonal
signs and $x^\star\geq0$ imply
\[
 (\ell_\rho-Qx^\star)_v
 \geq(\ell_\rho-Qx[U_k])_v>0,
\]
contradicting the inactive RPPR KKT inequality.  Hence every boundary
violation lies in $S^\star(\rho)$.

The formula \eqref{eq:adaptive-canonical-point} is unique, proving absorption
and policy independence at a fixed $U_k$.  Each nonterminal checkpoint adds
at least one new true-support vertex, so the number of expansions is finite.
If $\mathcal B(U_K)$ is empty, the restricted equations give the active KKT
equalities.  On a boundary coordinate, the stopping test gives
$\ell_\rho-Qx[U_K]\leq0$; also $b_v=0$ and the Stieltjes signs give
$b_v-(Qx[U_K])_v\geq0$.  These are exactly the lower and upper inactive RPPR
KKT inequalities.  A nonseed coordinate outside
$U_K\cup\partial U_K$ has no edge to $U_K$, so $Qx[U_K]=b_v=0$ there and its
inactive KKT interval holds as well.  The seed already lies in $U_K$.
The global KKT conditions and uniqueness imply
$x[U_K]=x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Revisit-controlled no-reset switching]
\label{cor:no-reset-switching}
In \Cref{thm:absorption-safe-switch}, let any number of proposal or
propagation policies run between checkpoints, but charge all of their work
to the bank of \Cref{thm:revisit-bank}.  Require charged row operations to
remain inside the current certified region together with coordinates already
admitted by the exact boundary certificate.  If an operation would overdraw
the bank, settle that certified region and switch policy at the canonical
checkpoint.  At every nonterminal checkpoint, still admit a nonempty subset
of the exact boundary violations as in \eqref{eq:adaptive-subset}.
Correctness is independent of the switch sequence, and the work obeys
\eqref{eq:banked-propagate-work} whenever the stated settlement-cost bound
holds.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The bank supplies the per-epoch propagation bound.  Exact settlement absorbs
the policy history, while \Cref{thm:absorption-safe-switch} supplies support
containment and correctness.  Apply
\Cref{cor:banked-propagate-settle}.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine no-reset switch theorem, but not yet a best-arm competitive
one.  It proves that revisit-triggered switching is state-compatible and
work-capped.  Matching the fastest arm still requires bounding
$\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$, maintaining incremental Schur state, or proving that
pre-switch work is useful to the arm chosen next.

\subsection{Why absorption alone cannot give a work oracle}

The obstruction occurs inside the exact RPPR gate itself.  It is therefore
unsafe to infer a competitive ratio merely from canonical settlement,
monotone support growth, or persistence of unadmitted violations.

\begin{theorem}[RPPR heavy-early obstruction on a tree]
\label{prop:batch-order-obstruction}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  For every integer $L\geq1$, there are a finite simple
unweighted tree, a single seed $o$, and $\rho>0$ for which the exact RPPR
boundary gate of \Cref{thm:absorption-safe-switch} has two legal batch traces
with the same terminal region and terminal canonical point, but
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathfrak R_{\rm set}^{\rm early}}
      {\mathfrak R_{\rm set}^{\rm late}}
 \geq \frac{L+1}{5}.
 \label{eq:rppr-heavy-early-ratio}
\end{equation}
Every batch before a possible common final batch is a singleton.  In
particular, exact RPPR absorption, true-support containment, and persistent
boundary violations do not imply a constant best-order settled-work bound,
even on trees.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 a:=\frac{1+\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 \beta:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 t:=\frac{\beta}{a}=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.
\]
For an integer $M$, form a tree from the path
$o-z_1-\cdots-z_L$, a vertex $h$ adjacent to $o$, and $M-1$ pendant leaves
$y_1,\ldots,y_{M-1}$ adjacent to $h$.  Thus
\[
 d_o=2,
 \quad d_h=M,
 \quad d_{z_i}=2\ (i<L),
 \quad d_{z_L}=d_{y_j}=1.
\]
Write $P_k:=\{o,z_1,\ldots,z_k\}$, with $P_0=\{o\}$.

We first make every next path vertex a strict violation at its path-only
prefix.  For $0\leq k<L$, define
\[
 r_k(\rho):=(\ell_\rho-Qx[P_k])_{z_{k+1}}.
\]
The principal system on $P_k$ is independent of $M$.  At $\rho=0$ it is an
irreducible Stieltjes system with positive load at $o$, so
$x[P_k]_{z_k}>0$ and
\[
 r_k(0)=-Q_{z_{k+1},z_k}x[P_k]_{z_k}>0.
\]
Moreover, $r_k$ is affine in $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
 r_k(\rho)=r_k(0)-\rho\gamma_k,
 \qquad
 \gamma_k:=\alpha\left(
  \sqrt{d_{z_{k+1}}}
  -Q_{z_{k+1},P_k}Q_{P_kP_k}^{-1}
       D_{P_k}^{1/2}\one
 \right)>0.
 \label{eq:path-prefix-margin}
\end{equation}
The last inequality follows from the Stieltjes signs.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \eta_L:=\frac12\min_{0\leq k<L}\frac{r_k(0)}{\gamma_k}>0
 \label{eq:path-prefix-eta}
\end{equation}
and $r_k(\rho)>0$ for every $k<L$ whenever $0<\rho<\eta_L$.

Now set
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\rho_M:=\frac{t}{4(M+t)}.
 \label{eq:heavy-rho}
\end{equation}
At the initial checkpoint $P_0$,
\[
 x_o[P_0]=\frac{\alpha(1-2\rho)}{a\sqrt2},
\]
and the violation residual at $h$ is
\begin{equation}
 (\ell_\rho-Qx[P_0])_h
 =\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt M}
  \left(\frac{t(1-2\rho)}2-\rho M\right).
 \label{eq:heavy-initial-margin}
\end{equation}
It is positive exactly when $\rho<t/[2(M+t)]$, hence at $\rho_M$.
Because $\rho_M\to0$, choose $M$ large enough that
\begin{equation}
 \rho_M<\eta_L,
 \qquad
 M\geq\frac32L^2+\frac{21}{2}L+6.
 \label{eq:heavy-M-choice}
\end{equation}

We shall use persistence inside the exact gate.  Suppose
$U'=U\cup B$ and every coordinate of $B$ violates at $x[U]$.  The Schur
formula \eqref{eq:adaptive-schur-positive} gives
$x[U']_B>0$ and $x[U']_U-x[U]_U\geq0$.  For every $v\notin U'$,
\begin{align}
 &(\ell_\rho-Qx[U'])_v-(\ell_\rho-Qx[U])_v \notag\\
 &\qquad={}-Q_{vU}(x[U']_U-x[U]_U)-Q_{vB}x[U']_B\geq0.
 \label{eq:rppr-violation-persistence}
\end{align}
Thus every unadmitted violation persists under every legal canonical
expansion.

Two traces are consequently legal.  The \emph{late} trace admits
$z_1,\ldots,z_L$ and then $h$: each $z_{k+1}$ violates on $P_k$ by
\eqref{eq:path-prefix-eta}, while $h$ persists.  For the \emph{early} trace,
first admit $h$.  At every later region $P_k\cup\{h\}$, comparison with the
legal expansion from $P_k$ gives
$x[P_k\cup\{h\}]_{P_k}\geq x[P_k]_{P_k}$.  Since $h$ is not adjacent to
$z_{k+1}$, the Stieltjes signs then give
\[
 (\ell_\rho-Qx[P_k\cup\{h\}])_{z_{k+1}}
 \geq r_k(\rho)>0.
\]
Thus the early trace may admit $h,z_1,\ldots,z_L$.  Both traces reach
\[
 U^\dagger:=P_L\cup\{h\},
\]
where absorption makes their canonical points identical.

Only the leaves $y_j$ remain outside $U^\dagger$.  They have equal degree,
equal shifted load, and the same sole active neighbor $h$, so their violation
residuals are tied.  If the common residual is nonpositive, both traces stop
at $U^\dagger$.  If it is positive, both admit all leaves in one common
final batch, after which the full graph is terminal.  Thus their suffix,
terminal point, and denominator $C(U_K)$ are identical.

It remains to compare the settled-charge numerators.  Here
\[
 C(P_k)=3+3k\quad(k<L),
 \qquad
 C(P_L)=3L+2,
 \qquad
 c(h)=M+1.
\]
Let $S$ denote the common optional final settlement charge.  Direct summation
gives
\begin{align}
 A_{\rm early}
 &=3+\sum_{k=0}^L\bigl(C(P_k)+M+1\bigr)+S \notag\\
 &=(L+1)M+\frac32L^2+\frac{11}{2}L+6+S,
 \label{eq:heavy-early}\\
 A_{\rm late}
 &=3+\sum_{k=1}^LC(P_k)+\bigl(C(P_L)+M+1\bigr)+S \notag\\
 &=M+\frac32L^2+\frac{15}{2}L+5+S.
 \label{eq:heavy-late}
\end{align}
The full graph has charge $3M+3L+1$, so $S$ is either zero or at most this
quantity.  Therefore
\[
 A_{\rm early}\geq(L+1)M,
 \qquad
 A_{\rm late}
 \leq4M+\frac32L^2+\frac{21}{2}L+6
 \leq5M,
\]
where the last inequality is \eqref{eq:heavy-M-choice}.  The common terminal
denominator cancels, proving \eqref{eq:rppr-heavy-early-ratio}.
\end{proof}

The expensive branch can therefore be admitted early and paid throughout a
long independent chain even though every addition is KKT-certified and both
orders end at the same exact solution.  A positive one-state oracle theorem
must use more than correctness and monotonicity: for example an
interference-monotone countdown, an explicit order rule whose charge can be
amortized, or incremental settlement state that does not repay the whole
region.

\begin{corollary}[No policy-blind accelerated-bank success theorem]
\label{cor:no-policy-blind-bank}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and any finite bank factor $\Gamma$.  There is a legal
support-safe exact RPPR trace on a finite unweighted tree whose settlement
touches alone violate $W\leq\Gamma C(S)$ at some prefix.  Consequently
support safety, exact checkpoint absorption, and the RPPR KKT gate cannot by
themselves prove that a global bank of scale
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ never blocks.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose $L$ with $(L+1)/4>\Gamma$ in
\Cref{prop:batch-order-obstruction} and use the early trace.  Its cumulative
settlement work is at least $(L+1)M$.  Its terminal region is contained in
the full graph, whose charge is $3M+3L+1\leq4M$ under
\eqref{eq:heavy-M-choice}.  Hence its terminal realized ratio is greater
than $\Gamma$.  The bank invariant must therefore fail at or before that
checkpoint.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A mergeable countdown certificate}

There is, however, a sharp positive condition under which all policy work can
be committed to one state without branching or reset.  Let $\mathcal Z$ be a
space of certified states with a terminal subset.  A paid policy block maps
one certified state to another and has a declared positive charge.  In the
RPPR application, these may be the canonical checkpoints of
\Cref{thm:absorption-safe-switch}.

\begin{theorem}[Shared-state portfolio from mergeable countdowns]
\label{thm:mergeable-countdown}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_K$ be policies acting on the same certified state.  Suppose
that for every $j$ there is a work-valued function
$\Phi_j:\mathcal Z\to\mathbb R_+$ with the following properties at every
nonterminal state $z$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item \emph{interference monotonicity:} every legal block of every policy,
 $z\mapsto z^+$, satisfies $\Phi_j(z^+)\leq\Phi_j(z)$;
 \item \emph{own-service progress:} every block of $P_j$ with charge $a$,
 including a block that reaches the terminal state, satisfies
 $\Phi_j(z^+)\leq\Phi_j(z)-a$; and
 \item $P_j$ always has a legal next block unless the common terminal
 certificate already holds.
\end{enumerate}
Run a work-fair scheduler with fixed positive shares
$\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K$, $\sum_j\nu_j=1$, committing every scheduled block to
the same state.  Assume that at every prefix of total charge $C$, policy $j$
has received at least $\nu_j(C-\Delta)$ service.  Then it terminates with
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm shared}
 \leq
 \min_{1\leq j\leq K}
 \frac{\Phi_j(z_0)}{\nu_j}+\Delta.
 \label{eq:mergeable-countdown-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, uniform shares give
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm shared}
 \leq K\min_j\Phi_j(z_0)+\Delta.
 \label{eq:mergeable-uniform}
\end{equation}
If $C_j$ is the standalone work of $P_j$ and the countdown is
$\kappa$-tight, meaning $\Phi_j(z_0)\leq\kappa C_j$ for every $j$, then the
shared irreversible run is $K\kappa$-competitive with the best arm, up to
$\Delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j$.  Blocks of other policies never increase $\Phi_j$.  Until
and including termination, every unit of service given to $P_j$ decreases
$\Phi_j$ by at least one unit.  Because $\Phi_j$ is nonnegative, the common
certificate must therefore hold by the time $P_j$ receives
$\Phi_j(z_0)$ service.  Work
fairness gives that amount of service by total charge at most
$\Phi_j(z_0)/\nu_j+\Delta$.  Minimize over $j$.  The last two conclusions are
immediate.
\end{proof}

Unlike the branchable portfolio, this theorem has only one evolving solution
state: every block from every arm is retained.  Unlike a restart portfolio,
no completed block is discarded.  The price is precisely
interference monotonicity.  The batch-order obstruction shows why that
property cannot be omitted from a settlement-volume countdown.

\subsection{Activation tokens: a tight mergeable countdown}

The abstract condition has a concrete realization when transport work is
represented by irreversible common-state events rather than by a smooth
numerical error.

\begin{lemma}[Irreversible activation-token countdown]
\label{lem:activation-token-countdown}
For policy $P_j$, let $T_j$ be a finite token set with positive weights
$w_j(t)$.  Suppose every certified shared state $z$ records a consumed set
$H_j(z)\subseteq T_j$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
 \item every legal block $z\mapsto z^+$ satisfies
 $H_j(z)\subseteq H_j(z^+)$;
 \item every nonterminal $P_j$ block of charge $a$ consumes new tokens
 $N=H_j(z^+)\setminus H_j(z)$ with
 $a\leq\sum_{t\in N}w_j(t)$;
 \item $P_j$ has a legal next block until the common terminal certificate;
 and
 \item
 $\sum_{t\in T_j\setminus H_j(z_0)}w_j(t)\leq\kappa_j C_j$,
 where $C_j$ is its standalone work from $z_0$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_j(z):=\sum_{t\in T_j\setminus H_j(z)}w_j(t)
 \label{eq:activation-token-potential}
\end{equation}
is an interference-monotone, $\kappa_j$-tight mergeable countdown for
\Cref{thm:mergeable-countdown}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity of $H_j$ makes $\Phi_j$ nonincreasing under every legal block.
For an own block,
\[
 \Phi_j(z)-\Phi_j(z^+)
 =\sum_{t\in H_j(z^+)\setminus H_j(z)}w_j(t)\geq a.
\]
The last hypothesis is exactly $\kappa_j$-tightness.
\end{proof}

The important restriction is that these are events in the common certified
state.  Tokens for an arm-private simulated trajectory would merely hide a
branch and would not establish interference monotonicity.

\begin{theorem}[A $\kappa=1$ shared-state countdown on endpoint paths]
\label{thm:path-token-countdown}
Consider single-seed RPPR on an endpoint-seeded path
$P_N=(v_0,\ldots,v_N)$, and suppose
\[
 S^\star(\rho)=U_J:=\{v_0,\ldots,v_J\}.
\]
Require the common checkpoint representation to store, for every admitted
prefix $U_k$, the exact forward Schur records described below.  Every legal
support expansion, regardless of which arm requests it, appends the unique
new record; support-preserving blocks leave the records unchanged.  Once the
terminal prefix is certified, the common verifier enters a terminalization
phase: reverse-recovery blocks are legal, while numerical policy updates that
could overwrite recovered output are not.

Then the exact path-Schur arm has an irreversible activation-token countdown
with $\kappa=1$.  Its declared standalone work and initial countdown are
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm path}=\Phi_{\rm path}(z_0)\leq2C(S^\star(\rho)),
 \label{eq:path-token-work}
\end{equation}
up to deletion of any seed-initialization token already paid at $z_0$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm path}\leq\frac4\rho,
 \label{eq:path-token-rppr}
\end{equation}
and a uniform work-fair shared-state portfolio containing this arm terminates
on the promised path class in at most
$2K C(S^\star)+\Delta\leq4K/\rho+\Delta$ declared work.  No reset or
arm-private numerical iterate is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 a_i:=Q_{v_i v_i},
 \qquad
 \beta_i:=-Q_{v_{i-1}v_i}>0\quad(i\geq1),
 \qquad
 \lambda_i:=(\ell_\rho)_{v_i}.
\]
The common representation stores the forward elimination records
\begin{align}
 \delta_0&=a_0,& \eta_0&=\lambda_0,\notag\\
 \delta_i&=a_i-\frac{\beta_i^2}{\delta_{i-1}},&
 \eta_i&=\lambda_i+\frac{\beta_i\eta_{i-1}}{\delta_{i-1}}.
 \label{eq:path-common-schur-record}
\end{align}
Positive definiteness gives $\delta_i>0$.  At prefix $U_k$, the unique
boundary residual has the sign of
$\eta_{k+1}=\lambda_{k+1}+\beta_{k+1}\eta_k/\delta_k$.
Thus it is positive for $k<J$ and nonpositive at $k=J$ unless $J=N$.
The next forward block is therefore always legal until the exact terminal
prefix is found.  The terminal solution is then recovered once in reverse:
\begin{equation}
 x_{v_J}=\frac{\eta_J}{\delta_J},
 \qquad
 x_{v_i}=\frac{\eta_i+\beta_{i+1}x_{v_{i+1}}}{\delta_i}
 \quad(i=J-1,\ldots,0).
 \label{eq:path-common-backsolve}
\end{equation}

Let $w_i:=c(v_i)=1+d_{v_i}$.  Give every $v_i\in S^\star$ one forward
token $f_i$ and one reverse token $b_i$, both of weight $w_i$.  The forward
token is consumed when $v_i$ is admitted or initialized and its record is
appended; the reverse token is consumed when
\eqref{eq:path-common-backsolve} recovers that coordinate.  If the current
prefix is $U_k$ and $H$ is the reverse-recovered set, the remaining-token
countdown is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\rm path}(U_k,H)
 =\sum_{i=k+1}^{J}w_i
  +\sum_{v_i\in S^\star\setminus H}w_i,
 \label{eq:path-token-potential}
\end{equation}
with the already initialized seed token handled identically on both sides.
Any compatible block either leaves this quantity unchanged, advances the
prefix, or recovers output.  A path-arm forward or reverse block has declared
charge exactly the token it consumes, so
\Cref{lem:activation-token-countdown} applies with $\kappa=1$ and initial
value at most $2C(S^\star)$.

The recurrence and backsolve use constant arithmetic per path vertex; the
declared charge dominates this work and its adjacency access by an absolute
factor.  Finally,
$C(S^\star)\leq2\vol(S^\star)\leq2/\rho$, proving
\eqref{eq:path-token-rppr}.  Apply
\Cref{thm:mergeable-countdown} with uniform shares for the portfolio bound.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the clean positive answer for serializable fronts: preserve
an activation-once transport representation in the common state, and charge
forward discovery and terminal recovery exactly once.  Extending it from
paths to arbitrary trees requires an order-independent dynamic response
merge.  The existing lazy tree response follows one sorted homotopy order,
whereas a legal exact gate can choose a different positive boundary branch;
no proof yet repairs the affected ancestor responses without recreating old
tokens.  The all-tree extension is therefore not claimed.

\begin{proposition}[Continuous numerical countdowns cannot pay activation]
\label{prop:no-continuous-activation-countdown}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  On the one-edge graph there are legal exact RPPR
activations of fixed charge two whose before-and-after canonical states
converge to one another.  Hence no countdown jointly continuous in
$(\rho,x)$ can decrease by the full activation charge uniformly over RPPR
instances.  In particular, an exact obstacle-energy countdown needs an
unbounded multiplier near the activation breakpoint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Retain $a=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $h=(1-\alpha)/2$.  For $0<\rho<h$, the shifted
load is $\ell_\rho=(\alpha(1-\rho),-\alpha\rho)$ and the root checkpoint is
\[
 x^-=(\alpha(1-\rho)/a,0).
\]
Its boundary demand at $v$ is
\[
 g_v=(\ell_\rho-Qx^-)_v=\frac{\alpha(h-\rho)}a>0.
\]
The Schur complement is $a-h^2/a=\alpha/a$, so the legal exact expansion
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 x_v^+=h-\rho,
 \qquad
 x_o^+-x_o^-=\frac{h(h-\rho)}a.
 \label{eq:vanishing-activation-jump}
\end{equation}
Thus $\|x^+-x^-\|\to0$ as $\rho\uparrow h$, although scanning and activating
$v$ always costs $c(v)=2$.  Joint continuity would force
$\Phi(\rho,x^-)-\Phi(\rho,x^+)\to0$, contradicting a uniform own-service
decrease of two.  The exact obstacle-energy drop is
\[
 \frac12\frac{g_v^2}{\alpha/a}
 =\frac{\alpha(h-\rho)^2}{2a}.
\]
An energy scaling that pays charge two must therefore be at least
$4a/[\alpha(h-\rho)^2]$, which diverges.
\end{proof}

The discontinuous support token in \Cref{thm:path-token-countdown} is not a
technical convenience; the proposition shows why a support jump must be
recorded explicitly.  Raw causal debts do not repair this issue either: an
exact neighbor push can create new exact-after-exact backflow from zero, and
a fresh over-relaxed push can create delayed-reflection magnitude from zero.
Neither raw magnitude is interference monotone without additional transport
aggregation.

\section{Energy facts for adaptive relaxation}
\label{sec:energy}

The next statements apply to the unregularized quadratic $f$ in the shared
model.  They do not assume monotone residual signs.
Define the error energy
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E(\widehat x)
 :=f(\widehat x)-f(x^0)
 =\frac12(\widehat x-x^0)^\mathsf TQ(\widehat x-x^0).
 \label{eq:error-energy}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact coordinate-energy ledger]
\label{lem:energy}
Let $\widehat x$ be any iterate, $r=b-Q\widehat x$, and let
$\widehat x^+$ be the row update \eqref{eq:row-update} on $u$.  For every
$0<\omega<2$,
\begin{equation}
 f(\widehat x)-f(\widehat x^+)
 =\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{2Q_{uu}}r_u^2
 =\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{1+\alpha}r_u^2.
 \label{eq:energy-drop}
\end{equation}
Consequently, along any finite adaptive run,
\begin{equation}
 f(\widehat x_0)-f(\widehat x_T)
 =\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
 \frac{\omega_t(2-\omega_t)}{1+\alpha}r_{t,u_t}^2.
 \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is computable from the live local residuals and does not
require knowing $x^0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $z=\omega r_u/Q_{uu}$.  The exact quadratic expansion gives
\[
 f(\widehat x+ze_u)-f(\widehat x)
 =z\nabla_u f(\widehat x)+\tfrac12Q_{uu}z^2
 =-\frac{\omega(2-\omega)}{2Q_{uu}}r_u^2.
\]
The shared matrix has $Q_{uu}=(1+\alpha)/2$.  Summing proves the telescoping
identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Myopic descent selects the exact rung]
\label{cor:myopic}
At a fixed iterate and coordinate, immediate objective decrease per charge
is uniquely maximized over $0<\omega<2$ by $\omega=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The residual and charge are fixed, while
$\omega(2-\omega)=1-(\omega-1)^2$ is uniquely maximized at one.
\end{proof}

Thus no controller based only on the current update's energy efficiency can
explain the measured advantage of over-relaxation.  The benefit must appear
over a block: over-relaxation changes which coordinates become live, how far
the frontier travels, and which expensive rows are revisited.  Equation
\eqref{eq:telescope} is still useful as an auditable block-progress
denominator, but comparing modes from the same starting state requires a
probe, a branch, or a structural theorem.

\subsection{A condition-free safety theorem}

The energy ledger proves that adaptive relaxation itself does not endanger
termination if it is clamped away from two.

\begin{theorem}[Safe causal variable relaxation]
\label{thm:safe-adaptive}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1]$.  Start from $\widehat x_0=0$.  At every paid step,
choose any coordinate satisfying the live guard
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\abs{r_{t,u_t}}}{\sqrt{d_{u_t}}}>g
 \label{eq:live}
\end{equation}
and choose $\omega_t\in[1,2-\delta]$ by any causal function of the entire
past, including the causal revisit ledger.  Require the controller to execute
such a live coordinate whenever \eqref{eq:note-gate} fails.  Then only
finitely much charged row work can occur before \eqref{eq:note-gate} holds,
and
\begin{equation}
 W\leq
 \frac{1+\alpha}
 {\delta(2-\delta)\,\alpha\epsppr^{2}}.
 \label{eq:safety-work}
\end{equation}
The statement is ordering independent and allows signed residuals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m_\delta:=\delta(2-\delta)$.  On $[1,2-\delta]$,
$\omega(2-\omega)\geq m_\delta$.  A paid live operation satisfies
$r_{t,u_t}^2>g^2d_{u_t}$.  Since $d_u\geq1$,
$c(u)=1+d_u\leq2d_u$.  By \Cref{lem:energy}, its objective decrease is at
least
\begin{equation}
 \frac{m_\delta g^2}{2(1+\alpha)}c(u_t).
 \label{eq:progress-charge}
\end{equation}

The minimum objective is $f(x^0)$, and the zero-start energy is
\[
 f(0)-f(x^0)=\tfrac12 b^\mathsf TQ^{-1}b
 \leq\frac{\norm{b}_2^2}{2\alpha}
 \leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\]
The last inequality follows from
$b_i=\alpha s_i/\sqrt{d_i}$, $d_i\geq1$, $s\geq0$, and
$\sum_i s_i=1$, which give $\norm{b}_2^2\leq\alpha^2$.  Sum
\eqref{eq:progress-charge}, use $g=\alpha\epsppr$, and solve for $W$ to
obtain \eqref{eq:safety-work}.  Infinite charged work would force more
objective decrease than the zero-start energy, so the paid process is finite.
If a live coordinate remained, the rule could make another paid step;
therefore exhaustion is exactly the note-scoped gate.
\end{proof}

The theorem permits the literal clipping interval $[1.10,1.68]$ used by the
frontier artifact, as well as a controller that raises or lowers $\omega$
after every operation.  It does not prove acceleration: the
$\epsppr^{-2}$ and $\alpha^{-1}$ scale is weaker than the intended
$\widetilde O(\mathsf V_{\rm loc}/\sqrt\alpha)$ target.  Its value is modular.  A learned rule
may be experimentally aggressive without carrying the correctness proof; the
clamp and live guard carry it.

This energy argument is also one unconditional but nonaccelerated instance
of \Cref{thm:mergeable-countdown}.  With
$m_\delta:=\delta(2-\delta)$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\rm en}(\widehat x)
 :=\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{m_\delta g^2}\mathcal E(\widehat x).
 \label{eq:energy-countdown}
\end{equation}
Every live update with relaxation in $[1,2-\delta]$ decreases this same
countdown by at least its charge, regardless of which policy requested the
update.  Hence it is interference monotone and recovers the safety theorem on
a genuinely shared state.  It does not distinguish the arms and has the
nonaccelerated scale.  The exact missing object for a lower-bound-matching
adaptive theorem is a $\kappa=\widetilde O(1)$ transport-block countdown for
at least one accelerated arm that other support-safe blocks cannot increase.
The delayed-reflection debt and the exact-after-exact backflow ledger are
concrete candidates for constructing such a countdown.

\subsection{A no-reset experimental prefix with exact fallback}

Even on one irreversible state, an adaptive policy can be tried for a
controlled budget and then handed to exact Gauss--Seidel.  The prefix's
energy decrease is not discarded.

\begin{theorem}[Budgeted adaptive prefix]
\label{thm:budgeted-prefix}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:safe-adaptive}, run an arbitrary causal
prefix of charged work $W_{\rm pre}$ with
$\omega_t\in[1,2-\delta]$.  From the resulting iterate, switch irreversibly
to live unit-relaxation updates until \eqref{eq:note-gate} holds.  Define
\begin{equation}
 H_0:=\frac{2(1+\alpha)\mathcal E(0)}{g^2}
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha\epsppr^2},
 \qquad m_\delta:=\delta(2-\delta).
 \label{eq:fallback-budget}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm total}
 \leq H_0+(1-m_\delta)W_{\rm pre}.
 \label{eq:fallback-total}
\end{equation}
In particular, imposing the hard cap $W_{\rm pre}\leq\theta H_0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm total}
 \leq[1+(1-m_\delta)\theta]H_0
 \leq(1+\theta)H_0.
 \label{eq:fallback-cap}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:progress-charge}, the prefix decreases $\mathcal E$ by at
least $m_\delta g^2W_{\rm pre}/(2(1+\alpha))$.  If
$\mathcal E_{\rm hand}$ is the energy at handoff, the same live-update
argument with $\omega=1$ bounds the exact tail by
\[
 W_{\rm tail}
 \leq\frac{2(1+\alpha)\mathcal E_{\rm hand}}{g^2}
 \leq H_0-m_\delta W_{\rm pre}.
\]
Add $W_{\rm pre}$ and apply its cap.  The upper bound on $H_0$ is the
zero-start calculation in \Cref{thm:safe-adaptive}.
\end{proof}

The theorem allows a revisit-based switch trigger on the actual single
state, with a work cap as its independent safety certificate.  It is not a
best-arm oracle inequality: the fallback ceiling is still the weak
energy-based exact-Gauss--Seidel scale.  A lower-bound-matching result would
need to replace $H_0$ by an accelerated local tail or show that a losing
prefix is chargeable to such a tail.

\section{A condition-free best-of-policy wrapper}
\label{sec:portfolio}

The nonidentifiability result concerns a controller forced to choose one
future policy.  Branching removes that information bottleneck.  Let
$P_1,\ldots,P_K$ be local solvers for the same instance and terminal
certificate.  They may include, for example, exact Gauss--Seidel, fixed
two-rung schedules at several band widths, an alpha-scaled ladder, and the
frontier adaptive ladder.  Each policy receives an independent sparse state
initialized from the same input.

For instance $I$, let $C_j(I)$ be the charged edge or row work policy $P_j$
would need if run alone until the verifier accepts it.  Fix positive service
shares $\nu_j$ with $\sum_j\nu_j=1$.  A fractional work scheduler gives arm
$j$ exactly a $\nu_j$ fraction of aggregate charged work and stops all arms
as soon as one certifies.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted portfolio oracle inequality]
\label{thm:portfolio}
Under fractional charged-work scheduling,
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm port}(I)
 =\min_{1\leq j\leq K}\frac{C_j(I)}{\nu_j}.
 \label{eq:portfolio-exact}
\end{equation}
In particular, uniform service satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm port}(I)=K\min_j C_j(I).
 \label{eq:uniform-portfolio}
\end{equation}

If work is scheduled in indivisible chunks and a fair scheduler maintains
received work $w_j(W)\geq\nu_jW-\Delta$ for every arm after aggregate work
$W$, then
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm port}(I)
 \leq\min_j\frac{C_j(I)+\Delta}{\nu_j}.
 \label{eq:portfolio-discrete}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After aggregate fractional work $W$, arm $j$ has received $\nu_jW$.  It
finishes exactly when $W=C_j(I)/\nu_j$; the first finishing arm proves
\eqref{eq:portfolio-exact}.  Uniform shares give
\eqref{eq:uniform-portfolio}.  Under the discrete invariant, aggregate work
$W=(C_j(I)+\Delta)/\nu_j$ guarantees
$w_j(W)\geq C_j(I)$, so arm $j$ has finished.  Minimize over $j$.
\end{proof}

The fractional model is natural for the manuscript's adjacency-edge work:
an adjacency scan can be paused and resumed after buffering its row update.
With atomic service operations, $\Delta$ can instead be taken as the largest
scheduling chunk.  The wrapper uses $K$ sparse solver states and may explore
the union of their frontiers; that storage and scalar-copy overhead must be
reported even though \eqref{eq:portfolio-exact} counts graph work exactly.
The benchmark service used by the measured artifact exposes only one mutable
state, so literal implementation there would require protocol support for
checkpoints or independent instances.

\subsection{Removing simultaneous branches by geometric restart}

If independent branch states are unavailable but the instance can be reset
to its common initial state, a universal geometric schedule retains a
constant-factor guarantee.  It uses one live solver state at a time.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Single-live-state restart portfolio}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE policies $P_1,\ldots,P_K$; base charged-work quantum $B_0>0$
\STATE test the terminal certificate at the common initial state
\FOR{$k=0,1,2,\ldots$}
  \FOR{$j=1,\ldots,K$}
    \STATE reset to the common initial state
    \STATE run $P_j$ for charged-work budget $2^kB_0$
    \IF{$P_j$ certifies within the budget}
      \RETURN its certified iterate
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Best-of-policy guarantee with one live state]
\label{thm:restart-portfolio}
Assume charged work can be paused at the declared budget and every policy is
deterministic from the common initial state.  Let
$C_\star:=\min_j C_j(I)$.  The restart portfolio returns a certified solution
using one live policy state and total work
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm restart}(I)<4K\max\{C_\star,B_0\}
 \leq4K(C_\star+B_0).
 \label{eq:restart-bound}
\end{equation}
With indivisible chunks, the same proof holds after adding the maximum
per-trial budget overshoot to $B_0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $k_\star$ be the first epoch with
$2^{k_\star}B_0\geq C_\star$.
A best policy certifies during that epoch.  Bounding every attempted policy
in every epoch by its full budget gives
\[
 C_{\rm restart}(I)
 \leq K\sum_{k=0}^{k_\star}2^kB_0
 <2K\,2^{k_\star}B_0.
\]
If $k_\star=0$, the last quantity is $2KB_0$.  Otherwise minimality gives
$2^{k_\star}B_0<2C_\star$.  Both cases imply
\eqref{eq:restart-bound}.
\end{proof}

This theorem removes simultaneous branching and all distributional
assumptions, at the price of recomputing prefixes.  It does require a true
reset.  A service exposing one irreversible mutable state, with neither
checkpointing nor reset, still needs a switch amortization proving that early
work under a losing mode is useful to the eventual winner.

\subsection{Safe learned allocation}

Let an arbitrary predictor output shares
$\widehat\nu_{t,1},\ldots,\widehat\nu_{t,K}$ for repeated instance $I_t$.
Its context may include graph identity, seed features, $\alpha$, gate
hardness, phase-wise touched charge, and the four-way causal revisit ledger.
For $\gamma\in(0,1]$, use
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{t,j}:=(1-\gamma)\widehat\nu_{t,j}+\frac{\gamma}{K}.
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Distribution-free safety of learned shares]
\label{cor:learned-safe}
For every sequence of instances and every predictor, the discrete portfolio
satisfies, separately on every $I_t$,
\begin{equation}
 C_{{\rm port},t}
 \leq\frac{K}{\gamma}
       \left(\min_j C_{t,j}+\Delta_t\right).
 \label{eq:floor-bound}
\end{equation}
If a best arm $j_t^\star$ receives predicted mass
$\widehat\nu_{t,j_t^\star}=\upsilon_t$, the sharper bound is
\begin{equation}
 C_{{\rm port},t}
 \leq
 \frac{C_{t,j_t^\star}+\Delta_t}
 {(1-\gamma)\upsilon_t+\gamma/K}.
 \label{eq:prediction-bound}
\end{equation}
No stationarity or transfer assumption is required.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:floor} gives $\nu_{t,j}\geq\gamma/K$ for every arm.
Apply \eqref{eq:portfolio-discrete} to a best arm for
\eqref{eq:floor-bound}, and retain its actual share for
\eqref{eq:prediction-bound}.
\end{proof}

This is the clean theoretical role for revisit memory.  It may improve the
constant by predicting a useful service allocation, but a wrong prediction
cannot remove any arm or invalidate the certificate.  The exploration floor,
not the empirical map from $\varrho_{\rm rev}$ to $\omega$, supplies the
worst-case theorem.

\subsection{Inheritance of accelerated work}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-menu inheritance]
\label{cor:inherit}
Let $\mathcal I$ be an instance class.  Suppose that for every
$I\in\mathcal I$ at least one policy in a fixed menu of size $K$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_j(I)\leq A(I).
 \label{eq:arm-bound}
\end{equation}
Then the uniform fractional portfolio satisfies
$C_{\rm port}(I)\leq K A(I)$.  In particular, if
$A(I)=\widetilde O(\mathsf V_{\rm loc}(I)/\sqrt\alpha)$ and $K$ is constant, the portfolio has
the same asymptotic scale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \eqref{eq:arm-bound} into \eqref{eq:uniform-portfolio}.
\end{proof}

The quantifiers matter.  The good arm may vary with the graph, seed,
accuracy, or local geometry; the portfolio does not need to identify it in
advance.  But the arm must already possess the asserted bound.  This wrapper
therefore removes the \emph{selection condition}, not the structural work
condition needed to prove an accelerated local arm.  It also does not turn an
algorithm-specific lower bound into a universal lower bound for the richer
branching access model.

\section{Recommended adaptive architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The proofs suggest three layers with distinct responsibilities.

\paragraph{Layer 1: a causal diagnostic, not a scalar verdict.}
Record $C(S)$, total work, all four $\mathsf{Rev}_{a\to b}$ entries,
phase-wise work,
and the block energy decrease from \eqref{eq:telescope}.  The pair
$\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm e}
 +\mathsf{Rev}_{\mathrm e\to\mathrm s}$ is certified
neighbor backflow.  A large scalar $\varrho_{\rm rev}$ without this
decomposition is ambiguous.  Also record full graph identity or a stable
fingerprint; graph size alone is not a theoretical context.
For support-safe RPPR traces, report the global fast-path certificate
$2\varrho_{\rm rev}/\rho$ from \Cref{cor:rppr-fast-path}, not merely the
dimensionless ratio.

\paragraph{Layer 2: a safety mechanism independent of prediction.}
For a single state, clamp every adaptive choice to $[1,2-\delta]$ and retain
the live guard, invoking \Cref{thm:safe-adaptive}; independently cap an
experimental prefix and invoke \Cref{thm:budgeted-prefix} before exact
fallback.  On nested RPPR regions, enforce \Cref{thm:revisit-bank} between
canonical settlements and switch only through the exact boundary gate of
\Cref{thm:absorption-safe-switch}; this preserves the irreversible state
without requiring cross-policy state transfer.  Also enforce a global
accelerated bank: successful runs receive \Cref{cor:rppr-fast-path}, and an
overdraw triggers canonicalization plus fallback under
\eqref{eq:accelerated-or-fallback}.  If mergeable countdowns are available,
\Cref{thm:mergeable-countdown} permits a work-fair shared-state portfolio.
With branchable state,
keep exact Gauss--Seidel and at least one conservative two-rung policy in the
portfolio, and enforce \eqref{eq:floor}.  Every returned answer is checked by
the same verifier-owned terminal gate.  If branches are unavailable but a
clean reset is allowed, use \Cref{thm:restart-portfolio}.

\paragraph{Layer 3: learning only changes allocation.}
Use the causal ledger to predict service shares, not to delete arms or alter
the terminal certificate.  One plausible qualitative prior is to distinguish
large exact-after-exact backflow from large spread-to-exact delivery work;
the former is direct evidence of reactivation after settlement, whereas the
latter can be planned debt cleanup.  Whether this distinction predicts the
best arm is empirical and must be evaluated out of sample.

For the presently measured family, a minimal menu is
\begin{equation}
 \left\{
  P_{\rm exact},\;
  P_{\rm 2rung}(\chi_{\rm band}=2.5,\omega_\star),\;
  P_{\rm scaled},\;
  P_{\rm adaptive}
 \right\},
 \label{eq:menu}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\rm scaled}$ uses an alpha-dependent band or relaxation motivated
by the delayed-reflection note, and $\chi_{\rm band}$ is the note-scoped
threshold multiplier rather than the reserved PageRank right-hand side $b$.
The value $2.5$ is only the best of six
tested band widths on the existing campaign; it is not a universal optimum.

\section{What remains for a matching bound}
\label{sec:remaining}

The third direction now has a sharp theoretical boundary.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item \textbf{Selection is solved for a finite branchable menu.}
 The portfolio chooses the fastest certifying arm up to a constant factor on
 every instance, without assuming that revisit statistics transfer between
 instances.

 \item \textbf{Arbitrary causal relaxation is safe.}
 The energy ledger proves termination and an explicit work ceiling, but that
 ceiling has the classical $1/\alpha$ rather than accelerated
 $1/\sqrt\alpha$ dependence and a quadratic tolerance cost.

 \item \textbf{Within-epoch multiplicity is enforceable.}
 The exact factorization \eqref{eq:two-level-factorization} connects the
 empirical ratio to the settled-volume ledger, and the revisit bank caps its
 within-epoch factor online.  A global bank also certifies the complete fast
 path whenever it succeeds.  For a theorem guaranteeing success, the
 remaining structural term is $\mathfrak R_{\rm set}$, unless incremental
 Schur state avoids repeated full settlement.

 \item \textbf{The accelerated fast path is certifiable but not universal.}
 Every support-safe terminal trace with
 $\varrho_{\rm rev}=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ already performs
 $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ charged work.  The heavy-early RPPR
 tree rules out policy-blind bank success, and the one-edge theorem proves
 that the literal fixed-band two-rung arm can take $\Omega(1/\alpha)$ even at
 fixed regularization and accuracy.  A universal positive result therefore
 needs a modified forced-spreading or incremental-transport arm in a fixed
 menu that performs
 $\widetilde O(\mathsf V_{\rm loc}/\sqrt\alpha)$ charged graph work under a clearly stated
 local-volume ledger $\mathsf V_{\rm loc}$.

 \item \textbf{The exact one-state competition condition is isolated.}
 A capped experimental prefix plus exact fallback is safe without reset, and
 the RPPR absorption checkpoint permits arbitrary causal policy switches
 while preserving support containment and correctness.  Separately,
 the geometric restart portfolio is constant-competitive without retaining
 parallel states when reset is available.  On a truly shared irreversible
 state, mergeable $\kappa$-tight countdowns are sufficient for a
 $K\kappa$-competitive theorem.  The heavy-early gate proves that absorption
 alone cannot supply them.  Activation tokens supply the missing countdown
 with $\kappa=1$ on endpoint paths, giving work at most $4/\rho$ for the exact
 Schur arm.  The two-vertex continuity obstruction explains why energy or raw
 debt magnitude cannot replace those discrete tokens.  The remaining
 amortization problem is an order-independent activation-once representation
 for branching and cyclic fronts.

 \item \textbf{A continuous $\omega$ family needs regularity.}
 A finite grid inherits the best grid arm.  Approximating the best continuous
 relaxation requires a theorem controlling how thresholded SOR work changes
 with $\omega$; live-set discontinuities make a naive Lipschitz assumption
 unsafe.
\end{enumerate}

Thus this direction can remove a major condition---knowing in advance which
local solver is appropriate---using multiple states or restart, and it now
removes the separate state-compatibility obstacle for irreversible RPPR
switching.  It also gives an a posteriori accelerated certificate without a
graph-structural hypothesis and a precise sufficient condition for a
shared-state best-arm ratio.  It cannot yet prove that the accelerated bank
always succeeds on general graphs.  It does construct the tight transport
countdown on serializable endpoint paths and proves why the literal two-rung,
arbitrary exact batching, and continuous state-energy routes cannot supply a
universal theorem.  This boundary prevents an adaptive wrapper from being
mistaken for a graph-uniform proof of the desired lower-bound-matching rate.

\section{Decisive experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The next empirical campaign should test the claims at their actual boundary.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item Log $C(S)$ and all four causal revisit charges, separately by rung.
 Verify that a paid revisit after an exact operation is preceded by a
 neighbor update in the trace.
 \item Run a global bank with
 $\Gamma_{\rm acc}=c\log^p(1/(\alpha\rho))/\sqrt\alpha$.  Report the fraction
 of instances certifying before overdraw, the overdraw checkpoint, and the
 exact fallback charge.  This directly tests \Cref{cor:rppr-fast-path}.
 \item Compare total work, not just $\varrho_{\rm rev}$, across policies.
 Report objective decrease from \eqref{eq:telescope} per charged work over
 complete rungs, since per-operation energy necessarily favors $\omega=1$.
 \item Replace the size-only memory key by graph fingerprint, seed class,
 $\alpha$, and hardness.  Evaluate transfer only from earlier to later
 instances; never leak a later arm's cost into the predictor.
 \item Simulate the weighted portfolio offline from per-arm work traces.  The
 measured total should obey \eqref{eq:portfolio-discrete}; report the chunk
 overhead $\Delta$ and peak union support.
 \item Stress distribution shift: alternate path-like, star-like, and
 thick-core instances at equal $n$.  The exploration floor should preserve
 the oracle bound even when the predictor is systematically wrong.
 \item Sweep smaller $\alpha$ and fit work exponents.  A better constant at
 $\alpha\in\{0.025,0.04\}$ does not establish the desired
 $\alpha^{-1/2}$ scale.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The revisit idea is promising, but its strongest theoretical form is not the
literal scalar-to-$\omega$ formula.  The exact scalar is a multiplicity
statistic.  Its causal refinement exposes neighbor backflow, while the energy
identity shows why over-relaxation must be judged over a transport block.
These facts explain both the signal's value and its ambiguity.

Several guarantees are already complete.  Causal variable relaxation is safe
under a compact clamp and live residual guard.  The empirical revisit ratio
factors exactly across nested epochs, and its within-epoch contribution can
be capped online.  On support-safe RPPR traces the global ratio is also a
direct a posteriori accelerated-work certificate.  A capped adaptive prefix
can fall back exactly on the same irreversible state with a quantified
ceiling.  Exact RPPR settlements form absorption-safe checkpoints for
history-dependent no-reset switching.  A
branchable finite portfolio is condition-free relative to its best arm and
can accept arbitrary
revisit-based predictions without sacrificing a per-instance oracle
inequality.  A geometric restart portfolio retains a constant-factor
best-arm theorem with one live state.  Together these supply a broader
framework for combining exact, two-rung, alpha-scaled, and adaptive local
solvers.

The actual RPPR batch-order obstruction shows why checkpoint safety is not a
work oracle, and the one-edge exact tail rules out the literal fixed-band
two-rung arm as a universal accelerated fallback.  The mergeable-countdown
theorem identifies the positive replacement.  It is fully instantiated on
endpoint paths by common activation-once Schur tokens, with $\kappa=1$ and
$O(C(S^\star))$ work.  The remaining graph-uniform target is an
order-independent version of that representation for branching and cyclic
fronts, or a forced-spreading arm with its own tight transport proof.  Revisit
feedback can select such theories safely; it is a controller, certificate,
and diagnostic, not by itself the source of the accelerated rate.
