\section{Evidence, provenance, and reusable artifacts}
The proof was developed in the task ``Prove conjecture 2 deterministically''
on September 5, 2026, consulting only the authorized problem-definition
\texttt{main.tex} from the manuscript during that research. Fresh internal
audits found no remaining proof gap. This is not a claim of external peer
review or machine-checked formal verification.

The immutable \texttt{proof.pdf} is the original 20-page proof.
The portable proof-and-solver ZIP in \texttt{archives/} contains its three
LaTeX sources, detailed reading guide, proof-to-code map, executable solver,
portable validation and benchmark runners, and curated audit records.
The separate research archive preserves the substantive working record,
including preliminary attempts and their corrections. Its manifest labels
all exclusions and hashes every retained file. Earlier exploratory claims
must be read together with the later status and audits; the final proof takes
precedence over obsolete attempts.

\paragraph{Measured.}
The same fixed 183-case exact integration manifest was exercised by several
backends. These are paired checks, not disjoint sets of graph instances.
Independent output certificates, prefix checks, and arithmetic audits provide
additional evidence. Small timing comparisons are empirical observations,
not universal performance claims and not substitutes for proof.

\paragraph{Source and comparison.}
Nesterov-style acceleration and proximal acceleration are established methods.
The accompanying \texttt{AESP\_CATALYST\_COMPARISON.md} records the comparison
requested after the proof was completed. AESP explicitly uses a localized
Catalyst-style proximal-point framework. This algorithm instead accelerates
the constrained correction directly within sparsity-continuation stages.
The additional local argument is the second energy and complete support-work
accounting. No literature-wide priority claim is made here.

\paragraph{Open.}
Independent external review, formal verification, a production floating-point
implementation with certified stability, and large-scale comparisons at
matched accuracy remain future work. They are distinct from the exact-real
theorem recorded here. The rational-input implementation does not establish
an unchecked floating-point theorem.
