\subsection{A computable initial energy and a shorter stage schedule}
\label{app:source-energy}

The initial bound $E_0\le1$ can be sharpened using the already assembled
source, without querying another graph word. Put
\[
 g_i=(s_i/w_i-\lambda)_+,\quad
 H_1=\sum_i d_i g_i,\quad H_2=\sum_i d_i g_i^2,\quad
 H_\infty=\max_i g_i,
\]
where an empty maximum is zero. Only stored source records contribute.
For the exact correction $e=\xi^*$, complementarity gives
\[
 E_0=\tfrac12 e^TQe+\tfrac\mu2\norm e^2,
 \qquad e^TQe=e^T(s-\lambda w).
\]
The box, mass, and spectral bounds respectively imply
\[
 e^TQe\le4rH_1,\qquad e^TQe\le\eta H_\infty,
 \qquad e^TQe\le H_2/\alpha.
\]
For the last inequality, combine
$e^TQe\le\norm e\sqrt{H_2}$ with
$\alpha\norm e^2\le e^TQe$; the zero case is immediate. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{app:initial-bound}
 E_0\le\overline E
 :=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{1+\mu/\alpha}{2}
       \min\{4rH_1,\eta H_\infty,H_2/\alpha\}\right\}
 \le3\alpha r\eta.
\end{equation}
The last bound follows from $H_\infty\le3\alpha r$. A single source pass
computes the statistics and is charged even when the resulting stage takes
no accelerated steps.

Exact $H_2$ can accumulate coprime degree denominators. For bounded
arithmetic write $\alpha=A_0/B_\alpha$, $r=P/R$, $h=1/H$, and let the
stored source density be $n_i/(2B_\alpha H d_i)$. Define
\[
 C=2B_\alpha HR,\qquad
 a_i=(Rn_i-2A_0HPd_i)_+.
\]
Then $g_i=a_i/(Cd_i)$, and the bounded-denominator statistics are
\[
 H_1=\frac{\sum_i a_i}{C},\qquad
 H_\infty=\frac{\max_i(a_i/d_i)}{C},\qquad
 H_2\le H_2^{\rm up}:=
       \frac{\sum_i\lceil a_i^2/d_i\rceil}{C^2}.
\]
If $M>0$ numerators are positive, the squared-statistic excess is less than
$M/C^2$; if $M=0$, it is zero. Each accumulation uses integers, and the
maximum retains only one degree denominator. Replacing $H_2$ by
$H_2^{\rm up}$ in \eqref{app:initial-bound} keeps both inequalities valid.

Keep the same grid, so $\Gamma<\tau/8$. Choose the least integer $q\ge0$
with $\overline E/2^q\le\tau/2$, and run $K=Tq$ iterations. The actual
gap is below $\tau$. At zero steps report $\overline E$ itself; after
positive steps a valid bound is
$\overline E/2^{\lfloor k/T\rfloor}+\Gamma$. If $q=0$, the unchanged
terminal repair still applies. The auxiliary energy and selected-flow
ledger hold for every such shorter prefix. No monotonicity of the original
objective along accelerated iterates is assumed.

\subsection{A tighter block contraction with fixed dyadic precision}
\label{app:binomial-block}

The factor $1/2$ per $T$ steps is conservative. A constant-size computation
gives a tighter certified factor without forming a large exact power.
For the integer $T\ge2$, set
\[
 m=\min\{6,T\},\qquad
 S_T=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom Tj(T-1)^{-j},\qquad
 \beta_T=2^{-16}\left\lceil\frac{2^{16}}{S_T}\right\rceil.
\]
The binomial theorem and positivity of omitted terms give
\[
 (1-1/T)^T
 =\left(1+\frac1{T-1}\right)^{-T}
 \le S_T^{-1}\le\beta_T<\frac12.
\]
For the strict upper bound, the first three terms give
$S_T\ge1+3T/(2(T-1))>5/2$, and
$2/5+2^{-16}<1/2$. In particular $\beta_2=1/4$ and
$\beta_4=81/256$. There are at most seven terms, with setup integers of
$O(1+\log T)$ bits.

In the preceding schedule, instead choose the least $q\ge0$ with
$\overline E\beta_T^q\le\tau/2$. The certificate after positive steps is
\[
 \overline E\beta_T^{\lfloor k/T\rfloor}+\Gamma.
\]
Store numerator and denominator powers of the fixed 16-bit dyadic factor.
Their bit lengths are $O(q)$, and they update only at completed blocks.
This changes neither the rounded trajectory nor its work proof; it changes
only the chosen endpoint. Zero-step stages use the preceding convention.

\subsection{An independent local certificate for a supplied output}
\label{app:independent-certificate}

There is also a certificate that does not use the acceleration proof.
For any sparse $x\ge0$ put $r^{\rm out}=Qx-b+\alpha\rho w$ and choose
\[
 \omega_i=\begin{cases}r^{\rm out}_i,&x_i>0,\\
                 \min\{r^{\rm out}_i,0\},&x_i=0.\end{cases}
\]
At a zero coordinate the Stieltjes signs give $(Qx-b)_i\le0$, so this
choice lies in the original weighted-absolute-value subgradient interval.
Thus $\omega\in\partial F_\rho(x)$. Strong convexity, minimizing its quadratic
lower model, proves
\begin{equation}\label{app:independent-gap}
 F_\rho(x)-F_\rho(x^*_\rho)\le\frac{\norm\omega^2}{2\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Only the supplied support, its immediate boundary, and the seed can have
nonzero $\omega$. Computing it scans each output row once; boundary rows are
not recursively scanned. Its local word charge is
$O((1+\vol(\supp x))\log(N+2))$, in addition to encoded scalar arithmetic.

For a common output denominator $x_i/w_i=B_i/H$, write
$\alpha=A_0/B_\alpha$, $\rho=P/R$, and $C=2B_\alpha HR$.
If $J_i=\sum_{j\sim i}B_j$, then $r^{\rm out}_i/w_i=t_i/(Cd_i)$ with
\[
 t_i=(B_\alpha+A_0)Rd_iB_i-(B_\alpha-A_0)RJ_i
       -2A_0HR\mathbf1_{i=v}+2A_0HPd_i.
\]
Retain $t_i$ on positive output coordinates and $\min\{t_i,0\}$ elsewhere.
If these selected integers are
$u_i$, an integer-accumulated upper bound is
\[
 \norm\omega^2\le\frac{\sum_i\lceil u_i^2/d_i\rceil}{C^2}.
\]
This avoids degree-denominator accumulation exactly as above. If the
resulting gap bound exceeds a requested tolerance, the certificate is
inconclusive; it does not disprove a tighter convergence certificate.

\subsection{Geometric cached checkpoints and a certified fallback}
\label{app:checkpoints}

The independent certificate can safely shorten the binomial-block stage.
Let $K=Tq$ be its prescribed horizon. At iteration indices
$k=T,2T,4T,\ldots$ strictly below $K$, materialize the actual full
candidate $x=\xb+\xi_k$ and compute the preceding independent gap bound.
Return that candidate only if the bound is strictly less than $\tau$.
If the test is inconclusive, discard its temporary records and continue
the same accelerated recurrence. At $K$, use the already proved
binomial-block certificate. If $K=0$, use the initial-energy bound without
performing a checkpoint. Every return therefore supplies exactly the
accuracy hypothesis needed by the terminal repair in
Appendix~\ref{app:pg}.

These checks use only cached graph data. A positive candidate coordinate
belongs to the baseline support or to a previously emitted kinetic support.
Its row was scanned during source initialization or its kinetic update,
respectively, and those scans exposed every neighboring degree. Hence the
certificate requires no new degree reply or adjacency list from the graph
oracle. Repeated cached reads are nevertheless charged. The check reads
the actual candidate and its rows, not the rounded neighbor-response
approximation used by the accelerated recurrence.

Let $W_k$ be the cumulative kinetic volume through iteration $k$ and
$V_k=\vol(\supp(\xb+\xi_k))$. The prefix work theorem gives
\[
 V_k\le\vol(\supp\xb)+W_k\le\frac{1+360k}{r}.
\]
If the actual return occurs at $k_{\rm ret}>0$, the checked indices satisfy
$\sum_j k_j<2k_{\rm ret}$. Thus
\[
 \sum_j V_{k_j}=O\!\left(\frac{k_{\rm ret}+1}{r}\right).
\]
Each check costs $O((V_{k_j}+1)\log(N+2))$ local operations, including
candidate materialization, every cached adjacency entry and degree lookup,
integer accumulation, and disposal of temporary balanced-tree records.
The added stage work is consequently
$O((k_{\rm ret}+1)\log(N+2)/r)$. Terminal repair pays for its own scans
again. The public continuation performs one such run per stage; any extra
user-requested materialization or manual continuation is additional work.

The candidate densities have a common denominator dividing $H^2$, so the
integer certificate retains the same scalar bit envelope. A successful
check retains only its scalar bound and the current iteration index.
Any later manual step invalidates that bound before changing the state;
the ordinary binomial bound then applies until another successful check.
At an unchanged iteration the checked candidate, numerical recurrence,
and all analytical energy bounds are exactly those of the binomial-only
method. In particular the tests do not modify the dynamics used in the
selected-work proof.

\subsection{Explicit local operation and bit bounds}
\label{app:explicit-complexity}

For the default complete integer wrappers in the nontrivial regime
$0<\alpha<1$ and $0<\rho<1/d_v$, let
\[
 \ell_\alpha=\lceil\log_2(1/\alpha)\rceil,\quad
 \ell_\rho=\lceil\log_2(1/\rho)\rceil,\quad
 \ell_\epsilon=\lceil\log_2\max\{1,1/\epsilon\}\rceil,\quad
 L=16+4\ell_\alpha+3\ell_\rho+\ell_\epsilon.
\]
These are analysis variables, not required algorithmic primitives. The
stage tolerances satisfy
\[
 \tau_j\ge\min\{\alpha^3\rho^2/32,
                         \alpha\epsilon\rho/64\}.
\]
Indeed, a last halving of the final shift leaves
$\delta^2>\epsilon\rho/8$; without such a halving, the ordinary shift
applies. Tolerances decrease along continuation. Since preceding baseline
denominators divide their old dyadic grid, the largest admissible dyadic
grid at the new tolerance already represents the baseline. Hence the
default grids obey
\[
 \theta\tau_j/512<h_j\le\theta\tau_j/256,
 \qquad \log_2(1/h_j)<L,\qquad K_j\le TL.
\]
Arbitrarily finer grids supplied to a separately invoked corrector are
outside this particular lower-grid estimate.

At a stage with $K$ steps, initialization and the prefix work bound give
$N\le363(K+1)/r$ exposed records. Thus $\log(N+2)=O(L)$. A rebase needs
more than $T/4$ steps since the last reset: while $S\le H$, each step
decreases $S$ by at most $H/T+1$, and $H\ge T$. There are at most
$4\theta K$ rebases. Each pays for every old and new record and key.
The two-tree reporter uses four breakpoint sequences and
$O(\log^2(N+2))$ work per root query. A conservative full stage charge is
\[
 O\!\left(L+K\log^2(N+2)
       +\frac{K+1}{r}(1+\theta K)\log(N+2)\right).
\]
This includes zero-step initialization and terminal work; the optional
checkpoint charge above is absorbed by the same bound. The geometric
schedule has $\sum_j1/r_j<3/\rho$. Consequently the bounded integer
realization uses
\begin{equation}\label{app:explicit-word}
 O\!\left(\frac{L^3}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \quad\hbox{arithmetic and word operations},\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{L}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \quad\hbox{adjacency inspections}.
\end{equation}
Integer quotient/remainder operations are included in this arithmetic
ledger. This does not silently add a floor primitive to the arbitrary-real
model of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Let $b_{\rm par}$ bound the total encoding length of the three rational
parameters and $b_{\rm graph}$ the largest encountered degree or vertex-label
encoding length. The common-denominator arguments above bound stored
integers, reduced rational components, and temporary products by
$B=O(b_{\rm par}+b_{\rm graph}+L)$ bits. Direct affine root reconstruction
introduces only a degree sum; scalar block powers use $O(L)$ bits. A
conservative schoolbook-arithmetic and Euclidean-gcd implementation therefore
has bit-arithmetic cost
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{B^3L^3}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right).
\]
Encoded oracle replies are paid; any additional computation inside a
user-supplied graph oracle must be added. These bounds separate parameter
and graph-word encoding costs from the original exact-real theorem.
The cases $\rho d_v\ge1$ and $\alpha=1$ use their direct branches, with
$O(1)$ scalar/word operations and $O(B^3)$ conservative bit work; the
displayed nontrivial-regime logarithms are not applied to those branches.

\subsection{Sharper work from the remaining mass}
\label{app:rounded-deficit}

The mass-deficit refinement \eqref{app:strong-work} also holds for the
actual complete integer wrappers, including their optional schedules,
checkpoints, and direct tree transitions. No grid refinement is needed.
At a nontrivial stage, the seed belongs to $\supp x_r^*$: if its value
were zero, Stieltjes signs would give $(Qx_r^*)_v\le0$, contradicting
$b_v-\alpha r w_v>0$. Equation~\eqref{app:deficit-volume} therefore gives
\[
 \eta\ge\eta_*(r)\ge r d_v\ge r,\qquad
 \vol(\supp\xb)\le\eta/r,\qquad\vol(C_r)\le2\eta/r.
\]
Retain $m_s=\alpha\eta$ in the perturbed auxiliary comparison instead
of bounding it by $\alpha$. Its proof gives
$B_k\le a^kB_0+\Gamma$, $B_0\le3\lambda m_s$, and
$\mathcal A(t)=\lambda m_s$. Dropping the nonnegative mass and mirror
terms yields $\norm{Q\xi_k-s}^2\le8\lambda m_s+2\Gamma$. The same
optimal-source estimate as before then proves
\begin{equation}\label{app:deficit-response}
 \norm{Q(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2
 \le18\alpha^2r\eta+4\Gamma.
\end{equation}
The actual default tolerances and grids already satisfy
\[
 \Gamma\le\frac{29h}{\theta}
 \le\frac{29\tau}{256}
 \le\frac{29\alpha^3r^2}{8192}
 \le\alpha^2r\eta.
\]
Here $\tau=\alpha\delta^2/8$ and $\delta\le\alpha r/2$; this extra
inequality is not asserted for arbitrary separately supplied stage grids.

In the existing perturbed selected-flow argument take
$B_0^{\rm core}=K\vol(C_r)\le2\eta K/r$ and
$H_2\le22\alpha^2r\eta K$. Absorbing $\nu\le\lambda/4$ and applying
the same Young inequality gives, for every actual executed prefix,
\begin{equation}\label{app:rounded-mass-work}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\vol(\supp z_{k+1})
 \le\frac{16H_2}{\lambda^2}+4B_0^{\rm core}
 \le\frac{360\eta K}{r}.
\end{equation}
Thus source records, baseline scans, emitted history, and direct exception
updates all inherit the factor $\eta/r$. In particular
$N\le363\eta(K+1)/r$. The fixed root-query and setup charges are
absorbed because $\eta/r\ge1$, including at zero-step stages.
Rebases still pay every retained record, and geometric checkpoints use
$V_k\le\eta(1+360k)/r$ and the same summation argument. All terminal
scans and temporary-state reclamation remain charged.

The actual rounded repair obeys \eqref{app:repair-deficit}; the diffuse-source
comparison \eqref{app:diffuse-deficit} and concavity therefore give
$\eta_j/r_j\le4\eta_*(\rho)/\rho$ at every stage. A conservative
per-stage word bound is $O((\eta_j/r_j)TL^3)$. Summing over $O(L)$ stages
gives the simultaneous bounds
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{\eta_*(\rho)L^4}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \quad\hbox{word/arithmetic operations},\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{\eta_*(\rho)L^2}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)
 \quad\hbox{adjacency inspections}.
\]
The bounds in \eqref{app:explicit-word} remain valid as well, so their
minimum may be taken. The unchanged scalar encoding gives the same
conservative $B^3$ multiplier for bit work. In soft notation the actual
rounded implementation consequently satisfies
$\widetilde O(\eta_*(\rho)/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$.
The stage-count logarithm is paid explicitly here; no constant-factor
geometric sum of mass-weighted stages is assumed.

For interpretation, the same KKT source inequalities imply
\[
 \vol(\supp x_\rho^*)\le\frac{\eta_*(\rho)}\rho
 \le\min\{1/\rho,\vol(V)\}.
\]
No total graph volume is queried. The final returned mass deficit brackets
$\eta_*(\rho)$ within the already proved factor $1+\alpha$.

\subsection{An optional exact singleton check}
\label{app:singleton}

A small deterministic precheck can recognize a seed-only exact optimum.
After the zero and $\alpha=1$ direct branches, the minimizer restricted
to the seed has density
\[
 f_v=\frac{2\alpha(1-\rho d_v)}{d_v(1+\alpha)}>0.
\]
Its seed derivative vanishes. Each neighboring zero coordinate has
density-coordinate gradient $\alpha\rho d_i-cf_v$, and every other zero
coordinate has gradient $\alpha\rho d_i>0$.
Thus this vector is the unique exact optimum
if and only if
$cf_v\le\alpha\rho d_i$ for every neighbor $i$ of $v$; equality is
accepted. With $\alpha=A_0/B_\alpha$ and $\rho=P/R$, the test is the
integer comparison
\[
 (B_\alpha-A_0)(R-Pd_v)
 \le P d_v(B_\alpha+A_0)d_i.
\]
Scan only the seed row, stopping at the first failed inequality. On success
return this exact vector with zero objective gap; on failure run the
certified solver and charge both the trial and its complete fallback.
A fixed seed-degree cutoff, such as $32$, can skip the trial on large rows.

The trial needs at most $d_v$ entries and $d_v+1$ degrees, constant
auxiliary scalar storage, and $O(d_v+1)$ word operations. It never reads a
boundary row and does not aggregate degree denominators. In the nontrivial
regime $d_v<1/\rho$ and $d_v\le\eta_*(\rho)/\rho$, so this prefix
preserves both proved local bounds. Its integers have the same encoding
envelope, with $O((d_v+1)B^3)$ conservative bit work. A failed trial may
cause repeated oracle reads in the fallback; those reads are counted again.
The exact singleton density may be rational rather than dyadic, so this is
a final-output check, not an unproved intermediate grid conversion.

\subsection{Choosing the next stage from the repaired source}
\label{app:adaptive}

An optional deterministic schedule can skip some of the prescribed halving
stages. After repair at $r_{\rm old}<1/d_v$, compute the exact scalar
\[
 M=\max_i\frac{(b-Q\xb)_i}{w_i}.
\]
The repair implies $M\le2\alpha r_{\rm old}$. Conversely,
$M<\alpha r_{\rm old}$ would make $\xb$ a nonnegative supersolution at
$r_{\rm old}$. Its least-supersolution property and $\xb\le x^*_{r_{\rm old}}$
would give equality. The nontrivial optimum has a positive seed, where
its source equals $\alpha r_{\rm old}w_v$, a contradiction. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{app:adaptive-source}
 \alpha r_{\rm old}\le M\le2\alpha r_{\rm old},\qquad
 r_{\rm next}=\max\{\rho,M/(4\alpha)\}.
\end{equation}
Initially $\xb=0$ and $r_0=1/d_v$ give $M=\alpha/d_v$ directly from
the queried seed degree, so the first stage is $\max\{\rho,r_0/4\}$.
The zero and $\alpha=1$ branches remain unchanged.

Every new stage has $0\le b-Q\xb\le4\alpha r_{\rm next}w$ and
$\xb\le x^*_{r_{\rm next}}$. These are exactly the hypotheses used by
the correction and repair proofs. A nonfinal stage satisfies
$r_{\rm old}/4\le r_{\rm next}\le r_{\rm old}/2$.
A final stage clamped to $\rho$ can be closer to $r_{\rm old}$; it still
strictly decreases the parameter. Use the same intermediate and final
shift rules and the same proved stage certificates as before. The number
of stages remains $O(1+\log(1/\rho))$, and their reciprocals satisfy
$\sum_j1/r_j<3/\rho$. The source-energy, binomial, and cached-checkpoint
options use only the current stage invariant and therefore remain valid.
A zero-iteration stage still pays initialization and repair. After the
final repair no further source maximum is computed.

To obtain $M$, assemble the source exactly from the retained baseline
support, its immediate boundary, and the seed. This costs
$O((1+\vol(\supp\xb))\log(N+2))$ word operations and
$O(\vol(\supp\xb))$ adjacency inspections. Cached entries are charged
again; a newly retained row may be scanned and is charged here. No boundary
row outside the retained support is scanned. Reusing this assembled source
is optional; assembling it again at the next corrector costs another paid
pass. Both charges fit the existing ledger. The initial maximum needs no
row scan. Hence the original word and adjacency bounds remain valid.
Equation~\eqref{app:diffuse-deficit} gives $\eta_j\le4\eta_*(r_j)$
at every new stage independently of its predecessor, so the sharper
mass-deficit bounds also remain valid.

The adaptive parameter does not cause growing rational coefficients.
If $\alpha=A_0/B_\alpha$ and the repaired densities are $P_i/H$, let
\[
 n_i=2A_0H\mathbf1_{i=v}-(B_\alpha+A_0)d_iP_i
             +(B_\alpha-A_0)\sum_{j\sim i}P_j.
\]
Then $s_i/w_i=n_i/(2B_\alpha H d_i)$. Choose a maximum by comparing
$n_i d_j$ and $n_j d_i$, retaining only the maximizing numerator and
degree. If $i_*$ attains it, the proposed parameter is
\[
 \frac{M}{4\alpha}=\frac{n_{i_*}}{8A_0H d_{i_*}}.
\]
This representation is rebuilt from the current dyadic baseline, without
a product of previous parameter denominators. A clamped step retains the
original target $\rho$. Tolerances still decrease, stay above the lower
bound in Appendix~\ref{app:explicit-complexity}, and produce nested grids
with $\log H=O(L)$. The extra integer accumulations, cross products, and
new parameter therefore retain $O(b_{\rm par}+b_{\rm graph}+L)$ bits.
The explicit word, adjacency, and conservative bit bounds remain unchanged.
This schedule changes the stage trajectory; it is not claimed to reduce
elapsed time on every input.

\subsection{A mass-scaled rounding grid}
\label{app:mass-grid}

The exact correction cap $\eta$ also permits a coarser grid. In the error
interface \eqref{app:error-model}, suppose all feasible states and both
analytical comparators have mass at most $\eta$. The same proof as
Lemma~\ref{app:two-energy} gives
\begin{equation}\label{app:mass-perturbation}
 E^+\le aE+\eta\zeta,\qquad B^+\le aB+\eta\zeta,
 \qquad
 \zeta=2\mu\kappa_r+\frac52\kappa_x+
                       \frac52(\theta+\mu)\kappa_p.
\end{equation}
Here the mass-face sector term is exactly
$\gamma(\alpha\eta-w^Ts)=0$, and each raw-error sector term is at most
$2\eta\kappa_r$. To check the remaining factor, let
$\xi_0^+=a\xi+\theta p_0$ and $d=\xi^+-\xi_0^+\le0$.
Writing $\kappa_d=\theta\kappa_p+\kappa_x$, its lost mass is at most
$\eta$, so $\norm d^2\le\eta\kappa_d$.
Also $w^T|Q\xi_0^+-s|\le(1+\alpha)\eta\le2\eta$.
Quadratic expansion therefore bounds the increase of either primal
objective by $5\eta\kappa_d/2$; both linear mass penalties decrease.
The mirror-distance increase costs at most $5\mu\eta\kappa_p/2$.
These estimates prove \eqref{app:mass-perturbation}, including when the
auxiliary energy is negative.

For the unchanged directed rounding and scalar rebase, define the effective
floor $\Gamma_\eta=\eta\zeta/\theta$. Equations
\eqref{app:rounding-constants} and \eqref{app:raw-rounding} imply
\[
 \Gamma_\eta\le\frac{29\eta h}{\theta}.
\]
At the actual tolerance $\tau=\alpha\delta^2/8$, with
$0<\delta\le\alpha r/2$, choose the largest reciprocal power of two
$h$ that represents the incoming baseline exactly and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{app:mass-grid-ceiling}
 h\le\psi:=\frac{\theta\tau}{256\eta}.
\end{equation}
The nontrivial stage has $r\le\eta\le1$, so
\[
 \psi\le\frac{\theta\alpha^3r^2}{8192\eta}
       \le\frac{\theta\alpha^3r}{8192}.
\]
This implies all former numerical guards: $h\le1/8$,
$2h\le\lambda/4$, $h\le\delta/2$, and
$h\le\theta\alpha^2r/29$. Consequently
\[
 \Gamma_\eta\le\frac{29\tau}{256}<\frac\tau8,
 \qquad \Gamma_\eta\le\alpha^2r\eta.
\]
Retaining $m_s=\alpha\eta$ in the auxiliary initialization gives
\[
 \norm{Q(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2
 \le18\alpha^2r\eta+4\Gamma_\eta.
\]
The proof of \eqref{app:rounded-mass-work} therefore applies unchanged,
including its repeated scans and all other ledger charges. Every stopping
schedule and accepted certificate continues to use the actual target
$\tau$. The relaxed quantity $\tau/\eta$ is only a grid-selection device;
it is not an admissible replacement for the requested stage tolerance.
The exact terminal gradient, downward repair, and monotone maximum with
the previous baseline are unchanged. Zero-step stages still use their
initial energy bound without an added rounding floor.

Exact representation of the baseline suffices; retaining unused bits of
the preceding grid is unnecessary. To verify the encoding bound, let
$g_{\rm base}$ be the reciprocal of the largest reduced denominator of
the incoming dyadic baseline (one for an empty baseline), and let
$g_{\rm num}$ be the largest reciprocal power of two at most $\psi$.
Then $g_{\rm base}\ge h_{\rm old}$ and
$h_{\rm new}=\min\{g_{\rm base},g_{\rm num}\}$.
Since $\eta\le1$ and actual tolerances are nonincreasing, induction gives
\[
 h_{\rm new}\ge\min\{h_{\rm old},g_{\rm num}\}
       >\frac{\theta\tau_{\rm new}}{512},
 \qquad H_{\rm new}<\frac{512}{\theta\tau_{\rm new}}.
\]
The first stage starts with the zero baseline. Thus the previous
logarithmic grid and integer-encoding bounds require no enlargement,
even if a final clamped stage permits coarsening. Computing $\eta$ from
the retained baseline uses one integer degree-weighted sum on its previous
grid and one bounded rational scalar; it creates no least common multiple
of degrees or product of historical denominators. The additional cached
baseline pass and any repeated scalar schedule construction are charged.
This option preserves the proved word, adjacency, and bit bounds. A
coarser grid can change the numerical trajectory, so smaller elapsed time
or fewer graph reads on every input is not asserted.
