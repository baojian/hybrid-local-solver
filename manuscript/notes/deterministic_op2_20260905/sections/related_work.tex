\section{Relationship to earlier work and the 2026 results}
\label{sec:det-op2-related}

The distinction is the combination of a constrained trajectory, diffuse
continuation and a second response energy. Accelerated objective convergence
alone does not pay for growing or repeatedly visited support. The new
trajectory permits a cumulative-volume proof and a matching deterministic
reporter. This is an audited research claim, not a claim of established
publication priority.

\paragraph{Fountoulakis--Mart\'inez-Rubio (2026).}
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21138v2}{FM26, version 2, Theorems 4.3--4.4}
analyzes classical FISTA through two regularization levels and cumulative
spurious work. Its bound includes a boundary contribution of order
$\widetilde O(\sqrt{\vol(B)}/(\rho\alpha^{3/2}))$ in addition to
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$, under the specified confinement
hypothesis; a structural no-percolation condition is sufficient.
It already avoids a global minimum KKT margin. Our distinction is therefore
not merely removing strict complementarity or considering two regularizers.
The box, mass cap and diffuse continuation establish a second-energy estimate
and pay for excursions without a supplied confinement set. This does not
prove the same unconditional bound for ordinary FISTA.

\paragraph{Wei--Yang (2026).}
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16339v1}{WY26, Theorem 1.3 (version 1)}
gives RPPR work depending on the optimum support:
$\widetilde O(k\vol(S^*))$, bounded by
$\widetilde O(1/\rho^2)$ in the worst case, together with its stated
objective and ACL guarantees. Its randomized implementation has only
polylogarithmic dependence on the damping parameter; a deterministic SDD
substitution is also discussed, with an additional $k^{o(1)}$ factor.
Thus it would be inaccurate to describe that work as having no deterministic
option. Our algorithm instead has linear inverse-$\rho$ dependence and an
explicit $\alpha^{-1/2}$ factor, with no linear-system solver primitive.
Ignoring logarithms, our worst-case envelope is smaller when
$\rho<\sqrt\alpha$; support-adaptive instances can favor WY26.
Teleportation conventions must first be aligned: lazy $\alpha$ corresponds
to nonlazy $2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$, with the associated objective rescaling.

\paragraph{Earlier acceleration and the user's notes.}
\href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/martinez-rubio23b.html}{ASPR (2023)}
provides deterministic acceleration through repeated restricted solves.
The user's \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15020}{2024 local evolving-set
framework} makes cumulative active volume central to the work analysis.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08010}{AESP (2025), Theorem 3.6 and
Section 3.3} gives accelerated local proximal bounds with a trajectory
parameter $R$ and explicitly discusses the difficulty of bounding it and
the possible role of simplex constraints. These are substantive precursors
to the present volume accounting and mass constraint. The present proof
changes the trajectory and adds a box and diffuse-source continuation;
it does not establish a universal bound for the original AESP parameter.
The earlier active-set, residual and threshold-reporter notes also help
separate valid certificates from unsupported support-discovery charges.

\paragraph{Practical comparison.}
An optimized, equally certified comparison with FISTA has not been completed.
Continuation, cap projection, ordered trees and safe repair add overhead.
The proved advantage is a worst-case local work guarantee; practical speed
depends on implementation and graph family. Small damping and expensive
spurious boundary activations are plausible favorable regimes, not measured
universal improvements. Detailed version pointers, prompt influence and
historical experiments are retained in \path{DISCUSSION.md}.
