\subsection{Purpose of this note}

The research question is whether one can combine an accelerated
$\widetilde O(1/\sqrt{\alpha})$ outer rate with the output-sensitive locality
of PageRank push methods.  In the $\ell_1$-regularized formulation, the target
degree-weighted work scale is
\begin{equation}
  \softO\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt{\alpha}}\right),
  \label{eq:target-work}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the note-scoped regularization/locality scale.  This note is a
proof ledger for that target.  It preserves successful lemmas, failed proof
routes, counterexamples, and the exact remaining alternatives instead of
retrofitting the discussion into a theorem that is not yet available.

The chronology was as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
  \item Empirically, accelerated local methods often reduce the error rapidly
  before their active-set volume reaches the peak of its bell-shaped
  trajectory.  This suggested an accelerated burn-in followed by a monotone
  local relaxation tail.
  \item Over-regularization and a lower retraction gave a rigorous handoff to
  a one-sided $\omega=1$ local Gauss--Seidel process with work at the target
  scale in~\eqref{eq:target-work}.  The only missing part was the warmup work.
  \item A generalized Bregman divergence was designed to charge a linear tax
  exactly when a new coordinate activates.  Every matched inner minimizer is
  $1/\rho$-local, but the same linear tax destroys the quadratic triangle
  scaling needed for acceleration.
  \item Ordinary Euclidean proximal subproblems were then shown to be local
  whenever their centers are certified lower subsolutions.  Classical
  extrapolation, however, does not preserve that order.
  \item An exact cumulative-volume identity reduced the warmup problem to a
  mass-weighted two-step deactivation flux.  The conjecture that this flux is
  $O(\alpha T)$ would have completed the proof.
  \item Radial computations on finite rooted trees exposed repeated
  activation/deactivation waves with rapidly increasing degree-weighted mass.
  An exact growing period-11 wave exists in the outward limiting recurrence.
  Thus the flux conjecture must not be used as a graph-uniform lemma.
  \item In parallel, a path-bundle construction gave a matching product lower
  bound for a natural persistent-support one-hop oracle.  It does not extend
  automatically to the broader locally evolving-set oracle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Status conventions}

The repository-wide residual decision is open.  All RPPR residuals,
thresholds, and accuracy statements below are therefore explicitly
\emph{note-scoped}; no conversion to the active manuscript's eventual
canonical stopping rule is asserted.

We use six claim labels.
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.8cm]
  \item[\SourceStatus] A statement taken from a cited paper in the source's
  own normalization.
  \item[\ProvedStatus] A self-contained algebraic or graph-theoretic statement
  proved in this note.
  \item[\ConditionalStatus] A theorem whose locality hypothesis is displayed
  explicitly.
  \item[\ComputedStatus] A reproducible finite-dimensional computation, not
  silently promoted to an interval-arithmetic proof.
  \item[\RefutedStatus] A proposed lemma or proof route for which an exact
  obstruction or counter-mechanism has been identified.
  \item[\OpenStatus] A missing theorem, reduction, or formal transfer step.
\end{description}
