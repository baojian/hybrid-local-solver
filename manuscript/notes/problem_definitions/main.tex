\begin{abstract}
This note fixes a compact reference for the two optimization problems used in
the project: personalized PageRank (PPR) and regularized personalized PageRank
(RPPR).  It records the exact graph, canonical single-seed input, parameter,
output, and local-access models; translates the lazy symmetric system into
lazy fixed-point, rescaled,
non-lazy mass, and non-lazy degree coordinates; and collects standard
spectral, positivity, normalization, optimality, sparsity, bias, and residual
properties.  Every substantive property is either a cited source result or an
elementary consequence of the displayed definitions.  No algorithm, proved
complexity claim, experiment, or new theorem is included.  The final section
records three explicitly open complexity questions, and the repository-wide
residual decision remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and status}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-scope}

\paragraph{Claim status.}
This is a \statussource{} reference document.  The reusable canonical fragment
is \texttt{manuscript/tex/shared/source\_aligned\_problem.tex}; the expanded
presentation below preserves its mathematical definitions while adding
explanation specific to this reference note.  Changes to a shared equation or
symbol must be synchronized with that fragment.  Statements labeled
``standard consequence'' are included only to make the reference
self-contained.  They are direct linear-algebra or convex-analysis
consequences of the cited source facts, not claims of novelty.

\paragraph{Included objects.}
The note covers the PPR linear system and fixed point, the RPPR convex
surrogate, the canonical point-source input and general-distribution
algebraic boundary, semantic output accuracy, a sufficient residual-to-error
implication, and the adjacency-list input and work unit.  It excludes every
specific solver, activation rule, momentum scheme, claimed convergence rate,
and experiment.  It records only the root-level and source-level open
complexity questions stated in
\cref{sec:problem-definitions-open-questions}; route-specific blockers remain
in their owning research notes.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
Personalized PageRank (PPR) denotes the exact linear-system problem in
\cref{prob:problem-definitions-ppr} and its probability vector $\bm{\pi}$.
Regularized personalized PageRank (RPPR) denotes the weighted
$\ell_1$-regularized problem in \cref{prob:problem-definitions-rppr}.
The running-time complexity of accelerated $\ell_1$-regularized PageRank was
posed explicitly as an open problem at COLT 2022 by
\citet{fountoulakis2022open}. That question motivates the RPPR research
program but does not add a condition to the problem definition below.
Approximate Personalized PageRank (APPR) denotes the classical local push
algorithm of \citet{andersen2006local} for approximating PPR; it is an
algorithm, not a third optimization problem.

\subsection{Common source-aligned PageRank and RPPR model}
\label{subsec:shared-source-aligned-problem}

All documents in this manuscript workspace use the following reference
language. Vectors and matrices are typeset in bold. The generic optimization
variable is \(\bm{x}\); \(\bm{x}^*\) denotes an optimum when the objective is
clear from context; \(\bm{x}_0^*\) and \(\bm{x}_\rho^*\) denote respectively
the unregularized PPR optimum and the RPPR optimum at regularization
\(\rho\). A note may impose a stronger assumption after this block, but it
must not redefine these objects or rename these three optimum vectors.

Let \(\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})\) be a finite, simple,
undirected, connected graph with \(n:=|\mathcal{V}|\geq2\) and unit edge
weights. Let \(\mathcal{V}=[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let
\(\bm{A}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\) be its symmetric adjacency matrix. For
\(i\in\mathcal{V}\), write
\[
    \mathcal N(i):=\{j\in\mathcal{V}:\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{E}\},
    \qquad
    d_i:=|\mathcal N(i)|,
    \qquad
    \bm{D}:=\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_n).
\]
Connectedness and \(n\geq2\) imply \(d_i\geq1\) for every vertex, so all
displayed powers of \(\bm{D}\) are well-defined.
For \(\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\), define
\[
    \mathcal N(\mathcal{S}):=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal N(i),
    \quad
    \partial\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal N(\mathcal{S})\setminus\mathcal{S},
    \quad
    \operatorname{Ext}(\mathcal{S}):=\mathcal{V}\setminus(\mathcal{S}\cup\partial\mathcal{S}),
\]
The volume of a vertex set is the sum of its degrees:
\begin{equation}
    \vol(\mathcal{S}):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}d_i.
    \label{eq:shared-volume}
\end{equation}
The support of a vector is
\(\supp(\bm{x}):=\{i\in[n]:x_i\neq0\}\). The symbols \(\preceq,\succeq\)
denote Loewner order; \(\leq,\geq\) denote entrywise order on vectors and
matrices. The seed is a column distribution
\begin{equation}
    \bm{s}\in\R_+^n,
    \qquad
    \inner{\one}{\bm{s}}=1.
    \label{eq:shared-seed}
\end{equation}
The single-seed specialization is always written \(\bm{s}=\bm{e}_v\), where
\(v\in\mathcal{V}\) is the seed vertex. Thus, \(\bm{s}\) is never used as a
vertex label.  The project's canonical end-to-end computational problem uses this single-seed
specialization.  The general unit-mass distribution above is retained as a
source-aligned algebraic extension; a theorem for it must state its additional
input and work dependence explicitly. For
\(\alpha\in(0,1]\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \bm{\mathcal L}&:=\bm{I}-\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{A}\bm{D}^{-1/2},\\
    \bm{Q}&:=\alpha\bm{I}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\bm{\mathcal L}
      =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\bm{I}
       -\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{A}\bm{D}^{-1/2},\\
    \bm{b}&:=\alpha\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{s}.
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:shared-pagerank-matrices}
\end{equation}
Since \(\bm{0}\preceq\bm{\mathcal L}\preceq2\bm{I}\),
\(\alpha\bm{I}\preceq\bm{Q}\preceq\bm{I}\). The shared smooth PageRank
quadratic is
\begin{equation}
    f(\bm{x}):=\frac12\inner{\bm{x}}{\bm{Q}\bm{x}}-\inner{\bm{b}}{\bm{x}},
    \qquad
    \nabla f(\bm{x})=\bm{Q}\bm{x}-\bm{b}.
    \label{eq:shared-pagerank-objective}
\end{equation}
Its unique unregularized minimizer and associated lazy personalized PageRank
vector are
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}_0^*:=\bm{Q}^{-1}\bm{b},
    \qquad
    \bm{\pi}:=\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}_0^*.
    \label{eq:shared-ppr-solution}
\end{equation}
For a regularization parameter \(\rho>0\), reserve \(g_\rho\) for the
nonsmooth weighted \(\ell_1\) term and define
\begin{equation}
    g_\rho(\bm{x}):=\alpha\rho\|\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}\|_1,
    \qquad
    F_\rho(\bm{x}):=f(\bm{x})+g_\rho(\bm{x}),
    \qquad
    \bm{x}_\rho^*:=\argmin_{\bm{x}\in\R^n}F_\rho(\bm{x}).
    \label{eq:shared-rppr-objective}
\end{equation}
When the objective is clear, \(\bm{x}^*\) may abbreviate its unique optimum;
the explicit symbols remain \(\bm{x}_0^*\) and \(\bm{x}_\rho^*\). Write
\[
    \mathcal{S}_\rho^*:=\supp(\bm{x}_\rho^*),
    \qquad
    \mathcal{I}_\rho^*:=[n]\setminus\mathcal{S}_\rho^*.
\]
The source records \(\bm{x}_\rho^*\geq\bm{0}\),
\(\vol(\mathcal{S}_\rho^*)\leq1/\rho\), and the coordinatewise conditions
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_i f(\bm{x}_\rho^*)
    \in
    \begin{cases}
        \{-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\}, & (\bm{x}_\rho^*)_i>0,\\
        [-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},0], & (\bm{x}_\rho^*)_i=0.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:shared-rppr-kkt}
\end{equation}

The work unit is one adjacency-list entry inspection. Scanning a vertex \(i\)
means reading every neighbor \(j\in\mathcal N(i)\) from \(i\)'s adjacency
list and costs \(d_i\). Scanning a set \(\mathcal{S}\) means doing this once
for every \(i\in\mathcal{S}\), at total cost
\(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}d_i=\vol(\mathcal{S})\). Repeated appearances of a
neighbor in different adjacency lists are charged separately; the cost is not
\(|\mathcal N(\mathcal{S})|\), and adjacency lists of neighbors outside
\(\mathcal{S}\) are not recursively scanned. Iteration count and wall-clock
time do not replace this degree-weighted work measure.

\paragraph{Computational model.}
Unless a theorem explicitly states otherwise, the complexity questions use an
exact-real algebraic word model: each scalar arithmetic operation, comparison,
stored-state read or write, degree reply, and emitted output word has unit
cost.  Every first or repeated adjacency-list entry inspection is charged as
above.  Global preprocessing and storage are reported separately and cannot
be hidden as free access to the graph.  A randomized result must state its
success probability and whether its work guarantee is expected or
high-probability.  Finite-precision stability, coefficient-bit complexity,
and rounding error are outside this baseline and require separate statements.

\section{Exact computational problems}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-problems}

\begin{problem}[Personalized PageRank (PPR)]
\label{prob:problem-definitions-ppr}
The input is the graph $\mathcal{G}$, i.e., a finite, simple, undirected, connected graph with \(n:=|\mathcal{V}|\geq2\) and unit edge
weights, one seed vertex $v$ with
$\bm{s}=\bm{e}_v$, and $\alpha\in(0,1]$. The exact target is
$\bm{x}_0^*=\bm{Q}^{-1}\bm{b}$, equivalently
$\bm{\pi}=\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}_0^*$. A sparse approximate output is a list
representing $\widehat{\bm{x}}\in\R^n$; unlisted coordinates are zero and
$\widehat{\bm{\pi}}:=\bm{D}^{1/2}\widehat{\bm{x}}$. For a
document-scoped tolerance $\epsppr>0$, the semantic accuracy requirement is
\begin{equation}
    \|\bm{D}^{-1}
      (\widehat{\bm{\pi}}-\bm{\pi})\|_\infty
    =
    \|\bm{D}^{-1/2}
      (\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}_0^*)\|_\infty
    \leq \epsppr.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr}
\end{equation}
This is an output property, not an activation rule or an adopted
implementation-wide stopping test.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Regularized personalized PageRank (RPPR)]
\label{prob:problem-definitions-rppr}
For the same connected graph $\mathcal{G}$, seed vertex $v$,
$\bm{s}=\bm{e}_v$, and $\alpha\in(0,1]$, together with a regularization
parameter $\rho>0$, the exact target is the unique minimizer
$\bm{x}_\rho^*$ of
$F_\rho$ in \cref{eq:shared-rppr-objective}. Its mass-coordinate
representation is $\bm{\pi}_\rho:=\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}_\rho^*$, with
$\mathcal{S}_\rho^*=\supp(\bm{x}_\rho^*)$.
When an approximate RPPR output is requested, it is likewise a sparse list
representing $\widehat{\bm{x}}\in\R^n$, with every unlisted coordinate
interpreted as zero; the applicable accuracy requirement must be stated
separately.
The scale $\rho$ is part of the objective and is not silently identified with
$\epsppr$, an objective-gap tolerance, or an algorithmic KKT diagnostic.
\end{problem}

The input graph is accessed through adjacency lists and the seed through its
vertex label.  Scanning vertex $i$ costs $d_i$ and scanning
$\mathcal{S}\subseteq \mathcal{V}$ costs $\vol(\mathcal{S})$. This defines
the common local-work unit; it does not prescribe an algorithm or assert a
work bound.

\section{Equivalent PPR formulations}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-equivalences}

Put $\bm{P}:=\bm{A}\bm{D}^{-1}$,
$\bm{P}_{\rm L}:=(\bm{I}+\bm{P})/2$, and
$\alpha_{\rm nl}:=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$.
Both $\bm{P}$ and $\bm{P}_{\rm L}$ act on column vectors, and both are
column stochastic. Direct multiplication of
$\bm{Q}\bm{x}_0^*=\bm{b}$ by $\bm{D}^{1/2}$ gives the exact dictionary below.
The lazy forms agree with the PPR formulations used by
\citet{zhou2024iterative} and \citet{huang2025accelerated}; the non-lazy mass
and degree forms are the undirected specializations used by
\citet{chen2023accelerating} and \citet{wei2026simple}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.97\textwidth}{@{}
    >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.3\textwidth}
    >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
Form & Exact equation and conversion \\\midrule
Lazy fixed point
& $\bm{\pi}=\alpha \bm{s}
   +(1-\alpha)\bm{P}_{\rm L}\bm{\pi}$. \\\midrule
Lazy symmetric
& $\bm{Q}\bm{x}_0^*=\bm{b}$ and
  $\bm{\pi}=\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}_0^*$. \\\midrule
Rescaled lazy symmetric
& $\bm{Q}_{\rm rs}\bm{x}_0^* = 2\bm{b}/(1+\alpha)$, where
  $\bm{Q}_{\rm rs}=2\bm{Q}/(1+\alpha)$. \\\midrule
Non-lazy mass
& $[\bm{I}-(1-\alpha_{\rm nl})\bm{P}]\bm{\pi}
   =\alpha_{\rm nl}\bm{s}$, where
  $\alpha_{\rm nl}=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$. \\\midrule
Non-lazy degree
& $[\bm{D}-(1-\alpha_{\rm nl})\bm{A}]\bm{y}
   =\alpha_{\rm nl}\bm{s}$, where
  $\bm{y}=\bm{D}^{-1}\bm{\pi}
   =\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{x}_0^*$. \\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The rescaled system changes neither $\alpha$ nor its solution.  Removing the
lazy self-loop changes the numerical teleportation parameter to
$\alpha_{\rm nl}$.  Therefore, a dependence on ``$\alpha$'' can be compared
across sources only after applying this conversion.

\section{General seed distributions are an extension}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-general-seed-extension}

The general unit-mass distribution in the shared algebra is useful for source
comparison, but it is not the canonical computational input.  To state its
exact relationship to the point-source problem, write
$\bm{x}_0^*(\bm{s})$ and $\bm{\pi}(\bm{s})$ when the source argument must be
visible.

\begin{proposition}[PPR source superposition; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition}
If $\bm{s}=\sum_v s_v\bm{e}_v$, then
\begin{equation}
 \bm{x}_0^*(\bm{s})=\sum_v s_v\bm{x}_0^*(\bm{e}_v),
 \qquad
 \bm{\pi}(\bm{s})=\sum_v s_v\bm{\pi}(\bm{e}_v).
 \label{eq:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition}
\end{equation}
If point-source outputs $\widehat{\bm{\pi}}^{(v)}$ obey
$\|\bm{D}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{\pi}}^{(v)}-
\bm{\pi}(\bm{e}_v))\|_\infty\leq\epsilon_v$, then their weighted sum obeys
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\bm{D}^{-1}\left(
  \sum_v s_v\widehat{\bm{\pi}}^{(v)}-\bm{\pi}(\bm{s})
 \right)\right\|_\infty
 \leq\sum_v s_v\epsilon_v.
 \label{eq:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The first identity follows from the linear map
$\bm{s}\mapsto \bm{x}_0^*(\bm{s})=
\alpha \bm{Q}^{-1}\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{s}$, and the second follows after
multiplication by $\bm{D}^{1/2}$.  The error statement is the triangle
inequality.
\end{proof}

This proposition is not a local-work reduction.  Separate point-source solves
can multiply discovery, numerical, merging, and output work by the source
mixture size.  The RPPR solution map is nonlinear because the weighted
$\ell_1$ threshold changes its optimal support and admission chronology.
Accordingly, no RPPR superposition identity is asserted; a theorem for a
general sparse distribution must provide its own merge-aware proof and work
ledger.  The project-owned three-vertex path example in
\texttt{aesp\_cd\_l1\_rppr} makes this boundary exact: two independent
source-component reporters stop, while their combined source activates
the middle vertex.

\section{Standard properties of PPR}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-ppr-properties}

The following facts are standard for the normalized Laplacian and the
discounted PageRank system; see the source formulations and spectral bounds
in \citet{fountoulakis2019variational,fountoulakis2026complexity,
zhou2024iterative,huang2025accelerated}.

\begin{proposition}[Spectrum, convexity, and uniqueness; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-spectrum}
The normalized Laplacian and PageRank matrix satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \bm{0}\preceq\bm{\mathcal L}\preceq2\bm{I},
    \qquad
    \alpha \bm{I}\preceq \bm{Q}\preceq \bm{I}.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\bm{Q}$ is symmetric positive definite,
$\kappa_2(\bm{Q})\leq1/\alpha$, $f$ is $\alpha$-strongly convex with
$1$-Lipschitz gradient, and $\bm{x}_0^*$ is unique. In addition,
the zero eigenvalue of $\bm{\mathcal L}$ is simple, with
$\ker(\bm{\mathcal L})=\operatorname{span}\{\bm{D}^{1/2}\one\}$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    f(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}_0^*)
    =\frac12\norm{\bm{x}-\bm{x}_0^*}_{\bm{Q}}^2
    \qquad(\bm{x}\in\R^n).
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-quadratic-gap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse positivity and probability mass; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-positivity}
The matrix $\bm{Q}$ is a nonsingular symmetric $M$-matrix (a Stieltjes
matrix), so $\bm{Q}^{-1}\geq\bm{0}$ coordinatewise. Hence
$\bm{x}_0^*\geq\bm{0}$ and $\bm{\pi}\geq\bm{0}$. In addition,
\begin{equation}
    \inner{\one}{\bm{\pi}}=1.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-unit-mass}
\end{equation}
If $0<\alpha<1$, then $\bm{\pi}$ is strictly positive at every vertex. At
$\alpha=1$, one simply has $\bm{\pi}=\bm{s}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The off-diagonal entries of $\bm{Q}$ are nonpositive, and
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-spectrum} gives positive definiteness; this is
the Stieltjes characterization.  The lazy fixed point and column
stochasticity of $\bm{P}_{\rm L}$ give
$\one^T\bm{\pi}=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\one^T\bm{\pi}$. Strict positivity everywhere follows
from connectedness and the Neumann series for the discounted walk.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient degree-normalized certificate; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}
For every $\widehat{\bm{x}}\in\R^n$,
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\bm{D}^{-1}
      (\widehat{\bm{\pi}}-\bm{\pi})}_\infty
    \leq
    \frac{1}{\alpha}
    \norm{\bm{D}^{-1/2}
      (\bm{Q}\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{b})}_\infty.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}
\end{equation}
Thus
$\norm{\bm{D}^{-1/2}
  (\bm{Q}\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{b})}_\infty\leq\alpha\epsppr$
is sufficient for \cref{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr}.  This is the
scaling used in the source-aligned local certificate of
\citet{zhou2024iterative}; it does not select the sign, strictness, or
evaluation schedule of a repository-wide residual.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
Let $\bm{B}:=\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{Q}\bm{D}^{1/2}$. It is a nonsingular
$M$-matrix, $\bm{B}^{-1}\geq\bm{0}$, and
$\bm{B}\one=\alpha\one$. Therefore
$\norm{\bm{B}^{-1}}_\infty=1/\alpha$. Applying $\bm{B}^{-1}$ to the scaled
residual gives \cref{eq:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}.
\end{proof}

\section{Standard properties of RPPR}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-rppr-properties}

The source variational formulation, KKT system, nonnegative minimizer, and
support-volume result are given in \citet[Section~4 and
Theorems~1--2]{fountoulakis2019variational}; the shared normalization follows
\citet[Section~3]{fountoulakis2026complexity}.

\begin{proposition}[Existence, optimality, and locality; source]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-source}
For every $\rho>0$, $F_\rho$ is $\alpha$-strongly convex and has one minimizer
$\bm{x}_\rho^*$. This minimizer is nonnegative, obeys the coordinatewise
KKT conditions in \cref{eq:shared-rppr-kkt}, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \vol(\mathcal{S}_\rho^*)\leq\frac{1}{\rho}.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-support-volume}
\end{equation}
In particular,
$F_\rho(\bm{x})-F_\rho(\bm{x}_\rho^*)\geq
\frac{\alpha}{2}\norm{\bm{x}-\bm{x}_\rho^*}_2^2$ for all $\bm{x}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Trivial point-source regularization regime; standard
consequence]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-zero-regime}
For the canonical source $\bm{s}=\bm{e}_v$,
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}_\rho^*=\bm{0}
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \rho\geq\frac{1}{d_v}.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-zero-regime}
\end{equation}
Thus the nonzero point-source RPPR regime is $0<\rho<1/d_v$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
At $\bm{x}=\bm{0}$, $\nabla f(\bm{0})=-\bm{b}$ and the weighted
$\ell_1$ subdifferential is
$\alpha\rho\bm{D}^{1/2}[-1,1]^n$.  All nonseed coordinates satisfy the
zero optimality condition automatically.  At the seed, that condition is
$\alpha/\sqrt{d_v}\leq\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}$, equivalently
$\rho\geq1/d_v$.  Strong convexity makes the minimizer unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regularization bias; standard consequence]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}
Let $\bm{\pi}_\rho=\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{x}_\rho^*$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \bm{0}
    \leq \bm{D}^{-1}(\bm{\pi}-\bm{\pi}_\rho)
    \leq \rho\one,
    \qquad
    \norm{\bm{D}^{-1}(\bm{\pi}-\bm{\pi}_\rho)}_\infty\leq\rho.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}
\end{equation}
Moreover, let $\epsobj\geq0$ denote an additive RPPR objective-gap target.
Every $\widehat{\bm{x}}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    F_\rho(\widehat{\bm{x}})-F_\rho(\bm{x}_\rho^*)\leq\epsobj
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-objective-gap}
\end{equation}
obeys
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\bm{D}^{-1/2}
      (\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}_\rho^*)}_\infty
    \leq\sqrt{\frac{2\epsobj}{\alpha}}
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-objective-to-solution}
\end{equation}
and hence the semantic PPR error bound
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\bm{D}^{-1}
      (\bm{D}^{1/2}\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{\pi})}_\infty
    \leq\rho+\sqrt{\frac{2\epsobj}{\alpha}}.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-total-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The KKT conditions provide a vector $\bm{z}\in[0,1]^n$ such that
$\bm{Q}\bm{x}_\rho^*-\bm{b}
=-\alpha\rho \bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{z}$. Since
$\bm{Q}\bm{x}_0^*=\bm{b}$,
\[
    \bm{x}_0^*-\bm{x}_\rho^*
    =\alpha\rho \bm{Q}^{-1}\bm{D}^{1/2}\bm{z}.
\]
Inverse positivity and
$\bm{Q}\bm{D}^{1/2}\one=\alpha\bm{D}^{1/2}\one$ give
$\bm{0}\leq \bm{x}_0^*-\bm{x}_\rho^*
\leq\rho \bm{D}^{1/2}\one$, which is
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}.  Strong convexity and
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-objective-gap} give
$\norm{\widehat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}_\rho^*}_2
\leq\sqrt{2\epsobj/\alpha}$.  Since $d_i\geq1$ for every coordinate,
this implies \cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-objective-to-solution}.
Combining that estimate with the bias bound proves
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-total-error}.
\end{proof}

Thus, $\rho$ has two separate, standard consequences: it bounds the exact
degree-normalized bias and it bounds the optimal support volume.  For example,
choosing
\begin{equation}
    \rho=\frac{\epsppr}{2},
    \qquad
    \epsobj\leq\frac{\alpha\epsppr^2}{8}
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-to-ppr-parameters}
\end{equation}
is sufficient for \cref{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr} and gives
$\vol(\mathcal{S}_\rho^*)\leq2/\epsppr$.  This is only a problem-level
conversion; it does not supply an algorithm for attaining the objective gap.

\section{Root open complexity questions}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-open-questions}

The definitions and standard properties above do not establish any of the
following bounds.  These are top-level research questions, not theorem
claims.  Here a \emph{local} algorithm learns the graph from the seed through
the charged degree and adjacency-list access described above; it is not given
the final support or any other graph-wide summary.  A bound is
\emph{graph-uniform} when its constants and omitted factors are independent
of the particular graph and of $n$ and $|\mathcal E|$.

In this section, $\widetilde O$ may hide factors polylogarithmic in the
explicitly displayed inverse parameters, the number of exposed or emitted
words, and the inverse failure probability.  It does not hide polynomial
factors in those quantities or unrestricted dependence on $n$ or
$|\mathcal E|$.  Any affirmative answer must charge adjacency-list work,
repeated numerical or response operations, state maintenance, certificate
evaluation, materialization, and output.  A negative answer requires a lower
bound in an explicit access or oracle model; failure of one proposed
algorithm is not such a lower bound.  Detailed route-specific missing lemmas
and counterexamples remain in their owning notes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{OP\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Graph-uniform semantic PPR.}
For \cref{prob:problem-definitions-ppr} in the nontrivial accuracy regime
$0<\epsppr<1$, does there exist a graph-uniform local algorithm that returns a
sparse output satisfying
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr} with fully charged work
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde O\!\left(
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}\,\epsppr}
    \right)?
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-open-ppr-work}
\end{equation}
The bound must not use free global preprocessing, a supplied final support, or
an uncharged burn-in or terminal scan.  If $\epsppr\geq1$, the zero vector
already satisfies the semantic requirement because $\bm{\pi}\geq\bm{0}$,
$\inner{\one}{\bm{\pi}}=1$, and $d_i\geq1$.

\item \textbf{Accelerated local RPPR.}
For \cref{prob:problem-definitions-rppr}, it suffices to consider the nonzero
point-source regime $0<\rho<1/d_v$ from
\cref{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-zero-regime}.  Given an additive
objective-gap target $\epsobj>0$, does there exist a graph-uniform local
algorithm returning $\widehat{\bm{x}}$ with
\begin{equation}
    F_\rho(\widehat{\bm{x}})-F_\rho(\bm{x}_\rho^*)
    \leq\epsobj
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-open-rppr-accuracy}
\end{equation}
in fully charged work
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde O\!\left(
      \frac{1}{\rho\sqrt{\alpha}}
    \right),
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-open-rppr-work}
\end{equation}
with at most polylogarithmic dependence on $1/\epsobj$?  Equivalently, can
acceleration improve the $1/\alpha$ dependence while retaining
graph-size-independent locality?  This is the source question posed by
\citet[Section~3]{fountoulakis2022open}, stated here in the project work
model.  The conversion in
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-to-ppr-parameters} shows one precise way
that an affirmative RPPR result would yield a semantic PPR guarantee.

\item \textbf{Reuse of nested SDD systems.}
In this item only, $\epsappr$ denotes the source-native ACL/APPR residual
accuracy, not the semantic tolerance $\epsppr$.  The growing-active-set method
of \citet[Theorem~1.2]{wei2026simple} has high-probability work
$\widetilde O(1/\epsappr^2)$ with only polylogarithmic dependence on
$1/\alpha$.  Can its successive nested SDD systems be reused to obtain
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde O\!\left(\frac{1}{\epsappr}\right)
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-open-nested-sdd-work}
\end{equation}
on arbitrary graphs while charging every system update, boundary-violation
query, materialized coordinate, and output word?  Wei and Yang identify this
direction explicitly in their concluding remarks
\citep[PDF p.~15]{wei2026simple}.  It is proved for paths in the project note
\texttt{incremental\_active\_set\_sdd}, but remains open for arbitrary
nonequitable cyclic graphs.  Without an accuracy-conversion theorem, this
source-level question is not the same as OP1.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Source audit and scope boundary}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-source-boundary}

The exact source audit trail is maintained in the literature notes:
\citet[PDF pp.~8--18]{fountoulakis2019variational} for the variational
objective, KKT system, nonnegativity, support bound, and relative stopping
rule; \citet[PDF pp.~1--3]{fountoulakis2022open} for the COLT open problem on
accelerated $\ell_1$-regularized PageRank;
\citet[PDF p.~3, Section~3]{fountoulakis2026complexity} for the shared RPPR
normalization; \citet[PDF pp.~1--3 and supplemental PDF
p.~17]{zhou2024iterative} for the lazy PPR systems and residual certificate;
\citet[PDF pp.~1--3]{huang2025accelerated} for the lazy PPR and symmetric
quadratic; \citet[PDF p.~3]{chen2023accelerating} for the non-lazy mass
system; and \citet[PDF pp.~2--4 and 8--10]{wei2026simple} for the non-lazy
degree-coordinate system and active-set RPPR properties. These pointers are
recorded in
\texttt{docs/literature/local-solvers.md} and
\texttt{docs/literature/acceleration.md}.

\paragraph{Boundary of the note.}
Nothing here chooses a solver, claims graph-uniform locality, equates distinct
accuracy quantities, or resolves the implementation-wide residual convention.
Those choices require their own definitions, decisions, and proofs.
