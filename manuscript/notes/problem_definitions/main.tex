\begin{abstract}
This note fixes a compact reference for the two optimization problems used in
the project: personalized PageRank (PPR) and regularized personalized PageRank
(RPPR).  It records the exact graph, canonical single-seed input, parameter,
output, and local-access models; translates the lazy symmetric system into
lazy fixed-point, rescaled,
non-lazy mass, and non-lazy degree coordinates; and collects standard
spectral, positivity, normalization, optimality, sparsity, bias, and residual
properties.  Every substantive property is either a cited source result or an
elementary consequence of the displayed definitions.  No algorithm,
complexity claim, experiment, or new research statement is included, and the
repository-wide residual decision remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact computational problems}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-problems}

\begin{problem}[Personalized PageRank]
\label{prob:problem-definitions-ppr}
The input is the graph $G$, one seed vertex $v$ with
$s=e_v$, and $\alpha\in(0,1]$. The exact target is
$x^0=Q^{-1}b$, equivalently
$\pi=D^{1/2}x^0$. A sparse approximate output is a list
representing $\widehat{x}\in\R^n$; unlisted coordinates are zero and
$\widehat{\pi}:=D^{1/2}\widehat{x}$. For a
document-scoped tolerance $\epsppr>0$, the semantic accuracy requirement is
\begin{equation}
    \norm{D^{-1}
      (\widehat{\pi}-\pi)}_\infty
    =
    \norm{D^{-1/2}
      (\widehat{x}-x^0)}_\infty
    \leq \epsppr.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr}
\end{equation}
This is an output property, not an activation rule or an adopted
implementation-wide stopping test.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Regularized personalized PageRank]
\label{prob:problem-definitions-rppr}
For the same $G,v,s=e_v,\alpha$ and a regularization
parameter $\rho>0$, the exact target is the unique minimizer
$x^\star(\rho)$ of
$F_\rho$ in \cref{eq:shared-rppr-objective}. Its mass-coordinate
representation is $\pi_\rho:=D^{1/2}x^\star(\rho)$, with
$S^\star(\rho)=\supp(x^\star(\rho))$.
The scale $\rho$ is part of the objective and is not silently identified with
$\epsppr$, an objective-gap tolerance, or an algorithmic KKT diagnostic.
\end{problem}

The input graph is accessed through adjacency lists and the seed through its
vertex label.  Scanning vertex $i$ costs $d_i$ and scanning
$\mathcal{S}\subseteq V$ costs $\vol(\mathcal{S})$. This defines
the common local-work unit; it does not prescribe an algorithm or assert a
work bound.

\begin{proposition}[Point-source seed-component reduction; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-seed-component-reduction}
Let $\widetilde G$ be a finite, simple, undirected, unit-weight graph whose
degrees are positive, but which may be disconnected.  For $s=e_v$, let $C(v)$
be the connected component containing $v$.  The PPR solution $x^0$ and every
RPPR minimizer $x^\star(\rho)$ vanish outside $C(v)$.  Their restrictions to
$C(v)$ are exactly the solutions obtained by applying the canonical
definitions to the connected graph $\widetilde G[C(v)]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
Order the vertices by connected components. Then $A,D,\mathcal L$, and $Q$
are block diagonal, while $b=\alpha D^{-1/2}e_v$ is supported only on the
$C(v)$ block.  Since every block of $Q$ is positive definite, $Qx^0=b$
forces every unseeded block of $x^0$ to be zero.  The RPPR objective is also
block separable.  On an unseeded block $B$, its contribution is
\[
 \frac12\inner{x_B}{Q_Bx_B}
 +\alpha\rho\norm{D_B^{1/2}x_B}_1,
\]
which is uniquely minimized at zero.  No edge leaves a connected component,
so degrees and adjacency-list charges inside $C(v)$ are unchanged.
\end{proof}

Thus the connected canonical graph loses no point-source solution behavior
and requires no free global connectivity test: a local algorithm simply
never encounters another component.  This reduction does not make an active
or restricted induced face connected.

\subsection{General seed distributions are an extension}
\label{subsec:problem-definitions-general-seed-extension}

The general unit-mass distribution in the shared algebra is useful for source
comparison, but it is not the canonical computational input.  To state its
exact relationship to the point-source problem, write
$x^0(s)$ and $\pi(s)$ when the source argument must be
visible.

\begin{proposition}[PPR source superposition; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition}
If $s=\sum_v s_ve_v$, then
\begin{equation}
 x^0(s)=\sum_v s_vx^0(e_v),
 \qquad
 \pi(s)=\sum_v s_v\pi(e_v).
 \label{eq:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition}
\end{equation}
If point-source outputs $\widehat{\pi}^{(v)}$ obey
$\|D^{-1}(\widehat{\pi}^{(v)}-
\pi(e_v))\|_\infty\leq\epsilon_v$, then their weighted sum obeys
\begin{equation}
 \left\|D^{-1}\left(
  \sum_v s_v\widehat{\pi}^{(v)}-\pi(s)
 \right)\right\|_\infty
 \leq\sum_v s_v\epsilon_v.
 \label{eq:problem-definitions-ppr-superposition-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The first identity follows from the linear map
$s\mapsto x^0(s)=
\alpha Q^{-1}D^{-1/2}s$, and the second follows after
multiplication by $D^{1/2}$.  The error statement is the triangle
inequality.
\end{proof}

This proposition is not a local-work reduction.  Separate point-source solves
can multiply discovery, numerical, merging, and output work by the source
mixture size.  The RPPR solution map is nonlinear because the weighted
$\ell_1$ threshold changes its optimal support and admission chronology.
Accordingly, no RPPR superposition identity is asserted; a theorem for a
general sparse distribution must provide its own merge-aware proof and work
ledger.  The project-owned three-vertex path example in
\texttt{aesp\_cd\_l1\_rppr} makes this boundary exact: two independent
source-component reporters stop, while their combined source activates
the middle vertex.

\section{Equivalent PPR formulations}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-equivalences}

Put $P:=AD^{-1}$,
$P_{\rm L}:=(I+P)/2$, and
$\alpha_{\rm nl}:=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$.
Both $P$ and $P_{\rm L}$ act on column vectors, and both are
column stochastic. Direct multiplication of
$Qx^0=b$ by $D^{1/2}$ gives the exact dictionary below.
The lazy forms agree with the PPR formulations used by
\citet{zhou2024iterative} and \citet{huang2025accelerated}; the non-lazy mass
and degree forms are the undirected specializations used by
\citet{chen2023accelerating} and \citet{wei2026simple}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.97\textwidth}{@{}
    >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.22\textwidth}
    >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
Form & Exact equation and conversion \\
\midrule
Lazy fixed point
& $\pi=\alpha s
   +(1-\alpha)P_{\rm L}\pi$. \\
Lazy symmetric
& $Qx^0=b$ and
  $\pi=D^{1/2}x^0$. \\
Rescaled lazy symmetric
& $Q_{\rm rs}x^0=b_{\rm rs}$, where
  $Q_{\rm rs}=2Q/(1+\alpha)$ and
  $b_{\rm rs}=2b/(1+\alpha)$. \\
Non-lazy mass
& $[I-(1-\alpha_{\rm nl})P]\pi
   =\alpha_{\rm nl}s$, where
  $\alpha_{\rm nl}=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$ and
  $\alpha=\alpha_{\rm nl}/(2-\alpha_{\rm nl})$. \\
Non-lazy degree
& $[D-(1-\alpha_{\rm nl})A]y
   =\alpha_{\rm nl}s$, where
  $y=D^{-1}\pi
   =D^{-1/2}x^0$. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The rescaled system changes neither $\alpha$ nor its solution.  Removing the
lazy self-loop changes the numerical teleportation parameter to
$\alpha_{\rm nl}$.  Therefore a dependence on ``$\alpha$'' can be compared
across sources only after applying this conversion.

\section{Standard properties of PPR}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-ppr-properties}

The following facts are standard for the normalized Laplacian and the
discounted PageRank system; see the source formulations and spectral bounds
in \citet{fountoulakis2019variational,fountoulakis2026complexity,
zhou2024iterative,huang2025accelerated}.

\begin{proposition}[Spectrum, convexity, and uniqueness; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-spectrum}
The normalized Laplacian and PageRank matrix satisfy
\begin{equation}
    0\preceq\mathcal L\preceq2I,
    \qquad
    \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $Q$ is symmetric positive definite,
$\kappa_2(Q)\leq1/\alpha$, $f$ is $\alpha$-strongly convex with
$1$-Lipschitz gradient, and $x^0$ is unique. In addition,
the zero eigenvalue of $\mathcal L$ is simple, with
$\ker(\mathcal L)=\operatorname{span}\{D^{1/2}\one\}$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    f(x)-f(x^0)
    =\frac12\norm{x-x^0}_{Q}^2
    \qquad(x\in\R^n).
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-quadratic-gap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse positivity and probability mass; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-positivity}
The matrix $Q$ is a nonsingular symmetric $M$-matrix (a Stieltjes
matrix), so $Q^{-1}\geq0$ coordinatewise. Hence
$x^0\geq0$ and $\pi\geq0$. In addition,
\begin{equation}
    \inner{\one}{\pi}=1.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-unit-mass}
\end{equation}
If $0<\alpha<1$, then $\pi$ is strictly positive at every vertex. At
$\alpha=1$, one simply has $\pi=s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The off-diagonal entries of $Q$ are nonpositive, and
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-spectrum} gives positive definiteness; this is
the Stieltjes characterization.  The lazy fixed point and column
stochasticity of $P_{\rm L}$ give
$\one^T\pi=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\one^T\pi$. Strict positivity everywhere follows
from connectedness and the Neumann series for the discounted walk.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient degree-normalized certificate; standard]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}
For every $\widehat{x}\in\R^n$,
\begin{equation}
    \norm{D^{-1}
      (\widehat{\pi}-\pi)}_\infty
    \leq
    \frac{1}{\alpha}
    \norm{D^{-1/2}
      (Q\widehat{x}-b)}_\infty.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}
\end{equation}
Thus
$\norm{D^{-1/2}
  (Q\widehat{x}-b)}_\infty\leq\alpha\epsppr$
is sufficient for \cref{eq:problem-definitions-semantic-ppr}.  This is the
scaling used in the source-aligned local certificate of
\citet{zhou2024iterative}; it does not select the sign, strictness, or
evaluation schedule of a repository-wide residual.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
Let $B:=D^{-1/2}QD^{1/2}$. It is a nonsingular
$M$-matrix, $B^{-1}\geq0$, and
$B\one=\alpha\one$. Therefore
$\norm{B^{-1}}_\infty=1/\alpha$. Applying $B^{-1}$ to the scaled
residual gives \cref{eq:problem-definitions-residual-certificate}.
\end{proof}

\section{Standard properties of RPPR}
\label{sec:problem-definitions-rppr-properties}

The source variational formulation, KKT system, nonnegative minimizer, and
support-volume result are given in \citet[Section~4 and
Theorems~1--2]{fountoulakis2019variational}; the shared normalization follows
\citet[Section~3]{fountoulakis2026complexity}.

\begin{proposition}[Existence, optimality, and locality; source]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-source}
For every $\rho>0$, $F_\rho$ is $\alpha$-strongly convex and has one minimizer
$x^\star(\rho)$. This minimizer is nonnegative, obeys the coordinatewise
KKT conditions in \cref{eq:shared-rppr-kkt}, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq\frac{1}{\rho}.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-support-volume}
\end{equation}
In particular,
$F_\rho(x)-F_\rho(x^\star)\geq
\frac{\alpha}{2}\norm{x-x^\star}_2^2$ for all $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Regularization bias; standard consequence]
\label{prop:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}
Let $\pi_\rho=D^{1/2}x^\star(\rho)$. Then
\begin{equation}
    0
    \leq D^{-1}(\pi-\pi_\rho)
    \leq \rho\one,
    \qquad
    \norm{D^{-1}(\pi-\pi_\rho)}_\infty\leq\rho.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}
\end{equation}
Consequently, any approximation $\widehat{x}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \norm{D^{-1/2}
      (\widehat{x}-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty\leq\tau
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-optimization-error}
\end{equation}
obeys the PPR error bound
\begin{equation}
    \norm{D^{-1}
      (D^{1/2}\widehat{x}-\pi)}_\infty
    \leq\rho+\tau.
    \label{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-total-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Standard verification]
The KKT conditions provide a vector $z\in[0,1]^n$ such that
$Qx^\star-b
=-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}z$. Since
$Qx^0=b$,
\[
    x^0-x^\star
    =\alpha\rho Q^{-1}D^{1/2}z.
\]
Inverse positivity and
$QD^{1/2}\one=\alpha D^{1/2}\one$ give
$0\leq x^0-x^\star
\leq\rho D^{1/2}\one$, which is
\cref{eq:problem-definitions-rppr-bias}.  The last bound is the triangle
inequality.
\end{proof}

Thus $\rho$ has two separate, standard consequences: it bounds the exact
degree-normalized bias and it bounds the optimal support volume.  For example,
choosing $\rho=\epsppr/2$ leaves an error budget $\tau=\epsppr/2$ for an
inexact RPPR solve and gives
$\vol(S^\star)\leq2/\epsppr$. This is only a problem-level
implication; it does not supply an algorithm for attaining $\tau$.
