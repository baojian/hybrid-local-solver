# OP2: support-safe acceleration and the remaining rate lemma

Date: 2026-08-29

## Status

This note records a focused attempt on OP2.  It uses `main.tex` in this
directory as its only project-internal mathematical source.  The support and
work lemmas below are proved.  The accelerated convergence rate is a sharply
isolated conjectural lemma; it is **not** claimed as a theorem.

The main new candidate is a maximal subsolution extrapolation.  Unlike
standard FISTA, every vector whose adjacency lists are scanned is supported
inside the exact RPPR support.  Consequently, a graph-uniform
`O_tilde(1/sqrt(alpha))` iteration bound for this candidate would prove OP2
immediately.  The ordinary `O_tilde(1/alpha)` bound follows already from the
same invariants.

## 1. Obstacle and fixed-point form

Put

\[
  w:=D^{1/2}\mathbf 1,
  \qquad
  h:=b-\alpha\rho w.
\]

The RPPR minimizer is nonnegative.  On the nonnegative orthant the objective
therefore differs by no constant from

\[
  \Phi(x):=\frac12x^TQx-h^Tx,
  \qquad x\geq0.
\]

The PageRank matrix satisfies

\[
  \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I,
  \qquad Q_{ij}\leq0\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Thus

\[
  R:=I-Q\geq0\quad\hbox{entrywise},
  \qquad \|R\|_2\leq1-\alpha.
\]

Define the unit-step proximal-gradient map

\[
  T(x):=[Rx+h]_+.
\]

The unique RPPR minimizer `x*` is the unique fixed point of `T`.  The map is
coordinatewise monotone and is a Euclidean contraction with factor at most
`1-alpha`.

### Definition 1 (subsolution)

A vector `x` is a subsolution if

\[
  x\geq0,
  \qquad x\leq T(x)
\]

coordinatewise.

Equivalently, for every positive coordinate of `x`,

\[
  (Qx-h)_i\leq0.
\]

There is no condition at a zero coordinate.  This last qualification is
essential because the KKT gradient is normally positive on inactive
coordinates.

### Lemma 2 (order certificate)

Every subsolution satisfies `x <= x*` coordinatewise.  In particular,

\[
  \operatorname{supp}(x)\subseteq S^*:=\operatorname{supp}(x^*).
\]

#### Proof

Starting from a subsolution, monotonicity gives

\[
  x\leq T(x)\leq T^2(x)\leq\cdots.
\]

The contraction property makes `T^j(x)` converge to the unique fixed point
`x*`.  Taking the coordinatewise limit proves the claim.

### Lemma 3 (one-step preservation)

If `x` is a subsolution, then `T(x)` is a subsolution and

\[
  x\leq T(x)\leq x^*.
\]

#### Proof

The first inequality is the definition.  Applying monotonicity to it gives
`T(x) <= T^2(x)`, so `T(x)` is a subsolution.  Lemma 2 supplies the upper
bound.

## 2. Maximal subsolution extrapolation

Initialize

\[
  x_0=0,
  \qquad x_1=T(x_0).
\]

Given monotone subsolutions `x_{k-1} <= x_k`, set

\[
  d_k:=x_k-x_{k-1}\geq0
\]

and choose the largest `theta_k >= 0` for which

\[
  z_k:=x_k+\theta_kd_k
\]

is still a subsolution.  Then set

\[
  x_{k+1}:=T(z_k).
  \tag{MSE}
\]

For a nonzero step, the maximal parameter is finite and has the explicit
formula

\[
  \theta_k
  =\min_{\substack{i\in\operatorname{supp}(x_k)\\
                    (Qd_k)_i>0}}
       \frac{h_i-(Qx_k)_i}{(Qd_k)_i}.
  \tag{1}
\]

Terms with `(Qd_k)_i <= 0` impose no upper bound.

To justify (1), note that
`supp(d_k) subseteq supp(x_k)`.  On this support the subsolution constraints
along the ray are exactly

\[
  (Qx_k-h)_i+\theta(Qd_k)_i\leq0.
\]

If the set in (1) were empty while `d_k` were nonzero, the whole ray would
consist of subsolutions.  Lemma 2 would then bound the unbounded ray by the
finite vector `x*`, a contradiction.

### Theorem 4 (support safety and fully charged per-step work)

All vectors `x_k` and `z_k` generated by (MSE) obey

\[
  0\leq x_{k-1}\leq x_k\leq z_k\leq x_{k+1}\leq x^*.
  \tag{2}
\]

Consequently all their supports are contained in `S*`.  One iteration can be
implemented in

\[
  O(\operatorname{vol}(S^*))
\]

fully charged adjacency-list work and linear storage in the scanned volume.

#### Proof

The initialization follows from Lemma 3.  Suppose the invariant holds
through `x_k`.  By construction, `z_k` is a subsolution and `z_k >= x_k`.
Lemma 3 applied to `z_k` gives

\[
  z_k\leq T(z_k)=x_{k+1}\leq x^*,

\]

and also says that `x_{k+1}` is a subsolution.  This proves (2) by induction.

To compute (1), multiply `d_k` by `Q` while scanning the adjacency lists of
`supp(x_k)`.  The same scan computes `T(z_k)` and accumulates possible new
boundary coordinates.  Those new coordinates do not require their own
adjacency scans in the current iteration.  By (2), every coordinate that
survives in `x_{k+1}` belongs to `S*`; hence it is safe to scan it in the next
iteration.  The RPPR locality theorem gives
`vol(S*) <= 1/rho`.

### Corollary 5 (unconditional baseline)

The iterates converge, and

\[
  \|x_{k+1}-x^*\|_2
  \leq(1-\alpha)\|x_k-x^*\|_2.
  \tag{3}
\]

Thus (MSE) already gives a support-safe

\[
  O\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho\alpha}
            \log\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon_{\rm obj}}\right)
\]

work bound, matching the usual nonaccelerated dependence.

#### Proof

Equation (2) gives
`0 <= x*-z_k <= x*-x_k`, so its Euclidean norm cannot increase.  The
contraction of `T` then gives (3).  Also, since both `x_k` and `x*` are
supported on `S*`, the KKT gradient at `x*` vanishes on their difference and

\[
  F_\rho(x_k)-F_\rho(x^*)
  =\frac12\|x_k-x^*\|_Q^2.
  \tag{4}
\]

The initial objective gap is at most `alpha/2`, and
`alpha I <= Q <= I`; these facts and (3) give the displayed work bound.

## 3. The single rate lemma that would prove OP2

The following is the precise unresolved statement.

### Accelerated MSE lemma (open)

There are universal constants `C,c>0` such that (MSE) satisfies

\[
  \|x_k-x^*\|_Q^2
  \leq
  C\exp(-c k\sqrt\alpha)\|x_0-x^*\|_Q^2
  \tag{5}

\]

for every finite simple undirected graph, seed distribution, `rho>0`, and
`alpha in (0,1]`.

If (5) holds, Theorem 4, (4), and `vol(S*) <= 1/rho` give

\[
  \widetilde O\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)

\]

fully charged work with only logarithmic dependence on the additive
objective tolerance.  This is exactly OP2; the established OP2-to-OP1
reduction then proves OP1 as well.

## 4. Residual recurrence and why the rate is plausible

Once the positive support is fixed and no thresholding occurs, let

\[
  r_k:=h-Qx_k\geq0

\]

on that support.  Since `Qd_k=r_{k-1}-r_k`, the extrapolated residual and the
next residual satisfy

\[
  s_k:=h-Qz_k
      =(1+\theta_k)r_k-\theta_kr_{k-1}\geq0,
  \qquad
  r_{k+1}=Rs_k.
  \tag{6}

Maximality makes at least one active coordinate of `s_k` zero.  In one
dimension this is Aitken's delta-squared extrapolation and solves the affine
fixed point exactly.  In several dimensions, (6) repeatedly removes a
currently limiting component while `R` mixes the remainder.  This explains
the observed Chebyshev-scale behavior, but it is not a proof of (5).

A simple per-step comparison with Nesterov momentum is false.  On paths and
cycles, individual `theta_k` values can be arbitrarily close to zero, even
though a very large extrapolation often follows shortly afterwards and the
aggregate convergence remains fast.  Any proof of (5) therefore has to
amortize contact steps and cannot assume

\[
  \theta_k\geq\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha}.

\]

There is a small exact witness.  Take the four-cycle, seed vertex `0`,
`alpha=1/4`, and `rho=1/10`.  Scale all coordinates by `sqrt(2)` so the
regular graph calculation is rational.  Then

\[
  Q_{ii}=\frac58,
  \qquad Q_{ij}=-\frac3{16}\quad(ij\in E),
  \qquad
  h=\left(\frac15,-\frac1{20},-\frac1{20},-\frac1{20}\right).
\]

The first two MSE iterates and the first momentum are

\[
  x_1=\left(\frac15,0,0,0\right),
  \qquad
  \theta_1=\frac35,
  \qquad
  x_2=\left(\frac8{25},\frac1{100},0,\frac1{100}\right).
\]

At the next extrapolation, coordinate `0` becomes tight at

\[
  \theta_2=\frac1{19}
  <\frac13
  =\frac{1-\sqrt{1/4}}{1+\sqrt{1/4}}.
\]

The exact optimum is

\[
  x^*=\left(\frac{68}{205},\frac4{205},0,\frac4{205}\right),
\]

so this is not an artifact of a coordinate outside the optimal support.  The
script `verify_mse_small_momentum.py` checks all these identities using
rational arithmetic.

The available unconditional energy argument only uses that the extrapolated
point lies between `x_k` and `x*`; it recovers (3), not (5).  The missing
inequality must extract quantitative progress from the maximal-contact
condition in (1) or (6).

## 5. Computational stress tests (evidence only)

The algorithm was tested in double precision against exact active-set solves
on paths, cycles, stars, random trees, sparse random connected graphs, and
chains of dense blocks.  The checks verified at every iteration that

1. `x_{k-1} <= x_k <= x*` within numerical tolerance;
2. the extrapolated point was a subsolution;
3. no iterate activated a coordinate outside `S*`; and
4. the exact quadratic identity (4) agreed with the reported gap.

For relative objective gap `10^{-10}`, the worst values seen in a search over
graphs with up to 120 vertices were approximately

| `alpha` | largest iteration count | `k sqrt(alpha)` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `0.03` | `51` | `8.83` |
| `0.01` | `91` | `9.10` |
| `0.003` | `191` | `10.46` |
| `0.001` | `337` | `10.66` |

The near constancy of the last column is consistent with (5).  It does not
exclude a larger adversarial graph family.

## 6. Other OP2 routes examined

### 6.1 Standard FISTA with over-regularization

Fountoulakis and Martinez-Rubio (2026) isolate the desired core term

\[
  O\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
            \log\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon_{\rm obj}}\right)

\]

for FISTA on an over-regularized objective.  Their arbitrary-graph obstacle
is cumulative transient work outside the less-regularized optimal support.
Under a confinement set `B`, their additional term is

\[
  O\!\left(
    \frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{vol}(B)}}
         {\rho\alpha^{3/2}}
  \right).

\]

The seed-at-leaf star shows that this is a real algorithmic issue: a single
false activation of the center can require graph-size work.  MSE removes
this issue at the invariant level instead of attempting to charge it after
the fact.

### 6.2 Active-set plus SDD solves

The Wei--Yang active-set method already proves OP2 whenever

\[
  |S^*|\leq\frac1{\sqrt\alpha},

\]

because its bound `O_tilde(|S*| vol(S*))` is then at most
`O_tilde(1/(rho sqrt(alpha)))`.  This instance-sensitive regime is slightly
stronger than the parameter-only regime `rho >= sqrt(alpha)`.

For larger supports, solving every restricted SDD system from scratch may
cost one near-linear solve per newly active vertex.  The exact telescoping
energy identity in the companion proof-attempt note does not pay for all
boundary reports.  A dynamic nested-SDD solver plus a boundary-heavy-hitter
structure remains a sufficient alternative route.

### 6.3 Grounded electrical LCP

The grounded-flow dual from the companion note makes OP2 a symmetric
`M`-matrix linear complementarity problem.  A solver that

1. starts from the local feasible flow that sends the seed excess directly
   to ground;
2. exposes only edges incident to constraints that become tight; and
3. uses `O_tilde(sqrt(1/alpha))` passes over the finally exposed edges

would also prove OP2.  Generic obstacle, interior-point, projected-CG, and
convex-flow methods do not provide item 2 in the adjacency-list model.  They
either assume the whole matrix is materialized or may inspect constraints
outside the optimal active region.

## 7. Current priority

The shortest remaining proof obligation is (5).  Three concrete attacks are
still viable:

1. construct a Lyapunov function that amortizes a small `theta_k` against the
   following large extrapolation;
2. interpret (6) as a positivity-constrained residual polynomial and prove a
   Chebyshev-type minimax bound; or
3. find a graph family on which `k sqrt(alpha)` diverges, thereby refuting
   this candidate before investing in implementation details.

Even if (5) is false, Theorem 4 is reusable: it gives a rigorous
support-preserving acceleration framework and pinpoints the rate, rather than
locality, as its only missing component.
