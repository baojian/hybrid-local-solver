\section{What this changes in the older two-stage attempts}
\paragraph{The AESP--CD retraction issue.}
The older \texttt{aesp\_cd\_l1\_rppr} proof keeps accelerated centers
order-safe, but must pay the loss caused by repeated nonlinear lower
retractions. The new recurrence removes those intermediate retractions.
Its kinetic support can leave the optimal support, and weighted signed
flow pays the repeated events directly. This is an alternative algorithm,
not a missing-lemma proof for the old recurrence.

\paragraph{The AESP--LOCSOR burn-in issue.}
The old note correctly charges a coordinate tail after a small energy or
residual-mass handoff, but does not prove the universal early-AESP work
factor. The new deterministic ramp has an explicit cumulative work theorem
and carries its numerical state into a final accelerated accuracy phase.
For a different composition, \cref{thm:revisit-randomized-gs} replaces the
unproved AESP prefix by certified randomized faces and uses an RPPR-relative
mass deficit for a reserve-preserving coordinate finish. The original
quantity $\Lambda_J$ is not bounded by these new proofs.
The separate ideal exact-inner analysis in
\cref{thm:revisit-exact-aesp-mass} also shows that a constant $R_J$
cannot be inferred from outer convergence and inner accuracy alone.
It does not analyze the actual thresholded inner trajectory.

\paragraph{The ranked two-rung SOR issue.}
The measured policy in \texttt{two\_rung\_sor} is analyzed further in
\texttt{two\_rung\_direct\_theory}. The latter already proves a
center-seeded three-vertex-path obstruction:
$W_{\rm spread}\geq\alpha^{-3/2}/1408$ for its stated fixed accuracy and
small-$\alpha$ regime, despite explored volume four. Repeated branch
reflection prevents a universal accelerated theorem for that unchanged
policy. Our batch kinetic updates and changing regularizer are different
from adjusting SOR's relaxation parameter. Empirical performance of the
old policy on its recorded benchmark retains its original scope.

\paragraph{The randomized and deterministic proofs.}
Threshold-batch depth and certified SDD solves already yield the existence
scale and an optional principal-PPR tail. The old point-source two-stage
note also already proves that an approximate inner face can replace an
exact support envelope. We do not claim those principles as new.
The deterministic orthant recurrence imports the two-energy and signed-flow
strategy, then changes the projection and regularization schedule.
The general-source extension adds the load comparison that compensates
for omitted source entries by lowering the first regularizer.

\paragraph{Four distinct handoffs.}
The main deterministic schedule switches from cooling to fixed accuracy,
with explicit actual-state energy at most $9\alpha\rho$ (or
$13\alpha\rho$ when rounded). The general-source OP2 construction switches
from a reduced source to the original objective on an approximate support.
The randomized composition switches from a face-relative gap certificate
to a reserve-preserving GS tail. The signed deterministic composition in
\cref{thm:revisit-deterministic-gs} instead switches at a coarse objective
or thresholded-residual certificate, before any clipping. Only the
general-source and randomized constructions make a principal-face solve.
A GS tail appended to the generic deterministic clipping routine at
its smallest relative-gap budget is redundant; the face-relative randomized
handoff and the new signed deterministic handoff have different certificates.

\section{Verification and unresolved directions}
All audits are under
\path{experiments/proof_audits/two_stage_revisited_20260906/}.
The dense rational trajectory audit checks both metric energies, the
parameter-change bank, moving-comparator selected flow, its weighted
telescope, response bounds, and cumulative degree charges at every tested
step. Its reference optima use exact elimination and are not local solver
primitives. The lazy-state audit instead checks the actual sparse reporter,
primal and kinetic records, output, and original-row ledger against a dense
reference. Separate directed-rounding checks verify the neighbor
underestimates, perturbed energies, grid states, scalar rebases, and output.

The completed sparse exact run has 1,618 graph cases. Directed rounding has
4,872 graph cases and four implicit stars. A 780-case audit independently
checks the point-source objective, KKT, and positive-residual completion
rules. Sparse-source semantic checks cover 324 graph cases and four
implicit stars, including an input with 1,000 source entries on a star
with $10^{12}$ leaves. Cold source rows and the hub row are not opened.
The general-source OP2 extension passed 10,228 exact/rounded cases; a later
652-case run added the maximum-principle and KKT/residual assertions.
These scopes are distinguished in the saved source snapshots.

The coarse randomized-face interface passed 36 path cases, of which 19
needed nonempty coordinate tails. On $P_{64}$ with $\alpha=1/64$ and
$\epsppr=2^{-24}$, the rounded tail performs 1,112 updates before meeting
the final ACL certificate. Exact face elimination in this audit proposes
candidates that must pass the same residual test as a randomized supplied-
face solve. It is not an implementation or timing claim for that solver.

The deterministic signed-delivery audit passed 256 conservative runs and
1,096 runs allowing the observed early residual gate. The former small-graph
tails were empty; 1,025 of the latter were nonempty. Twelve separate
tail-only witnesses start above PPR and exercise negative updates. Their
initial states are externally supplied references, not alleged prefix
outputs. The positive residual-excess audit also passed 256 cases, including
128 prefixes above the RPPR optimum. Every one of those cases exceeded
the older objective handoff budget while meeting the new excess budget.

The separated-front sign audit passed twelve finite trees, including
65,000-step cases, using integer trajectory certificates. Detailed atlas
coverage through seven vertices, run parameters, source hashes, repository
checks, and build status are recorded in the operational verification report.
Exact and numerical records are labelled separately.

\paragraph{What the counterexamples rule out.}
The exact P16 witness in \texttt{unsafe\_intermediate.py} disproves the
positive-coordinate RPPR subsolution condition during the ramp.
\Cref{prop:revisit-four-fails,prop:revisit-radial-failures} additionally
refute monotonicity, PPR lower order, nonnegative PPR residual, and several
constant tracking bounds. The large radial examples were found by a
floating-point search, but their certificates use integer trajectories,
proved error radii, and exact PPR equations. They are not assumptions of
any positive theorem. The separate asymptotic result in
\cref{sec:revisit-ramp-asymptotic} proves the graph limit using levelwise
energy bounds and stable causal recurrences. Numerical agreement with that
limit provides a check, not the proof.

\paragraph{Remaining scope.}
Independent review of the new proofs, practical comparisons under a common
work contract, review of the graph-limit argument, and the unchanged AESP-specific
locality claims remain open. No speedup over an existing implementation is
claimed from a smaller projection routine or from finite exact audit times.
