\begin{abstract}
The recent deterministic RPPR proof supplies a second comparison energy
and a signed-flow work argument. Reexamining those mechanisms yields a
simpler two-phase algorithm for semantic personalized PageRank. Ordinary
orthant projection satisfies the required second-metric comparison, so
neither an upper box nor a mass-cap projection is needed. Furthermore,
the regularizer can decrease at every accelerated step without resetting
either numerical vector or repairing intermediate iterates. A weighted
signed-flow telescope accommodates the changing RPPR comparison optimum.
The resulting degree-work estimate is
$44\sum_k r_k^{-1}$. Geometric cooling to $r=\epsppr/2$, followed by
fixed-regularizer acceleration and one downward clipping operation, gives
a deterministic exact-real semantic PPR algorithm with
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ fully charged work and output
support volume at most $2/\epsppr$. An alternative stopping rule solves
the RPPR objective problem. Directed rounding preserves both guarantees
using bounded-size integers, and a fixed degree filter is optional.
More directly, a coarse deterministic prefix can hand off to signed
Gauss--Seidel updates using a thresholded absolute-residual potential.
This yields incidence work
$O((1+\log(2/\alpha))/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ and removes the
accuracy logarithm from the frozen prefix phase.
A second construction handles an explicit sparse source with additive
input work by finding an approximate envelope under a reduced source,
then restoring the original objective on that envelope. A certified
randomized face prefix also admits a coarser handoff to a reserve-preserving
Gauss--Seidel finish. Exact trajectory and sparse-state audits accompany
the proofs. Certified tree counterexamples disprove several stronger
ramp-only invariants. A separated-front limit and a stable linear cascade
prove that no graph-uniform constant bounds ramp error by its current
regularizer, even at one fixed value of $\alpha$.
This changes the old algorithms;
it does not prove their original AESP-locality or fixed-SOR conjectures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, conventions, and provenance}
\input{../../tex/shared/source_aligned_problem}
The input is one seed label $v$, a finite simple connected unit-weight
graph with positive original degrees, $\alpha\in(0,1]$, and $0<\epsppr<1$.
The required sparse output obeys
\[
 \max_i\frac{|\widehat\pi_i-\pi_i|}{d_i}\leq\epsppr,
 \qquad \widehat{\vpi}=\mD^{1/2}\widehat\vx.
\]
Every degree reply, original adjacency incidence (including repeated
reads), arithmetic operation, data-structure update, threshold query,
materialization, and output word is charged. No graph-wide preprocessing
or supplied optimal support is available. Statements here use exact-real
algebraic word work except for the explicitly separate rounded theorem.

\paragraph{Source.}
The common RPPR facts, in the conventions of
\citet{fountoulakis2026complexity}, are nonnegativity, inverse positivity,
$\alpha\mI\preceq\mQ\preceq\mI$, monotonicity of $\vx_r^*$ as $r$
decreases, support volume at most $1/r$, and
$\vzero\leq\vx_0^*-\vx_r^*\leq r\mD^{1/2}\one$.
The two-energy and selected-flow strategy originates in
\path{deterministic_op2_independent_20260905} and is independently
audited in \path{deterministic_op2_20260905}. The precise import is its
accelerated-comparison lemma, with label \path{lem:det-op2-comparison}.
The equivalent active-manuscript label is \path{lem:det-comparison}.
The randomized threshold-batch theorem already gives the same existence
scale; it is context, not a proof dependency of our deterministic result.

\paragraph{Proved here.}
The orthant sector observation, the changing-regularizer bank, the weighted
moving-comparator flow, the no-restart work bound, and its direct semantic
completion are the new statements developed below. The directed rounding
construction and its fixed-pruning extension are proved separately.
The later sections extend the source model and give a different
randomized-to-coordinate handoff, as well as a deterministic signed-coordinate
handoff at a coarse objective target. The linear cascade amplification and its
graph limit together prove an asymptotic obstruction to constant ramp tracking.
Exact computations are
\textbf{Measured} checks, not asymptotic proofs. \textbf{Open} items include
independent review, practical comparisons,
and transfer to unchanged AESP or SOR. Unproved transfers are explicitly
\textbf{Conditional}. Previously \textbf{Refuted} policies remain refuted in
their precise original scopes.
