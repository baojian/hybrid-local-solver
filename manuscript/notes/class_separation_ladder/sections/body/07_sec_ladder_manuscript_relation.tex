\section{Relation to the active manuscript, and two open items}
\label{sec:ladder-manuscript-relation}

Each of the three manuscript results below is \emph{cited}, not subsumed. In
every case the manuscript statement fixes an instance, a literal method, or a
stopping rule, and the corresponding statement here is about a class under the
semantic stop. The namespaces $\epsappr$ and $\epsppr$ remain distinct
throughout. In the first comparison below, setting their numerical values
equal is a deliberate parameter choice for comparing two guarantees, not a
definition or a repository-wide conversion.

\paragraph{The APPR star theorem (\texttt{thm:appr-star-lower-bound}).}
Lazy \APPR{} is the $\Mpos$ member $\push(u,\tfrac{1+\alpha}{2}r_{u})$ under
the dictionary \eqref{eq:ladder-mass-dictionary}, and its termination rule
implies $\errop\le\epsappr$ by \ref{it:ladder-f4} along the route of
\texttt{eq:appr-accuracy}. Applying
\Cref{thm:ladder-monotone-lower-bound} after choosing
$\epsppr=\epsappr$ numerically reproduces the
$\Theta(1/(\alpha\epsappr))$ conclusion with constant $0.0397$ to $0.0433$
against the manuscript's $3/128$, while extending it to arbitrary step sizes,
signed steps, batching, output maps in $\OB$, and arbitrary stopping rules.
The manuscript theorem should be cited as the special case that fixed the
instance and the mechanism: its Steps~1--2 are the mass-scale ancestors of
\Cref{lem:ladder-kappa-step,lem:ladder-terminal}, and
\texttt{prop:appr-upper-bound} supplies the matching member upper bound that
makes rung one a $\Theta$.

\paragraph{The CF-Push and SOR star bound.}
The manuscript proves, for certificate-stopped CF-Push at $\omega^{\star}$,
a star result of order
\[
    \Theta\bigl(\log(1/\alpha)/(\sqrt\alpha\,\epsppr)\bigr)
\]
in \texttt{subsec:cf-star-lower} and \texttt{eq:cf-opt-sor-identities}. That
is a statement about one member of $\Rpm$ under one stopping rule.
\Cref{thm:ladder-signed-lower-bound} supplies the
missing \emph{class} lower bound $\Omega(1/(\sqrt\alpha\,\epsppr))$, and the
$\log(1/\alpha)$ between them may be a certificate artifact: the same member
under the semantic stop empirically runs in $\Theta(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ blocks
(\statusmeasured), but no theorem here removes the logarithm. Cite, do not
subsume: the manuscript statement is about the
method together with its certificate; the statement here is about the class
together with the semantics.

\paragraph{The CF-Push long spider.}
The $\Omega(1/(\alpha\epsppr))$ obstruction of \texttt{thm:cf-spider-lower} concerns a specific $\Rpm$ member
under the $\galpha\epsppr d$-activation certificate on a \emph{different}
instance, one whose semantic content is empty in the relevant regime:
$\mathcal S_{\epsppr}=\emptyset$ for the admissible dyadic $\alpha$ at
$\theta_{\mathrm{sp}}=1/8$, so the all-zero vector is a valid output at
no adjacency inspections and $O(1)$ full work. It is therefore a certificate
lower bound, fully compatible with
\Cref{thm:ladder-signed-upper-bound}, and it is the reason the hard instance
of this note is the star, which by \Cref{obs:ladder-nontrivial} is
semantically nontrivial. A dedicated audit of that theorem's scope, including
a pendant-seed repair that restores a nonempty $\mathcal S_{\epsppr}$ while preserving
the triangular wave, is in the source package \texttt{i5a}; it is a separate
piece of work from this note and is not asserted here.

\begin{problem}[General-graph terminal bound for output maps; \statusopen]
\label{open:ladder-phi}
\Cref{rem:ladder-phi-eliminated} removed the instance constant
$\Phi=\max_{u}d_{u}\pi_{u}/(d_{v}\pi_{v})$ from every main statement. What
remains open is the general-graph analogue of
\Cref{lem:ladder-terminal}\ref{it:ladder-terminal-star}: the terminal bound on
$\Psi$ for outputs $\bm{z}+\bm{M}\bm{r}$ with $B$ near the escape threshold of
\Cref{prop:ladder-escape}, where the route
$\Psi_{T}\le\Phi\norm{\bm{r}_{\mathrm{mass}}}_{1}$ re-enters and is sharp only if
$\Phi=1$. Conjecturally $\Phi=1$ whenever the seed maximizes $d_{u}\pi_{u}$,
equivalently $\pr(\bm{e}_{u})_{v}\le\pr(\bm{e}_{v})_{v}$ for all $u$; this is
\statusmeasured{} on ten zoo graphs at three values of $\alpha$. Until it is
proved or refuted, general-graph statements should use
\Cref{thm:ladder-monotone-general} or
\Cref{lem:ladder-terminal}\ref{it:ladder-terminal-general}, which are
$\Phi$-free but cover a smaller range of $B$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Does anything besides the star force the signed bound?;
\statusopen]
\label{open:ladder-beyond-star}
\Cref{thm:ladder-signed-lower-bound} is star-specific through exactly one
number, the seed's self-return amplification $\pi_{v}/\galpha$, which is
$\Theta(1/\alpha)$ only because a degree-$\Theta(1/\epsppr)$ seed reflects its
mass back in one step. \Cref{prop:ladder-signed-general} shows that
bounded-degree, one-dimensional-like families amplify only
$\Theta(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ and yield merely $\Omega(\alpha^{-1/4})$ seed
operations. In falsifiable form: exhibit a family with
$\vol(\mathcal S_{\epsppr})=\Theta(1/\epsppr)$ and \emph{bounded} seed degree on which
every $\Rpm$ member needs $\Omega(1/(\sqrt\alpha\,\epsppr))$ work; or prove
that $\pi_{v}/\galpha=\Theta(1/\alpha)$ --- hence a
$\Theta(1/\epsppr)$-degree seed, hence essentially the star --- is necessary,
making the star the unique hard instance shape for one-hop relaxation.
Either branch decides whether the class barrier is a local anomaly of
reflecting seeds or a graph-uniform phenomenon.
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}[A known scope limit that is not an open problem]
\label{rem:ladder-block-scope}
One-hop \emph{block} relaxations lie outside $\Rpm$ and defeat
\Cref{lem:ladder-displacement} by construction: the centre-plus-leaves block
solves the star in $\bigO(m)$. That is a statement about the cheapest
implementable primitive that is spectrally aligned with the slow mode, and it
is a design question rather than part of \Cref{open:ladder-beyond-star}. Two
further scope limits: the $\Mpm$ bounds assume the invariant-exact primitive
\eqref{eq:ladder-primitive}, so inexact or rounded arithmetic is outside the
model, and no finite-precision claim is made anywhere in this note.
\end{remark}
