\section{A fully charged OP2 algorithm}
\label{sec:op2-algorithm}

The depth theorem permits fresh face solves: there are only
$\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ of them.  This avoids the stronger dynamic-response
contract in \cref{sec:contract}.

For a requested failure probability $\zeta\in(0,1)$ and
$0<\epsobj<\alpha/2$, define
\begin{equation}
 \vartheta:=\frac18\sqrt{\alpha\rho\epsobj},
 \qquad
 \nu:=\frac{\vartheta}{4},
 \qquad
 J:=\left\lceil
 \frac{\log(64/\epsobj)}{2\log(1/q_\alpha)}
 \right\rceil,
 \qquad
 R:=\left\lceil\log_4\frac{J+1}{\zeta}\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:op2-parameters}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Threshold-batch local RPPR obstacle solver}
\label{alg:threshold-batch}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $U\gets\{v\}$.
\FOR{$k=0,1,\ldots,J$}
  \STATE Make at most $R$ independent local SDD calls at relative energy
         tolerance $\nu$.  After each call, scan the active rows and accept
         the first $\vz$ satisfying
         $\norm{\mQ_{UU}\vz_U-\vc_U}_2\leq\nu\sqrt\alpha$.
  \STATE If no call is certified, \textbf{return} $\vzero$ with a failure flag.
  \STATE Scan all rows in $U$ once and accumulate
         $\widehat r_i=c_i-(\mQ\vz)_i$ for every boundary candidate.
  \IF{$k=J$}
    \STATE \textbf{return} $[\vz]_+$.
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $B\gets\{i\in\partial U:\widehat r_i>\vartheta/2\}$.
  \IF{$B=\emptyset$}
    \STATE \textbf{return} $[\vz]_+$.
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Expose the adjacency lists of all $i\in B$ and set $U\gets U\cup B$.
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Graph-uniform local solution of OP2]
\label{thm:op2}
On every canonical nonzero RPPR instance with
$0<\epsobj<\alpha/2$, \cref{alg:threshold-batch} reports failure with
probability at most $\zeta$ and otherwise returns a feasible vector
$\widehat\vx$ such that
\begin{equation}
 F_\rho(\widehat\vx)-F_\rho(\vx_\rho^*)\leq\epsobj
 \label{eq:proved-op2-gap}
\end{equation}
Using independent capped trials of a randomized nearly-linear SDD solver on
every exposed face, its expected fully charged work is
\begin{equation}
 \softO\!\left(
   \frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
   \log\frac1{\epsobj}\log\frac2\zeta
 \right),
 \label{eq:proved-op2-work}
\end{equation}
where the soft factors are polylogarithmic in the declared input parameters.
The bound includes seed and degree access, construction of every principal
system, all adjacency and candidate incidences, every fresh solver state,
boundary accumulation and comparison, intermediate materialization, orthant
projection, and output.  It uses no supplied support, ambient preprocessing,
or uncharged certificate scan.
For $\epsobj\geq\alpha/2$, returning zero gives the same conclusion with
constant work.
The excluded trivial regime $\rho\geq1/d_v$ is recognized by one seed-degree
query and also has the exact zero solution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first justify the certified face-solve wrapper.  On every reachable face,
$\vc_U\leq\vb_U$ and $\mQ_{UU}^{-1}\geq0$, so
\begin{equation}
 \vzero\leq\vx_U^U\leq\mQ_{UU}^{-1}\vb_U,
 \qquad
 \norm{\vx^U}_{\mQ}^2
 =\vc_U^\trans\vx_U^U
 \leq\vb_U^\trans\mQ_{UU}^{-1}\vb_U
 \leq\alpha.
 \label{eq:face-solution-energy}
\end{equation}
Here the last inequality uses
$\norm{\vb}_2^2=\alpha^2/d_v\leq\alpha^2$ and
$\mQ_{UU}\succeq\alpha\mI$.

Run the Koutis--Miller--Peng chain construction with its constant failure
parameter $p=1/4$.  On its good-chain event, a call at relative energy
tolerance $\nu$ has error $\ve=\vz-\vx^U$ satisfying
$\norm{\ve}_{\mQ}\leq\nu\norm{\vx^U}_{\mQ}
\leq\nu\sqrt\alpha$ and hence
$\norm{\mQ_{UU}\ve_U}_2\leq\nu\sqrt\alpha$.  Thus every good call passes the
displayed residual test.  Conversely, every accepted call, whether or not its
random chain is good, satisfies deterministically
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\vz-\vx^U}_{\mQ}^2
 =\vr_U^\trans\mQ_{UU}^{-1}\vr_U
 \leq\frac1\alpha\norm{\vr_U}_2^2
 \leq\nu^2,
 \qquad
 \vr_U:=\mQ_{UU}\vz_U-\vc_U.
 \label{eq:certified-face-error}
\end{equation}
Independent calls therefore leave a phase uncertified with probability at
most $4^{-R}\leq\zeta/(J+1)$.  A union bound gives total reported-failure
probability at most $\zeta$.  In the remainder, condition only on no reported
failure; every accepted solve then has the deterministic error in
\cref{eq:certified-face-error}.

The boundary-residual error is uniformly small.  If
$\ve=\vz-\vx^U$, then $\mQ\preceq\mI$ gives
\begin{equation}
 |(\mQ\ve)_i|\leq\norm{\mQ\ve}_2
 \leq\norm{\ve}_{\mQ}\leq\nu=\vartheta/4.
 \label{eq:batch-boundary-error}
\end{equation}
Consequently every admitted coordinate has exact residual greater than
$\vartheta/4>0$ and is support-safe by \cref{thm:safe-pivots}.  Every
unreported boundary coordinate has exact residual at most
$3\vartheta/4<\vartheta$.  Thus the nonempty batches generated by the
algorithm form a $\vartheta$-batched sequence, every face lies in
$\mathcal S_\rho^*$, and
\begin{equation}
 \vol(U)\leq\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*)\leq1/\rho.
 \label{eq:batch-face-volume}
\end{equation}

If the loop reaches its cap, \cref{thm:batch-depth,eq:op2-parameters} gives
\begin{equation}
 \phi(\vx^U)-\phi(\vx_\rho^*)
 \leq8q_\alpha^{2J}+\frac{\vartheta^2}{\alpha\rho}
 \leq\frac{\epsobj}{8}+\frac{\epsobj}{64}.
 \label{eq:capped-face-gap}
\end{equation}
If instead $B=\emptyset$, every exact positive outside residual is at most
$3\vartheta/4$.  Only boundary coordinates can be positive and
$|\partial U|\leq\vol(U)\leq1/\rho$, so the exact-face KKT certificate gives
\begin{equation}
 \phi(\vx^U)-\phi(\vx_\rho^*)
 \leq\frac{9\vartheta^2}{32\alpha\rho}
 =\frac{9\epsobj}{2048}.
 \label{eq:empty-batch-gap}
\end{equation}

Finally $\vx^U\geq0$, so Euclidean orthant projection is nonexpansive relative
to $\vx^U$.  Restricted stationarity and
$\alpha\mI\preceq\mQ\preceq\mI$ imply
\begin{equation}
 \phi([\vz]_+)-\phi(\vx^U)
 =\tfrac12\norm{[\vz]_+-\vx^U}_{\mQ}^2
 \leq\frac{\nu^2}{2\alpha}
 =\frac{\rho\epsobj}{2048}
 \leq\frac{\epsobj}{2048}.
 \label{eq:batch-output-error}
\end{equation}
Together, \cref{eq:capped-face-gap,eq:empty-batch-gap,eq:batch-output-error}
prove \cref{eq:proved-op2-gap} with room to spare.

It remains to charge the solves.  In degree coordinates, the principal matrix
is
\begin{equation}
 \mH_{UU}:=\mD_U^{1/2}\mQ_{UU}\mD_U^{1/2}
 =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\mD_U-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\mA_{UU},
 \label{eq:degree-sdd-face}
\end{equation}
whose $i$th diagonal is $(1+\alpha)d_i/2$ while its absolute off-diagonal
row sum is at most $(1-\alpha)d_i/2$.  It is therefore SDD and has
$O(\vol(U))$ stored nonzeros.  Solving
$\mH_{UU}\vy_U=\mD_U^{1/2}\vc_U$ and returning
$\vz_U=\mD_U^{1/2}\vy_U$ preserves the energy norm exactly.  The certified
wrapper requests relative energy error
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{\rm sdd}:=\nu
 =\frac1{32}\sqrt{\alpha\rho\epsobj}.
 \label{eq:sdd-relative-target}
\end{equation}
The nearly-linear SDD theorem of \citet[Theorem~4.6]{koutis2011nearly}
solves this exposed matrix in expected work nearly linear in its local
nonzeros and logarithmic in $1/\eta_{\rm sdd}$.  Their BuildChain and
Lemma~4.5 give the constant-success call used above.  The $R$ capped calls,
including every residual-certification scan, have per-face expected work
\[
 \softO\!\left(
   \vol(U)\log\frac1{\eta_{\rm sdd}}\,R
 \right).
\]
Constructing the system, materializing its approximate solution, scanning the
active rows, maintaining candidate words, and returning the output each cost
$O(\vol(U))$ in that phase.  Exposing a certified batch costs
$O(\vol(B))$; since $U\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\subseteq\mathcal S_\rho^*$, the
combined phase exposure is $O(1/\rho)$ (equivalently it is charged to the
next face).  Only residual-certified batches are ever exposed, so
\cref{eq:batch-face-volume} holds on every execution prefix, including one
that later reports failure.  The expected-work bound is therefore
unconditional, not conditioned on successful randomness.  Finally,
\begin{equation}
 \log(1/q_\alpha)
 =\log\frac{\sqrt{2/\alpha}+1}{\sqrt{2/\alpha}-1}
 \geq\sqrt{2\alpha},
 \label{eq:q-depth-bound}
\end{equation}
so $J=O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsobj))$.  Combining this with
\cref{eq:batch-face-volume} proves \cref{eq:proved-op2-work} and charges every
item claimed in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact scope of the numerical claim]
\label{rem:numerical-scope}
The theorem is an exact-real randomized word-arithmetic result with
polylogarithmic solver accuracy and a declared failure probability.  It does
not claim deterministic bit complexity or stability of a particular
floating-point implementation.  Certified interval evaluation may replace
the probabilistic energy guarantee using \cref{cor:interval-threshold}, but a
polylogarithmic bit-length theorem is separate.
\end{remark}

\section{The fully charged active-edge contract}
\label{sec:contract}

For comparison, we retain the stronger persistent-continuation interface that
motivated this direction.  It is no longer needed for the end-to-end OP2 proof:
\cref{thm:op2} pays for a bounded number of fresh faces.  The contract remains
useful as a target for removing rebuilding, for exact continuation, or for
settings without the threshold-batch depth theorem.
Let $E$ be the total number of distinct active-row incidences exposed, plus
the boundary-candidate and degree words generated from them.  Let the work
ledger be
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 W={}&C_{\mathrm{exp}}+C_{\mathrm{false}}+C_{\mathrm{upd}}+C_{\mathrm{bnd}}
      +C_{\mathrm{num}}\\
    &+C_{\mathrm{state}}+C_{\mathrm{mat}}+C_{\mathrm{emit}}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:resource-ledger}
\end{equation}
The meanings are fixed in \cref{tab:ledger}; none may be suppressed as an
oracle call.

\begin{definition}[Threshold-certified active-edge continuation contract]
\label{def:active-edge-contract}
An active-edge primitive satisfies the contract if, for every canonical OP2
instance and $0<\epsobj<\alpha/2$, when coupled to the safe outer skeleton it
has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Sound discovery.}  Every admitted coordinate lies in $\mathcal S_\rho^*$.
Any speculative activation is restored and its entire cost is included in
$C_{\mathrm{false}}$.
\item \emph{Threshold-complete reporting.}  On a current face $U$, it
produces $\vz$, $\delta$, and a certified $\bar\eta$ satisfying
\cref{eq:threshold-parameters,eq:interval-residual-target}.  It maintains
boundary-slack intervals of width less than $2\bar\eta$ and either returns a
coordinate whose interval upper endpoint is below $-\bar\eta$, or certifies
that no such key exists and returns the projected vector in
\cref{eq:threshold-output}.  All interval construction and refinement is
included in $C_{\mathrm{bnd}}$.  By \cref{cor:interval-threshold}, no unknown
complementarity margin is assumed.
\item \emph{Exploration bound.}  Counting candidate words as above,
\begin{equation}
 E\leq\softO(\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*)).
 \label{eq:exploration-contract}
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Aggregate continuation bound.}  Across the entire nested
sequence, including all items in \cref{eq:resource-ledger},
\begin{equation}
 W\leq\softO\!\left(
 E\sqrt{\kappa(\mQ)}\log\frac1{\epsobj}
 \right).
 \label{eq:aggregate-contract}
\end{equation}
The soft factors may be polylogarithmic in $n$, $1/\alpha$, $1/\rho$, and a
declared failure probability, but not polynomial in $1/\epsobj$ or in an
unstated sign margin.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional OP2 theorem]
\label{thm:conditional-op2}
If a primitive satisfying \cref{def:active-edge-contract} exists, then
\cref{alg:skeleton} is a graph-uniform local algorithm satisfying OP2 with
fully charged work
\begin{equation}
 W=\softO\!\left(
   \frac{1}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\log\frac1{\epsobj}
 \right).
 \label{eq:conditional-op2-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Soundness and \cref{thm:safe-pivots,cor:interval-threshold} keep every exact
outer face in $\mathcal S_\rho^*$ and make every reported pivot safe.  A
no-report outcome has the objective gap by
\cref{cor:interval-threshold}.  The shared support-volume theorem gives
$\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*)\leq1/\rho$, while
$\kappa(\mQ)\leq1/\alpha$.  Substitution into
\cref{eq:exploration-contract,eq:aggregate-contract} proves
\cref{eq:conditional-op2-work}.  Every access and output item appears in
\cref{eq:resource-ledger}, so no boundary scan, preprocessing, or
materialization is uncharged.
\end{proof}

The mathematical outer loop makes $C_{\mathrm{false}}=0$.  A persistent
implementation of \cref{eq:aggregate-contract} would still be stronger than
\cref{thm:op2}: it would maintain changing Stieltjes response and threshold
keys rather than rebuilding them for each of the boundedly many batches.

\begin{proposition}[Terminal volume is insufficient]
\label{prop:terminal-insufficient}
There is a family of canonical endpoint-seeded path instances whose exact
safe-pivot faces are all prefixes $U_t=\{1,\ldots,t\}$, whose terminal volume
is $\Theta(n)$, and whose cumulative face volume is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^n\vol(U_t)=\Theta(n^2).
 \label{eq:path-prefix-sum}
\end{equation}
Thus a method that scans or materializes every current face once per pivot is
not controlled by terminal volume.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix an endpoint-seeded path $P_n$ and any $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  At $\rho=0$,
$\mQ^{-1}\vb$ is strictly positive because the inverse of the irreducible
Stieltjes matrix is positive.  By continuity, for all sufficiently small
$\rho>0$ the unconstrained shifted solution $\mQ^{-1}\vc$ remains positive,
so it is the obstacle optimum and $\mathcal S_\rho^*=[n]$.  On a proper prefix, the only
outside graph-boundary vertex is the next path vertex.  The current face is
not terminal, so \cref{thm:safe-pivots} implies that this unique boundary
vertex has negative slack and is admitted next.  Hence every prefix occurs.
For $t<n$, $\vol(U_t)=2t-1$, and $\vol(U_n)=2n-2$, proving
\cref{eq:path-prefix-sum}.
\end{proof}

This is a representation/implementation obstruction, not a class lower
bound.  A persistent elimination record solves the same issue on paths, while
\cref{thm:batch-depth} solves it on arbitrary graphs by proving that only a
square-root number of complete prefix scans are required for the requested
objective accuracy.

\begin{theorem}[Exact linear-work continuation on endpoint paths]
\label{thm:path-ldl}
Suppose the input is promised to be a unit-weight path, the seed is an
endpoint, and adjacency-list access starts at that endpoint.  In the exact-real
arithmetic model, the canonical RPPR obstacle problem can be solved, including
support discovery, state maintenance, one final materialization, and output,
in
\begin{equation}
 O(\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*))
 \label{eq:path-linear-work}
\end{equation}
work and $O(|\mathcal S_\rho^*|)$ stored words, with no global preprocessing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Number the path from the seed and use degree coordinates
$\vy=\mD^{-1/2}\vx$.  Write
\begin{equation}
 \mH:=\mD^{1/2}\mQ\mD^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \vh:=\mD^{1/2}\vc,
 \qquad
 \beta:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.
 \label{eq:path-degree-system}
\end{equation}
Then $H_{ii}=a_i:=(1+\alpha)d_i/2$,
$H_{i,i+1}=-\beta$, and
$h_i=\alpha\mathbf 1\{i=1\}-\alpha\rho d_i$.
Every reachable face is a prefix because the current nonempty prefix has only
one outside boundary coordinate; until termination that coordinate is the
next path vertex.

Maintain the scalar $LDL^\trans$ elimination of the current prefix.  With
$p_1=a_1$ and $q_1=h_1$, appending vertex $i$ performs
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i=-\frac{\beta}{p_{i-1}},
 \qquad
 p_i=a_i-\ell_i^2p_{i-1},
 \qquad
 q_i=h_i-\ell_iq_{i-1}.
 \label{eq:path-ldl-update}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness makes every $p_i>0$.  Eliminating the preceding
coordinates shows that the current endpoint value is exactly
\begin{equation}
 y_i^{U_i}=q_i/p_i.
 \label{eq:path-endpoint-value}
\end{equation}
The only possibly negative outside slack, expressed in degree coordinates,
is
\begin{equation}
 (\mH\vy^{U_i}-\vh)_{i+1}
 =-\beta y_i^{U_i}-h_{i+1}
 =\alpha\rho d_{i+1}-\beta y_i^{U_i}.
 \label{eq:path-next-slack}
\end{equation}
If it is negative, \cref{thm:safe-pivots} admits $i+1$ and
\cref{eq:path-ldl-update} costs constant arithmetic.  If it is nonnegative,
all other outside coordinates are nonboundary and have positive slack, so the
current face is the optimum.

Each admitted row is exposed once.  After termination, one backward
substitution
\[
 y_s=q_s/p_s,
 \qquad
 y_i=q_i/p_i-\ell_{i+1}y_{i+1}
\]
materializes all $s=|\mathcal S_\rho^*|$ values, followed by
$x_i=\sqrt{d_i}y_i$.  The exposure, state, arithmetic, materialization, and
output charges are all linear in the terminal path volume.  The result is the
exact obstacle optimum and therefore meets every positive objective tolerance.
\end{proof}

The theorem is structural and exact-real.  It neither supplies a
finite-precision bit bound nor extends the scalar recurrence to branching
graphs, where one admission can change many old cut-edge keys.

\section{Projected and face CG: exact verdict}
\label{sec:cg-verdict}

Exact restricted solutions enjoy the one-sided invariant in
\cref{thm:safe-pivots}.  It is tempting to replace each exact face solve by CG
and assume its intermediate iterates inherit
$\vzero\leq\vx_k\leq\vx_\rho^*$.  The following exact example disproves that
assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Four-vertex coordinatewise overshoot]
\label{thm:cg-counterexample}
Let the graph have vertices $\{0,1,2,3\}$ and edges
\begin{equation}
 \{01,12,13,23\},
 \label{eq:counterexample-graph}
\end{equation}
with degrees $(1,3,2,2)$, seed $0$, $\alpha=1/5$, and $\rho=1/1000$.
Let $U=\{0,1,2\}$ and start ordinary full-face CG from the exact
$U$-restricted solution, extended by zero.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the obstacle optimum is strictly positive on all four coordinates;
\item the first three CG iterates are strictly positive and the quadratic
energy decreases at every iteration;
\item the third iterate exceeds the optimum in coordinate $2$ by the exact
positive amount
\begin{equation}
 \frac{30473}{32130000};
 \label{eq:overshoot}
\end{equation}
\item orthant-projected CG follows the identical trajectory through this
iteration because every projection is inactive.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore feasibility, support monotonicity, objective monotonicity, and
within-face conjugacy do not imply coordinatewise monotonicity below the
optimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use degree coordinates $\vy=\mD^{-1/2}\vx$.  Direct substitution into
\cref{eq:c-and-phi} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mH:=\mD^{1/2}\mQ\mD^{1/2}=\frac35\mD-\frac25\mA,
 \qquad
 \vh:=\mD^{1/2}\vc=\frac15(\eunit{0}-\rho\vd).
 \label{eq:H-h}
\end{equation}
Exact rational elimination gives
\begin{align}
 \vy^*&=
 \left(\frac{6983}{17000},\frac{1983}{17000},
       \frac{983}{17000},\frac{983}{17000}\right),
 \label{eq:counter-optimum}\\
 \vy_0&=
 \left(\frac{24953}{63000},\frac{1987}{21000},
       \frac{983}{31500},0\right).
 \label{eq:counter-start}
\end{align}
The positivity of $\vy^*$ makes it the unconstrained and obstacle optimum.
Running the ordinary Euclidean CG recurrence in the original $\vx$
coordinates gives step sizes
\begin{equation}
 \frac53,\quad\frac{225}{74},\quad\frac{259}{120},\quad\frac{150}{119},
 \label{eq:counter-steps}
\end{equation}
and the first three degree-coordinate iterates
\begin{align*}
 \vy_1={}&\left(\frac{24953}{63000},\frac{1987}{21000},
  \frac{983}{31500},\frac{983}{23625}\right),\\
 \vy_2={}&\left(\frac{24953}{63000},\frac{259877}{2331000},
  \frac{65861}{1165500},\frac{10813}{194250}\right),\\
 \vy_3={}&\left(\frac{388057}{945000},\frac{4363}{37800},
  \frac{111079}{1890000},\frac{36371}{630000}\right).
\end{align*}
Every displayed entry is positive, while
\[
 (y_3)_2-(y^*)_2
 =\frac{111079}{1890000}-\frac{983}{17000}
 =\frac{30473}{32130000}>0.
\]
Positive diagonal scaling preserves coordinatewise order between $\vx$ and
$\vy$.  Standard exact CG line minimization decreases the quadratic, and the
verification script checks the corresponding rational energy values and
$\mQ$-conjugacy.  The fourth step reaches $\vy^*$.  Since no coordinate is
negative before the overshoot, orthant projection changes nothing.
\end{proof}

The exact script \texttt{verify\_counterexample.py} also prints transformed
residual sign patterns
\[
 0{+}{+}0,\qquad {+}{+}{-}0,\qquad {-}{+}{-}0,\qquad0000,
\]
so no fixed residual-sign invariant survives either.  The distinctions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Objective monotonicity} holds in the example by exact line
minimization.
\item \emph{Coordinatewise monotonicity} fails at iteration three.
\item \emph{Support monotonicity} holds in this example because all new-face
iterates are positive; it is too weak to certify safe values.
\item \emph{Residual-sign monotonicity} fails by the exact sign trace.
\item \emph{Conjugacy} holds only while the fixed-face recurrence is
unmodified.  If an orthant projection clips a coordinate, the actual
displacement is no longer the CG step $a_k\vp_k$ and the short residual and
conjugacy recurrences require a restart or reorthogonalization.
\end{itemize}

The theorem refutes only the ordinary/project-after-step route and any proof
that assumes its one-sided invariant.  It does not rule out accelerated
obstacle solvers with different estimate sequences, certified envelopes, or
persistent response representations.

\section{Algorithmic synthesis and remaining boundaries}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The route now separates cleanly into five layers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Outer mathematical logic (proved):} the K-LCP pivot from zero is
nested, support-safe, and boundary-local by \cref{thm:safe-pivots}.
\item \emph{Fixed-face numerical acceleration (proved with supplied face):}
CG contributes $\sqrt\kappa=O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ and logarithmic accuracy by
\cref{thm:supplied-cg}.
\item \emph{Approximate decision logic (proved):}
\Cref{thm:threshold-dichotomy} replaces exact negative-sign resolution by a
known threshold tied to $\epsobj$ and $|\partial U|$.
\item \emph{Batch depth (proved):} block-bidiagonal Cholesky inverse decay
limits the number of complete threshold batches to
$O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsobj))$ by \cref{thm:batch-depth}.
\item \emph{Fully charged implementation (proved):} fresh local SDD solves,
complete active-row scans, state construction, materialization, and output
over those batches give \cref{thm:op2}.
\end{enumerate}

The stronger persistent interface remains open.  A new block can cause a
dense positive correction on old coordinates, and a dynamic implementation
would have to communicate its threshold crossings without replaying all cut
keys.  This no longer blocks OP2 because the proved batch-depth theorem charges
each complete replay and bounds their number.  The remaining numerical scope
is deterministic finite precision and bit complexity, as stated in
\cref{rem:numerical-scope}.
