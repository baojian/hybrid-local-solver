\begin{abstract}
This note independently reconstructs the exact one-step Lyapunov master form
for a clipped accelerated proximal recurrence.  The identity is a statement
about an exact shifted solve on one positive face; it is not a convergence or
local-work theorem.  The reconstruction exposes two sign-visible channels,
leaving only mixed clipping as the analytic obstruction.  Fable's finite
exact decisions are retained as source evidence rather than promoted here.
An entrywise positive-association condition closes that obstruction on every
complete multipartite graph, including all unequal part vectors.  Exact
elimination of the high variable yields a different clipped-Laplacian route
and proves an infinite balanced six-cycle blow-up family strictly beyond the
positive-association class.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, coordinates, and evidence labels}

The shared RPPR objective uses the normalized variable in the imported
definition above.  This note temporarily uses degree-scaled coordinates
$u=D^{-1/2}x$, because clipping by a common degree-normalized threshold then
acts as $(u-c\one)_+$.  In these coordinates the quadratic matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde Q
 :=D^{1/2}QD^{1/2}
 =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}D-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A.
 \label{eq:psi-scaled-q}
\end{equation}
This is a note-scoped change of coordinates, not a change to the shared RPPR
convention.

\paragraph{Source.}
Fable's iteration-7 files propose the master form and report exact finite
decisions.  They are exploratory evidence, not proof authority for this note.
\paragraph{Proved here.}
The recurrence, admissible functional domain, master identity, the two
unmixed-sign inequalities, and both graph-family theorems below are derived
independently.
\paragraph{Open.}
Nonpositivity for mixed clipping beyond the stated structural condition,
proper-face transport, finite shifted solves, and every end-to-end work
conclusion remain open at this point in the note.

\section{Exact recurrence and admissible functional domain}

Fix $0<q\leq1/2$ and put
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2},\qquad
 \kappa=1-2\alpha,\qquad
 \beta=\frac{1-q}{1+q},\qquad
 \nu=\frac{q}{1+q}=\frac{1-\beta}{2}.
 \label{eq:psi-parameters}
\end{equation}
Let $\langle u,v\rangle_D=u^TDv$, let
$\mathsf v_G=\one^TD\one$, and let
\begin{equation}
 P=I-\frac{\one\one^TD}{\mathsf v_G}
 \label{eq:psi-projection}
\end{equation}
be the $D$-orthogonal projection onto $\one^{\perp_D}$.  Define the exact
shifted-resolvent operator
\begin{equation}
 M:=\kappa(\widetilde Q+\kappa D)^{-1}D,
 \qquad
 m_0:=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\alpha}=1-q^2.
 \label{eq:psi-resolvent}
\end{equation}
The matrix $M$ is $D$-self-adjoint, positive definite, entrywise
nonnegative, commutes with $P$, and satisfies $M\one=m_0\one$.  The last
display and \cref{eq:psi-parameters} give the two scalar identities
\begin{equation}
 \beta m_0=(1-q)^2,
 \qquad
 m_0(1+\beta)^2=4\beta.
 \label{eq:psi-scalar-identities}
\end{equation}

Consider one exact positive-face shifted step.  With
$e_t=x^\star-x_t$, $d_t=x_t-x_{t-1}=e_{t-1}-e_t\geq0$, and an arbitrary
cap $\Delta_t\geq0$, put
\begin{equation}
 y_t=\beta d_t-\Delta_t\one,
 \qquad
 \phi_t=(y_t)_+,
 \qquad
 \ell_t=x_t+\phi_t.
 \label{eq:psi-clip}
\end{equation}
Exact minimization of the shifted quadratic centered at $\ell_t$ is
equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 e_{t+1}=M(e_t-\phi_t).
 \label{eq:psi-error-recurrence}
\end{equation}
This reconstruction assumes that the obstacle solve stays on the stated
positive face.  It does not silently extend to a proper face.

Write
\begin{equation}
 h_{t-1}=Pe_{t-1},\quad h_t=Pe_t,\quad
 z_t=Py_t=\beta(h_{t-1}-h_t),\quad
 w_t=P(y_t)_-,\quad b_t=z_t-\nu h_t.
 \label{eq:psi-high-variables}
\end{equation}
Since $(y_t)_+=y_t+(y_t)_-$, one has
$P\phi_t=z_t+w_t$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
 h_{t+1}=M(h_t-z_t-w_t).
 \label{eq:psi-high-recurrence}
\end{equation}

The trajectory-free functional below is defined for every
$y\in\mathbb R^V$ and $h\in\one^{\perp_D}$.  This really is the full
algebraic cap domain: given any $y$, choosing
$\Delta\geq\max_i(-y_i)$ and $d=(y+\Delta\one)/\beta$ gives
$d\geq0$ and $y=\beta d-\Delta\one$.  The functional domain is nevertheless
stronger than reachability by a single fixed RPPR trajectory, because it
forgets the preceding history and the positive-face inequalities.

\section{Independent master-identity reconstruction}

For $u,v\in\one^{\perp_D}$ define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V(u,v)
 :=\norm{u}_D^2-(1+\beta)\langle u,Mv\rangle_D
     +\beta\langle v,Mv\rangle_D.
 \label{eq:psi-lyapunov}
\end{equation}
Also put
\begin{equation}
 G:=m_0I-2M+M^2.
 \label{eq:psi-g-operator}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact clipped master identity]
\label{lem:psi-master-identity}
For every step satisfying \cref{eq:psi-error-recurrence},
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V(h_{t+1},h_t)-(1-q)^2\mathcal V(h_t,h_{t-1})
 =\Psi(y_t,h_t),
 \label{eq:psi-master-identity}
\end{equation}
where, for arbitrary $y$ and $h\in\one^{\perp_D}$,
\begin{align}
 z&:=Py,& w&:=P(y_-),& b&:=z-\nu h,\notag\\
 \Psi(y,h)
 &:=\norm{M(b+w)}_D^2-m_0\langle b,Mb\rangle_D
       -\nu^2\norm{Mh}_D^2-\beta\langle h,Gh\rangle_D.
 \label{eq:psi-master-form}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\bar\phi=P\phi=z+w$ and $c=b/\beta$.  From
$z=\beta(h_{t-1}-h_t)$ one has
$h_{t-1}=h_t+z/\beta$ and $z=\beta c+\nu h_t$.
Substitute these two relations and
$h_{t+1}=M(h_t-\bar\phi)$ into \cref{eq:psi-lyapunov}.  Using
$D$-self-adjointness of $M$ and the two scalar identities in
\cref{eq:psi-scalar-identities}, direct collection gives
\begin{align}
 &\mathcal V(h_{t+1},h_t)-(1-q)^2\mathcal V(h_t,h_{t-1})\notag\\
 &\quad=\norm{M\bar\phi}_D^2
 -(1-\beta)\langle Mh_t,M\bar\phi\rangle_D
 -\beta^2m_0\langle c,Mc\rangle_D
 -\beta\langle h_t,Gh_t\rangle_D.
 \label{eq:psi-collected-form}
\end{align}
Finally, $b=\beta c=z-\nu h_t$ and
$M(b+w)=M(\bar\phi-\nu h_t)$.  Expanding its squared norm and using
$1-\beta=2\nu$ turns \cref{eq:psi-collected-form} exactly into
\cref{eq:psi-master-form}.
\end{proof}

The calculation uses neither the trigger that selected $\Delta_t$ nor a
stage-type case split.  Conversely, it uses exact shifted minimization and a
fixed positive face; it is not an identity for a finite inner solve without
additional residual terms.

\section{A sign-visible decomposition}

Expanding only the first square in \cref{eq:psi-master-form} gives
\begin{align}
 \Psi(y,h)
 &=\norm{Mw}_D^2+2\langle M^2w,b\rangle_D
   -\langle b,M(m_0I-M)b\rangle_D\notag\\
 &\qquad-\nu^2\norm{Mh}_D^2-\beta\langle h,Gh\rangle_D.
 \label{eq:psi-slack-decomposition}
\end{align}
This representation keeps the complementarity $y_+y_-=0$ visible; replacing
$y_+$ and $y_-$ by independent vectors would discard the main structure.

Let $\mu_2$ be the first nonzero normalized-Laplacian eigenvalue.  On a mode
with normalized-Laplacian eigenvalue $\mu$, the generalized eigenvalue of
$\widetilde Q$ is
$\lambda(\mu)=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\mu/2$, and the eigenvalue of $M$ is
$m(\mu)=\kappa/(\kappa+\lambda(\mu))$.  If
\begin{equation}
 \mu_2\geq2q,
 \label{eq:psi-hgap}
\end{equation}
then $0<m(\mu)\leq1-q<m_0$ on $\one^{\perp_D}$.  Since
$m_0-2m+m^2\geq0$ for $0<m\leq1-q$, both
$M(m_0I-M)$ and $G$ are positive semidefinite on the high space.

Two entire channels now have a visible sign:
\begin{itemize}
 \item If $y\geq0$, then $w=0$, and every term in
 \cref{eq:psi-slack-decomposition} is nonpositive.
 \item If $y\leq0$, then $w=-z$ and $b+w=-\nu h$, so
 \begin{equation}
  \Psi(y,h)=-m_0\langle b,Mb\rangle_D
            -\beta\langle h,Gh\rangle_D\leq0.
 \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Thus only a genuinely mixed sign pattern can violate the master inequality.
This conclusion is analytic and family-independent under
\cref{eq:psi-hgap}; it does not rely on finite enumeration.

\section{A structural mixed-clipping theorem}

The remaining mixed term admits a sufficient condition with a probabilistic
sign interpretation.  Assume
\begin{equation}
 (M^2)_{ij}\leq \frac{m_0^2d_j}{\mathsf v_G}
 \qquad(i\neq j).
 \label{eq:psi-hk}
\end{equation}
This condition is entrywise and stronger than the desired conclusion, but it
can be checked analytically on graph families.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel positive association]
\label{lem:psi-kernel-positive-association}
Under \cref{eq:psi-hk}, for every $a\in\mathbb R^V$ and threshold
$\Delta\in\mathbb R$, if
\begin{equation*}
 f=(a-\Delta\one)_+,
 \qquad
 g=a-f=\min\{a,\Delta\one\},
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation}
 \langle MPf,MPg\rangle_D\geq0.
 \label{eq:psi-positive-association}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\pi_i=d_i/\mathsf v_G$ and define
\begin{equation*}
 K_{ij}:=\frac{d_i(M^2)_{ij}}{m_0^2\mathsf v_G}.
\end{equation*}
Entrywise nonnegativity of $M$, $D$-self-adjointness, and
$M^2\one=m_0^2\one$ show that $K$ is a symmetric probability coupling with
both marginals $\pi$.  Condition \cref{eq:psi-hk} is precisely
$K_{ij}\leq\pi_i\pi_j$ off the diagonal.  Centering by $P$ and using the
marginals gives the exact two-point identity
\begin{align}
 \langle MPf,MPg\rangle_D
 &=m_0^2\mathsf v_G
   \left(\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}f_ig_j
        -\sum_i\pi_if_i\sum_j\pi_jg_j\right)\notag\\
 &=\frac{m_0^2\mathsf v_G}{2}
   \sum_{i,j}(\pi_i\pi_j-K_{ij})(f_i-f_j)(g_i-g_j).
 \label{eq:psi-two-point-kernel}
\end{align}
Both $f$ and $g$ are nondecreasing functions of the same scalar $a_i$, so
the difference product is nonnegative.  Its diagonal value is zero, and
every off-diagonal coefficient is nonnegative by \cref{eq:psi-hk}.  This
proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structural nonpositivity of the master form]
\label{thm:psi-hk-nonpositivity}
If $\mu_2\geq2q$ and \cref{eq:psi-hk} holds, then
\begin{equation}
 \Psi(y,h)\leq0
 \qquad
 \text{for every }y\in\mathbb R^V,
 \quad h\in\one^{\perp_D}.
 \label{eq:psi-hk-conclusion}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given $y$, choose $\Delta\geq\max_i(-y_i)$ and put
$a=y+\Delta\one$.  Then $f=y_+$, $z=Py=Pa$, and
$Pg=z-Pf$.  Hence \cref{lem:psi-kernel-positive-association} says
\begin{equation}
 \langle MPf,M(z-Pf)\rangle_D\geq0,
 \quad\text{or equivalently}\quad
 \langle MPf,Mz\rangle_D\geq\norm{MPf}_D^2.
 \label{eq:psi-kernel-payment}
\end{equation}
In \cref{eq:psi-collected-form}, write $c=b/\beta$ and note
$z=\beta c+\nu h$.  The first two terms of that form satisfy
\begin{align*}
 L
 &:=\norm{MPf}_D^2-2\nu\langle Mh,MPf\rangle_D\\
 &=\norm{MPf}_D^2-2\langle M(z-\beta c),MPf\rangle_D\\
 &\leq-\norm{MPf}_D^2+2\beta\langle Mc,MPf\rangle_D\\
 &\leq\beta^2\norm{Mc}_D^2
 \leq\beta^2m_0\langle c,Mc\rangle_D.
\end{align*}
The first inequality uses \cref{eq:psi-kernel-payment}, the second completes
the square, and the third uses $0\prec M\preceq m_0I$ in the
$D$-inner product.  This pays the $c$-term of
\cref{eq:psi-collected-form}.  The remaining term
$-\beta\langle h,Gh\rangle_D$ is nonpositive under
$\mu_2\geq2q$, proving \cref{eq:psi-hk-conclusion}.
\end{proof}

\section{An infinite non-scalar family: complete bipartite graphs}

Condition \cref{eq:psi-hk} has a $q$-independent graph form.  Indeed,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde Q+\kappa D
 =\frac{1-\alpha}{2}(3D-A),
 \qquad
 M=\frac{2(1-2\alpha)}{1-\alpha}N,
 \qquad
 N:=(3D-A)^{-1}D.
 \label{eq:psi-n-resolvent}
\end{equation}
Since $N\one=\one/2$, condition \cref{eq:psi-hk} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 4\mathsf v_G(N^2)_{ij}\leq d_j
 \qquad(i\neq j).
 \label{eq:psi-hk-n}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Master nonpositivity on every complete bipartite graph]
\label{thm:psi-complete-bipartite}
Let $G=K_{a,b}$ with integers $a,b\geq1$.  For every $0<q\leq1/2$,
condition \cref{eq:psi-hgap} holds and
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{y\in\mathbb R^{a+b},\ h\in\one^{\perp_D}}\Psi(y,h)=0.
 \label{eq:psi-complete-bipartite-sup}
\end{equation}
In particular, the exact positive-face recurrence satisfies the one-step
contraction \cref{eq:psi-master-identity} on the entire infinite family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ and $R$ be the two parts, with $|L|=a$ and $|R|=b$; degrees are $b$
on $L$ and $a$ on $R$, and $\mathsf v_G=2ab$.  Solving
$(3D-A)z=Dx$ by the two part sums gives
\begin{equation}
 N=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \frac13I_a+\frac1{24a}J_a&\frac1{8b}J_{a\times b}\\[2mm]
 \frac1{8a}J_{b\times a}&\frac13I_b+\frac1{24b}J_b
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:psi-kab-n}
\end{equation}
Squaring the four blocks shows, for distinct vertices,
\begin{equation}
 (N^2)_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}
  13/(288a),&i,j\in L,\\
  13/(288b),&i,j\in R,\\
  3/(32b),&i\in L,\ j\in R,\\
  3/(32a),&i\in R,\ j\in L.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:psi-kab-n2}
\end{equation}
The corresponding left-to-right ratios in \cref{eq:psi-hk-n} are
$13/36$ within either part and $3/4$ across the cut.  Thus
\cref{eq:psi-hk-n} holds strictly.

The normalized-Laplacian spectrum of $K_{a,b}$ is
$\{0,1^{(a+b-2)},2\}$ when $a+b\geq3$, while $K_{1,1}$ has spectrum
$\{0,2\}$.  Hence $\mu_2\geq1\geq2q$.  The structural theorem
\cref{thm:psi-hk-nonpositivity} gives $\Psi\leq0$.  Equality is attained at
$y=0,h=0$, so the supremum is zero.
\end{proof}

This family is not a disguised scalar-high-space case: when $a+b\geq3$, the
normalized Laplacian has the two distinct high eigenvalues $1$ and $2$.
Thus the theorem goes beyond the complete-graph truncation-covariance proof.
It includes every star $K_{1,b}$, but it remains a positive-face Lyapunov
statement rather than a locality or work theorem.

\paragraph{Novelty boundary.}
The read-only Fable evidence already lists $K_{a,b}$ inside its proposed
\textup{(HK)} subclass.  The calculation above is therefore an independent
analytic closure and normalization of that listed family, not a new family
discovery.  The unequal-part complete-multipartite closure below is the
strict extension pursued here.

\section{Unequal complete multipartite graphs}

Let $G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_k}$, where $k\geq2$ and every $n_r\geq1$.  Put
$n=\sum_r n_r$, $d_r=n-n_r$, and let $r(i)$ denote the part containing
vertex $i$.  The direct block inverse already shows that unequal part sizes
do not create a singular threshold.  With
\begin{equation}
 c_r=3n-2n_r,
 \qquad
 C=1-\sum_{r=1}^k\frac{n_r}{c_r},
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-block-parameters}
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
 N_{ij}
 =\frac13\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}
  +\frac{d_{r(j)}}{c_{r(i)}C c_{r(j)}}
  -\frac{\mathbf1_{\{r(i)=r(j)\}}}{3c_{r(i)}}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-block-inverse}
\end{equation}
The following result proves that the favorable kernel sign persists for
every part vector.

\begin{theorem}[Master nonpositivity on every complete multipartite graph]
\label{thm:psi-complete-multipartite}
For every $G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_k}$ with $k\geq2$, every $n_r\geq1$, and every
$0<q\leq1/2$, condition \cref{eq:psi-hk-n} holds.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{y\in\mathbb R^n,\ h\in\one^{\perp_D}}\Psi(y,h)=0.
 \label{eq:psi-complete-multipartite-sup}
\end{equation}
This assertion is for the exact positive-face recurrence and carries no
local-work conclusion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\begin{equation}
 p_r=\frac{n_r}{n},\qquad
 \delta=\sum_u p_u(1-p_u),\qquad
 \Pi_r=\frac{p_r(1-p_r)}{\delta}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-masses}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Pi_r$ is the stationary mass of part $r$ and the stationary mass of
one vertex in that part is $\pi_j=\Pi_r/n_r$.  Let
$R$ be the random-walk chain lumped by parts:
\begin{equation}
 R_{rs}=\begin{cases}
 p_s/(1-p_r),&r\neq s,\\
 0,&r=s.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-part-chain}
\end{equation}
Since $N=(3I-D^{-1}A)^{-1}$, the stochastic kernel
$\widehat N:=2N$ lumps to
$\widehat R:=2(3I-R)^{-1}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 x_r=\frac{2}{3-2p_r},\qquad
 m=\sum_u\Pi_u x_u,\qquad
 t=\frac{\sum_u\Pi_u x_u^2}{m}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-xmt}
\end{equation}
A rank-one inversion of \cref{eq:psi-kmultipartite-part-chain} gives the
exact formula
\begin{equation}
 \widehat R_{rs}
 =\left(1-\frac{x_r}{2}\right)\mathbf1_{\{r=s\}}
   +\frac{x_rx_s}{2m}\Pi_s.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-rhat}
\end{equation}
In particular, for $r\neq s$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(\widehat R^2)_{rs}}{\Pi_s}
 =\frac{x_rx_s}{4m}(4-x_r-x_s+t).
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-cross-ratio}
\end{equation}

We prove that the last ratio is at most one.  The interval
$2/3\leq x_u\leq2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 x_u^2\leq\frac83x_u-\frac43,
 \qquad
 t\leq\frac83-\frac{4}{3m}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-t-bound}
\end{equation}
Fix the two distinct parts and abbreviate $p=p_r$, $s=p_s$, $x=x_r$,
$y=x_s$, and $r_0=1-p-s$.  Since $p+s\leq1$,
$(3-2p)(3-2s)\geq3$, hence $xy\leq4/3$.  Also $m\geq2/3$, so
$xy\leq3m^2$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 F(m):=4m-xy\left(\frac{20}{3}-x-y-\frac{4}{3m}\right)
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-f}
\end{equation}
is nondecreasing in $m$.  If $w(u)=u(1-u)$, the remaining parts have total
weight $W=\sum_{u\neq r,s}w(p_u)\leq r_0$ and contribute at least
$2W/3$ to the numerator of $m$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 m\geq m_L
 :=\frac{w(p)x+w(s)y+(2/3)r_0}{w(p)+w(s)+r_0}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-ml}
\end{equation}
Here the last replacement $W\mapsto r_0$ is valid because
$(A+(2/3)W)/(B+W)$ is nonincreasing when $A/B\geq2/3$, with
$A=w(p)x+w(s)y$ and $B=w(p)+w(s)$.
The monotonicity of $F$ reduces the claim to one exact bivariate polynomial
certificate.  For degree $d$, define the simplex Bernstein polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_d[c](p,s)
 :=\sum_{i+j\leq d}c_{ij}\frac{d!}{i!j!(d-i-j)!}
 p^is^j(1-p-s)^{d-i-j}.
 \label{eq:psi-simplex-bernstein}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal P=\mathcal B_8[c]$, where row $i$ below lists
$c_{i0},\ldots,c_{i,8-i}$:
{\scriptsize
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|l}
0&(81,54,459/14,243/14,54/7,81/28,27/28,0,0)\\
1&(54,2025/56,1247/56,3399/280,1587/280,65/28,27/28,0)\\
2&(459/14,1247/56,2977/210,4581/560,869/210,13/7,27/28)\\
3&(243/14,3399/280,4581/560,1457/280,85/28,3/2)\\
4&(54/7,1587/280,869/210,85/28,81/35)\\
5&(81/28,65/28,13/7,3/2)\\
6&(27/28,27/28,27/28)\\
7&(0,0)\\
8&(0)
\end{array}
\label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-p-coefficients}
\end{equation}
}
Also let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(p,s)
 =2p^2s-3p^2+2ps^2+4ps-6p-3s^2-6s+9.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-l}
\end{equation}
Its degree-three Bernstein coefficient rows are
$(9,7,4,0)$, $(7,17/3,4)$, $(4,4)$, and $(0)$.
Direct rational simplification gives
\begin{equation}
 F(m_L)=
 \frac{32\mathcal P(p,s)}
 {3(3-2p)^2(3-2s)^2(1-p^2-s^2)\mathcal L(p,s)}.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-bernstein-certificate}
\end{equation}
Every displayed Bernstein coefficient is nonnegative.  Since $p,s>0$ and
$p+s\leq1$, the denominator is positive; hence $F(m_L)\geq0$.
Together with \cref{eq:psi-kmultipartite-t-bound,eq:psi-kmultipartite-f},
this proves $(\widehat R^2)_{rs}\leq\Pi_s$ for $r\neq s$.

It remains to check distinct vertices in the same part.  The normalized
adjacency is congruent to the adjacency matrix and has one positive eigenvalue,
$k-1$ negative eigenvalues, and $n-k$ zero eigenvalues.  One elementary way
to see this is to restrict the adjacency quadratic form to part-constant
vectors: it has one positive direction and is negative definite on the
codimension-one subspace whose weighted part sum is zero; every within-part
zero-sum vector lies in the kernel.  Thus every nonconstant eigenvalue of
$R$ lies in $[-1,0)$, and every nonconstant eigenvalue of
$\widehat R^2$ is at most $4/9$.  Reversibility then gives
\begin{equation}
 (\widehat R^2)_{rr}
 \leq\Pi_r+\frac49(1-\Pi_r).
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-diagonal-covariance}
\end{equation}
On a within-part zero-sum vector, $\widehat N^2$ has eigenvalue $4/9$.
Consequently, for distinct $i,j$ in part $r$,
\begin{equation}
 (\widehat N^2)_{ij}
 =\frac{(\widehat R^2)_{rr}-4/9}{n_r}
 \leq\frac{5\Pi_r}{9n_r}
 \leq\pi_j.
 \label{eq:psi-kmultipartite-same-part}
\end{equation}
For vertices in different parts, block constancy and the cross-part result
give $(\widehat N^2)_{ij}=(\widehat R^2)_{rs}/n_s\leq\pi_j$.
Because $\widehat N^2=4N^2$, these two bounds are exactly
\cref{eq:psi-hk-n}.

Finally, the nontrivial normalized-adjacency spectrum is nonpositive apart
from the Perron eigenvalue, with possible zero modes.  Hence the first
nonzero normalized-Laplacian eigenvalue is at least one, so
$\mu_2\geq1\geq2q$.  The structural theorem gives $\Psi\leq0$, and
$y=h=0$ attains equality.
\end{proof}

\section{Eliminating the high variable: a route beyond \textup{(HK)}}

The entrywise condition \cref{eq:psi-hk} is not necessary.  A second
sufficient condition appears after eliminating $h$ exactly while preserving
the disjointness of the positive and negative parts of $y$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 u=P(y_+),\qquad v=P(y_-),
 \label{eq:psi-eliminate-uv}
\end{equation}
and define, on $\one^{\perp_D}$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_q
 &:=\frac1{m_0}M(m_0I-M)^2(I-M)^{-1},\notag\\
 \mathcal B_q
 &:=m_0MG(I-M)^{-1}(m_0I-M)^{-1},\notag\\
 \mathcal C_q
 &:=M(m_0I-M)(I-M)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:psi-abc-kernels}
\end{align}
All factors commute.  The inverse of $m_0I-M$ is only used on the high
space, where it is positive definite.

\begin{lemma}[Exact elimination of $h$]
\label{lem:psi-eliminate-h}
Under \cref{eq:psi-hgap}, for every fixed $y$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{h\in\one^{\perp_D}}\Psi(y,h)
 =-\langle u,\mathcal A_qu\rangle_D
  -\langle v,\mathcal B_qv\rangle_D
  +2\langle u,\mathcal C_qv\rangle_D.
 \label{eq:psi-eliminated-form}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\mathcal A_q$ and $\mathcal B_q$ are positive semidefinite and
$\mathcal C_q\one=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $Py=u-v$ and $P(y_-)=v$, direct expansion of
\cref{eq:psi-master-form} gives a concave quadratic in $h$.  Its negative
Hessian and linear vector are
\begin{align*}
 S&=\nu^2m_0M+\beta G
   =\beta(I-M)(m_0I-M),\\
 r_h&=M\bigl((m_0I-M)u-m_0v\bigr).
\end{align*}
Here the factorization uses $m_0=1-q^2$ and
$\nu^2m_0=\beta q^2$.  Completing the square adds
$\nu^2\langle r_h,S^{-1}r_h\rangle_D$ to the constant term
$\langle u,M^2u\rangle_D-m_0\langle u-v,M(u-v)\rangle_D$.
Collecting the $u^2$, $v^2$, and $uv$ coefficients yields exactly
\cref{eq:psi-eliminated-form}.  The high-space spectral bounds used after
\cref{eq:psi-hgap} give $0\prec M\prec m_0I\prec I$ and $G\succeq0$, so
the two diagonal operators are positive semidefinite.  Finally,
$(m_0I-M)\one=0$, hence $\mathcal C_q\one=0$.
\end{proof}

Consider the following $q$-dependent clipped-Laplacian condition:
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal C_q)_{ij}\leq0
 \qquad(i\neq j).
 \label{eq:psi-cl}
\end{equation}
Unlike \textup{(HK)}, this is a sign condition on a zero-row-sum kernel and
need not compare a positive resolvent entry with the shrinking stationary
mass.

\begin{theorem}[Clipped-Laplacian sufficient condition]
\label{thm:psi-cl}
If \cref{eq:psi-hgap,eq:psi-cl} hold, then
$\Psi(y,h)\leq0$ for every $y\in\mathbb R^V$ and
$h\in\one^{\perp_D}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $f=y_+$ and $g=y_-$.  Since $fg=0$ coordinatewise and
$\mathcal C_q\one=0$, projection can be removed from the cross term:
\begin{equation}
 \langle u,\mathcal C_qv\rangle_D
 =\langle f,\mathcal C_qg\rangle_D
 =\sum_{i\neq j}d_if_i(\mathcal C_q)_{ij}g_j\leq0.
 \label{eq:psi-cl-cross}
\end{equation}
The two diagonal terms in \cref{eq:psi-eliminated-form} are nonpositive,
so the supremum over $h$ is nonpositive.  The original value is no larger.
\end{proof}

\section{Edgewise payment beyond the clipped-Laplacian sign condition}

Condition \textup{(CL)} discards every positive off-diagonal entry of the
cross kernel.  A positive entry can instead be paid locally when the two
negative diagonal energies carry enough weight on the same unordered pair.
For the next statement, extend $\mathcal A_q$ and $\mathcal B_q$ by zero on
the constant mode and put
\begin{equation*}
 K^{\mathrm A}:=D\mathcal A_q,
 \qquad K^{\mathrm B}:=D\mathcal B_q,
 \qquad K^{\mathrm C}:=D\mathcal C_q.
\end{equation*}
These matrices are symmetric and have zero row sums.

\begin{theorem}[Edgewise Young payment]
\label{thm:psi-edgewise-young}
Assume \cref{eq:psi-hgap}.  Suppose, for every $i\neq j$,
\begin{equation}
 K^{\mathrm A}_{ij}\leq0,
 \qquad K^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\leq0,
 \qquad
 \bigl(K^{\mathrm C}_{ij}\bigr)^2
 \leq
 \bigl(-K^{\mathrm A}_{ij}\bigr)
 \bigl(-K^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\bigr)
 \quad\hbox{whenever }K^{\mathrm C}_{ij}>0.
 \label{eq:psi-edgewise-young-condition}
\end{equation}
Then $\Psi(y,h)\leq0$ for every $y\in\mathbb R^V$ and
$h\in\one^{\perp_D}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $f=y_+$ and $g=y_-$.  Projection can be removed from all three terms in
\cref{eq:psi-eliminated-form}, because the three extended kernels annihilate
$\one$.  Set
\begin{equation*}
 a_{ij}:=-K^{\mathrm A}_{ij},
 \qquad b_{ij}:=-K^{\mathrm B}_{ij},
 \qquad c_{ij}:=K^{\mathrm C}_{ij}
 \quad(i\neq j).
\end{equation*}
The zero-row-sum identities and the first two sign assumptions give
\begin{equation*}
 \langle f,\mathcal A_qf\rangle_D
 =\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}(f_i-f_j)^2,
 \qquad
 \langle g,\mathcal B_qg\rangle_D
 =\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}(g_i-g_j)^2.
\end{equation*}
Also
\begin{equation*}
 \langle f,\mathcal C_qg\rangle_D
 =\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(f_ig_j+f_jg_i),
\end{equation*}
because $f_ig_i=0$.  Terms with $c_{ij}\leq0$ are nonpositive.  If
$c_{ij}>0$, coordinatewise disjointness implies that at most one of
$f_ig_j$ and $f_jg_i$ is nonzero.  In the first case $g_i=f_j=0$, and
Young's inequality plus \cref{eq:psi-edgewise-young-condition} gives
\begin{equation*}
 2c_{ij}f_ig_j
 \leq a_{ij}f_i^2+b_{ij}g_j^2
 =a_{ij}(f_i-f_j)^2+b_{ij}(g_i-g_j)^2.
\end{equation*}
The other orientation is identical.  Summing over unordered pairs proves
\begin{equation*}
 2\langle f,\mathcal C_qg\rangle_D
 \leq
 \langle f,\mathcal A_qf\rangle_D
 +\langle g,\mathcal B_qg\rangle_D.
\end{equation*}
Insert this bound in \cref{eq:psi-eliminated-form}; the supremum over $h$ is
nonpositive, and so is every original value.
\end{proof}

\section{An infinite cycle-blow-up family strictly beyond \textup{(HK)}}

For $a\geq1$, let $B_a=C_6[\overline K_a]$ be the balanced independent-set
blow-up of the six-cycle: replace each cycle vertex by an independent part
of size $a$, and join two parts completely exactly when their cycle
vertices are adjacent.  This is not a complete multipartite graph.

\begin{theorem}[Cycle-blow-up master nonpositivity beyond \textup{(HK)}]
\label{thm:psi-c6-blowup}
For every integer $a\geq1$ and every $0<q\leq1/4$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{y\in\mathbb R^{6a},\ h\in\one^{\perp_D}}\Psi(y,h)=0
 \qquad\text{on }B_a.
 \label{eq:psi-c6-blowup-sup}
\end{equation}
Nevertheless, \textup{(HK)} fails strictly on every $B_a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathsf H=2(3I-D^{-1}A)^{-1}$, so $M=m_0\mathsf H$.  On functions
constant within each part, $\mathsf H$ is the six-cycle circulant with
eigenvalues
\begin{equation*}
 1,\quad (4/5)^{(2)},\quad(4/7)^{(2)},\quad1/2.
\end{equation*}
On every within-part zero-sum subspace its eigenvalue is $2/3$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_q
 =m_0^2\mathsf H(I-\mathsf H)(I-m_0\mathsf H)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:psi-c6-blowup-c}
\end{equation}
Put $r=q^2$ and
$\mathcal D(r)=140(1+r)(1+4r)(3+4r)$.  Spectral inversion of the six-cycle
gives the first row $c_0,\ldots,c_5$ of the part-level kernel, with
$c_5=c_1$, $c_4=c_2$, and
\begin{align}
 c_0&=\frac{(1-r)^2(656r^2+848r+227)}{\mathcal D(r)},\notag\\
 c_1&=-\frac{(1-r)^2(272r^2+256r+19)}{\mathcal D(r)},\notag\\
 c_2&=-\frac{(1-r)^2(48r^2+144r+61)}{\mathcal D(r)},\notag\\
 c_3&=-\frac{(1-r)^2(16r^2+48r+67)}{\mathcal D(r)}.
 \label{eq:psi-c6-circulant-entries}
\end{align}
Thus every part-level off-diagonal entry is strictly negative for
$0<r<1$.  The within-part eigenvalue is
\begin{equation*}
 c_{\rm in}=\frac{2(1-r)^2}{3(1+2r)},
\end{equation*}
and exact subtraction gives
\begin{equation}
 c_{\rm in}-c_0
 =\frac{(1-r)^2(544r^3+1904r^2+1414r+159)}
 {420(1+r)(1+2r)(1+4r)(3+4r)}>0.
 \label{eq:psi-c6-within-margin}
\end{equation}
For vertices in different parts at cyclic distance $d$, the full kernel
entry is $c_d/a<0$.  For distinct vertices in the same part it is
$(c_0-c_{\rm in})/a<0$.  Hence \textup{(CL)} holds strictly.

The normalized-Laplacian spectrum contains the six-cycle part modes and
the within-part eigenvalue $1$, so $\mu_2=1/2$.  Therefore
$q\leq1/4$ is exactly the required high-gap range, and
\cref{thm:psi-cl} proves \cref{eq:psi-c6-blowup-sup}; equality occurs at
$y=h=0$.

To separate this result from the earlier kernel route, let
$N=(3I-D^{-1}A)^{-1}$.  On the base six-cycle,
$(N^2)_{0,1}=1737/39200$ for adjacent parts.  The corresponding entry on
$B_a$ is smaller by $a$, while $\mathsf v_G=12a^2$ and $d_j=2a$.
Thus the left-to-right ratio in \cref{eq:psi-hk-n} is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{4\mathsf v_G(N^2)_{ij}}{d_j}
 =24\frac{1737}{39200}
 =\frac{5211}{4900}>1.
 \label{eq:psi-c6-hk-failure}
\end{equation}
So \textup{(HK)} fails on every member of the family even though the exact
master form is nonpositive.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An exact cycle beyond \textup{(CL)}.}
Condition \textup{(CL)} is not universal even within cycles.  On $C_{10}$
at $q=1/20$, exact rational inversion gives
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal C_q)_{0,1}
 =\frac{6998345705403423}{396849260156782400}>0.
 \label{eq:psi-c10-cl-failure}
\end{equation}
Thus the simple off-diagonal sign proof cannot cover this cycle.  The new
edgewise condition nevertheless pays its two positive adjacent entries.

\begin{theorem}[Ten-cycle blow-ups beyond \textup{(CL)}]
\label{thm:psi-c10-edgewise}
For every integer $a\geq1$, let
$B_a^{(10)}=C_{10}[\overline K_a]$ be the balanced independent-set blow-up
of the ten-cycle.  For every
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{25}\leq q\leq\frac7{100},
 \label{eq:psi-c10-edgewise-q-window}
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{y\in\mathbb R^{10a},\ h\in\one^{\perp_D}}\Psi(y,h)=0.
 \label{eq:psi-c10-edgewise-sup}
\end{equation}
This conclusion holds even though \textup{(CL)} fails throughout the same
parameter window on every member.  In particular, $q=1/20$ gives the exact
calibration in \cref{eq:psi-c10-cl-failure}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The normalized-Laplacian gap is
$\mu_2=1-\cos(\pi/5)=(3-\sqrt5)/4$.  Since $\sqrt5<9/4$,
$\mu_2>3/16>7/50\geq2q$, so
\cref{eq:psi-hgap} holds; the additional within-part modes have normalized-
Laplacian eigenvalue one.

Put $r=q^2$.  On the part-constant subspace the three kernels are the base
$C_{10}$ circulants.  Diagonalizing the cycle over
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)(r)$ and pairing conjugate Fourier modes makes each entry a
rational function in $r$.  Cancel its exact polynomial gcd and substitute
\[
 r=\frac1{625}
 +t\left(\frac{49}{10000}-\frac1{625}\right),
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
The following table records the exact Bernstein certificate.  In every row,
all Bernstein coefficients of the signed numerator and denominator are
strictly positive.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
quantity & numerator degree & denominator degree & certified sign\\
\midrule
$\mathcal A_q(0,d)$, $1\leq d\leq5$ & $6$ & $5$ & negative\\
$\mathcal B_q(0,d)$, $1\leq d\leq5$ & $7$ & $5$ & negative\\
$\mathcal C_q(0,1)$ & $6$ & $5$ & positive\\
$\mathcal C_q(0,d)$, $2\leq d\leq5$ & $6$ & $5$ & negative\\
within-part $\mathcal A_q,\mathcal B_q,\mathcal C_q$
 & $7,8,7$ & $6,6,6$ & negative\\
$\mathcal A_q(0,1)\mathcal B_q(0,1)-\mathcal C_q(0,1)^2$
 & $8$ & $5$ & positive\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus the only positive off-diagonal entries of $\mathcal C_q$ are the two
adjacent entries, while every off-diagonal entry of $\mathcal A_q$ and
$\mathcal B_q$ is negative.  The last row is exactly the adjacent edgewise
Young margin.  At the interior calibration $q=1/20$, the common degree factor
cancels and the exact edgewise ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(\mathcal C_q)_{0,1}^2}
 {(-\mathcal A_q{}_{0,1})(-\mathcal B_q{}_{0,1})}
 =\frac{9190540374100260057432724000}
 {35964609239043602890432954263}<1.
 \label{eq:psi-c10-edgewise-ratio}
\end{equation}
On a within-part zero-sum mode the resolvent eigenvalue is $m_0(2/3)$.
Subtracting the corresponding scalar kernel eigenvalue from the base diagonal
entry at $q=1/20$ gives the three calibration values
\begin{equation*}
 -\frac{8569107687113450384299}{189685215671218537987200},\quad
 -\frac{5779278789418042112799}{53177800861008841600000},\quad
 -\frac{1662576823382931953}{26588900430504420800}
\end{equation*}
for $\mathcal A_q,\mathcal B_q,\mathcal C_q$, respectively.  They are all
strictly negative, consistently with the interval certificate.  Every
cross-part entry is the corresponding base entry divided by $a$, while every
within-part off-diagonal entry is its certified value divided by $a$.  The
positive adjacent ratio is therefore unchanged, and
\cref{eq:psi-edgewise-young-condition} holds for every $a$ throughout
\eqref{eq:psi-c10-edgewise-q-window}.
Consequently
\cref{thm:psi-edgewise-young} proves nonpositivity.  Equality occurs at
$y=h=0$.  The adjacent cross entry stays positive after division by $a$, so
\textup{(CL)} fails on every member.
\end{proof}

\section{Current boundary}

Three distinct routes are now analytic: positive association closes every
complete multipartite graph, the eliminated-$h$ clipped-Laplacian route
closes every balanced independent-set blow-up of $C_6$ despite strict
\textup{(HK)} failure, and edgewise Young payment closes the exact
$C_{10}[\overline K_a]$ family throughout
$1/25\leq q\leq7/100$ despite failure of \textup{(CL)}.  The next falsifiable
target is a quotient-varying edgewise certificate, payment of positive cross
entries not covered by \cref{eq:psi-edgewise-young-condition}, or an exact
admissible positive witness.  A one-step
inequality implies Lyapunov absorption only for the exact positive-face
recurrence above.  It does not by itself charge graph discovery, clipping,
shifted inverse work, proper-face changes, finite precision, or final PPR
output.
