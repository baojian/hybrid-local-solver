\section{Scope and status}

This standalone note records a proposed Accelerated Evolving Set Process with
local proximal coordinate descent (AESP-CD) for regularized PageRank.  It
separates the shared problem definition, proved implementation facts, and the
remaining outer-locality target.  The coordinate-level theory is closed: the
minimum-norm KKT mass contracts under every exact proximal coordinate update,
including sign changes and zero-coordinate clipping, and the KKT diagnostic
certifies degree-normalized solution error.  These statements give a rigorous
local inner oracle for composite AESP.  They do not bound the KKT mass of an
arbitrary extrapolated Catalyst center.  A relative-accuracy continuation
below proves that, on any fixed certified envelope of degree volume $V$, the
inner work is only $\softO(V)$ per outer stage and hence
$\softO(V/\sqrt\alpha)$ overall.  Taking the envelope to be the optimal RPPR
support gives $V\leq1/\rho$; discovering such an envelope locally without an
oracle remains open.  The same heap implementation does, however, admit an
oracle-free bound in terms of the maximum volume it actually explores; this
reduces the remaining universal question to controlling that explored volume.
For any certified lower center, a comparison argument controls it
automatically: the full inner trajectory stays in the optimal RPPR support
and costs $\softO(1/\rho)$.  The unresolved step is therefore strictly outer:
retaining accelerated continuation while the centers are kept order-safe.  A
safeguarded extrapolation analyzed below makes the iterates and their supports
monotone and, crucially, makes the cumulative KKT start mass telescope with no
$1/\alpha$ loss.  Its only remaining defect is an explicit positive momentum
correction whose cumulative degree-weighted mass is at most one.  An exact
Moreau-envelope calculation below shows how that correction enters the
Nesterov potential and pays it from monotone Euclidean progress, but also
exhibits a three-vertex path on which the pointwise potential defect is
positive.  A multiplicative ledger nevertheless bounds the inflation of the
classical potential by two per correction and reduces the target to cumulative
log-inflation.  A two-vertex exact family below has a singleton support but a
nonzero correction every other stage, proving that the raw correction count
cannot be charged to support additions or made accuracy-independent.  All of
those corrections have nonpositive potential defect, however, so their
normalized log-inflation is zero.  A positive-log lemma now bounds every
$\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}$ by the normalized remaining error on coordinates
clipped collaterally by the common retraction.  It may overcharge a benign
collateral round, and its clipping amplitude has the exact sharp-collapse
form only for $t\geq2$.  A second exact two-vertex
family shows that the monotone Euclidean log-error cannot provide that
packing with an $\alpha$-independent constant: one harmful collateral charge
is order $q$, whereas the corresponding Euclidean log-progress is only order
$q^2$.  Round 022 then shows that the scalar ledgers alone cannot prove the
needed rate.  Round 023 goes further on one fixed exact $P_4$ RPPR objective:
an invariant cone proves positive-density harmful inflation forever, even
though the primal error contracts geometrically.  Thus a horizon-uniform or
transient-only inflation target is false; the live target is a net exponent
$-qT+\mathcal I_T=-\Omega(qT)$, which remains consistent with that example.
For finite shifted solves, exact identities below expose the additional end
residual in the fixed-row relation, start mass, and next collapse.  A dual
relative-plus-absolute stop controls it at logarithmic extra cost, and a
complete rate-to-gate contract is recorded conditionally on a net packing
bound for the actual finite trajectory.  Round 024 records two sharper
boundaries.  First, the support-indexed lower retraction is discontinuous at
a support entry and has sharp fixed-support local amplification
$1+1/\alpha$, so ambient-norm Lipschitz shadowing cannot transfer an exact
packing theorem to finite solves at accelerated work.  Second, full
corrections are separated on a settled exact face, and every instance whose
optimal face has sufficiently large Dirichlet eigenvalue admits a direct
finite-error accelerated rate and cached resource vector without any
log-inflation theorem.  The latter is an a-posteriori structural branch; the
low-Dirichlet graph-uniform case remains open.  Round 025 shows that the raw
support-entry discontinuity is not realized by the exact safeguarded
recurrence: a new support row cannot control its first retraction.  For a
finite solve, its entire contribution is bounded by the preceding end
residual, and an explicit affordable absolute stop makes the cumulative
entry-dominated inflation an arbitrarily small multiple of $qT$ before the
fresh gate.  On a settled face, the round also identifies the exact
two-stage post-full collapse operator as a spectral high-pass filter.  These
facts reduce the remaining obstruction to persistent-row partial
corrections and their coordinatewise positive-part mixing; they do not prove
the net exponent.  Round 026 gives that remaining controller an exact
windowed square-energy budget on the actual finite recurrence.  The common
truncation and its surviving momentum also contract Dirichlet energy, with
both bounds telescoping through the monotone primal error.  A dense-seed
$K_8$ instance makes the mixed pulse in Round 025 fully reachable: it has one
persistent partial correction, one full correction, two positive inflation
terms before the fresh gate, and then no corrections forever.  This exposes
the still-missing interface sharply.  The new banks are in $Q$-energy, while
the collateral defect is Euclidean; the available norm conversion loses
polynomial factors and does not prove the required net exponent.  The
$K_8$ pulse is absorbed by an additive constant and is not a counterexample
to such an exponent.  No unconditional
graph-uniform accelerated solver is claimed.
Nothing here changes the repository-wide residual decision.

\section{Problem formulation}

\input{../../tex/shared/source_aligned_problem}

The accelerated shifted analysis in this note specializes the shared model to
$\alpha\in(0,1/2)$; its frozen accelerated arm uses $\alpha<1/4$.  The
unshifted zero-start certificate and fallback cover
$1/4\leq\alpha\leq1$, so together the two algorithmic branches retain the
full shared range $0<\alpha\leq1$.  Both solve the same composite objective
$F_\rho$.  The final, note-scoped diagnostic target is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)}{\alpha}\leq\epskkt.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-target}
\end{equation}
This is not identified with $\epsobj$, $\epspg$, or $\epsppr$.

\section{KKT diagnostic}

Define the note-scoped minimum KKT-violation vector
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rho,i}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\nabla_i f(x)+\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},&x_i>0,\\
\nabla_i f(x)-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},&x_i<0,\\
\nabla_i f(x)-\operatorname{clip}
  \bigl(\nabla_i f(x),-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},
                    \alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\bigr),&x_i=0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-vector}
\end{equation}
and the scalar diagnostic
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)
  :=\norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_\rho(x)}_\infty.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
The implication from this diagnostic to a solution-error norm is a required
lemma proved below, but it is not an adopted repository-wide stopping
convention.

\section{AESP outer framework}

At outer iteration $t$, the proposed method approximately minimizes
\begin{equation}
  F_{\rho,t}(z):=F_\rho(z)
  +\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}{2}
       \norm{z-y^{(t-1)}}_2^2,
  \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm A}:=1-2\alpha,
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-subproblem}
\end{equation}
with AESP-scoped momentum
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\mathrm A}
  :=\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha}-\sqrt\alpha}
          {\sqrt{1-\alpha}+\sqrt\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-momentum}
\end{equation}
The inner initialization is $z_t^{(0)}=y^{(t-1)}$.

\section{Inner local proximal coordinate descent}

The smooth part of the inner objective has gradient
\begin{equation}
  \nabla f_t(z)
  =(Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I)z-b
    -\kappa_{\mathrm A}y^{(t-1)}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-inner-gradient}
\end{equation}
Define $\kappa_t(z)$ from \cref{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-vector} by replacing
$\nabla f$ with $\nabla f_t$, and set
\begin{equation}
  C_t(z):=\norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_t(z)}_1.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-mass}
\end{equation}

Let
\begin{equation}
  H_t:=Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I,
  \qquad
  \mu_t:=\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-hessian}
\end{equation}
Then $H_t$ has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t I\preceq H_t,
  \qquad
  H_tD^{1/2}\one=\mu_tD^{1/2}\one.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-properties}
\end{equation}

For coordinate $i$, exact proximal coordinate descent uses
\begin{equation}
  z_i^+
  =\prox_{(\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}/\ell)\abs{\cdot}}
     \left(z_i-\frac{\nabla_i f_t(z)}{\ell}\right),
  \qquad
  \ell=(Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I)_{ii}
      =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-coordinate-update}
\end{equation}
Only coordinate $i$ and its neighbors require gradient updates, so the shared
work charge for this update is $d_i$.

\section{KKT certificate and local monotonicity}

\begin{lemma}[KKT diagnostic certifies inner solution error]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error}
Let $z_t^\star$ be the unique minimizer of $F_{\rho,t}$.  Then every
$z\in\R^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}(z-z_t^\star)}_\infty
  \leq
  \frac{1}{\mu_t}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_t(z)}_\infty.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-inner-error}
\end{equation}
In particular, for the unshifted RPPR objective,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}(x-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty
  \leq \frac{R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)}{\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-error}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose subgradients $\xi\in\partial\norm{D^{1/2}z}_1$ and
$\xi^\star\in\partial\norm{D^{1/2}z_t^\star}_1$ so that
\begin{equation*}
  \kappa_t(z)=H_t(z-z_t^\star)
  +\alpha\rho(\xi-\xi^\star).
\end{equation*}
The coordinate definition of $\kappa_t$ is precisely the minimum-magnitude
valid choice at $z$, while optimality supplies a choice with zero residual at
$z_t^\star$.

Put $e=z-z_t^\star$ and choose $i$ maximizing
$M:=\abs{e_i}/\sqrt{d_i}$.  If $M=0$, the result is immediate.  Otherwise,
the normalized maximum principle and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-properties} give
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)(H_te)_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq \mu_tM.
\end{equation*}
Coordinatewise monotonicity of the weighted absolute-value subdifferential
gives
$e_i(\xi_i-\xi_i^\star)\geq0$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)\kappa_{t,i}(z)}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq \mu_tM,
\end{equation*}
which proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-inner-error}.  Set
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}=0$ for \cref{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-error}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact KKT-mass decrease]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-mass}
For a sequential proximal coordinate update at coordinate $i$,
\begin{equation}
  C_t(z^+)
  \leq C_t(z)
  -\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)},
  \qquad
  \tau_{\mathrm{cd}}
  :=\frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})}
          {1+\alpha+2\kappa_{\mathrm A}}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-mass-target}
\end{equation}
Under $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-2\alpha$, the displayed coefficient is $2/3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\lambda_i:=\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}$ and
$\Delta_i:=z_i^+-z_i$.  Exact scalar proximal minimization makes the new
coordinate KKT violation zero and gives
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{t,i}(z^+)=0,
  \qquad
  \ell\abs{\Delta_i}\leq\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-displacement}
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows directly from the three scalar soft-threshold
cases: the update stays on the same nonzero face, reaches zero, or crosses to
the opposite face.  Equality holds in the first case; thresholding can only
shorten the displacement in the latter two.

For every $j\neq i$, the value $z_j$ is unchanged.  As a function of its
smooth gradient, the minimum KKT violation is either a translate
$g\mapsto g\pm\lambda_j$ on a nonzero face or the soft-threshold map
$g\mapsto g-\operatorname{clip}(g,-\lambda_j,\lambda_j)$ at zero.  Each map
is one-Lipschitz.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \abs{\kappa_{t,j}(z^+)}
 \leq
 \abs{\kappa_{t,j}(z)}+\abs{H_{t,ji}\Delta_i}.
\end{equation*}
Only neighbors of $i$ have a nonzero off-diagonal entry, and
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{j\neq i}\sqrt{d_j}\abs{H_{t,ji}\Delta_i}
 =\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\Delta_i}.
\end{equation*}
Using \cref{eq:aesp-cd-displacement}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 C_t(z^+)
 &\leq C_t(z)-\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}
       +\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\Delta_i}\\
 &\leq C_t(z)
 -\left(1-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\ell}\right)
  \sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}.
\end{align*}
Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 1-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\ell}
 =\frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})}
        {1+\alpha+2\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
 =\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}.
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stieltjes-quadratic inner locality]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 \Psi(z)=\frac12 z^\top Hz-c^\top z
          +\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_j\abs{z_j},
 \qquad \lambda_j\geq0,
\end{equation*}
where $H$ is symmetric positive definite and $H_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$.
Suppose there are $\upsilon\in\R_{++}^n$ and $\mu>0$ such that
$H\upsilon\geq\mu\upsilon$ coordinatewise.  Let $\kappa_\Psi(z)$ be the
minimum-magnitude KKT vector, and define
\begin{equation*}
 C_\upsilon(z):=\sum_j\upsilon_j\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,j}(z)},
 \qquad
 \theta_i:=\frac{(H\upsilon)_i}{H_{ii}\upsilon_i}.
\end{equation*}
Then $0<\mu/H_{ii}\leq\theta_i\leq1$, and an exact proximal update of
coordinate $i$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_\upsilon(z^+)
 \leq C_\upsilon(z)
       -\theta_i\upsilon_i\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-mass}
\end{equation}
If $z_\Psi^\star$ minimizes $\Psi$, then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\operatorname{diag}(\upsilon)^{-1}
             (z-z_\Psi^\star)}_\infty
 \leq\frac1\mu
 \norm{\operatorname{diag}(\upsilon)^{-1}
             \kappa_\Psi(z)}_\infty.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-error}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta_i=z_i^+-z_i$.  Exact scalar proximal minimization gives
$\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z^+)=0$ and
$H_{ii}\abs{\Delta_i}\leq\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}$.  The minimum KKT map
at every untouched coordinate is one-Lipschitz in its smooth gradient, so
\begin{align*}
 C_\upsilon(z^+)
 &\leq C_\upsilon(z)-\upsilon_i\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}
       +\sum_{j\neq i}\upsilon_j\abs{H_{ji}\Delta_i}\\
 &\leq C_\upsilon(z)
       -\left(\upsilon_i
         -\frac{\sum_{j\neq i}\upsilon_j\abs{H_{ji}}}{H_{ii}}\right)
        \abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}\\
 &=C_\upsilon(z)-\theta_i\upsilon_i
        \abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}.
\end{align*}
The last identity uses symmetry and the nonpositive off-diagonal entries.
Those same signs show $\theta_i\leq1$, while
$H\upsilon\geq\mu\upsilon$ gives
$\theta_i\geq\mu/H_{ii}>0$.

For the error certificate, put $e=z-z_\Psi^\star$ and choose $i$ maximizing
$M:=\abs{e_i}/\upsilon_i$.  The weighted maximum principle gives
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)(He)_i}{\upsilon_i}
 \geq M\frac{(H\upsilon)_i}{\upsilon_i}\geq\mu M.
\end{equation*}
Monotonicity of each absolute-value subdifferential makes its contribution
nonnegative after multiplication by $\operatorname{sign}(e_i)$.  Therefore
$\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}/\upsilon_i\geq\mu M$, which proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-error}.
\end{proof}

The PageRank result is the specialization of
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes} with
$H=H_t$, $\upsilon=D^{1/2}\one$, and
$H_t\upsilon=\mu_t\upsilon$.  Since every diagonal entry is $\ell$, one has
$\theta_i=\mu_t/\ell=\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}$ for every coordinate.  Thus the
proved inner-locality mechanism applies not only to RPPR, but also to shifted
RPPR subproblems and, more generally, to separable $\ell_1$ regularization of
any Stieltjes quadratic admitting a positive supersolution.  It does not by
itself control how an outer extrapolation chooses its successive starts.

Thus the composite analogue of the ordinary-PPR residual-mass contraction is
valid for arbitrary signed iterates; no nonnegativity hypothesis is needed.

\section{Inner stopping proposal}

The proposed active set is
\begin{equation}
  S_t^{(k)}
  :=\left\{i:
       \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z^{(k)})}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
       \geq\epsin
     \right\}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-active-set}
\end{equation}
If an oracle does not expose $m=|E|$, the simple-graph bound
$m\leq n(n-1)/2$ is a correctness-preserving but potentially conservative
choice when calibrating an inner objective certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Local inner-oracle work]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work}
Starting from $z^{(0)}$, repeatedly update any coordinate in
$S_t^{(k)}$ until this set is empty.  Then the method terminates with
\begin{align}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 &\leq
 \frac{C_t(z^{(0)})}{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\epsin},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-work}\\
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(z^{(K_t)}-z_t^\star)}_\infty
 &<\frac{\epsin}{\mu_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-output-error}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)
 \leq
 \frac{\epsin C_t(z^{(0)})}{2\mu_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-output-gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently, choosing
\begin{equation}
 \epsin=\frac{2\mu_t\varphi_t}{C_t(z^{(0)})}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-phi-threshold}
\end{equation}
is a valid local oracle for the AESP inner requirement
$F_{\rho,t}(z)-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)\leq\varphi_t$, with work
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 \leq
 \frac{C_t(z^{(0)})^2}
      {2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\mu_t\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every active update has
$\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}\geq\epsin d_i$.
Sum \cref{eq:aesp-cd-mass-target} and use nonnegativity of $C_t$ to prove
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-work}.  At termination, the KKT diagnostic is below
$\epsin$, so \cref{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error} proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-output-error}.

The function $F_{\rho,t}$ is $\mu_t$-strongly convex and
$\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})\in\partial F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)
 \leq\frac{\norm{\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_2^2}{2\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
At termination and by \cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass},
\begin{align*}
 \norm{\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_2^2
 &\leq
 \norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_\infty
 \norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_1\\
 &<\epsin C_t(z^{(K_t)})
 \leq\epsin C_t(z^{(0)}).
\end{align*}
This proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-output-gap}; substitution yields
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work}.
\end{proof}

For the AESP shift $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-2\alpha$, one has
$\mu_t=1-\alpha$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=2/3$.  Thus
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 \leq
 \frac{3C_t(z^{(0)})^2}{4(1-\alpha)\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-specialized-work}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Composite AESP plug-in]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-plugin}
Suppose a composite AESP outer loop accepts its stage-$t$ inner output when
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)\leq\varphi_t
\end{equation*}
and initializes the inner solve at $z_t^{(0)}=y^{(t-1)}$.  If
$C_t(y^{(t-1)})=0$, the initial point is already optimal.  Otherwise,
thresholded exact coordinate descent with
\begin{equation*}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{in},t}
 =\frac{2(1-\alpha)\varphi_t}{C_t(y^{(t-1)})}
\end{equation*}
satisfies every such inner-oracle condition, even when the extrapolated
centers are signed.  Across any $T$ outer stages its total degree work obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_{\mathrm{inner}}(T)
 \leq\frac{3}{4(1-\alpha)}
       \sum_{t=1}^T
       \frac{C_t(y^{(t-1)})^2}{\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work} independently at every stage and sum
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-specialized-work}.  The signed-center claim follows because
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass} made no sign assumption.
\end{proof}

\Cref{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface} is the precise interface supplied to
the outer acceleration proof.  It removes inner correctness and sign
monotonicity from the open problem: a graph-uniform end-to-end theorem now
requires a bound on the displayed cumulative start-mass functional, or an
outer safeguard that bounds it while retaining accelerated convergence.

\section{Outer-locality continuation: relative accuracy}
\label{sec:aesp-cd-relative}

The absolute-gap interface above is valid but too coarse to preserve the
accelerated locality target.  This section first records the obstruction and
then replaces the raw center by the standard composite Catalyst warm start.

\begin{lemma}[The shift cancels at the raw center]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-center-cancel}
At every outer stage,
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_t(y^{(t-1)})=\kappa_\rho(y^{(t-1)}),
 \qquad
 C_t(y^{(t-1)})
 =\norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_\rho(y^{(t-1)})}_1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-center-cancel}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitution into \cref{eq:aesp-cd-inner-gradient} gives
$\nabla f_t(y^{(t-1)})=Qy^{(t-1)}-b=\nabla f(y^{(t-1)})$.
The coordinatewise minimum-magnitude subgradient choices are consequently
identical.
\end{proof}

There is no dimension-free inequality that upper-bounds the right-hand side
of \cref{eq:aesp-cd-center-cancel} by the current objective gap.  Indeed, for
the one-dimensional Stieltjes objective
\begin{equation*}
 \Psi(w)=\frac{h}{2}w^2+\lambda\abs w,
 \qquad h,\lambda>0,
\end{equation*}
the minimizer is zero, while for $w>0$,
\begin{equation*}
 \kappa_\Psi(w)=hw+\lambda,
 \qquad
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_\Psi(w)}^2}{\Psi(w)-\Psi(0)}
 =\frac{(hw+\lambda)^2}{hw^2/2+\lambda w}
 \longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\text{as }w\downarrow0.
\end{equation*}
Thus a proof that merely combines a decaying outer objective gap with the raw
KKT mass cannot close the cumulative bound.  The discontinuity is caused by
a nonzero point approaching an $\ell_1$ kink; it is not a graph-volume issue.

We next give the relative-accuracy repair.  Let $U\subseteq V$ be a fixed
coordinate envelope, put
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal V_U:=\vol(U),
\end{equation*}
and restrict every vector and objective to coordinates in $U$.  A matrix-vector
product with $Q_{UU}$ is charged at most $\mathcal V_U$, since scanning the
adjacency lists of $U$ also exposes every internal entry and cut edge.  Write
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm A}:=1+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=2(1-\alpha),
 \qquad
 \chi_{\mathrm A}:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
               =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm A}:=
 \frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}{2-\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters}
\end{equation}
For a stage center $y\in\R^U$, let $p_U(y)$ minimize the restricted shifted
objective $F_{\rho,t}^U$.  Define the composite proximal warm start
\begin{equation}
 u_U(y):=\prox_{g_{\rho,U}/L_{\mathrm A}}
 \left(y-\frac{\nabla f_{t,U}(y)}{L_{\mathrm A}}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Smoothed start-mass bound]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}
The restricted minimum KKT mass at \cref{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(u_U(y))
 \leq 2(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\mathcal V_U}
       \norm{y-p_U(y)}_2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $u=u_U(y)$ and $H_U=Q_{UU}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I_U$.  Proximal
optimality supplies a subgradient for which the shifted KKT vector at $u$ is
\begin{equation*}
 (L_{\mathrm A}I_U-H_U)(y-u)=(I_U-Q_{UU})(y-u).
\end{equation*}
The minimum KKT vector can only have smaller coordinate magnitudes.  The
matrix $I-Q$ is entrywise nonnegative and, with
$v=D^{1/2}\one$, satisfies $(I-Q)v=(1-\alpha)v$.  Restriction to $U$ can
only decrease its weighted column sums, so
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(u)
 \leq(1-\alpha)\norm{D_U^{1/2}(y-u)}_1
 \leq(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\mathcal V_U}\norm{y-u}_2.
\end{equation*}
The forward map in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start} is nonexpansive because
$0\preceq I_U-H_U/L_{\mathrm A}\preceq I_U$, and the proximal map is
nonexpansive.  Since $p_U(y)$ is a fixed point of their composition,
$\norm{u-p_U(y)}_2\leq\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.  The triangle inequality now gives
$\norm{y-u}_2\leq2\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Greedy KKT-mass contraction in degree work]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}
Starting from any $z^{(0)}\in\R^U$, choose at each update
\begin{equation*}
 i_k\in\argmax_{i\in U}
       \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,U,i}(z^{(k)})}}{\sqrt{d_i}}.
\end{equation*}
If $W_K:=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}d_{i_k}$, then
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(z^{(K)})
 \leq C_{t,U}(z^{(0)})
       \exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}W_K}{\mathcal V_U}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The principal system satisfies
$H_UD_U^{1/2}\one\geq\mu_tD_U^{1/2}\one$.  Hence
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes} gives at least the PageRank contraction
$\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=\mu_t/\ell$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 \max_{i\in U}
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,U,i}(z)}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq\frac{C_{t,U}(z)}{\mathcal V_U}.
\end{equation*}
The chosen update therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(z^+)
 \leq C_{t,U}(z)
       \left(1-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}d_i}{\mathcal V_U}\right)
 \leq C_{t,U}(z)
       \exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}d_i}{\mathcal V_U}\right).
\end{equation*}
Multiplication over the updates proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The greedy key can be maintained in a heap.  A coordinate update changes the
smooth gradient, and hence the KKT key, only at the updated vertex and its
neighbors.  Its data-structure overhead is therefore logarithmic per touched
edge and is hidden by $\softO$; no repeated scan of all of $U$ is required.

For the relative Catalyst condition at center $y$, require
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rho,t}^U(z)-F_{\rho,t}^{U,\star}
 \leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}}2
             \norm{z-y}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-c2}
\end{equation}
The following theorem makes this condition locally certifiable and bounds
its work.

\begin{theorem}[Local relative-accuracy oracle]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle}
Initialize at $u_U(y)$ and run the greedy updates of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear} until
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(z)^2
 \leq\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t
       \norm{z-y}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop}
\end{equation}
Then \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2} holds.  Including the proximal warm start, the
degree work of the stage is at most
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_U\left[
 2+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
 \log_+\left(
  \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_U}
       {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
 \right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strong-convexity subgradient bound gives, for every $z$,
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}^U(z)-F_{\rho,t}^{U,\star}
 \leq\frac{\norm{\kappa_{t,U}(z)}_2^2}{2\mu_t}
 \leq\frac{C_{t,U}(z)^2}{2\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} implies
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2}.

Put $r=\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.  If $r=0$, the proximal warm start is already
optimal.  Otherwise, it is sufficient to reduce the KKT mass until
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(z)^2
 \leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}{4}r^2.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the preceding gap bound and strong convexity then give
$\norm{z-p_U(y)}_2\leq r/2$, so
$\norm{z-y}_2\geq r/2$ and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} follows.
By \cref{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}, the squared ratio between the initial
mass and this sufficient target is at most
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_U}
      {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
Now apply \cref{eq:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}.  Crossing the target can overshoot
the corresponding work budget by at most one coordinate cost, which is at
most $\mathcal V_U$.  The proximal warm start costs another
$\mathcal V_U$, yielding \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}.
\end{proof}

The fixed envelope in \cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} is useful for a
graph-uniform corollary, but it is not required to implement the method.  For
a full-graph stage, let $E_t$ be the union of the supports of the center and
proximal displacement, every coordinate whose KKT
key becomes nonzero, and every coordinate updated before
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} holds.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}:=\vol(E_t),
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}
 :=\max_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{theorem}[Oracle-free explored-volume bound]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-explored-volume}
Run full-graph strongly convex Catalyst with parameters
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters}.  At every stage, compute the proximal
warm start locally, maintain all nonzero normalized KKT keys in a heap, run
the greedy coordinate updates, and stop by
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop}.  Then each inner output satisfies the full
relative condition \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2}, and the total degree work through
$T$ stages is at most
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^T \mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
 \left[
  3+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
  \log_+\left(
   \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}}
        {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
  \right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work}
\end{equation}
Consequently, reaching objective gap $\epsobj$ costs
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(
   \frac{\mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}}{\sqrt\alpha}
   \log\frac1{\epsobj}
 \right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-explored-rate}
\end{equation}
No support oracle or graph-wide scan is used in this trajectory-dependent
statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a sparse $z$ and center $y$, a zero coordinate outside
$\supp(z)\cup N(\supp(z))\cup\supp(y)\cup\supp(b)$ has zero smooth gradient
and hence zero minimum KKT key.  Initially, the proximal displacement is
found by scanning the center support and its incident edges; entries of the
sparse source require only scalar accesses unless they enter the displacement.
Thereafter an exact coordinate update changes KKT keys only at the
updated coordinate and its neighbors.  Thus the heap contains every nonzero
key and its maximum is the global maximum; newly exposed coordinates are
discovered while scanning already charged incident edges.

The proof of \cref{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass} on the full graph uses
Cauchy--Schwarz only on the support of the proximal displacement, whose
volume is at most $\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}$.  At every later update, the
support of the KKT mass also has volume at most
$\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}$.  Hence the proofs of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear,thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} apply
retrospectively with this explored volume.  Two local scans initialize the
proximal point and its heap keys, and the last coordinate update may overshoot
the mass budget by one coordinate cost; these give the constant three in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work}.

Every stage satisfies the full relative Catalyst condition, so the outer
rate used in \cref{cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope} applies without
restriction.  Bound every explored volume in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work} by
$\mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}$ and use the
$\bigO(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsobj))$ stage count.
\end{proof}

The explored-volume theorem becomes graph-uniform as soon as the outer center
is an order-safe lower point.  This is the exact sense in which the solved
inner-locality problem applies to the safe-envelope program.  Define the
one-sided RPPR residual
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x)
 :=b-Qx-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one.
\end{equation*}
Call $\ell$ a \emph{lower certificate} if $\ell\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)\geq0
 \qquad\text{for every }i\in\supp(\ell).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate}
\end{equation}
Here $[v]_-:=\max\{-v,0\}$ coordinatewise.

\begin{lemma}[Local lower retraction]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}
For any signed finite-support trial point $a$, put $A:=\supp(a)$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(a):=\frac1\alpha
  \norm{D_A^{-1/2}
    [\zeta_{\rho,A}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)]_-}_\infty,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(a):=[a-\Delta(a)D^{1/2}\one]_+.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal L(a)$ is a lower certificate and hence
$0\leq\mathcal L(a)\leq x^\star(\rho)$.  Constructing it requires only the
trial support and its incident edges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $w=D^{1/2}\one$, $\Delta=\Delta(a)$, and
$\ell=\mathcal L(a)$.  If $\ell_i>0$, then $i\in A$,
$\ell_i=a_i-\Delta w_i$, and $\ell_j\geq a_j-\Delta w_j$ for every $j$.
The off-diagonal signs of $Q$ and $Qw=\alpha w$ give
\begin{align*}
 (Q\ell)_i
 &\leq(Q(a-\Delta w))_i
   =(Qa)_i-\alpha\Delta w_i,\\
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)
 &\geq\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)
       +\alpha\Delta w_i\geq0.
\end{align*}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate} holds.  Stieltjes obstacle
comparison with the RPPR KKT system gives the last claim.  Evaluating
$\zeta_{\rho,A}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)$ scans only adjacency lists in $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Safe-center inner locality]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}
Let $\ell$ satisfy \cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate}, and let
$S^\star(\rho):=\supp(x^\star(\rho))$.  Consider the full-graph shifted
subproblem centered at $\ell$, and write $p(\ell)$ for its minimizer.  Then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\ell\leq p(\ell)\leq x^\star(\rho),
 \qquad
 \supp(p(\ell))\subseteq S^\star(\rho).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the proximal warm start and every subsequent greedy exact
coordinate iterate satisfy the same sandwich with $p(\ell)$ in place of
$x^\star(\rho)$.  No coordinate outside $S^\star(\rho)$ ever has a nonzero
minimum KKT key.  Hence the full-graph heap implementation needs no support
oracle and reaches the relative stopping rule using degree work at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac1\rho\left[
 2+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
 \log_+\left(
  \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2}
       {\rho\,\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
 \right)\right],
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-center-work}
\end{equation}
apart from constant-time accesses to the sparse source.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the nonnegative orthant, the RPPR objective is the obstacle quadratic with
linear term $c_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one$.  The standard Stieltjes
comparison principle applied to
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate} gives $\ell\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
The shifted minimizer is nonnegative: replacing a vector by its coordinatewise
absolute value cannot increase the Stieltjes quadratic, the weighted
$\ell_1$ term, or its squared distance to $\ell\geq0$.

Put $H:=Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I$ and
$d:=c_\rho+\kappa_{\mathrm A}\ell$.  The shifted obstacle residual is
$R(z):=d-Hz$, and
$R(\ell)=\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)$.  Thus $\ell$ is a subsolution on
its positive coordinates.  Conversely, the RPPR KKT conditions and
$x^\star(\rho)\geq\ell$ give
\begin{equation*}
 Hx^\star(\rho)-d
 =Qx^\star(\rho)-c_\rho
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x^\star(\rho)-\ell)\geq0,
\end{equation*}
so $x^\star(\rho)$ is a supersolution of the shifted obstacle problem.
Stieltjes comparison between the subsolution, its solution, and the
supersolution proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-sandwich}.

At a positive coordinate of $\ell$, the lower-certificate residual is
nonnegative.  At a zero coordinate, $Q\ell-b\leq0$ because $Q$ has
nonpositive off-diagonal entries and $b\geq0$.  The composite proximal warm
start therefore has the explicit orthant form
\begin{equation*}
 u=\ell+\frac1{L_{\mathrm A}}
       [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)]_+\geq\ell.
\end{equation*}
The forward--backward map is order preserving because
$I-H/L_{\mathrm A}$ is entrywise nonnegative.  Since $p(\ell)$ is its fixed
point, $u\leq p(\ell)$.  Furthermore, for every $i\in\supp(u)$,
\begin{equation*}
 R_i(u)
 =\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)
  -\frac1{L_{\mathrm A}}
    \sum_j H_{ij}[\zeta_{\rho,j}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)]_+
 \geq0,
\end{equation*}
using $H_{ii}\leq L_{\mathrm A}$ and $H_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$.
Thus $u$ is again a lower subsolution.

From any lower subsolution $z\leq p(\ell)$, a nonzero minimum KKT key points
in the increasing direction.  Its exact coordinate update increases that
coordinate to the one-dimensional obstacle minimizer, preserves the lower
subsolution inequalities, and remains below $p(\ell)$ by coordinatewise
Stieltjes comparison.  Induction proves the claimed sandwich for the entire
greedy trajectory.

Finally take $i\notin S^\star(\rho)$.  Along this trajectory
$z_i=p_i(\ell)=\ell_i=0$, and
\begin{equation*}
 R_i(z)-R_i(p(\ell))
 =-\sum_{j\neq i}H_{ij}(z_j-p_j(\ell))\leq0.
\end{equation*}
Obstacle optimality gives $R_i(p(\ell))\leq0$, hence $R_i(z)\leq0$.
The opposite signed $\ell_1$ direction cannot activate either, because at
such a zero coordinate the smooth gradient is nonpositive.  Therefore its
minimum KKT key is zero.  All displacement and update scans are confined to
$S^\star(\rho)$.  Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} retrospectively
with $U=S^\star(\rho)$ and use
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$ to obtain
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-center-work}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Safeguarded outer continuation and a telescoping start-mass ledger}

The lower retraction can be combined with extrapolation without losing the
safe support invariant.  The following exact construction isolates what this
does, and does not, yet prove about acceleration.  For vectors, $a\vee b$
denotes the componentwise maximum, and
$\mathfrak m(a):=\norm{D^{1/2}a}_1$ when $a\geq0$.

\begin{lemma}[Lattice closure of lower certificates]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-lower-max}
If $a$ and $c$ are lower certificates, then $a\vee c$ is a lower
certificate and satisfies $a\vee c\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $q=a\vee c$.  If $q_i>0$, choose $h\in\{a,c\}$ with $q_i=h_i$.
Since $q_j\geq h_j$ for every $j$ and $Q_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$,
$(Qq)_i\leq(Qh)_i$.  Therefore
$\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(q)\geq
\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(h)\geq0$.  The comparison conclusion in
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-lower-retraction} gives $q\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{proof}

Initialize $x_{-1}=x_0=0$ and $\ell_{-1}=0$.  For $t\geq0$, define the
exact safeguarded recurrence
\begin{align}
 d_t&:=x_t-x_{t-1},
 &y_t&:=x_t+\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,\notag\\
 \ell_t&:=x_t\vee\mathcal L(y_t),
 &x_{t+1}&:=p(\ell_t),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}
\end{align}
where $p(\ell_t)$ is the exact shifted minimizer from
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}.  Write
\begin{equation}
 r_t:=y_t-\ell_t,
 \qquad
 s_t:=x_t-\ell_{t-1} \quad(t\geq1),
 \qquad s_0:=0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-defects}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Safe outer ledger]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-safe-outer-ledger}
The recurrence \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer} is graph-local and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x_{t-1}\leq\ell_{t-1}\leq x_t\leq\ell_t
 \leq x_{t+1}\leq x^\star(\rho).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-chain}
\end{equation}
Consequently every support in the recurrence is contained in
$S^\star(\rho)$ and has volume at most $1/\rho$.

More strongly, let $C_{t+1}(x_t)$ denote the minimum KKT mass at $x_t$ for
the shifted subproblem centered at $\ell_t$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 C_{t+1}(x_t)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mathfrak m(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1}),
 \qquad t\geq1,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-exact}
\end{equation}
while $C_1(x_0)\leq\alpha$.  Hence, for every $T\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=0}^T C_{t+1}(x_t)
 \leq\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t=0}^T C_{t+1}(x_t)^2\leq(1-\alpha)^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-ledger}
\end{equation}
Thus the cumulative stage-start mass itself has no volume factor and no
$1/\alpha$ penalty.

Finally, if $\Delta_t:=\Delta(y_t)$ is the retraction magnitude in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}, then
\begin{align}
 r_t
 &=\min\{\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one\}
 \quad\text{componentwise},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction}\\
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(r_t)&\leq\beta_{\mathrm A},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger}\\
 \alpha\Delta_t
 &=\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 \bigl[\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}
       -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t\bigr]_+}_\infty,
 \qquad A_t:=\supp(x_t),\quad t\geq2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse}
\end{align}
In particular, after the first extrapolation a correction can be triggered
only where the current proximal displacement has fallen below
$\beta_{\mathrm A}/(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})<1/2$ of the preceding one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t=0$, one has $x_{-1}=\ell_{-1}=x_0=\ell_0=0$, and
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center} gives $0\leq x_1\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
Now let $t\geq1$ and suppose inductively that $\ell_{t-1}$ is a lower
certificate.  By
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center},
$\ell_{t-1}\leq x_t\leq x^\star(\rho)$.  On every positive coordinate of
$x_t$, shifted optimality gives
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x_{t,i}-\ell_{t-1,i})\geq0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack}
\end{equation}
Thus $x_t$ is a lower certificate.  Both $\mathcal L(y_t)$ and $x_t$ are
lower certificates, so \cref{lem:aesp-cd-lower-max} proves the same statement
for $\ell_t$.  A second application of
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center} yields \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-chain}.
The support-volume conclusion follows from
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$.  All operations used to construct
$\mathcal L(y_t)$ scan only the current support and its incident edges, and
the shifted solve is local by \cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}.

The formula for $\ell_t$ and $d_t\geq0$ give
\begin{equation*}
 \ell_t=x_t+
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t-\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one]_+.
\end{equation*}
In particular, $\supp(\ell_t)=\supp(x_t)$.  At a positive coordinate of
$x_t$, subtracting the two shifted centers in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack} shows that the KKT vector for the next
subproblem at $x_t$ is
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}(\ell_{t-1}-\ell_t)$.  At a zero coordinate,
$\ell_{t-1,i}=\ell_{t,i}=0$.  Shifted obstacle optimality gives
$Qx_t-b+\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one\geq0$ there, while the Stieltjes signs and
$b\geq0$ give $Qx_t-b\leq0$; hence the signed $\ell_1$ KKT key is zero.
This proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-exact} for $t\geq1$.  At the initial
point the shift cancels and the seed normalization gives
$C_1(x_0)=C_\rho(0)\leq\alpha$.

The center increments are nonnegative and telescope.  Moreover
$\mathfrak m(x^\star(\rho))\leq1$: multiplying its active KKT equations by
$D^{1/2}\one$ and using
$Q_{AA}D_A^{1/2}\one\geq\alpha D_A^{1/2}\one$ gives
$\alpha\mathfrak m(x^\star(\rho))
\leq(D_A^{1/2}\one)^Tb_A\leq\alpha$.
It follows that
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1})
 =\mathfrak m(\ell_T)\leq1.
\end{equation*}
Together with the initial mass bound, this proves the first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-ledger}; the second follows from
$\sum_t a_t^2\leq(\sum_t a_t)^2$ for $a_t\geq0$.

Subtracting the displayed formula for $\ell_t$ from
$y_t=x_t+\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction}.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(r_t)
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}\sum_{t=0}^T\mathfrak m(d_t)
 =\beta_{\mathrm A}\mathfrak m(x_T)\leq\beta_{\mathrm A},
\end{equation*}
which is \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger}.

It remains to identify when a correction occurs after the initial shifted
solve.  Since the residual map is affine and the coefficients of $y_t$ sum
to one,
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)
 =(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
  -\beta_{\mathrm A}
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_{t-1}).
\end{equation*}
On a coordinate positive in both iterates,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack} identifies the two residuals with
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_t$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}$.  If the coordinate
is new at time $t\geq2$, shifted obstacle optimality at time $t-1$ gives a
nonpositive old residual, so neither side of the corresponding negative-part
identity is positive.  Hence, on $A_t$,
\begin{equation*}
 [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_-
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}
   [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}
     -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+.
\end{equation*}
Substitution in the definition of $\Delta_t$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse} and the final claim.
\end{proof}

The theorem removes the cumulative start-mass obstruction in a stronger
form than a per-stage volume estimate: the exact warm-start masses telescope
over an arbitrarily long run.  It also suppresses the tree-wave mechanism,
because supports cannot deactivate.  What it does not prove is that replacing
the Catalyst center $y_t$ by $\ell_t=y_t-r_t$ preserves the classical
accelerated estimate.  Exact shifted optimality can be written as
\begin{equation}
 0\in\partial F_\rho(x_{t+1})
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x_{t+1}-y_t)
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}r_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-perturbed-app}
\end{equation}

The exact effect on the accelerated potential can be exposed through the
Moreau envelope
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E(a):=\min_z\left\{
 F_\rho(z)+\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}2\norm{z-a}_2^2
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-moreau-envelope}
\end{equation}
Write $p(a)$ for the unique minimizer in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-moreau-envelope}.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 q:=\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\mathcal E}:=\kappa_{\mathrm A}\chi_{\mathrm A}
                  =\kappa_{\mathrm A}q^2.
\end{equation*}
The envelope is $\kappa_{\mathrm A}$-smooth and
$\mu_{\mathcal E}$-strongly convex, with minimizer $x^\star(\rho)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact estimate-sequence correction defect]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-estimate-defect}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence, define
\begin{align}
 z_t&:=x_t+\left(\frac1q-1\right)d_t,
 &\widetilde z_t
 &:=z_t-\frac{1+q}{q}r_t,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states}\\
 \Phi_t&:=\mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
       +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
        \norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2,
 &\mathfrak D_t
 &:=\norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
   -\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-potential-defect}
\end{align}
Then the safe center and the next momentum state obey the exact Nesterov
relations
\begin{align}
 \ell_t&=\frac{x_t+q\widetilde z_t}{1+q},
 \qquad
 x_{t+1}=\ell_t-\frac1{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
                    \nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov}\\
 z_{t+1}
 &=(1-q)\widetilde z_t+q\ell_t
   -\frac q{\mu_{\mathcal E}}\nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{t+1}
 \leq(1-q)\Phi_t
   +\frac{(1-q)\mu_{\mathcal E}}2\mathfrak D_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $t\geq1$, with $e_t:=x^\star-x_t$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_t
 \leq\frac{1-q}{q}
 \left(\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2-\norm{e_t}_2^2\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope}
\end{equation}
Thus the correction defect is paid by monotone Euclidean progress, but the
black-box payment carries one factor $1/q=\Theta(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ too many for
an immediate accelerated contraction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The standard resolvent identities for an $\alpha$-strongly convex function
give
\begin{equation*}
 \nabla\mathcal E(a)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}(a-p(a)),
 \qquad
 L_{\mathcal E}\leq\kappa_{\mathrm A},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\mathcal E}
 =\frac{\alpha\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
        {\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}.
\end{equation*}
Since
$\beta_{\mathrm A}=(1-q)/(1+q)$, substitution of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states} gives the first identity in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov}.  The second is the definition of the
exact shifted minimizer.  Using
$d_{t+1}=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t-r_t
-\nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t)/\kappa_{\mathrm A}$ in the definition of
$z_{t+1}$ gives \cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}.

Equations
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov,eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}
are exactly one strongly convex Nesterov step initialized by
$(x_t,\widetilde z_t)$.  Combining smooth descent from $\ell_t$, convexity
between $\ell_t$ and $x_t$, and strong convexity between $\ell_t$ and
$x^\star$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal E(x_{t+1})-\mathcal E(x^\star)
 +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2\norm{z_{t+1}-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq(1-q)\left[
 \mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
 +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
       \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \right].
\end{equation*}
Adding and subtracting the uncorrected momentum distance proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}.

For the defect bound, put
\begin{equation*}
 a:=\frac{1-q}{q},
 \qquad
 h_t:=\frac{1+q}{q}r_t.
\end{equation*}
By \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction},
$0\leq h_t\leq a d_t$ coordinatewise.  Since
$z_t-x^\star=-e_t+ad_t$, direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak D_t
 &=\norm{h_t}_2^2+2\ip{e_t}{h_t}
    -2a\ip{d_t}{h_t}\\
 &\leq2a\ip{e_t}{d_t}\\
 &\leq a\left(\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2-\norm{e_t}_2^2\right),
\end{align*}
where the first inequality uses $0\leq h_t\leq ad_t$ and $e_t\geq0$, and the
second uses $e_{t-1}=e_t+d_t$.  This is
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope}.
\end{proof}

The additive form \cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} is not the only
useful ledger.  Because the safeguard moves each momentum coordinate toward
the current monotone iterate, its loss can also be recorded multiplicatively.

\begin{lemma}[Multiplicative correction ledger]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-multiplicative-ledger}
Whenever $\Phi_t>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_t
 :=1+\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}\mathfrak D_t}{2\Phi_t}
 =\frac{
   \mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
   +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
      \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2}
  {\Phi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-correction-inflation}
\end{equation}
Then $0\leq\gamma_t\leq2$, and $\gamma_t=1$ whenever $r_t=0$.
Consequently, for every $T\geq1$ before termination,
\begin{align}
 \Phi_T
 &\leq(1-q)^T\Phi_0\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_t,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-product-ledger}\\
 &\leq2^{N_T}(1-q)^T\Phi_0,
 \qquad
 N_T:=\card{\{0\leq t<T:r_t\neq0\}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-count-ledger}
\end{align}
More precisely, with
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-log-inflation}
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_T\leq\exp(-qT+\mathcal I_T)\Phi_0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-log-ledger}
\end{equation}
Thus any graph-uniform estimate
$\mathcal I_T=\softO(\log(\Phi_0/\epsobj))$ retains an
$\softO(q^{-1}\log(\Phi_0/\epsobj))$ outer iteration bound.  The cruder
correction count $N_T$ may replace $\mathcal I_T$ when it has the same type of
bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The safe chain and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction} imply
\begin{equation*}
 x_t\leq\widetilde z_t\leq z_t
 \qquad\text{coordinatewise}.
\end{equation*}
For a scalar, the squared distance to $x_i^\star$ is convex, so its maximum
on the interval $[x_{t,i},z_{t,i}]$ occurs at an endpoint.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq\norm{x_t-x^\star}_2^2
     +\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2.
\end{equation*}
Strong convexity of $\mathcal E$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
 \norm{x_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq\mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star).
\end{equation*}
Substitution in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-correction-inflation} proves
$0\leq\gamma_t\leq2$.  If $r_t=0$, then
$\widetilde z_t=z_t$ and $\gamma_t=1$.  Rewriting
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} as
\begin{equation*}
 \Phi_{t+1}\leq(1-q)\gamma_t\Phi_t
\end{equation*}
and iterating proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-product-ledger,eq:aesp-cd-count-ledger}.  Finally,
$\log(1-q)\leq-q$ and
$\log\gamma_t\leq\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}$ give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-log-ledger}.  If some $\gamma_t=0$, the one-step inequality
already gives $\Phi_{t+1}=0$, so the subsequent claims are immediate.
\end{proof}

The positive part of this ledger admits a sharper localization.  A correction
is called \emph{collateral} at coordinate $i$ when the common retraction clips
that coordinate even though its extrapolate is still below the optimum.

\begin{lemma}[Positive inflation is charged only to collateral clipping]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}
For $t\geq1$ before termination, put
\begin{equation*}
 e_t:=x^\star-x_t,
 \qquad
 a_q:=\frac{1-q}{q},
 \qquad
 c_q:=\frac{1+q}{q},
 \qquad
 h_t:=c_qr_t,
\end{equation*}
and define the collaterally clipped set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 :=\{i:r_{t,i}>0\ \text{and}\ y_{t,i}<x_i^\star\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-set}
\end{equation}
The note-scoped normalized collateral charge is
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 :=\frac{
  \displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}}
  \min\{e_{t,i}^2,\,2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}\}}
 {\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}
 \leq\mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 \leq
 \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}}e_{t,i}^2}
      {\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2}
 \leq1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-positive-only-charge}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T
 \leq\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}
\end{equation}
For $t\geq2$, every quantity $h_{t,i}$ in the numerator has the exact
collapse representation
\begin{equation}
 h_{t,i}
 =\min\left\{
 a_qd_{t,i},
 \frac{c_q\kappa_{\mathrm A}}{\alpha}\sqrt{d_i}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+}_\infty
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-collapse}
\end{equation}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative} upper-bounds only the positive
log term using collateral coordinates, rather than counting all nonzero
corrections.  The analytical charge can nevertheless be positive on a benign
collateral round; for $t\geq2$ its clipping amplitude has the exact
sharp-collapse form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The definitions of $z_t$ and $\widetilde z_t$ give the coordinate
decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_t
 =\sum_i\mathfrak D_{t,i},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak D_{t,i}
 =h_{t,i}^2+2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}
   -2a_qd_{t,i}h_{t,i}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-coordinate-defect}
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction},
$0\leq h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$.  If $h_{t,i}>0$ and
$y_{t,i}\geq x_i^\star$, then
$\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{t,i}\geq e_{t,i}$ and
\begin{equation*}
 a_qd_{t,i}
 =c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{t,i}
 \geq c_qe_{t,i}\geq2e_{t,i}.
\end{equation*}
The last comparison is strict when $e_{t,i}>0$; when $e_{t,i}=0$,
$h_{t,i}>0$ implies $a_qd_{t,i}>0$, so in either case
$a_qd_{t,i}>2e_{t,i}$.  It follows from
$h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$ that
$\mathfrak D_{t,i}<0$.  Hence only indices in
$\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}$ can contribute positively.  On every such
index, again using $h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$,
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D_{t,i}
 \leq2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}-h_{t,i}^2
 \leq\min\{e_{t,i}^2,2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}\}.
\end{equation*}
The second inequality uses
$e_{t,i}^2-(2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}-h_{t,i}^2)
=(e_{t,i}-h_{t,i})^2\geq0$.

Strong convexity of the Moreau envelope gives
\begin{equation*}
 2\Phi_t
 \geq\mu_{\mathcal E}
 \left(\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2\right).
\end{equation*}
If $\gamma_t\leq1$, the first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-positive-only-charge} is immediate.  Otherwise,
$\mathfrak D_t>0$, and $\log\gamma_t\leq\gamma_t-1$ together with the
preceding coordinate and strong-convexity bounds proves it.  The other two
inequalities follow directly from the numerator in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}.  Since $r_0=0$, summing proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.

Finally,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction,eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse} and
$c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}=a_q$ give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-collapse}.
\end{proof}

This is an a posteriori proof charge, not a locally computable certificate:
the set \cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-set} uses the unknown optimum.  It removes
the need to count every correction uniformly, but it may overcharge a benign
collateral round and does not yet bound the cumulative collateral fraction in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.  That packing problem is the
remaining outer lemma.

A first attempt to pack the charge is to divide the Euclidean progress
telescope in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope} by the current squared error.
This would suggest an estimate of the form
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^m \mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 \leq K\log\frac{\norm{e_0}_2^2}{\norm{e_m}_2^2}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-candidate}
\end{equation}
with an absolute, $\alpha$-independent constant $K$.  The next exact family
rules out this particular normalization, already on the first two nontrivial
stages.

\begin{proposition}[Euclidean log-error does not pack the collateral charge]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-fails}
Let $G$ consist of one edge, seed vertex $1$, fix $\rho=9/20$, and choose
\begin{equation}
 0<q\leq\frac1{100},
 \qquad
 \alpha:=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-parameters}
\end{equation}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the RPPR optimum has full support, while the
first two shifted solutions have the form
\begin{align}
 x^\star
 &=\left(\frac1{20}+\frac\alpha2,
          \frac1{20}-\frac\alpha2\right),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-optimum}\\
 x_1&=(u_1,0),
 &u_1&=\frac{11q^2}{30},\notag\\
 x_2&=(u_2,0),
 &u_2&=\frac{11q^2(7-4q)}{90}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates}
\end{align}
There is no correction at stage $1$, whereas stage $2$ is a full correction:
\begin{equation}
 r_1=0,
 \qquad
 r_2=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_2,
 \qquad
 d_2=(\delta,0),
 \qquad
 \delta:=\frac{22q^2(1-q)}{45}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-correction}
\end{equation}
Moreover, this second correction is collateral and harmful.  More precisely,
put
\begin{equation*}
 e:=x_1^\star-u_2,
 \qquad
 h:=a_q\delta=\frac{22q(1-q)^2}{45},
 \qquad
 E_j:=\norm{x^\star-x_j}_2^2.
\end{equation*}
Then $\mathcal B_2^{\mathrm{col}}=\{1\}$, $e>2h$, and
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak D_2&=h(2e-h)>0,
 &\gamma_2&>1,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-defect}\\
 \mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}
 &=\frac{2eh}
 {E_2+(e-h)^2+(x_2^\star)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-charge}
\end{align}
As $q\downarrow0$ through the admissible interval,
\begin{align}
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 \frac{\mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}{q}
 &=\frac{44}{9},
 &
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 \frac1{q^2}\log\frac{E_1}{E_2}
 &=\frac{88}{9},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-local-limits}\\
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 q\frac{\mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}
         {\log(E_1/E_2)}
 &=\frac12,
 &
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 q\frac{\mathfrak C_1^{\mathrm{col}}+
         \mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}
        {\log(E_0/E_2)}
 &=\frac27.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-packing-limits}
\end{align}
Consequently no $\alpha$-independent constant $K$ can make either the
pointwise charge by $\log(E_1/E_2)$ or the cumulative candidate
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-candidate} valid for all admissible runs.
More generally, any coefficient $K(q)=o(1/q)$ fails, including every
polylogarithmic dependence on $1/\alpha$.
This refutes only packing by the monotone Euclidean log-error.  It does not
refute a cumulative accelerated-scale bound using the collapse history, a
different potential, or a locally checkable surrogate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For this graph, with
$\lambda:=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $m:=(1-\alpha)/2$, the obstacle system is
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&-m\\-m&\lambda\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 c_\rho=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}11/20\\-9/20\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The sum and difference eigenvectors of $Q$ have eigenvalues $\alpha$ and
$1$, respectively.  Solving the two active KKT equations gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-optimum}; both coordinates are positive
because $q<1/3$.

In terms of $q$,
\begin{equation*}
 \lambda=\frac{1+2q^2}{2(1+q^2)},
 \qquad
 m=\frac1{2(1+q^2)},
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\mathrm A}=\frac{1-q^2}{1+q^2},
 \qquad
 \lambda+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=\frac3{2(1+q^2)}.
\end{equation*}
Solving the seed coordinate of the first two shifted obstacle problems gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates}.  These are the full shifted
solutions, because their inactive-coordinate obstacle residuals are
\begin{equation*}
 -\alpha\rho+m u_1
 =-\frac{4q^2}{15(1+q^2)}<0,
 \qquad
 -\alpha\rho+m u_2
 =-\frac{q^2(11q+1)}{45(1+q^2)}<0.
\end{equation*}
Here the first center is zero and the second center is
\begin{equation*}
 y_1=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})x_1
 =\left(\frac{11q^2}{15(1+q)},0\right).
\end{equation*}
Its seed-coordinate lower residual equals
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{11q^2(1-q)(4q+1)}
 {60(1+q)(1+q^2)}>0,
\end{equation*}
so $\ell_1=y_1$ and $r_1=0$.  Substituting this center in the next shifted
solve gives $u_2$ and $\delta=u_2-u_1$ in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-correction}.

At stage $2$ the extrapolated seed coordinate is
\begin{equation*}
 y_{2,1}=\frac{11q^2(11-5q)}{90(1+q)}.
\end{equation*}
Its lower residual is
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_{\rho,1}^{\mathrm{low}}(y_2)
 =\frac{11q^2(1-q)^2(5q-1)}
 {90(1+q)(1+q^2)}<0.
\end{equation*}
Thus the retraction magnitude is
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta_2
 =\frac{11(1-q)^2(1-5q)}{90(1+q)},
\end{equation*}
and direct subtraction gives
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta_2-\beta_{\mathrm A}\delta
 =\frac{11(1-q)^2(1-5q-4q^2)}{90(1+q)}>0.
\end{equation*}
The last sign holds throughout \cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-parameters}.
Therefore $\ell_2=x_2$ and the correction is full.

The exact collateral inequality follows from
\begin{equation*}
 180(1+q)(1+q^2)(x_1^\star-y_{2,1})
 =9+9q-143q^2+209q^3-242q^4+110q^5>0.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, on $q\leq1/100$ the right-hand side is at least
$9-143/10^4-242/10^8>0$.  Likewise,
\begin{equation*}
 180(1+q^2)(e-2h)
 =9-176q+297q^2-264q^3+198q^4-88q^5>0,
\end{equation*}
because it is at least
$9-176/100-264/10^6-88/10^{10}>0$ on the same interval.
Thus $\mathcal B_2^{\mathrm{col}}=\{1\}$ and the minimum in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge} is $2eh$.  A full correction has
$h_2=(h,0)$ and $\widetilde z_2=x_2$, which proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-defect,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-charge}.
Since $x_2\neq x^\star$, one has $\Phi_2>0$, and the positive defect gives
$\gamma_2>1$.

Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 e\longrightarrow\frac1{20},
 \qquad
 \frac hq\longrightarrow\frac{22}{45},
 \qquad
 E_0,E_1,E_2\longrightarrow\frac1{200}.
\end{equation*}
Since $E_1-E_2=2e\delta+\delta^2$,
$\delta/q^2\to22/45$, and
$E_0-E_2=2eu_2+u_2^2$ with $u_2/q^2\to77/90$, the elementary limit
$\log(1+v)/v\to1$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-local-limits,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-packing-limits}.
\end{proof}

The correction count and normalized inflation are genuinely different
objects.  The next source-aligned family shows why the raw count cannot be
charged to support additions or bounded independently of terminal accuracy.
It simultaneously confirms that such a count is too coarse for the
multiplicative ledger: every correction in the family is benign.

\begin{proposition}[A singleton support retains logarithmically many corrections]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-singleton-corrections}
Let $G$ consist of one edge, let the seed be one endpoint, and choose
\begin{equation}
 0<q\leq q_0:=\frac{\sqrt{41}-5}{8},
 \qquad
 \alpha:=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2},
 \qquad
 \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\leq\rho<1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-parameters}
\end{equation}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the RPPR optimum and every nonzero iterate are
supported only on the seed.  Let $e_t:=x_1^\star-x_{t,1}$ denote the scalar
error on that coordinate and define
\begin{equation}
 \vartheta:=\frac{2(1-q^2)}{3},
 \qquad
 u:=\frac{(1-q)(1+4q)}{3(1+q)},
 \qquad
 \xi:=\vartheta u.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-ratios}
\end{equation}
Then, for every $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 e_{2k}=\xi^ke_0,
 \qquad
 e_{2k+1}=\vartheta\xi^ke_0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors}
\end{equation}
There is no correction at an odd stage, whereas every even stage $2k\geq2$
is a full correction:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{2k+1}&=0,&&k\geq0,\\
 r_{2k}&=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{2k},&&k\geq1.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-schedule}
\end{equation}
Thus the raw count through $T$ stages is
\begin{equation}
 N_T=\left\lfloor\frac{T-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-count}
\end{equation}
Nevertheless, every one of these corrections satisfies
$\mathfrak D_{2k}<0$ and $\gamma_{2k}<1$, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T=0
 \qquad\text{for every }T.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-zero-inflation}
\end{equation}

For fixed admissible $\rho$, reaching scalar error $\eta$ takes
$\Theta(\log(e_0/\eta))$ stages and incurs the same order of nonzero
correction calls.  Equivalently, since
$F_\rho(x_t)-F_\rho(x^\star)=((1+\alpha)/4)e_t^2$, reaching objective gap
$\epsobj$ incurs $\Theta(\log(1/\epsobj))$ corrections.  The seed entry and
the only edge are discovered once.  Each subsequent correction/rekey call
has degree work one, so this is cumulative correction work rather than
repeated support-discovery work.  It is a statement about the safeguarded
recurrence, not a lower bound for a different algorithm or a cached response
primitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let the seed be vertex $1$.  On this graph
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}
 (1+\alpha)/2&-(1-\alpha)/2\\
 -(1-\alpha)/2&(1+\alpha)/2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 c_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho\one
 =\alpha\begin{pmatrix}1-\rho\\-\rho\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Writing $\lambda:=(1+\alpha)/2$, the obstacle KKT conditions give
\begin{equation*}
 x^\star
 =\begin{pmatrix}\alpha(1-\rho)/\lambda\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the inactive-coordinate inequality is exactly
$\rho\geq(1-\alpha)/2$.  The same direct KKT check, or Stieltjes comparison,
shows that every shifted minimizer centered below $x^\star$ also has zero
second coordinate.  Thus the entire recurrence is scalar after the one-time
edge exposure.

Put $\kappa:=1-2\alpha$.  For a scalar safe center $\ell$, exact shifted
minimization gives
\begin{equation*}
 p(\ell)=\frac{\lambda x^\star+\kappa\ell}{\lambda+\kappa},
 \qquad
 x^\star-p(\ell)=\vartheta(x^\star-\ell),
 \qquad
 \vartheta=\frac{\kappa}{\lambda+\kappa}
            =\frac{2(1-q^2)}3.
\end{equation*}
Also $\beta_{\mathrm A}=(1-q)/(1+q)$.  Starting immediately after a full
correction, one shifted solve therefore changes the error from $e$ to
$\vartheta e$.  At the following odd stage,
\begin{equation*}
 x^\star-y
 =\bigl((1+\beta_{\mathrm A})\vartheta
        -\beta_{\mathrm A}\bigr)e
 =ue>0.
\end{equation*}
There is no correction, and the next shifted solve changes the error to
$\vartheta ue=\xi e$.

At the following even stage, the extrapolate instead satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 y-x^\star
 =\vartheta\bigl(\beta_{\mathrm A}
            -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u\bigr)e,
 \qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm A}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u
 =\frac{(1-q)(1-5q)}{3(1+q)^2}>0.
\end{equation*}
For this one-coordinate trial point, the retraction magnitude is
$\Delta=(\lambda/\alpha)(y-x^\star)$.  The condition that it removes the
entire extrapolation, $\Delta\geq\beta_{\mathrm A}d$, reduces after
cancelling positive factors to
\begin{equation*}
 4q^2+5q\leq1,
\end{equation*}
which is exactly $q\leq q_0$.  Hence $\ell=x$ at every even correction and
the same two-stage calculation repeats at every scale.  This proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors,eq:aesp-cd-singleton-schedule} and the
count in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-count}.

At every correction put $a_q:=(1-q)/q$ and
$h:=((1+q)/q)r=a_qd$.  Its defect is
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D=h(2e-h)<0,
\end{equation*}
because overshoot gives $\beta_{\mathrm A}d>e$ and hence
$a_qd=((1+q)/q)\beta_{\mathrm A}d>2e$.  Noncorrection stages have
$\gamma_t=1$, proving \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-zero-inflation}.  Finally,
$0<\xi<1$ and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors} give the logarithmic
accuracy statements.  The work statement follows because the seed vertex
has graph degree one and the shared model charges its adjacency scan.
\end{proof}

For a concrete rational instance, take $q=1/7$, so
$\alpha=1/50$, $\beta_{\mathrm A}=3/4$,
$\vartheta=32/49$, $u=11/28$, and $\xi=88/343$; every
$\rho\in[49/100,1)$ is admissible.  This exact example shows that the
collapse identity cannot remove the logarithm from the \emph{number} of
correction calls, even when the support is a singleton and never expands.
It also shows why $N_T$ must not replace $\mathcal I_T$ indiscriminately:
the normalized inflation correctly assigns zero cost to all of these
benign resets.

In the singleton family,
$\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}=\varnothing$ at every correction, so
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge} also proves
$\mathcal I_T=0$.  In contrast, the positive three-path defect below forces
$\mathcal B_3^{\mathrm{col}}\neq\varnothing$ and is charged by
$\mathfrak C_3^{\mathrm{col}}$.

\subsection{A ledger-only obstruction and fixed-operator finite traces}

The safe-chain and collapse identities are necessary consequences of the
exact safeguarded recurrence, but they do not encode all of its dynamics.  The
next proposition makes that logical boundary explicit.

\begin{proposition}[The scalar safeguard ledgers alone do not pack inflation]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-ledger-only-insufficient}
Fix $0<q\leq1/10$ and put
\begin{equation}
 \alpha:=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2},\qquad
 \kappa:=\frac{1-q^2}{1+q^2}=1-2\alpha,\qquad
 \beta:=\frac{1-q}{1+q},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-parameters}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation*}
 \tau:=\frac52q^2,\qquad
 R:=1-\tau\left(1+\frac32\beta\right),\qquad
 x^\star:=1.
\end{equation*}
For any $N\geq1$, there is a scalar sequence consisting of $N$ two-step
cycles.  At the start of cycle $j$, let $E_j:=R^{j-1}$ and
$x_{2j-2}=\ell_{2j-2}:=1-E_j$.  Define
\begin{align}
 d_{2j-1}&:=\tau E_j,
 &s_{2j-1}&:=d_{2j-1},
 &r_{2j-1}&:=0,\notag\\
 d_{2j}&:=\frac32\beta d_{2j-1},
 &s_{2j}&:=\frac12\beta d_{2j-1},
 &r_{2j}&:=\beta d_{2j},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-cycle}
\end{align}
with $x_t=x_{t-1}+d_t$ and
$\ell_t=x_t+\beta d_t-r_t$.  If $s_0:=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{2j-1}:=0,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{2j}:=\frac{\kappa}{\alpha}
       \frac{\beta(1-\beta)}2d_{2j-1},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-collapse}
\end{equation}
then the sequence satisfies all of the following scalar ledgers:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the full safe chain between every pair of constructed consecutive
       stages;
 \item $s_t=d_t-\beta d_{t-1}+r_{t-1}$ and
       $r_t=\min\{\beta d_t,\Delta_t\}$;
 \item for every $t\geq2$,
       $\alpha\Delta_t=\kappa
       [\beta s_{t-1}-(1+\beta)s_t]_+$;
 \item $\sum_{t=1}^{2N}r_t\leq
       \beta\sum_{t=1}^{2N}d_t<\beta$, and, if
       $C_1:=\alpha$ and, for $1\leq t\leq2N$,
       $C_{t+1}:=\kappa(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1})$, then
       $\sum_{t=0}^{2N}C_{t+1}<\alpha+\kappa=1-\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}

Nevertheless these ledgers permit accelerated-scale packing to fail.  For
any $\mu>0$, put $e_t:=1-x_t$, $a:=(1-q)/q$, and define the saturated
quadratic ledger
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_t:=\frac\mu2\left(e_t^2+(e_t-ad_t)^2\right),
 \qquad d_0:=0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-potential}
\end{equation}
With $h_t:=((1+q)/q)r_t$ and
$\mathfrak D_t:=h_t^2+2e_th_t-2ad_th_t$, it obeys
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{t+1}\leq(1-q)\left(
    \Phi_t+\frac\mu2\mathfrak D_t\right),
 \qquad 0\leq t<2N.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-defective-contraction}
\end{equation}
Every odd correction is zero, whereas every even correction is full,
collateral, and harmful.  More precisely, at an even stage let
$u:=h_{2j}/e_{2j}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac52q\leq u\leq\frac25,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak D_{2j}=h_{2j}(2e_{2j}-h_{2j})>0,
 \qquad
 \gamma_{2j}=\frac{2}{1+(1-u)^2},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-inflation}
\end{equation}
and $\log\gamma_{2j}\geq q$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{2N}\geq Nq.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-linear-inflation}
\end{equation}
Taking $N=\lceil q^{-2}\rceil$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{2N}=\Omega(q^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \log\frac{\Phi_0}{\Phi_{2N}}=O(1).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-stop}
\end{equation}
Thus the listed scalar ledgers alone cannot imply a bound of the form
$\mathcal I_T=O(q^2T+\log(\Phi_0/\Phi_T))$, nor the desired soft-logarithmic
packing.

This is an abstract ledger countermodel, not an RPPR instance, not an exact
proximal trajectory, and not an algorithmic lower bound.  In particular, no
fixed Stieltjes operator $Q$ or boundary multipliers are supplied.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The choice of $R$ gives $15/16\leq R<1$.  Direct substitution in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-cycle} gives
\begin{equation*}
 x_{2j-2}\leq x_{2j-1}\leq\ell_{2j-1}
 \leq x_{2j}=\ell_{2j}\leq x^\star,
 \qquad
 x^\star-x_{2j}=RE_j.
\end{equation*}
At an even stage,
\begin{equation*}
 \beta s_{2j-1}-(1+\beta)s_{2j}
 =\frac{\beta(1-\beta)}2d_{2j-1}>0.
\end{equation*}
At a later odd stage the corresponding bracket is nonpositive because
$\beta^2/2\leq(1+\beta)R$.  Moreover,
$(\kappa/\alpha)(1-\beta)\geq3\beta$, so the even value of
$\Delta_t$ is at least $\beta d_t$.  These observations prove the chain,
collapse, and correction identities.  Their two sums telescope because the
$d_t$ and the $\ell_t-\ell_{t-1}$ are nonnegative and their endpoints are
strictly below one.

For the potential calculation, define
\begin{align}
 P_{\rm o}&:=\left(1-\frac52q^2\right)^2
             +\left(1-\frac52q\right)^2,\notag\\
 H&:=\frac{15}{4}\frac{q(1-q)^2}{1+q},
 &P_{\rm e}&:=R^2+(R-H)^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-polynomials}
\end{align}
The odd and even potentials in cycle $j$ are respectively
$(\mu/2)E_j^2P_{\rm o}$ and $(\mu/2)E_j^2P_{\rm e}$.  Exact expansion gives
\begin{align}
 (1-q)P_{\rm o}-P_{\rm e}
 &=\frac{q}{16(1+q)^2}
 \left(8-69q+418q^2-750q^3+430q^4-225q^5-100q^6\right)>0,
 \notag\\
 2(1-q)-P_{\rm o}
 &=\frac q4(12-5q-25q^3)>0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-differences}
\end{align}
The first line pays the uncorrected odd-to-even step; the second pays the
even-to-next-odd step after adding the even defect.  It also pays the initial
zero-to-odd step.  This proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-defective-contraction}.

At an even stage, $h_{2j}=ad_{2j}=HE_j$ and $e_{2j}=RE_j$.
The elementary bounds $H\geq(5/2)q$, $H\leq(15/4)q$, and
$R\geq15/16$ yield the interval for $u$.  Full correction changes the second
squared term in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-potential} from
$(e_{2j}-h_{2j})^2$ to $e_{2j}^2$, which proves the formulas for
$\mathfrak D_{2j}$ and $\gamma_{2j}$.  Since
$\log v\geq1-1/v$ for $v\geq1$,
\begin{equation*}
 \log\gamma_{2j}\geq u-\frac{u^2}{2}
 \geq\frac45u\geq2q\geq q.
\end{equation*}
Finally,
$\Phi_{2N}=(\mu/2)R^{2(N-1)}P_{\rm e}$.  Because
$1-R=\Theta(q^2)$ uniformly on $0<q\leq1/10$, choosing
$N=\lceil q^{-2}\rceil$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-ledger-countermodel-stop}.
\end{proof}

What is missing from the countermodel is exactly a fixed-operator dynamical
constraint.  On every active row of an actual exact shifted RPPR minimizer,
subtracting the shifted and terminal KKT equations gives
\begin{equation}
 (Qe_t)_i=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,i},
 \qquad i\in\supp(x_t),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-fixed-operator-relation}
\end{equation}
while the inactive rows satisfy the corresponding complementarity
inequalities and are coupled through the same fixed Stieltjes matrix.  A
successful next proof must exploit this spectral/boundary relation across
several stages; replaying only the scalar ledgers cannot suffice.

The fixed-operator recurrence itself does exhibit positive inflation long
after its support has stabilized.  The following statement is a finite exact
trace, not an asymptotic counterexample.

\begin{proposition}[A fixed-support $P_4$ trace has late positive inflation]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-p4-late-inflation}
Let $G=P_4=(0,1,2,3)$, seed endpoint $0$, and run the exact safeguarded
recurrence through $T=500$ with
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac18,\qquad \alpha=\frac1{65},\qquad
 \rho=\frac7{40}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-parameters}
\end{equation}
In degree-scaled coordinates $\widehat x=D^{-1/2}x$, the degree and optimum
vectors are
\begin{equation}
 d=(1,2,2,1),\qquad
 \widehat x^\star=\left(
 \frac{16513}{423720},\frac{37}{2568},
 \frac{713}{423720},0\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-vectors}
\end{equation}
The iterate supports are exactly
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \supp(\widehat x_0)&=\varnothing,
 &\supp(\widehat x_1)&=\{0\},\\
 \supp(\widehat x_t)&=\{0,1\}\quad(2\leq t\leq6),
 &\supp(\widehat x_t)&=\{0,1,2\}\quad(7\leq t\leq500).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-support-schedule}
\end{equation}
There are $135$ nonzero correction stages, exactly
\begin{equation}
 \{3,7,14\}\cup
 \bigcup_{j=0}^{43}\{21+11j,22+11j,25+11j\},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-correction-schedule}
\end{equation}
and the $44$ stages with $\gamma_t>1$ are exactly
\begin{equation}
 \{25+11j:0\leq j\leq43\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-positive-schedule}
\end{equation}
High-precision evaluation of the logarithms of the exact rational quantities
gives
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{500}&\simeq1.0236455928394953,
 &\log\frac{\Phi_0}{\Phi_{500}}
 &\simeq348.2581722210201408,\notag\\
 \frac{\mathcal I_{500}}{\log(\Phi_0/\Phi_{500})}
 &\simeq0.0029393297113782,
 &\frac{\mathcal I_{500}}{qT}
 &\simeq0.0163783294854319.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-metrics}
\end{align}
Thus positive inflation is not confined to support-addition stages and need
not vanish after the final support discovery.  This does not prove infinite
periodicity, an exponent or polylogarithmic packing obstruction, or any
finite-inner or end-to-end work lower bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The companion checker works entirely in degree-scaled rational coordinates.
At every stage it solves the shifted Stieltjes obstacle system by exact active
sets, verifies primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementarity,
then checks the safeguard, safe chain, support, correction, and exact rational
sign of $\gamma_t-1$.  It asserts
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-vectors,eq:aesp-cd-p4-support-schedule,eq:aesp-cd-p4-correction-schedule,eq:aesp-cd-p4-positive-schedule}
directly.  The displayed decimal values are evaluated only after all exact
sign and schedule checks have passed.
\end{proof}

As corroborating proof scaffolding, the same exact recurrence on endpoint-
seeded $P_7$ with $q=1/5$, $\alpha=1/26$, $\rho=1/20$, and $T=800$ has
degree-scaled optimal support $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$.  Its support changes only at
stages $1,2,5,9$ and is fixed thereafter; it has $122$ correction stages and
$39$ positive-inflation stages, the last at stage $796$, with
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{800}\simeq7.2945227043682557,
 \qquad
 \log\frac{\Phi_0}{\Phi_{800}}
 \simeq521.4681823472578688.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p7-scaffolding}
\end{equation}
This is a second finite trace only.  It supplies neither an infinite
recurrence theorem nor a scaling obstruction.  No new eleven-resource vector
is attached to either trace: both use idealized exact shifted minimizers and
establish no finite-inner implementation or end-to-end work theorem.

The $P_4$ trace can in fact be continued rigorously for every horizon.  The
proof below is a finite exact-rational cone certificate for a homogeneous
piecewise-linear tail.  It upgrades the finite observation above, but it also
shows why a horizon-uniform bound on $\mathcal I_T$ is the wrong remaining
target: positive inflation persists with positive density even though the
actual error contracts rapidly.

\begin{proposition}[The fixed-operator $P_4$ tail has linear inflation]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-inflation}
Retain the graph, seed, and parameters of
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-p4-late-inflation}.  Let
$A=\{0,1,2\}$ be the optimal support and write
$\widehat e_t=D_A^{-1/2}(x^\star-x_t)_A$.  From stage $44$ onward, the
exact recurrence has the periodic phase word
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf F\,\mathsf N\,\mathsf N\,\mathsf F\,
 \mathsf N^6\,\mathsf P_0
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-word}
\end{equation}
of length eleven.  Here $\mathsf N$ means no correction, $\mathsf F$ means
that all three active coordinates receive the full correction
$r_t=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t$, and $\mathsf P_0$ means that row zero uniquely
sets a positive common retraction magnitude strictly below
$\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{t,i}$ on every $i\in A$.  The second full phase in each
word, at stages
\begin{equation}
 47+11j,\qquad j\geq0,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-positive-stages}
\end{equation}
has positive potential defect and obeys
\begin{equation}
 \log\gamma_{47+11j}\geq\frac{315}{33280}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-gamma-floor}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every integer $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{44+11N}\geq\frac{315}{33280}N,
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_T=\Theta(T)
 \quad\text{along this one fixed RPPR instance.}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-inflation-floor}
\end{equation}

This is not a convergence obstruction.  At the word boundaries,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\widehat e_{55+11j}}_\infty
 \leq\frac{11}{500}
       \norm{\widehat e_{44+11j}}_\infty,
 \qquad j\geq0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-error-contraction}
\end{equation}
Thus the exact primal error still converges geometrically.  The proposition
rules out a bound on $\mathcal I_T$ that depends only on the fixed support and
fixed parameters and is uniform in the horizon.  It does not refute an
accuracy-logarithmic allowance, a rate-sensitive estimate such as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T\leq(1-c)qT+\operatorname{polylog}
        (q^{-1},\rho^{-1},\epsobj^{-1}),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-net-packing-target}
\end{equation}
or any equivalent net exponent $-\Omega(qT)$.  Since $q=1/8$ is fixed, it
also gives no $q\downarrow0$ scaling obstruction.  The shifted solves remain
ideal exact minimizers, so no finite-inner work theorem or eleven-resource
vector follows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All calculations in this proof use degree-scaled active coordinates.  Put
$\beta=7/9$.  Once the support is $A$, the exact shifted map sends a
safe-center error $g$ to $Mg$, where
\begin{equation}
 M=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 245/352&21/88&7/176\\
 21/176&63/88&21/176\\
 7/352&21/176&119/176
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-fixed-map}
\end{equation}
For the candidate error
$v=(1+\beta)\widehat e_t-\beta\widehat e_{t-1}$, the three phase maps are
\begin{equation}
 g=
 \begin{cases}
 v,&\mathsf N,\\
 \widehat e_t,&\mathsf F,\\
 R_0v,&\mathsf P_0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 R_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -32&32&0\\
 -33&33&0\\
 -33&32&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-phase-maps}
\end{equation}
Indeed, row zero of the active degree-scaled $Q$ is
$(33,-32,0)/65$ and $\alpha d_0=1/65$, so $R_0v$ adds the unique common
amount that makes that row tight.

The finite cone certificate is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K:=\left\{e>0:
 \frac{997}{1000}\leq\frac{e_1}{e_0}\leq\frac{998}{1000},
 \quad
 \frac{856}{1000}\leq\frac{e_2}{e_0}\leq\frac{857}{1000}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-invariant-cone}
\end{equation}
An exact replay of the finite prefix gives $\widehat e_{44}\in\mathcal K$
and verifies the full correction at stage $44$.

For completeness, define the exact word map without printing its large
rational entries.  Let $A_0=I$, $A_1=M$, and
\begin{equation*}
 A_{j+1}=M\bigl((1+\beta)A_j-\beta A_{j-1}\bigr),
 \qquad j=1,2.
\end{equation*}
Put $B_0=A_3$, $B_1=MA_3$, and
\begin{equation*}
 B_{j+1}=M\bigl((1+\beta)B_j-\beta B_{j-1}\bigr),
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq6.
\end{equation*}
Then the boundary-to-boundary map is
\begin{equation}
 T:=MR_0\bigl((1+\beta)B_7-\beta B_6\bigr).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-word-map}
\end{equation}
The four extreme rays of $\mathcal K$ give a finite exact-rational
certificate.  Every monotonicity, residual-sign, unique-row, and full- or
partial-cap inequality needed for the eleven intervening decisions has
strict margin at least $e_0/200$.  The same four-ray calculation gives
\begin{align}
 \frac1{48}
 &<\frac{(Te)_0}{e_0}<\frac{11}{500},
 \notag\\
 \frac{1247}{1250}
 &<\frac{(Te)_1}{(Te)_0}<\frac{9977}{10000},
 &
 \frac{107}{125}
 &<\frac{(Te)_2}{(Te)_0}<\frac{2141}{2500}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-cone-images}
\end{align}
All phase tests and the inequalities in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-cone-images} are linear after denominators of known
positive sign are cleared, so checking the four extreme rays proves them on
the whole cone.  In particular, $T\mathcal K\subset\mathcal K$, the phase
word repeats forever, and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-error-contraction} follows.  The inactive row
remains dual feasible because every active iterate stays below the RPPR
optimum and its boundary slack is the optimum slack plus a nonnegative
Stieltjes term.

It remains to certify a uniformly harmful phase.  Let $E=e_0$ at a word
boundary, let $\mathfrak D_{\mathsf F,2}$ and
$\Phi_{\mathsf F,2}$ denote the defect and potential at the second full phase
of that word, and use $D_A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,2)$.  Substitution of the matrices above
gives quadratic forms in $(1,e_1/e_0,e_2/e_0)$.  Exact interval evaluation of
their six monomials on the rectangle in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-invariant-cone} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_{\mathsf F,2}\geq\frac15E^2,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{\mathsf F,2}\leq\frac2{25}E^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-p4-defect-potential-box}
\end{equation}
Here the envelope part of the second form uses the exact active-face Hessian
\begin{equation*}
 \widehat B
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}D_A
  -\kappa_{\mathrm A}^2D_A
   \widehat H_A^{-1}D_A.
\end{equation*}
Since $\mu_{\mathcal E}=\kappa_{\mathrm A}q^2=63/4160$,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-defect-potential-box} yields
\begin{equation*}
 \gamma-1
 =\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}\mathfrak D}{2\Phi}
 \geq\frac{315}{16640}.
\end{equation*}
The already proved bound $1\leq\gamma\leq2$ and
$\log\gamma\geq(\gamma-1)/\gamma$ now prove
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-gamma-floor}.  Summing one such term per word
proves the lower bound in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-p4-infinite-inflation-floor}; the matching upper order is
the general bound $\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}\leq\log2$.

\end{proof}

The fixed-operator certificate above uses exact shifted minimizers.  For an
implementable finite inner solve, the exact active-row relation, collapse,
and start-mass telescope acquire residual terms.  The following identities
locate every one of those terms before any packing claim is attempted.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-inner residual identities]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-finite-inner-identities}
Replace each exact shifted minimizer in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer} by the finite output $x_t$, $t\geq1$, obtained from the
proximal warm start at center $\ell_{t-1}$ followed by increasing greedy exact
coordinate updates.  Put
\begin{equation}
 w:=D^{1/2}\one,
 \quad c_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho w,
 \quad H:=Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I,
 \quad s_t:=x_t-\ell_{t-1},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-notation}
\end{equation}
and define the shifted obstacle residual and its two parts by
\begin{equation}
 \varpi_t:=c_\rho+\kappa_{\mathrm A}\ell_{t-1}-Hx_t,
 \qquad
 a_t:=[\varpi_t]_+,
 \qquad
 \lambda_t:=[-\varpi_t]_+.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-residuals}
\end{equation}
Let $\lambda^\star:=Qx^\star-c_\rho\geq0$.  Then the finite recurrence still
satisfies the safe chain
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x_{t-1}\leq\ell_{t-1}\leq x_t\leq\ell_t
 \leq x_{t+1}\leq x^\star.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-chain}
\end{equation}
The minimum shifted KKT vector at the end of stage $t$ is $-a_t$, and hence
$C_{\mathrm{end},t}=\langle w,a_t\rangle$.  Moreover,
\begin{align}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
 &=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_t+a_t-\lambda_t,
 \notag\\
 Q(x^\star-x_t)
 &=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_t+a_t-\lambda_t+\lambda^\star,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-fixed-row}\\
 [Q(x^\star-x_t)]_{A_t}
 &=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,A_t}+a_{t,A_t},
 \qquad A_t:=\supp(x_t).
 \notag
\end{align}
The next shifted start mass has the exact perturbed telescope
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{start},t+1}
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mathfrak m(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1})
  +C_{\mathrm{end},t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-start-mass}
\end{equation}
Finally, for $t\geq2$, on $A_t$ the extrapolate has the exact negative-part
identity
\begin{align}
 [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_{-,A_t}
 =\bigl[&\kappa_{\mathrm A}
       (\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t)
       +\beta_{\mathrm A}a_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})a_t
       -\beta_{\mathrm A}\lambda_{t-1}\bigr]_{+,A_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse}
\end{align}
Consequently, for $t\geq2$, the retraction remains exactly
\begin{equation}
 r_t=\min\{\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,\Delta_t w\},
 \qquad
 \alpha\Delta_t
 =\norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_{-,A_t}}_\infty,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-retraction}
\end{equation}
and it obeys the residual-robust upper bound
\begin{align}
 \alpha\Delta_t
 \leq{}&\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+}_\infty
 \notag\\
 &+\beta_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}a_{t-1,A_t}}_\infty.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse-upper}
\end{align}
Thus every failed exact solve is charged once in the next start mass and once
in the next collapse; the exact identities cannot simply be reused with
$a_t$ deleted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proximal warm start is a lower subsolution above $\ell_{t-1}$, and every
subsequent increasing coordinate update remains between that center and the
exact shifted minimizer.  The comparison argument in
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}, followed by the same lower retraction as in
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-outer-ledger}, proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-chain}.

At a positive coordinate of $x_t$, lower-subsolution feasibility gives
$\varpi_{t,i}\geq0$; at a zero coordinate, the minimum obstacle KKT vector is
the negative part of the smooth derivative, namely
$-[\varpi_{t,i}]_+$.  Thus the full minimum KKT vector is $-a_t$.  The
definition of $\varpi_t$ also gives
\begin{equation*}
 c_\rho-Qx_t
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x_t-\ell_{t-1})
   +a_t-\lambda_t.
\end{equation*}
Adding the terminal obstacle equation
$Qx^\star=c_\rho+\lambda^\star$ proves the first two relations in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-fixed-row}.  If $i\in A_t$, then both $x_{t,i}$
and $x_i^\star$ are positive, so $\lambda_{t,i}=\lambda_i^\star=0$; this
proves the active-row relation.

For the next shifted subproblem, the obstacle residual at $x_t$ is
\begin{equation*}
 \varpi_t+\kappa_{\mathrm A}(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1}).
\end{equation*}
On $A_t$ both summands are nonnegative.  Outside $A_t$, both centers vanish,
so its positive part is just $a_t$.  Taking the $w$-weighted mass proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-start-mass}.

Because the coefficients of $y_t=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})x_t-
\beta_{\mathrm A}x_{t-1}$ sum to one,
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)
 =(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
  -\beta_{\mathrm A}
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_{t-1}).
\end{equation*}
Substitute the first line of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-fixed-row} and use $\lambda_{t,A_t}=0$.
Taking the negative part gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse}.  The definition of the lower
retraction proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-retraction}.  Finally the
two terms involving $a_t$ and $\lambda_{t-1}$ in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse} have nonpositive sign, while
$a_{t-1}\geq0$.  Coordinatewise subadditivity of the positive part and the
weighted maximum norm prove
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse-upper}.
\end{proof}

The ordinary relative inner stopping rule does not make the new residual
terms vanish, even in one dimension.

\begin{proposition}[Relative C2 stopping does not imply exact residuals]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-c2-residual-stop}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 h(z)=h^\star+\frac{\mu}{2}(z-p)^2,
 \qquad p>0,\quad \mu>0,
\end{equation*}
be a scalar shifted objective with center zero.  For any $\delta,\kappa>0$,
put
\begin{equation*}
 A:=\sqrt{\frac{\delta\kappa}{\mu}},
 \qquad z:=\frac{p}{1+A},
 \qquad v:=p-z=Az.
\end{equation*}
Then the relative condition holds at equality,
\begin{equation}
 h(z)-h^\star=\frac{\delta\kappa}{2}z^2,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-c2-residual-equality}
\end{equation}
but the lower-subsolution residual is
\begin{equation}
 a=\mu(p-z)=\sqrt{\mu\delta\kappa}\,z>0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-c2-residual-size}
\end{equation}
For the AESP choice $\delta=\delta_{\mathrm A}=q/(2-q)$ with
$\mu,\kappa=\Theta(1)$, one has $a/z=\Theta(\sqrt q)$.  Therefore C2 alone
cannot justify the exact fixed-row, collapse, or start-mass identities.  This
is a logical interface obstruction, not a fixed-objective RPPR rate lower
bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of $A$ gives
$\mu v^2/2=\delta\kappa z^2/2$, while differentiation gives
$-h'(z)=\mu v$.  These are exactly
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2-residual-equality,eq:aesp-cd-c2-residual-size}.
\end{proof}

There is nevertheless a local way to make the finite residual absolutely
small.  It costs only an additional soft logarithm on the already explored
envelope; what remains open is how small it must be over the complete outer
run.

\begin{corollary}[Dual absolute inner polish]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop}
Let $p=p(\ell)$ be the exact shifted minimizer and suppose its support has
volume at most $V$.  Continue the increasing greedy inner solve until both
the relative condition \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} and
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{end}}=\langle w,a\rangle
 \leq\frac{\mu_t\xi}{\sqrt V}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop}
\end{equation}
Then its finite output $x$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x-p}_2\leq\xi.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-error}
\end{equation}
Starting from the proximal warm start, the degree work needed for the
absolute stop alone is at most
\begin{equation}
 V\left[3+\frac1{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
       \log_+\left(\frac{2V}{\xi}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-work}
\end{equation}
Enforcing both conditions has degree work at most the sum of this bound and
the right-hand side of \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}.  For the safeguarded
RPPR envelope one may take $V=1/\rho$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put $v=p-x\geq0$ and choose a coordinate maximizing
$v_i/\sqrt{d_i}$.  If the maximum is positive, then $p_i>0$, so the exact
shifted obstacle residual vanishes at $p_i$.  The normalized Stieltjes
maximum principle therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
 \mu_t\norm{D^{-1/2}v}_\infty
 \leq\norm{D^{-1/2}a}_\infty
 \leq C_{\mathrm{end}}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\supp(v)\subseteq\supp(p)$,
$\norm{v}_2\leq\sqrt V\norm{D^{-1/2}v}_\infty$; the stopping condition proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-error}.

By \cref{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}, the proximal warm-start mass is at most
$2(1-\alpha)\sqrt V\norm{\ell-p}_2$.  The safe sandwich and
$\mathfrak m(x^\star)\leq1$ give
$\norm{\ell-p}_2\leq1$, while $\mu_t=1-\alpha$.  Hence the ratio of the
warm-start mass to the target in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop} is at most $2V/\xi$.
Apply \cref{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}.  Two cached envelope passes initialize
the warm start and every positive heap key, and the final update can overshoot
the mass target by at most one coordinate cost.  These give the three
additive copies of $V$ in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-work}.  If the relative
stop takes longer, adding the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work} is a valid combined bound; equivalently, a
single greedy run stops when both conditions hold.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-024 stability boundary and structural positive branch}

The absolute polish controls the error of one shifted solve.  It does not,
by itself, justify a black-box trajectory-shadowing argument through the
support-indexed lower retraction.  The obstruction is already exact on one
edge.

\begin{proposition}[The lower retraction is discontinuous and sharply ill-conditioned]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-retraction-shadowing-stop}
Let $G=P_2$, use the endpoint seed, fix $0<\alpha<1/4$, and write
\begin{equation*}
 \lambda:=\frac{1+\alpha}{2},
 \qquad \nu:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2},
 \qquad \rho:=\frac{\nu}{4}.
\end{equation*}
For the support-indexed map $\mathcal L$ in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}, put
\begin{equation*}
 a:=\left(\frac\alpha8,0\right),
 \qquad a^\eta:=\left(\frac\alpha8,\eta\right),\quad \eta>0.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(a)=a,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(a^\eta)=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}
 \norm{\mathcal L(a^\eta)-\mathcal L(a)}_\infty
 =\frac\alpha8.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-retraction-discontinuity}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal L$ is discontinuous when a zero coordinate enters the trial
support.

Even with the support fixed and every input and output coordinate positive,
the sharp local amplification is $1+1/\alpha$.  Specifically, put
\begin{equation*}
 u:=\left(\frac\lambda2,\frac\nu2\right),
 \qquad h:=(1,-1),
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon<\frac{\alpha\nu}{4}.
\end{equation*}
Both $u$ and $u+\varepsilon h$ have full support, the same row uniquely
controls their retractions, neither output clips, and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{\mathcal L(u+\varepsilon h)-\mathcal L(u)}_\infty}
      {\norm{\varepsilon h}_\infty}
 =1+\frac1\alpha.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-retraction-sharp-amplification}
\end{equation}
On the fixed full support of $P_2$, this is also an upper Lipschitz bound.
Consequently, a proof that shadows an exact trajectory only by iterating this
ambient Lipschitz constant over $T$ outer stages must take a uniform local
perturbation of order
$(1+1/\alpha)^{-T}$ (up to the requested final error).  Substitution in the
certified upper ledger \cref{eq:aesp-cd-dual-inner-work} then contains a
$VT^2\log(1/\alpha)$ inner-log term, so this template cannot certify the
target accelerated work.  This is a STOP for that
black-box shadowing template, not an instability theorem for the actual
finite recurrence and not a counterexample to direct finite-sequence
packing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $P_2$,
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&-\nu\\-\nu&\lambda\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 c_\rho=\alpha(1-\rho,-\rho)^T,
 \qquad \lambda+\nu=1,
 \qquad \lambda-\nu=\alpha.
\end{equation*}
At $a$, row zero is feasible, while the excluded row-one lower residual is
$-\alpha\nu/8$.  Hence the maximum over $\supp(a)=\{0\}$ is zero and
$\mathcal L(a)=a$.  Once $\eta>0$, row one enters the support and uniquely
sets
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta(a^\eta)=\frac\nu8+\frac\lambda\alpha\eta.
\end{equation*}
Because $\nu>\alpha$ and $\lambda>\alpha$, this common shift exceeds both
coordinates of $a^\eta$, proving
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-retraction-discontinuity}.

For the second claim, row zero is feasible at $u$, row one is the unique
violating row, and $\Delta(u)=\rho=\nu/4$.  Since $Qh=h$, the same row
controls $u+\varepsilon h$ and
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta(u+\varepsilon h)=\frac\nu4-\frac\varepsilon\alpha.
\end{equation*}
The stated range keeps the shift positive and both retracted coordinates
strictly positive.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal L(u+\varepsilon h)-\mathcal L(u)
 =\varepsilon\left(1+\frac1\alpha,
                    -1+\frac1\alpha\right),
\end{equation*}
which proves the lower bound.  On a common full support,
$\Delta(v)=\alpha^{-1}\max\{0,(Qv-c_\rho)_0,(Qv-c_\rho)_1\}$.
Every row of $Q$ has absolute row sum one, so
\begin{equation*}
 \abs{\Delta(v)-\Delta(w)}
 \leq\alpha^{-1}\norm{v-w}_\infty.
\end{equation*}
The positive-part map is nonexpansive, proving the matching upper bound.
Iterating that bound gives the stated shadowing and work consequence.
\end{proof}

The fixed operator does supply structure that the preceding ambient STOP
throws away.  In particular, a full correction cannot repeat immediately on
an exact settled face.

\begin{lemma}[Full corrections are separated on a settled face]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-full-correction-separation}
Let $A=S^\star(\rho)$ and suppose $\supp(x_t)=A$, $\ell_t=x_t$, and
$x_t$ is an admissible exact or finite safeguarded output, so that
$Q_{AA}(x^\star-x_t)\geq0$.  All displayed identities are restricted to
this same positive face.  Let
$H_A:=Q_{AA}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I$, and define
\begin{equation}
 M_A:=\kappa_{\mathrm A}H_A^{-1},
 \qquad
 S_A:=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})M_A-\beta_{\mathrm A}I.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-full-correction-maps}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 S_A\succeq q\beta_{\mathrm A}I,
 \qquad S_A\geq0\quad\text{entrywise},
 \qquad S_AQ_{AA}=Q_{AA}S_A.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-full-correction-positive-map}
\end{equation}
If the solve after this full center is exact, so
$x_{t+1}=p(x_t)$, then the stage-$t+1$ trial has no correction.

For a finite solve after the same full center, assume that $x_{t+1}$ remains
positive on $A$ and zero off $A$, write
$p(x_t)-x_{t+1}=u_{t+1}\geq0$ and let
$a_{t+1}=H_Au_{t+1}$ be its end residual.  The next trial error is
\begin{equation}
 x^\star-y_{t+1}
 =S_A(x^\star-x_t)+(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})H_A^{-1}a_{t+1},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-after-full-trial}
\end{equation}
and any adjacent correction is residual-only:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\Delta_{t+1}
 \leq2(1-q)
 \norm{D_A^{-1/2}a_{t+1}}_\infty
 \leq2(1-q)C_{\mathrm{end},t+1}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-after-full-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus exact full corrections are separated, while an immediately adjacent
finite correction vanishes with the absolute polish.  This does not bound
the density or cumulative inflation of partial corrections.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum of $Q_{AA}$ lies in $[\alpha,1]$.  The corresponding scalar
eigenvalue map for $S_A$ is decreasing on this interval, so its minimum is
at least the value at the upper endpoint $1$, namely
\begin{equation*}
 (1+\beta_{\mathrm A})
 \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}{1+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
 -\beta_{\mathrm A}
 =q\frac{1-q}{1+q}=q\beta_{\mathrm A}.
\end{equation*}
The inverse of the Stieltjes matrix $H_A$ is entrywise nonnegative.
Therefore the off-diagonal entries of $S_A$ are nonnegative, while its
positive-semidefinite lower bound makes every diagonal entry nonnegative.
Both $M_A$ and $S_A$ are rational functions of $Q_{AA}$, proving
commutation.

Put $e_t=x^\star-x_t$.  Exact fixed-row optimality gives
$Q_{AA}e_t\geq0$; for the finite statement, the corresponding identity is
$Q_{AA}e_t=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,A}+a_{t,A}\geq0$.  A full correction yields
$e_{t+1}=M_Ae_t$, and the next trial error is $S_Ae_t$.  Consequently
$Q_{AA}S_Ae_t=S_AQ_{AA}e_t\geq0$, so the next extrapolate is already a
lower certificate and its correction is zero.

For the finite output, $u_{t+1}=H_A^{-1}a_{t+1}$ gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-after-full-trial}.  Applying $Q_{AA}$ and using
$Q_{AA}H_A^{-1}=I-M_A$ shows that its only possibly negative part is bounded
coordinatewise by
$(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})M_Aa_{t+1}$.  Since
$Q_{AA}D_A^{1/2}\one\geq\alpha D_A^{1/2}\one$, Stieltjes comparison gives
\begin{equation*}
 M_AD_A^{1/2}\one
 \leq\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
              {\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}D_A^{1/2}\one
 =(1-q^2)D_A^{1/2}\one.
\end{equation*}
Thus the normalized negative part is at most
$(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})(1-q^2)
\norm{D_A^{-1/2}a_{t+1}}_\infty=2(1-q)$ times that norm.
The definition of the lower retraction and
$\norm{D_A^{-1/2}a}_{\infty}\leq\langle D_A^{1/2}\one,a\rangle$
prove \cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-after-full-bound}.
\end{proof}

There is also a genuine structural regime in which the missing inflation
packing is unnecessary.

\begin{theorem}[High-Dirichlet optimal supports bypass inflation packing]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-branch}
Let the optimal support $A=S^\star(\rho)$ be nonempty and put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_A:=\lambda_{\min}(Q_{AA}),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_A:=\frac{\lambda_A}
                    {\kappa_{\mathrm A}+\lambda_A}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-parameter}
\end{equation}
For every finite safeguarded stage satisfying
$\norm{p(\ell_t)-x_{t+1}}_2\leq\xi_t$, one has the direct recursion
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x^\star-x_{t+1}}_2
 \leq(1-\vartheta_A)\norm{x^\star-x_t}_2+\xi_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-recursion}
\end{equation}
Suppose, a posteriori, that
$\vartheta_A\geq c_0q$ for an absolute $c_0>0$.  For a gate tolerance
$0<\tau\leq1$, set
\begin{equation*}
 \eta_{\mathrm{gate}}:=\frac{2\alpha\tau}{1+\alpha},
 \qquad
 \xi_t\leq\frac{c_0q\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{2},
 \qquad
 T\geq\frac1{c_0q}
       \log\frac{2}{\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}}.
\end{equation*}
Then $x_T$ passes the fresh gate
$H_\rho(x_T)\leq\alpha\tau$ and has RPPR error at most $\tau$.
No hypothesis on $\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}}$ is used.

In particular, for $\alpha<1/4$, $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, and
$\rho=\tau=\varepsilon/2$, the cached implementation on this promised
structural class has the unconditional vector
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 &(C_{\mathrm{adj}},R_{\mathrm{adj}},R_{\mathrm{int}},C_{\mathrm{pre}},
 C_{\mathrm{ctl}},C_{\mathrm{rec}},C_{\mathrm{resp}},M_{\mathrm{pers}},
 M_{\mathrm{tmp}},C_{\mathrm{mat}},C_{\mathrm{emit}})\\
 &\quad=
 (O(V_\varepsilon),O(V_\varepsilon),1,0,
 O(W_\varepsilon),O(W_\varepsilon),0,
 O(V_\varepsilon),O(V_\varepsilon),O(W_\varepsilon),\Theta(k+1)),
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-vector}
\end{equation}
where
$V_\varepsilon=\operatorname{nnz}(s)+2/\varepsilon$,
$W_\varepsilon=\operatorname{nnz}(s)
+\widetilde O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon))$,
$k\leq2/\varepsilon$, and
$C_{\mathrm{ctl}}+C_{\mathrm{rec}}+C_{\mathrm{mat}}=O(W_\varepsilon)$.
The emitted fresh gate is the one at $x_T$ above; the RPPR bias bound with
$\rho=\tau=\varepsilon/2$ gives total degree-normalized PPR error at most
$\varepsilon$.
The condition on the unknown optimal face is structural and not
algorithmically certified; hence this is not a graph-uniform solver theorem.
The low-Dirichlet case $\vartheta_A=o(q)$ remains the central open branch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On $\R^A$, the obstacle objective is $\lambda_A$-strongly convex.  Its
shifted resolvent is therefore
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}/(\kappa_{\mathrm A}+\lambda_A)
=1-\vartheta_A$ Lipschitz.  Since $p(x^\star)=x^\star$ and the safe chain
gives $x_t\leq\ell_t\leq x^\star$, one has
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{x^\star-p(\ell_t)}_2
 \leq(1-\vartheta_A)\norm{x^\star-\ell_t}_2
 \leq(1-\vartheta_A)\norm{x^\star-x_t}_2.
\end{equation*}
The finite error triangle inequality proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-recursion}.  Iteration and
$\norm{x^\star}_2\leq1$ give
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{x^\star-x_T}_2
 \leq e^{-c_0qT}+\frac{\max_t\xi_t}{c_0q}
 \leq\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}.
\end{equation*}
Because $x_T$ is a lower certificate, the same Stieltjes row bound used in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-frozen-conditional-contract} gives
\begin{equation*}
 H_\rho(x_T)
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
       \norm{D^{-1/2}(x^\star-x_T)}_\infty
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\norm{x^\star-x_T}_2
 \leq\alpha\tau.
\end{equation*}
The KKT maximum principle then proves the RPPR error statement.

Finally, \cref{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop} enforces the displayed
$\xi_t$ with only soft logarithms.  Safe-center locality confines all rows
to volume $1/\rho$, and the horizon is $\widetilde O(1/q)$.  The cached-row,
fresh-gate, materialization, and emission accounting in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-frozen-conditional-contract} therefore applies verbatim,
but without its packing hypothesis, and proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-vector}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-025 boundary shielding and the post-full spectral filter}

The discontinuity in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-retraction-shadowing-stop} concerns the raw map
$\mathcal L$ on arbitrary inputs.  The realized recurrence reaches a support
boundary with substantially more structure.  In particular, the first
retraction containing a newly positive coordinate is one-sided stable.

\begin{lemma}[Support entries are shielded by the previous end residual]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-support-entry-shield}
Consider the actual finite recurrence of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-finite-inner-identities}.  For $t\geq2$, put
\begin{equation*}
 A_t:=\supp(x_t),\qquad
 E_t:=A_t\setminus A_{t-1},\qquad
 P_t:=A_t\cap A_{t-1},
\end{equation*}
and let
\begin{equation*}
 g_t:=[\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_{-,A_t},\qquad
 G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}
 :=\norm{D_{E_t}^{-1/2}g_{t,E_t}}_\infty,
 \qquad
 G_t^{\mathrm{per}}
 :=\norm{D_{P_t}^{-1/2}g_{t,P_t}}_\infty,
\end{equation*}
where the maximum over an empty set is zero.  Then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq g_{t,E_t}\leq\beta_{\mathrm A}a_{t-1,E_t},
 \qquad
 G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}
       \norm{D_{E_t}^{-1/2}a_{t-1,E_t}}_\infty
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}C_{\mathrm{end},t-1},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-support-entry-residual-bound}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\Delta_t=\max\{G_t^{\mathrm{ent}},G_t^{\mathrm{per}}\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-entry-persistent-split}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with exact shifted solves, a newly entered row contributes
zero to its first retraction and cannot be its positive controlling row.  In
the finite recurrence, if
$G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}>G_t^{\mathrm{per}}$, then the entire common correction
is residual-controlled:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\Delta_t\leq
 \beta_{\mathrm A}C_{\mathrm{end},t-1}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-entry-dominant-delta}
\end{equation}
Thus the support-entry jump of the raw map cannot be charged as an
uncontrolled state perturbation along the realized recurrence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $i\in E_t$, support monotonicity gives
$x_{t-1,i}=\ell_{t-2,i}=0$, and hence $s_{t-1,i}=0$.  Since $x_{t,i}>0$,
one also has $s_{t,i}=x_{t,i}>0$, $\lambda_{t,i}=0$, and $a_{t,i}\geq0$.
The exact finite collapse identity
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse} therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
 g_{t,i}
 =\left[-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,i}
       +\beta_{\mathrm A}a_{t-1,i}
       -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})a_{t,i}
       -\beta_{\mathrm A}\lambda_{t-1,i}\right]_+
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}a_{t-1,i}.
\end{equation*}
Taking the normalized maximum proves the first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-support-entry-residual-bound}.  The second follows from
$\norm{D^{-1/2}a}_{\infty}\leq\langle D^{1/2}\one,a\rangle$ in the shared
unweighted no-isolate graph model.  Splitting the maximum in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-retraction} over the disjoint union
$A_t=E_t\mathbin{\dot\cup}P_t$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-persistent-split,eq:aesp-cd-entry-dominant-delta}.
For an exact solve, $a_{t-1}=0$.
\end{proof}

The same finite identity gives a one-sided stability interface on every
support, not only at an entry.  It compares corrections at the same realized
states and therefore does not iterate the ill-conditioned ambient map.

\begin{lemma}[Finite residuals create only polynomial one-sided correction excess]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-one-sided-correction-excess}
For $t\geq2$, define the residual-free same-state driver
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha\bar\Delta_t
 :=\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}
  -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+}_\infty,
 \qquad
 \bar r_t:=\min\{\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,
                  \bar\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one\},
\end{equation*}
and put
\begin{equation*}
 \delta_t:=\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}}\alpha
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}a_{t-1,A_t}}_\infty.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_t\leq\bar\Delta_t+\delta_t,
 \qquad
 0\leq[r_t-\bar r_t]_+
 \leq\delta_tD^{1/2}\one
 \quad\text{coordinatewise}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-one-sided-correction-excess}
\end{equation}
If $\vol(A_t)\leq V$ and the combined C2-plus-absolute stop in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop} is imposed at stage $t-1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{[r_t-\bar r_t]_+}_2
 \leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}{\alpha}\xi_{t-1},
 \qquad
 \frac{1+q}{q}\norm{[r_t-\bar r_t]_+}_2
 \leq\frac{1-q}{q}\frac{\mu_t}{\alpha}\xi_{t-1}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-one-sided-momentum-excess}
\end{equation}
Thus the additional correction created by a finite end residual has a
one-stage polynomial factor $O(1/(\alpha q))$, rather than the iterated
$(1+1/\alpha)^T$ factor of black-box trajectory shadowing.  The statement is
one-sided: the current residual and boundary multiplier may suppress a
same-state correction, so it is not an absolute trajectory-distance bound
and does not prove finite net packing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Coordinatewise subadditivity of the positive part in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-collapse}, after discarding the two
nonpositive terms containing $a_t$ and $\lambda_{t-1}$, gives
\begin{equation*}
 [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_{-,A_t}
 \leq\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+
 +\beta_{\mathrm A}a_{t-1,A_t}.
\end{equation*}
Taking the normalized maximum proves the bound on $\Delta_t$.  For fixed
$u\geq0$, the scalar map $z\mapsto\min\{u,z\}$ can increase by at most the
increase in $z$; applying this coordinatewise proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-one-sided-correction-excess}.  Finally,
$\norm{D^{-1/2}a_{t-1}}_\infty\leq C_{\mathrm{end},t-1}$ and the dual stop
give
\begin{equation*}
 \delta_t\sqrt V
 \leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}}\alpha
       C_{\mathrm{end},t-1}\sqrt V
 \leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}\alpha\xi_{t-1}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\norm{D_{A_t}^{1/2}\one}_2=\sqrt{\vol(A_t)}\leq\sqrt V$, this is the
first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-one-sided-momentum-excess}.  The identity
$(1+q)\beta_{\mathrm A}=1-q$ proves the second.
\end{proof}

The preceding residual term can be made harmless at the accelerated scale
without knowing how many coordinates enter.  This is a direct finite-prefix
statement, rather than trajectory shadowing.

\begin{corollary}[Entry-dominated inflation has an affordable finite ledger]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-entry-inflation-ledger}
Assume $\alpha<1/2$, so $0<q<1$, and fix a fresh-gate tolerance
$0<\tau\leq1$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 \eta_{\mathrm{gate}}:=\frac{2\alpha\tau}{1+\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Consider any pre-gate prefix of the actual finite recurrence: for every
index in the prefix,
$H_\rho(x_t)>\alpha\tau$.  Fix $\delta>0$ and enforce the locally
maintained end-mass stop
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{end},t}
 \leq\delta\alpha\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}^2q^2
 \qquad\text{at every finite shifted solve.}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-entry-end-mass-stop}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal T_{\mathrm{ent}}
 :=\{t\geq2:G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}>G_t^{\mathrm{per}}\}
\end{equation*}
be the entry-dominated stages.  Then every such stage satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \log\max\{1,\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\}\leq4\delta q,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{ent}},\,t<T}
 \log\max\{1,\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\}
 \leq4\delta qT.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-entry-inflation-bound}
\end{equation}
The bound is independent of the number of support entries.

On an envelope of volume at most $V$, the stop
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-end-mass-stop} can be imposed by the same increasing
greedy heap run as C2.  The degree work for this end-mass requirement alone
at one stage is at most
\begin{equation}
 V\left[3+\frac1{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
 \log_+\left(
 \frac{2(1-\alpha)\sqrt V}
      {\delta\alpha\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}^2q^2}
 \right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-entry-polish-work}
\end{equation}
Thus $V\leq1/\rho$ and $T=\widetilde O(1/q)$ keep this residual shielding
within $\widetilde O(1/(\rho q))$ degree work, including the coordinate
updates and their cached rekeys.  It supplies no resource vector by itself,
because the inflation on persistent-row-dominated stages remains open.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $e_t=x^\star-x_t$, $a_q=(1-q)/q$,
$c_q=(1+q)/q$, and $h_t=c_qr_t$.  The finite safe chain gives
$e_t\geq0$ and $0\leq h_t\leq a_qd_t$.  Direct expansion yields
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 =\norm{h_t}_2^2+2\langle e_t,h_t\rangle
   -2a_q\langle d_t,h_t\rangle
 \leq2\langle e_t,h_t\rangle.
\end{equation*}
At an entry-dominated stage,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-dominant-delta} and
$r_t\leq\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one$ give
\begin{equation*}
 \langle e_t,h_t\rangle
 \leq c_q\Delta_t\langle D^{1/2}\one,e_t\rangle
 \leq\frac{c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}}\alpha
       C_{\mathrm{end},t-1},
\end{equation*}
where the RPPR mass bound gives
$\langle D^{1/2}\one,e_t\rangle\leq1$.

On the other hand, the row bound used for the terminal gate gives
\begin{equation*}
 H_\rho(x_t)
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
       \norm{D^{-1/2}e_t}_\infty.
\end{equation*}
Gate failure therefore implies
$\norm{e_t}_2\geq\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}$.  Strong convexity of the Moreau
envelope gives
$\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\geq
\mu_{\mathcal E}\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}^2/2$.  If the defect is positive,
$\log\gamma\leq\gamma-1$ and the preceding bounds imply
\begin{equation*}
 \log\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 \leq
 \frac{2c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}C_{\mathrm{end},t-1}}
      {\alpha\eta_{\mathrm{gate}}^2}
 \leq4\delta q,
\end{equation*}
using $c_q\leq2/q$, $\beta_{\mathrm A}\leq1$, and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-end-mass-stop}.  A nonpositive defect contributes
zero, and summation proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-inflation-bound}.

For the work claim, the proximal warm-start mass is at most
$2(1-\alpha)\sqrt V$.  The greedy mass contraction used in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop}, with the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-end-mass-stop} as its target, proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-polish-work}.  The heap already stores the exact end
mass; no graph-wide residual scan is introduced.
\end{proof}

After a full correction, the settled-face algebra has a complementary
spectral interpretation.  It explains why the next possible reset is driven
by relatively high Dirichlet modes, but it does not commute with the
coordinatewise positive part well enough to finish the packing theorem.

\begin{proposition}[The two-stage post-full collapse is a high-pass filter]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-post-full-high-pass}
Use exact shifted solves on the settled positive face
$A=S^\star(\rho)$, and suppose the stage-$t$ correction is full:
$\ell_t=x_t$.  Put $e_t=x^\star-x_t$, $H_A=Q_{AA}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I$,
$M_A=\kappa_{\mathrm A}H_A^{-1}$, and
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_q:=\beta_{\mathrm A}(2+\beta_{\mathrm A})
 =\frac{(1-q)(3+q)}{(1+q)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-sigma}
\end{equation}
If $Q_{AA}e_t\geq0$, then
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-full-correction-separation} gives $r_{t+1}=0$.  Before
the stage-$t+2$ retraction, its exact collapse driver is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 c_{t+2}
 &:=\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t+1}
       -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_{t+2}\\
 &=C_Ae_t,
 \qquad
 C_A:=Q_{AA}H_A^{-2}
          (\sigma_qQ_{AA}-\kappa_{\mathrm A}I).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\Delta_{t+2}
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_A^{-1/2}[C_Ae_t]_+}_\infty.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter-retraction}
\end{equation}
On a $Q_{AA}$ eigenmode of eigenvalue $\lambda$, the filter coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_q(\lambda)
 =\frac{\lambda(\sigma_q\lambda-\kappa_{\mathrm A})}
        {(\kappa_{\mathrm A}+\lambda)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter-eigenvalue}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the nonnegative error $e_t$ is a single eigenmode, that
mode cannot trigger the stage-$t+2$ retraction unless
\begin{equation}
 \frac\lambda{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
 >\frac1{\sigma_q}
 =\frac{(1+q)^2}{(1-q)(3+q)}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-high-threshold}
\end{equation}
For a general nonnegative error, however, the coordinatewise map
$[C_Ae_t]_+$ mixes spectral modes.  This limitation is already exact on
$K_8$.  Take $q=1/10$, so
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha=\frac1{101},\quad
 \kappa_{\mathrm A}=\frac{99}{101},\quad
 \beta_{\mathrm A}=\frac9{11},\quad
 \sigma_q=\frac{279}{121},
\end{equation*}
and put
$e=(5693/5000,1,\ldots,1)^T$.  The two eigenvalues of $Q$ are
$1/101$ and $407/707$.  Exact decomposition into the constant and
orthogonal modes gives
\begin{equation}
 Qe=\left(\frac{40343}{505000},
          \frac1{10100},\ldots,\frac1{10100}\right)^T>0,
 \qquad
 (C_Ae)_1=\frac{21267}{121000000}>0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-k8-mixing}
\end{equation}
Thus even $Q_{AA}e_t\geq0$ and a small
$\lambda_{\min}(Q_{AA})$ do not make the positive part vanish under the
algebraic hypotheses alone.  This vector is a filter stress test, not a
claimed reachable outer state within this proposition.  Round 026 below
realizes a positive scaling of the same direction from the zero start.  The
present proposition therefore supplies an exact
local/window identity, not a graph-uniform two-step separation, cumulative
$\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}}$ bound, or solver theorem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first exact solve after the full center gives
$e_{t+1}=M_Ae_t$.  Since $r_{t+1}=0$, its extrapolated center error is
\begin{equation*}
 g_{t+1}=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})e_{t+1}
             -\beta_{\mathrm A}e_t,
\end{equation*}
and the next exact error is $e_{t+2}=M_Ag_{t+1}$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 s_{t+1}&=(I-M_A)e_t,\\
 s_{t+2}&=(I-M_A)
       ((1+\beta_{\mathrm A})M_A-\beta_{\mathrm A}I)e_t.
\end{align*}
Substitution gives
\begin{equation*}
 c_{t+2}
 =(I-M_A)
 \left[\sigma_qI-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})^2M_A\right]e_t.
\end{equation*}
Now $I-M_A=Q_{AA}H_A^{-1}$ and
$\sigma_q-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})^2=-1$.  Since all factors commute, this is
exactly \cref{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter}.  The exact collapse identity
proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter-retraction}.  Evaluating the
rational matrix function on an eigenvalue gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-post-full-filter-eigenvalue}; its sign changes only at
$\lambda=\kappa_{\mathrm A}/\sigma_q$.  If $e_t\geq0$ is one eigenmode, a
nonpositive coefficient makes $[C_Ae_t]_+=0$, proving the threshold.  No
coordinatewise conclusion follows from the signs of the modal coefficients
for a mixed error, which is the stated limitation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-026 persistent-row energy banks and a reachable pulse}

The finite residual terms in the collapse do not disappear coordinatewise.
On a row that stays active, however, they are already part of the exact
fixed-row residual $Qe_t$.  This gives a windowed square budget without
comparing a finite trajectory to an exact one.

\begin{lemma}[Persistent finite controllers have a windowed square-energy budget]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-persistent-square-ledger}
Consider the actual finite recurrence of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-finite-inner-identities}.  For $t\geq2$, retain the sets
$P_t$, the vectors $g_t$, and the quantities $G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}$ and
$G_t^{\mathrm{per}}$ from
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-support-entry-shield}, and put
\begin{equation*}
 e_t:=x^\star-x_t,
 \qquad u_t:=Qe_t,
 \qquad \mathfrak E_t^Q:=\langle e_t,Qe_t\rangle.
\end{equation*}
Then, on the persistent rows,
\begin{equation}
 g_{t,P_t}
 =\bigl[\beta_{\mathrm A}u_{t-1,P_t}
        -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u_{t,P_t}\bigr]_+
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}[Qd_t]_{+,P_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-driver-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, for every integer window $2\leq k\leq \ell$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{t=k}^{\ell}G_t^{\mathrm{per}}
 &\leq
 \frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}(1+\alpha)}2
 \mathfrak m(x_\ell-x_{k-1})
 \leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}(1+\alpha)}2,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-l1-window}\\
 \sum_{t=k}^{\ell}(G_t^{\mathrm{per}})^2
 &\leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2
 \bigl(\mathfrak E_{k-1}^Q-\mathfrak E_\ell^Q\bigr).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-square-window}
\end{align}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal T_{\mathrm{per}}[k,\ell]
 :=\{t\in\{k,\ldots,\ell\}:
       G_t^{\mathrm{per}}\geq G_t^{\mathrm{ent}}\}.
\end{equation*}
The tie is deliberately assigned to the persistent class.  On this class,
$\alpha\Delta_t=G_t^{\mathrm{per}}$, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \alpha^2\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{per}}[k,\ell]}
       \Delta_t^2
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2
       \bigl(\mathfrak E_{k-1}^Q-\mathfrak E_\ell^Q\bigr)
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-delta-square-window}
\end{equation}
All statements are for the realized finite states and retain both end
residuals in the collapse.  They require neither an exact-solve shadow
trajectory nor the C2-plus-absolute stop.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $i\in P_t$, both $x_{t-1,i}$ and $x_{t,i}$ are positive.  The active-row
identity in \cref{lem:aesp-cd-finite-inner-identities} and complementarity
therefore give
\begin{equation*}
 u_{t,i}=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,i}+a_{t,i},
 \qquad
 u_{t-1,i}=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1,i}+a_{t-1,i},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{t-1,i}=0.
\end{equation*}
Substitution into the exact finite collapse identity proves the equality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-driver-bound}.  Since $u_{t,i}\geq0$,
\begin{equation*}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}u_{t-1,i}
   -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u_{t,i}]_+
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}[u_{t-1,i}-u_{t,i}]_+
 =\beta_{\mathrm A}[Qd_t]_i{}_+,
\end{equation*}
which proves the inequality.

The shared matrix has constant diagonal
$Q_{ii}=(1+\alpha)/2$ and nonpositive off-diagonal entries.  Because
$d_t\geq0$, one has
$[Qd_t]_{i,+}\leq(1+\alpha)d_{t,i}/2$.  The graph has no isolates, so
$d_i\geq1$ and
\begin{equation*}
 G_t^{\mathrm{per}}
 \leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm A}(1+\alpha)}2
       \mathfrak m(d_t).
\end{equation*}
Summing and using
$\sum_{t=k}^{\ell}d_t=x_\ell-x_{k-1}\leq x^\star$ and
$\mathfrak m(x^\star)\leq1$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-l1-window}.

For the square estimate, $0\preceq Q\preceq I$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{g_{t,P_t}}_2^2
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\norm{Qd_t}_2^2
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\langle d_t,Qd_t\rangle.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, $\supp(d_t)\subseteq A_t$ and the finite active-row identity gives
$[Qe_t]_{A_t}=\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t,A_t}+a_{t,A_t}\geq0$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak E_{t-1}^Q-\mathfrak E_t^Q
 =2\langle d_t,Qe_t\rangle+\langle d_t,Qd_t\rangle
 \geq\langle d_t,Qd_t\rangle.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-q-energy-drop}
\end{equation}
Since $G_t^{\mathrm{per}}\leq\norm{g_{t,P_t}}_2$, summation proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-square-window}.  On the declared persistent
class, \cref{eq:aesp-cd-entry-persistent-split} gives
$\alpha\Delta_t=G_t^{\mathrm{per}}$.  The first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-persistent-delta-square-window} follows by restriction to a
subset of the window.  Finally,
$\mathfrak E_{k-1}^Q\leq\norm{e_{k-1}}_2^2\leq\norm{x^\star}_2^2\leq1$.
\end{proof}

The common cap itself is also compatible with Dirichlet energy.  This uses
the boundary-aware normalized coordinate on which the cap is a scalar
truncation, rather than an ambient Lipschitz estimate for the lower
retraction.

\begin{lemma}[The correction and surviving momentum contract $Q$-energy]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-truncation-q-energy}
For every actual finite stage, put
\begin{equation*}
 p_t:=\ell_t-x_t=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t-r_t.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \langle r_t,Qr_t\rangle
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\langle d_t,Qd_t\rangle,
 \qquad
 \langle p_t,Qp_t\rangle
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\langle d_t,Qd_t\rangle.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-truncation-q-energy}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $1\leq k\leq\ell$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{t=k}^{\ell}\langle r_t,Qr_t\rangle
 &\leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2
       (\mathfrak E_{k-1}^Q-\mathfrak E_\ell^Q),
 \notag\\
 \sum_{t=k}^{\ell}\langle p_t,Qp_t\rangle
 &\leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2
       (\mathfrak E_{k-1}^Q-\mathfrak E_\ell^Q).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-truncation-q-window}
\end{align}
These are two separate bounds; their left-hand sides are not being added
under a single copy of the right-hand side.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $w=D^{1/2}\one$, $z=D^{-1/2}d_t\geq0$, and define on
$[0,\infty)$
\begin{equation*}
 f(a):=\min\{\beta_{\mathrm A}a,\Delta_t\},
 \qquad
 \bar f(a):=\beta_{\mathrm A}a-f(a)
             =[\beta_{\mathrm A}a-\Delta_t]_+.
\end{equation*}
Then $r_{t,i}=\sqrt{d_i}f(z_i)$ and
$p_{t,i}=\sqrt{d_i}\bar f(z_i)$.  Both scalar maps vanish at zero, are
nondecreasing, and are $\beta_{\mathrm A}$-Lipschitz.  Thus, for either
$v_i=\sqrt{d_i}F(z_i)$ with $F\in\{f,\bar f\}$,
\begin{align*}
 \norm{v}_2^2
 &=\sum_i d_iF(z_i)^2
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\sum_i d_iz_i^2,
 \\
 \langle v,\mathcal Lv\rangle
 &=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(F(z_i)-F(z_j))^2
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}^2
       \sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(z_i-z_j)^2
 =\beta_{\mathrm A}^2\langle d_t,\mathcal Ld_t\rangle.
\end{align*}
Combining the two inequalities in
$Q=\alpha I+(1-\alpha)\mathcal L/2$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-truncation-q-energy}.  Summation and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-q-energy-drop} prove
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-truncation-q-window}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A reachable $K_8$ prefix separates raw defect from $Q$-energy]

 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k8-q-family-seed}

proposition does not refute a $q^{-1}$-weighted, spectrally split, or

\begin{proof}

 \alpha,
 \qquad \lambda_h=\frac{4+3\alpha}{7}.
\end{equation*}

 =\frac\alpha7(s-7\rho\one),
\end{equation*}
it is the positive optimum.

\begin{equation*}
 m_0=1-q^2,
 \qquad m_h=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
                  {\kappa_{\mathrm A}+\lambda_h},
 \qquad
 s_j=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})m_j-\beta_{\mathrm A}
 \quad(j\in\{0,h\}).
\end{equation*}
Both $s_0$ and $s_h$ lie in $(0,1)$.  The first two iterate errors have

\begin{equation*}
 \frac1{112}(m_0,12q^2m_h),
 \qquad

and $m_0s_0=1-3q^2+2q^3$.  Direct use of

 \left(\frac{99/100}{1+q^2}-\frac{18}{35}\right)>0;

\begin{equation*}
 \psi_q(\alpha)=-q^2+q^4(1+\sigma_q),
 \qquad
 \sigma_q\geq\frac{29}{10},
 \qquad

 \qquad

This proves

The two energy lemmas and the reachable pulse do not supply a bound on

windowed $Q$-energy banks do not absorb the Euclidean collateral cross term
with graph-uniform accelerated constants.  Persistent-row-dominated
inflation therefore remains the sole low-Dirichlet packing gap.

The absolute stop gives a clean but conditional finite-outer interface.
For the finite trajectory define
\begin{align*}
 z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 &:=x_t+(q^{-1}-1)(x_t-x_{t-1}),
 &\widetilde z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 &:=z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}-\frac{1+q}{q}r_t,\\
 \Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 &:=\mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
   +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
       \norm{z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}-x^\star}_2^2,
 &\mathfrak D_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 &:=\norm{\widetilde z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}-x^\star}_2^2
      -\norm{z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}-x^\star}_2^2,
\end{align*}
and, whenever $\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}>0$, put
\begin{equation*}
 \gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 :=1+\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}\mathfrak D_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}
          {2\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}.
\end{equation*}
Now put $v_{t+1}:=p(\ell_t)-x_{t+1}$ and
\begin{equation*}
 \bar z_{t+1}:=p(\ell_t)
   +(q^{-1}-1)(p(\ell_t)-x_t).
\end{equation*}
Then $z_{t+1}^{\mathrm{fin}}=\bar z_{t+1}-v_{t+1}/q$.  If
$\norm{v_{t+1}}_2\leq\xi_t\leq1$, the exact virtual step followed by this
comparison gives
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{t+1}^{\mathrm{fin}}
 \leq(1-q)\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
      +2\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t
      +\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-potential}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $0\leq\bar z_{t+1}\leq x^\star/q$ and
$\norm{x^\star}_2\leq1$.  The momentum-square comparison costs at most
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t+
\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t^2/2$.  Also
$\norm{\nabla\mathcal E(a)}_2\leq\kappa_{\mathrm A}$ for
$0\leq a\leq x^\star$.  Envelope smoothness therefore shows that replacing
$p(\ell_t)$ by $x_{t+1}$ costs at most
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t+\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t^2/2$.
Adding the two comparisons proves the displayed constants.

\subsection{Round-027 weighted-reserve boundary}

The Round-026 square banks suggest adding a reserve to the accelerated
potential.  The most direct Euclidean reserve is valid for the realized finite
sequence and retains the finite shifted-solve error, but its graph-uniform
drift is only of order $q^2$.

\begin{lemma}[The exact $q^{-1}$ Euclidean reserve has only $q^2$ drift]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-reserve}
For $t\geq1$, put $e_t:=x^\star-x_t$ and
\begin{equation*}
 \omega_q^{\mathrm E}
 :=\frac{(1-q)^2\mu_{\mathcal E}}{2q},
 \qquad
 \Psi_t^{\mathrm E}
 :=\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
   +\omega_q^{\mathrm E}\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2,
 \qquad
 \eta_t:=2\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t
          +\kappa_{\mathrm A}\xi_t^2.
\end{equation*}
Then every actual finite stage satisfying the hypothesis of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-potential} obeys
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_{t+1}^{\mathrm E}
 \leq \Psi_t^{\mathrm E}-q\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}+\eta_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-reserve}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_t^{\mathrm E}
 \leq\frac{1+q+q^2}{q}\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}},
 \qquad
 \Psi_{t+1}^{\mathrm E}
 \leq
 \left(1-\frac{q^2}{1+q+q^2}\right)\Psi_t^{\mathrm E}
 +\eta_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-drift}
\end{equation}
Thus the reserve is monotone for exact shifted solves, but its unconditional
comparison gives $O(q^{-2})$ stages rather than an accelerated
$O(q^{-1})$ stage bound.  The statement uses the realized finite states and
does not erase $a_t$, $\lambda_t$, or the finite error $\eta_t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $a_q=(1-q)/q$ and
$h_t=(1+q)r_t/q$.  The finite safe chain and the actual common retraction
still give $0\leq h_t\leq a_qd_t$ coordinatewise.  The same coordinate
expansion as in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-coordinate-defect} therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
 \leq a_q\bigl(\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2-\norm{e_t}_2^2\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Since
$\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}
=\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}+\mu_{\mathcal E}
\mathfrak D_t^{\mathrm{fin}}/2$, substitution in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-potential} proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-reserve}.

Let $v_t=z_t^{\mathrm{fin}}-x^\star$.  The definitions give the exact
identity
\begin{equation*}
 e_{t-1}=\frac{e_t+qv_t}{1-q}.
\end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2
 \leq\frac{1+q^2}{(1-q)^2}
       \bigl(\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{v_t}_2^2\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Strong convexity of the envelope gives
$2\Phi_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\geq
\mu_{\mathcal E}(\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{v_t}_2^2)$.
Multiplying the preceding bound by $\omega_q^{\mathrm E}$ proves the first
comparison in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-drift}; combining that
comparison with \cref{eq:aesp-cd-q-weighted-euclidean-reserve} proves the
second.
\end{proof}

The analogous additive reserve in unsplit $Q$-energy has a sharper local
payment, but two exact reachable families show that direct stagewise payment
and a uniform one-step accelerated comparison cannot be obtained from the
same coefficient.

\begin{proposition}[A two-family STOP for the unsplit $q^{-1}Q$-energy reserve]

\begin{align}

 &=\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2(1-q)^{2t}
   \bigl((1+tq)^2+1\bigr)S,
 \notag\\

 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k2-low-mode-reserve-data}

\begin{equation*}

 \qquad t\geq1,
\end{equation*}
put $\vartheta=1-q$ and $b_q=(1-q^2)/2$.  Its first persistent transition,

 \frac{\Psi_3^A}{\Psi_2^A}
 =\vartheta^2
   \frac{b_q\vartheta^2((1+3q)^2+1)+(A/q)(1+2q)^2}
        {b_q\vartheta^2((1+2q)^2+1)+(A/q)(1+q)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k2-low-mode-reserve-ratio}

For every $A\geq A_\star$,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq1-\frac{\Psi_3^A}{\Psi_2^A}

 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k2-low-mode-q2-stop}
\end{equation}
Therefore no absolute $c>0$ gives the one-step comparison
$\Psi_{t+1}^A\leq(1-cq)\Psi_t^A$ on all actual exact trajectories while the

 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k2-low-mode-global-bound}

For absolute $A$, this loses only $O(\log q^{-1})$ in the initial

 \label{eq:aesp-cd-k8-high-band-payment-limit}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional finite-inner acceptance]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-acceptance}
Suppose the \emph{actual finite} trajectory satisfies, at a chosen horizon
$T$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
 :=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\max\{1,\gamma_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\}
 \leq(1-c)qT+B
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-actual-finite-net-packing}
\end{equation}
for declared constants $0<c\leq1$ and $B\geq0$, and use a common absolute
tolerance $\xi_t\leq\xi\leq1$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
 \leq e^{-cqT+B}\Phi_0^{\mathrm{fin}}
       +3\kappa_{\mathrm A}T\xi
          e^{\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-bound}
\end{equation}
The zero start obeys $\Phi_0^{\mathrm{fin}}\leq\alpha$.  Hence, for a target
$0<\theta\leq\alpha$, the following choices depend only on declared
parameters and are sufficient:
\begin{equation}
 T\geq\frac{B+\log(2\alpha/\theta)}{cq},
 \qquad
 \xi\leq
 \frac{\theta\exp(-(1-c)qT-B)}
      {6\kappa_{\mathrm A}T}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-choices}
\end{equation}
The extra absolute polish in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop} then contributes only logarithmic factors
in $1/\xi$.  Neither C2 nor the exact-only cone certificate proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-actual-finite-net-packing}; a residual-stability or direct
finite-sequence packing theorem is still required.  Therefore the
accelerated rate-to-gate, complete accelerated work ledger, and any
accelerated eleven-resource vector remain conditional at this point outside
the high-Dirichlet structural branch in
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-branch}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The mass bound $\mathfrak m(x^\star)\leq1$ implies
$\norm{x^\star}_2\leq1$.  Also $\norm{b}_2\leq\alpha$ and
\begin{equation*}
 F_\rho(x)\geq\frac\alpha2\norm{x}_2^2
             -\alpha\norm{x}_2\geq-\frac\alpha2.
\end{equation*}
Since $\mathcal E(0)\leq F_\rho(0)=0$,
$\mathcal E(x^\star)=F_\rho(x^\star)$, and
$\mu_{\mathcal E}\leq\alpha$, this proves
$\Phi_0^{\mathrm{fin}}\leq\alpha$.

Iterate \cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-inner-potential}.  The unperturbed product is
at most $\exp(-qT+\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}})$, and every suffix product
following an additive error is at most
$\exp(\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}})$.  Since
$2\xi+\xi^2\leq3\xi$, this proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-bound}.  Use
$\mathcal J_T^{\mathrm{fin}}\leq(1-c)qT+B$ and the initial bound above.
The two choices in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-choices} then make
the two terms at most $\theta/2$ each.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Frozen conditional rate-to-gate contract]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-frozen-conditional-contract}
Assume \cref{eq:aesp-cd-actual-finite-net-packing} holds for the actual finite
trajectory with declared $c,B$, and enforce both finite-inner stops in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-dual-inner-stop}.  For a terminal tolerance
$0<\tau\leq1$, put
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{\mathrm{gate}}
 :=\frac{2\mu_{\mathcal E}\alpha^2\tau^2}{(1+\alpha)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-gate-threshold}
\end{equation}
It is sufficient to take
\begin{equation}
 T\geq\frac{B+\log(2\alpha/\theta_{\mathrm{gate}})}{cq},
 \qquad
 \xi\leq\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{gate}}
       \exp(-(1-c)qT-B)}{6\kappa_{\mathrm A}T}\right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-finite-parameters}
\end{equation}
Then $\Phi_T^{\mathrm{fin}}\leq\theta_{\mathrm{gate}}$, and the fresh
unshifted gate
\begin{equation}
 H_\rho(\ell_T)
 :=\norm{D^{-1/2}[c_\rho-Q\ell_T]_+}_\infty
 \leq\alpha\tau
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-terminal-gate}
\end{equation}
holds.  It certifies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(\ell_T-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty\leq\tau.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-terminal-error}
\end{equation}
The obstacle comparison
$0\leq x^\star(0)-x^\star(\rho)\leq\rho D^{1/2}\one$ therefore gives total
error at most $\rho+\tau$ from the unregularized target.

For the following resource specialization, additionally assume
$c\geq c_0>0$ for an absolute constant and
$B=\operatorname{polylog}(\alpha^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1},
\operatorname{nnz}(s)+2/\varepsilon)$.
Conditionally on that frozen-strength packing bound, choose
$0<\varepsilon\leq1$, $\rho=\tau=\varepsilon/2$, and $\alpha<1/4$.  With
\begin{equation*}
 V_\varepsilon:=\operatorname{nnz}(s)+\frac2\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 W_\varepsilon:=\operatorname{nnz}(s)
   +\widetilde O\left(\frac1{\sqrt\alpha\,\varepsilon}\right),
 \qquad k\leq\frac2\varepsilon,
\end{equation*}
the cached-row implementation has the conditional resource vector
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 &(C_{\mathrm{adj}},R_{\mathrm{adj}},R_{\mathrm{int}},C_{\mathrm{pre}},
 C_{\mathrm{ctl}},C_{\mathrm{rec}},C_{\mathrm{resp}},M_{\mathrm{pers}},
 M_{\mathrm{tmp}},C_{\mathrm{mat}},C_{\mathrm{emit}})\\
 &\quad=
 (O(V_\varepsilon),O(V_\varepsilon),1,0,
 O(W_\varepsilon),O(W_\varepsilon),0,
 O(V_\varepsilon),O(V_\varepsilon),O(W_\varepsilon),\Theta(k+1)),
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-conditional-vector}
\end{equation}
with the joint charge
$C_{\mathrm{ctl}}+C_{\mathrm{rec}}+C_{\mathrm{mat}}=O(W_\varepsilon)$.
The vector is conditional only because its accelerated rate-to-gate uses the
unproved finite net packing assumption; it is not attached as an
unconditional implementation theorem.

For $1/4\leq\alpha\leq1$, no packing assumption is needed.  With the same
$\rho=\tau=\varepsilon/2$, zero-start unshifted greedy coordinate descent
stopped at $H_\rho(x)\leq\alpha\tau$ uses degree work at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\tau}\leq\frac5\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-fallback-work}
\end{equation}
The gate and bias comparison again give total error at most $\varepsilon$,
and the same resource vector holds unconditionally with
$W_\varepsilon=\operatorname{nnz}(s)+O(1/\varepsilon)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{cor:aesp-cd-conditional-finite-acceptance} with
$\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{gate}}$.  Since
$\ell_T\geq x_T$ and both points lie below $x^\star$, the Stieltjes signs and
$Q_{ii}=(1+\alpha)/2$ give
\begin{equation*}
 H_\rho(\ell_T)
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
       \norm{D^{-1/2}(x^\star-x_T)}_\infty
 \leq\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
       \sqrt{\frac{2\Phi_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{\mu_{\mathcal E}}}.
\end{equation*}
The definition of $\theta_{\mathrm{gate}}$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-terminal-gate};
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error} proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-frozen-terminal-error}.  The bias comparison is the same
normalized Stieltjes maximum principle applied to the two obstacle systems.

For completeness, the implementation ledger is as follows.  Ingest the
sparse seed, its labels, and its degrees once at initialization; do not rescan
the full seed at every outer stage.  Cache an adjacency row when its
coordinate first becomes positive in a warm start or update.  Every such
coordinate lies in $S^\star(\rho)$, so total cached row incidence is at most
$1/\rho$; distinct boundary labels and degrees have the same volume bound.
Every adaptive new-row or boundary-degree discovery batch is counted in
$R_{\mathrm{adj}}$, and $C_{\mathrm{adj}}$ counts every returned degree and
row incidence, including the constant initial and terminal seed passes.
The shifted inner heap stores every positive normalized key together with the
exact mass $C$.  Updating coordinate $i$ deterministically rekeys
$\{i\}\cup N(i)$ from the cached row, with a fixed label order breaking ties.
Warm starts, outer retractions, and shifted-key rebuilds are cached supported
products and are included in $C_{\mathrm{ctl}}+C_{\mathrm{rec}}$.

The terminal gate is not read from that shifted heap.  It is one fresh
unshifted product over
$\supp(\ell_T)\cup N(\supp(\ell_T))\cup\supp(s)$, accumulated from cached
active rows; outside this coverage its key is zero, so no boundary adjacency
row is scanned.  This single terminal certificate accounts for
$R_{\mathrm{int}}=1$, while $C_{\mathrm{pre}}=C_{\mathrm{resp}}=0$.
Persistent storage is the cached rows, labels, degrees, heap, exact mass,
and the active $x_{t-1},x_t,\ell_t,d_t$ state vectors; temporary storage is
one supported product and rekey frontier.  Every active-state cell write
during warm starts, coordinate updates, retractions, rekeys, and supported
products is charged to $C_{\mathrm{mat}}$; this includes one final
materialization of the terminal certificate.  Emitting its $k$ labeled
coordinates plus status is $\Theta(k+1)$.  The inner bounds above,
$T=\widetilde O(1/q)$, and
$q=\Theta(\sqrt\alpha)$ prove both the displayed componentwise vector and the
joint work charge.  Finally
$k\leq\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$.

For the fallback, \cref{cor:aesp-cd-zero-start} gives the displayed work.
The same monotone support and cache argument applies, the fresh unshifted
gate invokes \cref{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error}, and the bias comparison completes
the unconditional large-$\alpha$ branch.
\end{proof}

Outside the high-Dirichlet promised class, the accelerated rate for
\emph{reaching} a terminal gate is conditional on
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-actual-finite-net-packing}.  Correctness of a fresh
unshifted KKT gate follows unconditionally from
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error}, and the large-$\alpha$ zero-start fallback in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-zero-start} is likewise independent of packing: for
$\alpha\geq1/4$ and terminal tolerance $\varepsilon$, its work is
$O(1/\varepsilon)$.  Cached-row accounting is also orthogonal bookkeeping.
What cannot yet be attached graph-uniformly is a complete accelerated
rate-to-gate ledger or its eleven-resource vector on low-Dirichlet supports.

It is tempting to hope that every safe correction decreases the momentum
distance, so that $\mathfrak D_t\leq0$ and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} closes the proof.  The three-vertex
path already rules this out.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise momentum nonexpansion is false]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-momentum-expansion}
Take the three-vertex path, use an endpoint seed, and set
$\alpha=1/100$ and $\rho=1/1000$.  For the exact recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the correction at $t=3$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_3
 =\frac{
 105528900258547165289
 -31691781844105995447\sqrt{11}}
 {7072772362029000000}
 \in(0.0592,0.0593).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-three-path-defect}
\end{equation}
Hence the safeguard can increase the classical estimate-sequence momentum
distance even though all primal iterates and centers remain monotone below
$x^\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the path degrees $(1,2,1)$,
\begin{equation*}
 Q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 101/200&-99/(200\sqrt2)&0\\
 -99/(200\sqrt2)&101/200&-99/(200\sqrt2)\\
 0&-99/(200\sqrt2)&101/200
 \end{pmatrix},
 \quad
 c_\rho=
 \begin{pmatrix}999/100000\\-\sqrt2/100000\\-1/100000\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Here $q=1/\sqrt{99}=\sqrt{11}/33$.  The first three shifted obstacle
solutions are strictly positive, so they are obtained by solving their
$3\times3$ linear systems.  At $t=3$, only the endpoint has negative
one-sided trial residual; applying
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction,eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states} and simplifying
in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{11})$ gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-three-path-defect}.  The displayed interval follows, for
example, from $3.3166<\sqrt{11}<3.3167$.
\end{proof}

Thus the strictly outer problem is now sharper than generic perturbation
control.  By \cref{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}, it is enough to pack the
normalized collateral charges in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.  The unit mass ledger
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger} and the Euclidean telescope
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope} are insufficient by themselves: a proof
must additionally exploit the sharp-collapse identity
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse}, which says that corrections are generated by
fast proximal-displacement modes.  This is the precise remaining potential
lemma.

\begin{corollary}[Accelerated work on a certified envelope]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope}
Assume a fixed envelope $U$ contains the optimal RPPR support, so minimizing
$F_\rho$ over $\R^U$ has the same solution and value as the full problem.
Run strongly convex Catalyst on this restricted objective with
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters} and use
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} at every stage.  To obtain
$F_\rho(x^{(T)})-F_\rho(x^\star)\leq\epsobj$, it is sufficient to take
\begin{equation*}
 T\leq
 \left\lceil\frac{2}{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}
 \log\left(\frac{2(F_\rho(x^{(0)})-F_\rho(x^\star))}{\epsobj}\right)
 \right\rceil.
\end{equation*}
The total degree work is consequently
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(\frac{\mathcal V_U}{\sqrt\alpha}
                  \log\frac1{\epsobj}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-certified-envelope-work}
\end{equation}
If an oracle supplies $U=S^\star(\rho)$, then
$\mathcal V_U\leq1/\rho$ and the bound becomes
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(\frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
                  \log\frac1{\epsobj}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-support-oracle-work}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2} is the relative Catalyst criterion of
\citet{lin2018catalyst}.  With
$\chi_{\mathrm A}=\alpha/(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm A}=\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}/(2-\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}})$,
the standard strongly convex Catalyst estimate is
\begin{equation*}
 F_\rho(x^{(T)})-F_\rho(x^\star)
 \leq2\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}{2}\right)^T
       (F_\rho(x^{(0)})-F_\rho(x^\star)).
\end{equation*}
Combine this estimate with \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}.  All factors
other than $\mathcal V_U/\sqrt\alpha$ are logarithmic in the displayed
parameters.  The last claim uses the RPPR support-volume theorem.
\end{proof}

This closes the start-mass and outer-rate interaction on every fixed certified
envelope, while \cref{thm:aesp-cd-explored-volume} gives an oracle-free
trajectory-dependent result.  It also explains why the absolute interface
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface} loses a power of the final tolerance:
it uses a one-shot $C^2/\varphi$ estimate where the relative oracle instead
uses geometric mass contraction.  For unrestricted Catalyst, the remaining
graph-uniform algorithmic task is to control the automatically explored
envelope.  The safeguarded recurrence \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer} already
supplies the stronger alternative: its envelope stays inside
$S^\star(\rho)$ and its stage-start masses telescope.  Its separate remaining
task is exactly the correction-defect amortization in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}.

\begin{corollary}[Zero-start RPPR coordinate descent]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-zero-start}
For the unshifted objective, $C_\rho(0)\leq\alpha$.  Coordinate descent from
zero, stopped when $R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)<\alpha\epskkt$, therefore
returns
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(x-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty<\epskkt
\end{equation*}
with degree work at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\epskkt}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-zero-start-work}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At zero, the only KKT violations are seed coordinates and
\begin{equation*}
 C_\rho(0)
 =\alpha\sum_i(s_i-\rho d_i)_+
 \leq\alpha\sum_i s_i=\alpha.
\end{equation*}
Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work} with $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=0$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$, and
$\epsin=\alpha\epskkt$.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation record and remaining open question}

Any experiment for this proposal must record the shared graph and work model,
$\alpha$, $\rho$, $\epskkt$, $\epsin$, random seed, stopping formula, and code
version.  It should report both $R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)$ and the KKT-mass
decrease diagnostics.

\Cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass,thm:aesp-cd-inner-work,cor:aesp-cd-plugin} close the
inner local-monotonicity and oracle-correctness tasks.
\Cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle,cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope} additionally
close the interaction between start mass and the accelerated outer rate on a
fixed certified envelope.  For the implementable safeguarded continuation,
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-outer-ledger} now proves oracle-free envelope control,
monotone support, and a dimension-free telescoping start-mass bound.  The
high-Dirichlet case is closed directly by
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-high-dirichlet-branch}; the remaining central task is the
low-Dirichlet outer perturbation estimate isolated by
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-estimate-defect,lem:aesp-cd-multiplicative-ledger}.
Therefore this note does not yet claim the target
$\softO(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ for a fully implementable graph-uniform
algorithm: the Euclidean progress telescope alone loses one factor
$1/\sqrt\alpha$, and \cref{prop:aesp-cd-momentum-expansion} rules out
pointwise nonexpansion.  Moreover,
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-fails} shows that dividing that
Euclidean progress by the current error to form a logarithmic telescope still
loses an unbounded factor $1/q$.  The positive-log upper bound
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative} reduces a complete proof to packing
the normalized collateral charges at accelerated scale.  This remains an
a posteriori proof obligation because the charge uses $x^\star$; no local
certificate or cumulative bound is claimed.  By
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-singleton-corrections}, a raw count of correction calls
cannot be substituted: it can grow logarithmically with accuracy even when
there is only one support discovery, while all of its normalized inflation
terms vanish.
