\section{Scope and status}

This standalone note records a proposed Accelerated Evolving Set Process with
local proximal coordinate descent (AESP-CD) for regularized PageRank.  It
separates the shared problem definition, proved implementation facts, and the
remaining outer-locality target.  The coordinate-level theory is closed: the
minimum-norm KKT mass contracts under every exact proximal coordinate update,
including sign changes and zero-coordinate clipping, and the KKT diagnostic
certifies degree-normalized solution error.  These statements give a rigorous
local inner oracle for composite AESP.  They do not bound the KKT mass of an
arbitrary extrapolated Catalyst center.  A relative-accuracy continuation
below proves that, on any fixed certified envelope of degree volume $V$, the
inner work is only $\softO(V)$ per outer stage and hence
$\softO(V/\sqrt\alpha)$ overall.  Taking the envelope to be the optimal RPPR
support gives $V\leq1/\rho$; discovering such an envelope locally without an
oracle remains open.  The same heap implementation does, however, admit an
oracle-free bound in terms of the maximum volume it actually explores; this
reduces the remaining universal question to controlling that explored volume.
For any certified lower center, a comparison argument controls it
automatically: the full inner trajectory stays in the optimal RPPR support
and costs $\softO(1/\rho)$.  The unresolved step is therefore strictly outer:
retaining accelerated continuation while the centers are kept order-safe.  A
safeguarded extrapolation analyzed below makes the iterates and their supports
monotone and, crucially, makes the cumulative KKT start mass telescope with no
$1/\alpha$ loss.  Its only remaining defect is an explicit positive momentum
correction whose cumulative degree-weighted mass is at most one.  An exact
Moreau-envelope calculation below shows how that correction enters the
Nesterov potential and pays it from monotone Euclidean progress, but also
exhibits a three-vertex path on which the pointwise potential defect is
positive.  A multiplicative ledger nevertheless bounds the inflation of the
classical potential by two per correction and reduces the target to cumulative
log-inflation.  A two-vertex exact family below has a singleton support but a
nonzero correction every other stage, proving that the raw correction count
cannot be charged to support additions or made accuracy-independent.  All of
those corrections have nonpositive potential defect, however, so their
normalized log-inflation is zero.  A positive-log lemma now bounds every
$\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}$ by the normalized remaining error on coordinates
clipped collaterally by the common retraction.  It may overcharge a benign
collateral round, and its clipping amplitude has the exact sharp-collapse
form only for $t\geq2$.  The final lemma must pack those collateral charges
over time, not merely count correction calls.  A second exact two-vertex
family shows that the monotone Euclidean log-error cannot provide that
packing with an $\alpha$-independent constant: one harmful collateral charge
is order $q$, whereas the corresponding Euclidean log-progress is only order
$q^2$.  This leaves a genuinely multistep collapse-aware potential or a
different one-sided surrogate as the live route.
Nothing here changes the repository-wide residual decision.

\section{Problem formulation}

\input{../../tex/shared/source_aligned_problem}

This note specializes the shared model to $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$ and solves the
same composite objective $F_\rho$.  Its final, note-scoped diagnostic target is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)}{\alpha}\leq\epskkt.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-target}
\end{equation}
This is not identified with $\epsobj$, $\epspg$, or $\epsppr$.

\section{KKT diagnostic}

Define the note-scoped minimum KKT-violation vector
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rho,i}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\nabla_i f(x)+\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},&x_i>0,\\
\nabla_i f(x)-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},&x_i<0,\\
\nabla_i f(x)-\operatorname{clip}
  \bigl(\nabla_i f(x),-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},
                    \alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\bigr),&x_i=0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-vector}
\end{equation}
and the scalar diagnostic
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)
  :=\norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_\rho(x)}_\infty.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
The implication from this diagnostic to a solution-error norm is a required
lemma proved below, but it is not an adopted repository-wide stopping
convention.

\section{AESP outer framework}

At outer iteration $t$, the proposed method approximately minimizes
\begin{equation}
  F_{\rho,t}(z):=F_\rho(z)
  +\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}{2}
       \norm{z-y^{(t-1)}}_2^2,
  \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm A}:=1-2\alpha,
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-subproblem}
\end{equation}
with AESP-scoped momentum
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\mathrm A}
  :=\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha}-\sqrt\alpha}
          {\sqrt{1-\alpha}+\sqrt\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-momentum}
\end{equation}
The inner initialization is $z_t^{(0)}=y^{(t-1)}$.

\section{Inner local proximal coordinate descent}

The smooth part of the inner objective has gradient
\begin{equation}
  \nabla f_t(z)
  =(Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I)z-b
    -\kappa_{\mathrm A}y^{(t-1)}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-inner-gradient}
\end{equation}
Define $\kappa_t(z)$ from \cref{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-vector} by replacing
$\nabla f$ with $\nabla f_t$, and set
\begin{equation}
  C_t(z):=\norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_t(z)}_1.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-kkt-mass}
\end{equation}

Let
\begin{equation}
  H_t:=Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I,
  \qquad
  \mu_t:=\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-hessian}
\end{equation}
Then $H_t$ has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t I\preceq H_t,
  \qquad
  H_tD^{1/2}\one=\mu_tD^{1/2}\one.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-properties}
\end{equation}

For coordinate $i$, exact proximal coordinate descent uses
\begin{equation}
  z_i^+
  =\prox_{(\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}/\ell)\abs{\cdot}}
     \left(z_i-\frac{\nabla_i f_t(z)}{\ell}\right),
  \qquad
  \ell=(Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I)_{ii}
      =\frac{1+\alpha}{2}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-coordinate-update}
\end{equation}
Only coordinate $i$ and its neighbors require gradient updates, so the shared
work charge for this update is $d_i$.

\section{KKT certificate and local monotonicity}

\begin{lemma}[KKT diagnostic certifies inner solution error]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error}
Let $z_t^\star$ be the unique minimizer of $F_{\rho,t}$.  Then every
$z\in\R^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}(z-z_t^\star)}_\infty
  \leq
  \frac{1}{\mu_t}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_t(z)}_\infty.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-inner-error}
\end{equation}
In particular, for the unshifted RPPR objective,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{D^{-1/2}(x-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty
  \leq \frac{R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)}{\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-error}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose subgradients $\xi\in\partial\norm{D^{1/2}z}_1$ and
$\xi^\star\in\partial\norm{D^{1/2}z_t^\star}_1$ so that
\begin{equation*}
  \kappa_t(z)=H_t(z-z_t^\star)
  +\alpha\rho(\xi-\xi^\star).
\end{equation*}
The coordinate definition of $\kappa_t$ is precisely the minimum-magnitude
valid choice at $z$, while optimality supplies a choice with zero residual at
$z_t^\star$.

Put $e=z-z_t^\star$ and choose $i$ maximizing
$M:=\abs{e_i}/\sqrt{d_i}$.  If $M=0$, the result is immediate.  Otherwise,
the normalized maximum principle and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-shifted-properties} give
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)(H_te)_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq \mu_tM.
\end{equation*}
Coordinatewise monotonicity of the weighted absolute-value subdifferential
gives
$e_i(\xi_i-\xi_i^\star)\geq0$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)\kappa_{t,i}(z)}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq \mu_tM,
\end{equation*}
which proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-inner-error}.  Set
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}=0$ for \cref{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-error}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact KKT-mass decrease]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-mass}
For a sequential proximal coordinate update at coordinate $i$,
\begin{equation}
  C_t(z^+)
  \leq C_t(z)
  -\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)},
  \qquad
  \tau_{\mathrm{cd}}
  :=\frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})}
          {1+\alpha+2\kappa_{\mathrm A}}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-mass-target}
\end{equation}
Under $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-2\alpha$, the displayed coefficient is $2/3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\lambda_i:=\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}$ and
$\Delta_i:=z_i^+-z_i$.  Exact scalar proximal minimization makes the new
coordinate KKT violation zero and gives
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{t,i}(z^+)=0,
  \qquad
  \ell\abs{\Delta_i}\leq\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-displacement}
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows directly from the three scalar soft-threshold
cases: the update stays on the same nonzero face, reaches zero, or crosses to
the opposite face.  Equality holds in the first case; thresholding can only
shorten the displacement in the latter two.

For every $j\neq i$, the value $z_j$ is unchanged.  As a function of its
smooth gradient, the minimum KKT violation is either a translate
$g\mapsto g\pm\lambda_j$ on a nonzero face or the soft-threshold map
$g\mapsto g-\operatorname{clip}(g,-\lambda_j,\lambda_j)$ at zero.  Each map
is one-Lipschitz.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \abs{\kappa_{t,j}(z^+)}
 \leq
 \abs{\kappa_{t,j}(z)}+\abs{H_{t,ji}\Delta_i}.
\end{equation*}
Only neighbors of $i$ have a nonzero off-diagonal entry, and
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{j\neq i}\sqrt{d_j}\abs{H_{t,ji}\Delta_i}
 =\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\Delta_i}.
\end{equation*}
Using \cref{eq:aesp-cd-displacement}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 C_t(z^+)
 &\leq C_t(z)-\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}
       +\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\Delta_i}\\
 &\leq C_t(z)
 -\left(1-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\ell}\right)
  \sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}.
\end{align*}
Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 1-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\ell}
 =\frac{2(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})}
        {1+\alpha+2\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
 =\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}.
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stieltjes-quadratic inner locality]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 \Psi(z)=\frac12 z^\top Hz-c^\top z
          +\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_j\abs{z_j},
 \qquad \lambda_j\geq0,
\end{equation*}
where $H$ is symmetric positive definite and $H_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$.
Suppose there are $\upsilon\in\R_{++}^n$ and $\mu>0$ such that
$H\upsilon\geq\mu\upsilon$ coordinatewise.  Let $\kappa_\Psi(z)$ be the
minimum-magnitude KKT vector, and define
\begin{equation*}
 C_\upsilon(z):=\sum_j\upsilon_j\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,j}(z)},
 \qquad
 \theta_i:=\frac{(H\upsilon)_i}{H_{ii}\upsilon_i}.
\end{equation*}
Then $0<\mu/H_{ii}\leq\theta_i\leq1$, and an exact proximal update of
coordinate $i$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_\upsilon(z^+)
 \leq C_\upsilon(z)
       -\theta_i\upsilon_i\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-mass}
\end{equation}
If $z_\Psi^\star$ minimizes $\Psi$, then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\operatorname{diag}(\upsilon)^{-1}
             (z-z_\Psi^\star)}_\infty
 \leq\frac1\mu
 \norm{\operatorname{diag}(\upsilon)^{-1}
             \kappa_\Psi(z)}_\infty.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-error}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta_i=z_i^+-z_i$.  Exact scalar proximal minimization gives
$\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z^+)=0$ and
$H_{ii}\abs{\Delta_i}\leq\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}$.  The minimum KKT map
at every untouched coordinate is one-Lipschitz in its smooth gradient, so
\begin{align*}
 C_\upsilon(z^+)
 &\leq C_\upsilon(z)-\upsilon_i\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}
       +\sum_{j\neq i}\upsilon_j\abs{H_{ji}\Delta_i}\\
 &\leq C_\upsilon(z)
       -\left(\upsilon_i
         -\frac{\sum_{j\neq i}\upsilon_j\abs{H_{ji}}}{H_{ii}}\right)
        \abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}\\
 &=C_\upsilon(z)-\theta_i\upsilon_i
        \abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}.
\end{align*}
The last identity uses symmetry and the nonpositive off-diagonal entries.
Those same signs show $\theta_i\leq1$, while
$H\upsilon\geq\mu\upsilon$ gives
$\theta_i\geq\mu/H_{ii}>0$.

For the error certificate, put $e=z-z_\Psi^\star$ and choose $i$ maximizing
$M:=\abs{e_i}/\upsilon_i$.  The weighted maximum principle gives
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\operatorname{sign}(e_i)(He)_i}{\upsilon_i}
 \geq M\frac{(H\upsilon)_i}{\upsilon_i}\geq\mu M.
\end{equation*}
Monotonicity of each absolute-value subdifferential makes its contribution
nonnegative after multiplication by $\operatorname{sign}(e_i)$.  Therefore
$\abs{\kappa_{\Psi,i}(z)}/\upsilon_i\geq\mu M$, which proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-stieltjes-error}.
\end{proof}

The PageRank result is the specialization of
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes} with
$H=H_t$, $\upsilon=D^{1/2}\one$, and
$H_t\upsilon=\mu_t\upsilon$.  Since every diagonal entry is $\ell$, one has
$\theta_i=\mu_t/\ell=\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}$ for every coordinate.  Thus the
proved inner-locality mechanism applies not only to RPPR, but also to shifted
RPPR subproblems and, more generally, to separable $\ell_1$ regularization of
any Stieltjes quadratic admitting a positive supersolution.  It does not by
itself control how an outer extrapolation chooses its successive starts.

Thus the composite analogue of the ordinary-PPR residual-mass contraction is
valid for arbitrary signed iterates; no nonnegativity hypothesis is needed.

\section{Inner stopping proposal}

The proposed active set is
\begin{equation}
  S_t^{(k)}
  :=\left\{i:
       \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z^{(k)})}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
       \geq\epsin
     \right\}.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-active-set}
\end{equation}
If an oracle does not expose $m=|E|$, the simple-graph bound
$m\leq n(n-1)/2$ is a correctness-preserving but potentially conservative
choice when calibrating an inner objective certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Local inner-oracle work]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work}
Starting from $z^{(0)}$, repeatedly update any coordinate in
$S_t^{(k)}$ until this set is empty.  Then the method terminates with
\begin{align}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 &\leq
 \frac{C_t(z^{(0)})}{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\epsin},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-work}\\
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(z^{(K_t)}-z_t^\star)}_\infty
 &<\frac{\epsin}{\mu_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-output-error}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)
 \leq
 \frac{\epsin C_t(z^{(0)})}{2\mu_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-output-gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently, choosing
\begin{equation}
 \epsin=\frac{2\mu_t\varphi_t}{C_t(z^{(0)})}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-phi-threshold}
\end{equation}
is a valid local oracle for the AESP inner requirement
$F_{\rho,t}(z)-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)\leq\varphi_t$, with work
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 \leq
 \frac{C_t(z^{(0)})^2}
      {2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}\mu_t\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every active update has
$\sqrt{d_i}\abs{\kappa_{t,i}(z)}\geq\epsin d_i$.
Sum \cref{eq:aesp-cd-mass-target} and use nonnegativity of $C_t$ to prove
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-work}.  At termination, the KKT diagnostic is below
$\epsin$, so \cref{lem:aesp-cd-kkt-error} proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-output-error}.

The function $F_{\rho,t}$ is $\mu_t$-strongly convex and
$\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})\in\partial F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)
 \leq\frac{\norm{\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_2^2}{2\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
At termination and by \cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass},
\begin{align*}
 \norm{\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_2^2
 &\leq
 \norm{D^{-1/2}\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_\infty
 \norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_t(z^{(K_t)})}_1\\
 &<\epsin C_t(z^{(K_t)})
 \leq\epsin C_t(z^{(0)}).
\end{align*}
This proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-output-gap}; substitution yields
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work}.
\end{proof}

For the AESP shift $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-2\alpha$, one has
$\mu_t=1-\alpha$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=2/3$.  Thus
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-gap-work} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{K_t-1}d_{i_k}
 \leq
 \frac{3C_t(z^{(0)})^2}{4(1-\alpha)\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-specialized-work}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Composite AESP plug-in]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-plugin}
Suppose a composite AESP outer loop accepts its stage-$t$ inner output when
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}(z^{(K_t)})-F_{\rho,t}(z_t^\star)\leq\varphi_t
\end{equation*}
and initializes the inner solve at $z_t^{(0)}=y^{(t-1)}$.  If
$C_t(y^{(t-1)})=0$, the initial point is already optimal.  Otherwise,
thresholded exact coordinate descent with
\begin{equation*}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{in},t}
 =\frac{2(1-\alpha)\varphi_t}{C_t(y^{(t-1)})}
\end{equation*}
satisfies every such inner-oracle condition, even when the extrapolated
centers are signed.  Across any $T$ outer stages its total degree work obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_{\mathrm{inner}}(T)
 \leq\frac{3}{4(1-\alpha)}
       \sum_{t=1}^T
       \frac{C_t(y^{(t-1)})^2}{\varphi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work} independently at every stage and sum
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-specialized-work}.  The signed-center claim follows because
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass} made no sign assumption.
\end{proof}

\Cref{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface} is the precise interface supplied to
the outer acceleration proof.  It removes inner correctness and sign
monotonicity from the open problem: a graph-uniform end-to-end theorem now
requires a bound on the displayed cumulative start-mass functional, or an
outer safeguard that bounds it while retaining accelerated convergence.

\section{Outer-locality continuation: relative accuracy}
\label{sec:aesp-cd-relative}

The absolute-gap interface above is valid but too coarse to preserve the
accelerated locality target.  This section first records the obstruction and
then replaces the raw center by the standard composite Catalyst warm start.

\begin{lemma}[The shift cancels at the raw center]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-center-cancel}
At every outer stage,
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_t(y^{(t-1)})=\kappa_\rho(y^{(t-1)}),
 \qquad
 C_t(y^{(t-1)})
 =\norm{D^{1/2}\kappa_\rho(y^{(t-1)})}_1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-center-cancel}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitution into \cref{eq:aesp-cd-inner-gradient} gives
$\nabla f_t(y^{(t-1)})=Qy^{(t-1)}-b=\nabla f(y^{(t-1)})$.
The coordinatewise minimum-magnitude subgradient choices are consequently
identical.
\end{proof}

There is no dimension-free inequality that upper-bounds the right-hand side
of \cref{eq:aesp-cd-center-cancel} by the current objective gap.  Indeed, for
the one-dimensional Stieltjes objective
\begin{equation*}
 \Psi(w)=\frac{h}{2}w^2+\lambda\abs w,
 \qquad h,\lambda>0,
\end{equation*}
the minimizer is zero, while for $w>0$,
\begin{equation*}
 \kappa_\Psi(w)=hw+\lambda,
 \qquad
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_\Psi(w)}^2}{\Psi(w)-\Psi(0)}
 =\frac{(hw+\lambda)^2}{hw^2/2+\lambda w}
 \longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\text{as }w\downarrow0.
\end{equation*}
Thus a proof that merely combines a decaying outer objective gap with the raw
KKT mass cannot close the cumulative bound.  The discontinuity is caused by
a nonzero point approaching an $\ell_1$ kink; it is not a graph-volume issue.

We next give the relative-accuracy repair.  Let $U\subseteq V$ be a fixed
coordinate envelope, put
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal V_U:=\vol(U),
\end{equation*}
and restrict every vector and objective to coordinates in $U$.  A matrix-vector
product with $Q_{UU}$ is charged at most $\mathcal V_U$, since scanning the
adjacency lists of $U$ also exposes every internal entry and cut edge.  Write
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm A}:=1+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=2(1-\alpha),
 \qquad
 \chi_{\mathrm A}:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
               =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm A}:=
 \frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}{2-\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters}
\end{equation}
For a stage center $y\in\R^U$, let $p_U(y)$ minimize the restricted shifted
objective $F_{\rho,t}^U$.  Define the composite proximal warm start
\begin{equation}
 u_U(y):=\prox_{g_{\rho,U}/L_{\mathrm A}}
 \left(y-\frac{\nabla f_{t,U}(y)}{L_{\mathrm A}}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Smoothed start-mass bound]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}
The restricted minimum KKT mass at \cref{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(u_U(y))
 \leq 2(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\mathcal V_U}
       \norm{y-p_U(y)}_2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $u=u_U(y)$ and $H_U=Q_{UU}+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I_U$.  Proximal
optimality supplies a subgradient for which the shifted KKT vector at $u$ is
\begin{equation*}
 (L_{\mathrm A}I_U-H_U)(y-u)=(I_U-Q_{UU})(y-u).
\end{equation*}
The minimum KKT vector can only have smaller coordinate magnitudes.  The
matrix $I-Q$ is entrywise nonnegative and, with
$v=D^{1/2}\one$, satisfies $(I-Q)v=(1-\alpha)v$.  Restriction to $U$ can
only decrease its weighted column sums, so
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(u)
 \leq(1-\alpha)\norm{D_U^{1/2}(y-u)}_1
 \leq(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\mathcal V_U}\norm{y-u}_2.
\end{equation*}
The forward map in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-smoothed-start} is nonexpansive because
$0\preceq I_U-H_U/L_{\mathrm A}\preceq I_U$, and the proximal map is
nonexpansive.  Since $p_U(y)$ is a fixed point of their composition,
$\norm{u-p_U(y)}_2\leq\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.  The triangle inequality now gives
$\norm{y-u}_2\leq2\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Greedy KKT-mass contraction in degree work]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}
Starting from any $z^{(0)}\in\R^U$, choose at each update
\begin{equation*}
 i_k\in\argmax_{i\in U}
       \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,U,i}(z^{(k)})}}{\sqrt{d_i}}.
\end{equation*}
If $W_K:=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}d_{i_k}$, then
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(z^{(K)})
 \leq C_{t,U}(z^{(0)})
       \exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}W_K}{\mathcal V_U}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The principal system satisfies
$H_UD_U^{1/2}\one\geq\mu_tD_U^{1/2}\one$.  Hence
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-stieltjes} gives at least the PageRank contraction
$\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=\mu_t/\ell$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 \max_{i\in U}
 \frac{\abs{\kappa_{t,U,i}(z)}}{\sqrt{d_i}}
 \geq\frac{C_{t,U}(z)}{\mathcal V_U}.
\end{equation*}
The chosen update therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(z^+)
 \leq C_{t,U}(z)
       \left(1-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}d_i}{\mathcal V_U}\right)
 \leq C_{t,U}(z)
       \exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}d_i}{\mathcal V_U}\right).
\end{equation*}
Multiplication over the updates proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The greedy key can be maintained in a heap.  A coordinate update changes the
smooth gradient, and hence the KKT key, only at the updated vertex and its
neighbors.  Its data-structure overhead is therefore logarithmic per touched
edge and is hidden by $\softO$; no repeated scan of all of $U$ is required.

For the relative Catalyst condition at center $y$, require
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rho,t}^U(z)-F_{\rho,t}^{U,\star}
 \leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}}2
             \norm{z-y}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-c2}
\end{equation}
The following theorem makes this condition locally certifiable and bounds
its work.

\begin{theorem}[Local relative-accuracy oracle]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle}
Initialize at $u_U(y)$ and run the greedy updates of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear} until
\begin{equation}
 C_{t,U}(z)^2
 \leq\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t
       \norm{z-y}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop}
\end{equation}
Then \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2} holds.  Including the proximal warm start, the
degree work of the stage is at most
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_U\left[
 2+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
 \log_+\left(
  \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_U}
       {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
 \right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strong-convexity subgradient bound gives, for every $z$,
\begin{equation*}
 F_{\rho,t}^U(z)-F_{\rho,t}^{U,\star}
 \leq\frac{\norm{\kappa_{t,U}(z)}_2^2}{2\mu_t}
 \leq\frac{C_{t,U}(z)^2}{2\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} implies
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2}.

Put $r=\norm{y-p_U(y)}_2$.  If $r=0$, the proximal warm start is already
optimal.  Otherwise, it is sufficient to reduce the KKT mass until
\begin{equation*}
 C_{t,U}(z)^2
 \leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}{4}r^2.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the preceding gap bound and strong convexity then give
$\norm{z-p_U(y)}_2\leq r/2$, so
$\norm{z-y}_2\geq r/2$ and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} follows.
By \cref{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass}, the squared ratio between the initial
mass and this sufficient target is at most
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_U}
      {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}.
\end{equation*}
Now apply \cref{eq:aesp-cd-greedy-linear}.  Crossing the target can overshoot
the corresponding work budget by at most one coordinate cost, which is at
most $\mathcal V_U$.  The proximal warm start costs another
$\mathcal V_U$, yielding \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}.
\end{proof}

The fixed envelope in \cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} is useful for a
graph-uniform corollary, but it is not required to implement the method.  For
a full-graph stage, let $E_t$ be the union of the supports of the center and
proximal displacement, every coordinate whose KKT
key becomes nonzero, and every coordinate updated before
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop} holds.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}:=\vol(E_t),
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}
 :=\max_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{theorem}[Oracle-free explored-volume bound]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-explored-volume}
Run full-graph strongly convex Catalyst with parameters
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters}.  At every stage, compute the proximal
warm start locally, maintain all nonzero normalized KKT keys in a heap, run
the greedy coordinate updates, and stop by
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-stop}.  Then each inner output satisfies the full
relative condition \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2}, and the total degree work through
$T$ stages is at most
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^T \mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
 \left[
  3+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
  \log_+\left(
   \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}}
        {\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
  \right)\right].
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work}
\end{equation}
Consequently, reaching objective gap $\epsobj$ costs
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(
   \frac{\mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}}{\sqrt\alpha}
   \log\frac1{\epsobj}
 \right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-explored-rate}
\end{equation}
No support oracle or graph-wide scan is used in this trajectory-dependent
statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a sparse $z$ and center $y$, a zero coordinate outside
$\supp(z)\cup N(\supp(z))\cup\supp(y)\cup\supp(b)$ has zero smooth gradient
and hence zero minimum KKT key.  Initially, the proximal displacement is
found by scanning the center support and its incident edges; entries of the
sparse source require only scalar accesses unless they enter the displacement.
Thereafter an exact coordinate update changes KKT keys only at the
updated coordinate and its neighbors.  Thus the heap contains every nonzero
key and its maximum is the global maximum; newly exposed coordinates are
discovered while scanning already charged incident edges.

The proof of \cref{lem:aesp-cd-smoothed-mass} on the full graph uses
Cauchy--Schwarz only on the support of the proximal displacement, whose
volume is at most $\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}$.  At every later update, the
support of the KKT mass also has volume at most
$\mathcal V_t^{\mathrm{exp}}$.  Hence the proofs of
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-greedy-linear,thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} apply
retrospectively with this explored volume.  Two local scans initialize the
proximal point and its heap keys, and the last coordinate update may overshoot
the mass budget by one coordinate cost; these give the constant three in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work}.

Every stage satisfies the full relative Catalyst condition, so the outer
rate used in \cref{cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope} applies without
restriction.  Bound every explored volume in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-explored-work} by
$\mathcal V_{\max}^{\mathrm{exp}}$ and use the
$\bigO(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsobj))$ stage count.
\end{proof}

The explored-volume theorem becomes graph-uniform as soon as the outer center
is an order-safe lower point.  This is the exact sense in which the solved
inner-locality problem applies to the safe-envelope program.  Define the
one-sided RPPR residual
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x)
 :=b-Qx-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one.
\end{equation*}
Call $\ell$ a \emph{lower certificate} if $\ell\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)\geq0
 \qquad\text{for every }i\in\supp(\ell).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate}
\end{equation}
Here $[v]_-:=\max\{-v,0\}$ coordinatewise.

\begin{lemma}[Local lower retraction]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}
For any signed finite-support trial point $a$, put $A:=\supp(a)$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(a):=\frac1\alpha
  \norm{D_A^{-1/2}
    [\zeta_{\rho,A}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)]_-}_\infty,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(a):=[a-\Delta(a)D^{1/2}\one]_+.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal L(a)$ is a lower certificate and hence
$0\leq\mathcal L(a)\leq x^\star(\rho)$.  Constructing it requires only the
trial support and its incident edges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $w=D^{1/2}\one$, $\Delta=\Delta(a)$, and
$\ell=\mathcal L(a)$.  If $\ell_i>0$, then $i\in A$,
$\ell_i=a_i-\Delta w_i$, and $\ell_j\geq a_j-\Delta w_j$ for every $j$.
The off-diagonal signs of $Q$ and $Qw=\alpha w$ give
\begin{align*}
 (Q\ell)_i
 &\leq(Q(a-\Delta w))_i
   =(Qa)_i-\alpha\Delta w_i,\\
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)
 &\geq\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)
       +\alpha\Delta w_i\geq0.
\end{align*}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate} holds.  Stieltjes obstacle
comparison with the RPPR KKT system gives the last claim.  Evaluating
$\zeta_{\rho,A}^{\mathrm{low}}(a)$ scans only adjacency lists in $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Safe-center inner locality]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}
Let $\ell$ satisfy \cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate}, and let
$S^\star(\rho):=\supp(x^\star(\rho))$.  Consider the full-graph shifted
subproblem centered at $\ell$, and write $p(\ell)$ for its minimizer.  Then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\ell\leq p(\ell)\leq x^\star(\rho),
 \qquad
 \supp(p(\ell))\subseteq S^\star(\rho).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the proximal warm start and every subsequent greedy exact
coordinate iterate satisfy the same sandwich with $p(\ell)$ in place of
$x^\star(\rho)$.  No coordinate outside $S^\star(\rho)$ ever has a nonzero
minimum KKT key.  Hence the full-graph heap implementation needs no support
oracle and reaches the relative stopping rule using degree work at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac1\rho\left[
 2+\frac{1}{2\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}}
 \log_+\left(
  \frac{16(1-\alpha)^2}
       {\rho\,\delta_{\mathrm A}\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mu_t}
 \right)\right],
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-center-work}
\end{equation}
apart from constant-time accesses to the sparse source.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the nonnegative orthant, the RPPR objective is the obstacle quadratic with
linear term $c_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one$.  The standard Stieltjes
comparison principle applied to
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-certificate} gives $\ell\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
The shifted minimizer is nonnegative: replacing a vector by its coordinatewise
absolute value cannot increase the Stieltjes quadratic, the weighted
$\ell_1$ term, or its squared distance to $\ell\geq0$.

Put $H:=Q+\kappa_{\mathrm A}I$ and
$d:=c_\rho+\kappa_{\mathrm A}\ell$.  The shifted obstacle residual is
$R(z):=d-Hz$, and
$R(\ell)=\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)$.  Thus $\ell$ is a subsolution on
its positive coordinates.  Conversely, the RPPR KKT conditions and
$x^\star(\rho)\geq\ell$ give
\begin{equation*}
 Hx^\star(\rho)-d
 =Qx^\star(\rho)-c_\rho
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x^\star(\rho)-\ell)\geq0,
\end{equation*}
so $x^\star(\rho)$ is a supersolution of the shifted obstacle problem.
Stieltjes comparison between the subsolution, its solution, and the
supersolution proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-sandwich}.

At a positive coordinate of $\ell$, the lower-certificate residual is
nonnegative.  At a zero coordinate, $Q\ell-b\leq0$ because $Q$ has
nonpositive off-diagonal entries and $b\geq0$.  The composite proximal warm
start therefore has the explicit orthant form
\begin{equation*}
 u=\ell+\frac1{L_{\mathrm A}}
       [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)]_+\geq\ell.
\end{equation*}
The forward--backward map is order preserving because
$I-H/L_{\mathrm A}$ is entrywise nonnegative.  Since $p(\ell)$ is its fixed
point, $u\leq p(\ell)$.  Furthermore, for every $i\in\supp(u)$,
\begin{equation*}
 R_i(u)
 =\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)
  -\frac1{L_{\mathrm A}}
    \sum_j H_{ij}[\zeta_{\rho,j}^{\mathrm{low}}(\ell)]_+
 \geq0,
\end{equation*}
using $H_{ii}\leq L_{\mathrm A}$ and $H_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$.
Thus $u$ is again a lower subsolution.

From any lower subsolution $z\leq p(\ell)$, a nonzero minimum KKT key points
in the increasing direction.  Its exact coordinate update increases that
coordinate to the one-dimensional obstacle minimizer, preserves the lower
subsolution inequalities, and remains below $p(\ell)$ by coordinatewise
Stieltjes comparison.  Induction proves the claimed sandwich for the entire
greedy trajectory.

Finally take $i\notin S^\star(\rho)$.  Along this trajectory
$z_i=p_i(\ell)=\ell_i=0$, and
\begin{equation*}
 R_i(z)-R_i(p(\ell))
 =-\sum_{j\neq i}H_{ij}(z_j-p_j(\ell))\leq0.
\end{equation*}
Obstacle optimality gives $R_i(p(\ell))\leq0$, hence $R_i(z)\leq0$.
The opposite signed $\ell_1$ direction cannot activate either, because at
such a zero coordinate the smooth gradient is nonpositive.  Therefore its
minimum KKT key is zero.  All displacement and update scans are confined to
$S^\star(\rho)$.  Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} retrospectively
with $U=S^\star(\rho)$ and use
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$ to obtain
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-center-work}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Safeguarded outer continuation and a telescoping start-mass ledger}

The lower retraction can be combined with extrapolation without losing the
safe support invariant.  The following exact construction isolates what this
does, and does not, yet prove about acceleration.  For vectors, $a\vee b$
denotes the componentwise maximum, and
$\mathfrak m(a):=\norm{D^{1/2}a}_1$ when $a\geq0$.

\begin{lemma}[Lattice closure of lower certificates]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-lower-max}
If $a$ and $c$ are lower certificates, then $a\vee c$ is a lower
certificate and satisfies $a\vee c\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $q=a\vee c$.  If $q_i>0$, choose $h\in\{a,c\}$ with $q_i=h_i$.
Since $q_j\geq h_j$ for every $j$ and $Q_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$,
$(Qq)_i\leq(Qh)_i$.  Therefore
$\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(q)\geq
\zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(h)\geq0$.  The comparison conclusion in
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-lower-retraction} gives $q\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
\end{proof}

Initialize $x_{-1}=x_0=0$ and $\ell_{-1}=0$.  For $t\geq0$, define the
exact safeguarded recurrence
\begin{align}
 d_t&:=x_t-x_{t-1},
 &y_t&:=x_t+\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,\notag\\
 \ell_t&:=x_t\vee\mathcal L(y_t),
 &x_{t+1}&:=p(\ell_t),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}
\end{align}
where $p(\ell_t)$ is the exact shifted minimizer from
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}.  Write
\begin{equation}
 r_t:=y_t-\ell_t,
 \qquad
 s_t:=x_t-\ell_{t-1} \quad(t\geq1),
 \qquad s_0:=0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-defects}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Safe outer ledger]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-safe-outer-ledger}
The recurrence \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer} is graph-local and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq x_{t-1}\leq\ell_{t-1}\leq x_t\leq\ell_t
 \leq x_{t+1}\leq x^\star(\rho).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-chain}
\end{equation}
Consequently every support in the recurrence is contained in
$S^\star(\rho)$ and has volume at most $1/\rho$.

More strongly, let $C_{t+1}(x_t)$ denote the minimum KKT mass at $x_t$ for
the shifted subproblem centered at $\ell_t$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 C_{t+1}(x_t)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}\mathfrak m(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1}),
 \qquad t\geq1,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-exact}
\end{equation}
while $C_1(x_0)\leq\alpha$.  Hence, for every $T\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=0}^T C_{t+1}(x_t)
 \leq\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t=0}^T C_{t+1}(x_t)^2\leq(1-\alpha)^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-ledger}
\end{equation}
Thus the cumulative stage-start mass itself has no volume factor and no
$1/\alpha$ penalty.

Finally, if $\Delta_t:=\Delta(y_t)$ is the retraction magnitude in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction}, then
\begin{align}
 r_t
 &=\min\{\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t,\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one\}
 \quad\text{componentwise},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction}\\
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(r_t)&\leq\beta_{\mathrm A},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger}\\
 \alpha\Delta_t
 &=\kappa_{\mathrm A}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 \bigl[\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}
       -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t\bigr]_+}_\infty,
 \qquad A_t:=\supp(x_t),\quad t\geq2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse}
\end{align}
In particular, after the first extrapolation a correction can be triggered
only where the current proximal displacement has fallen below
$\beta_{\mathrm A}/(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})<1/2$ of the preceding one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t=0$, one has $x_{-1}=\ell_{-1}=x_0=\ell_0=0$, and
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center} gives $0\leq x_1\leq x^\star(\rho)$.
Now let $t\geq1$ and suppose inductively that $\ell_{t-1}$ is a lower
certificate.  By
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center},
$\ell_{t-1}\leq x_t\leq x^\star(\rho)$.  On every positive coordinate of
$x_t$, shifted optimality gives
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{\rho,i}^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x_{t,i}-\ell_{t-1,i})\geq0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack}
\end{equation}
Thus $x_t$ is a lower certificate.  Both $\mathcal L(y_t)$ and $x_t$ are
lower certificates, so \cref{lem:aesp-cd-lower-max} proves the same statement
for $\ell_t$.  A second application of
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center} yields \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-chain}.
The support-volume conclusion follows from
$\vol(S^\star(\rho))\leq1/\rho$.  All operations used to construct
$\mathcal L(y_t)$ scan only the current support and its incident edges, and
the shifted solve is local by \cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-center}.

The formula for $\ell_t$ and $d_t\geq0$ give
\begin{equation*}
 \ell_t=x_t+
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t-\Delta_tD^{1/2}\one]_+.
\end{equation*}
In particular, $\supp(\ell_t)=\supp(x_t)$.  At a positive coordinate of
$x_t$, subtracting the two shifted centers in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack} shows that the KKT vector for the next
subproblem at $x_t$ is
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}(\ell_{t-1}-\ell_t)$.  At a zero coordinate,
$\ell_{t-1,i}=\ell_{t,i}=0$.  Shifted obstacle optimality gives
$Qx_t-b+\alpha\rho D^{1/2}\one\geq0$ there, while the Stieltjes signs and
$b\geq0$ give $Qx_t-b\leq0$; hence the signed $\ell_1$ KKT key is zero.
This proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-exact} for $t\geq1$.  At the initial
point the shift cancels and the seed normalization gives
$C_1(x_0)=C_\rho(0)\leq\alpha$.

The center increments are nonnegative and telescope.  Moreover
$\mathfrak m(x^\star(\rho))\leq1$: multiplying its active KKT equations by
$D^{1/2}\one$ and using
$Q_{AA}D_A^{1/2}\one\geq\alpha D_A^{1/2}\one$ gives
$\alpha\mathfrak m(x^\star(\rho))
\leq(D_A^{1/2}\one)^Tb_A\leq\alpha$.
It follows that
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1})
 =\mathfrak m(\ell_T)\leq1.
\end{equation*}
Together with the initial mass bound, this proves the first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-start-ledger}; the second follows from
$\sum_t a_t^2\leq(\sum_t a_t)^2$ for $a_t\geq0$.

Subtracting the displayed formula for $\ell_t$ from
$y_t=x_t+\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction}.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{t=0}^T \mathfrak m(r_t)
 \leq\beta_{\mathrm A}\sum_{t=0}^T\mathfrak m(d_t)
 =\beta_{\mathrm A}\mathfrak m(x_T)\leq\beta_{\mathrm A},
\end{equation*}
which is \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger}.

It remains to identify when a correction occurs after the initial shifted
solve.  Since the residual map is affine and the coefficients of $y_t$ sum
to one,
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)
 =(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_t)
  -\beta_{\mathrm A}
     \zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(x_{t-1}).
\end{equation*}
On a coordinate positive in both iterates,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-prox-slack} identifies the two residuals with
$\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_t$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}$.  If the coordinate
is new at time $t\geq2$, shifted obstacle optimality at time $t-1$ gives a
nonpositive old residual, so neither side of the corresponding negative-part
identity is positive.  Hence, on $A_t$,
\begin{equation*}
 [\zeta_\rho^{\mathrm{low}}(y_t)]_-
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}
   [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}
     -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+.
\end{equation*}
Substitution in the definition of $\Delta_t$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse} and the final claim.
\end{proof}

The theorem removes the cumulative start-mass obstruction in a stronger
form than a per-stage volume estimate: the exact warm-start masses telescope
over an arbitrarily long run.  It also suppresses the tree-wave mechanism,
because supports cannot deactivate.  What it does not prove is that replacing
the Catalyst center $y_t$ by $\ell_t=y_t-r_t$ preserves the classical
accelerated estimate.  Exact shifted optimality can be written as
\begin{equation}
 0\in\partial F_\rho(x_{t+1})
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}(x_{t+1}-y_t)
   +\kappa_{\mathrm A}r_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-safe-perturbed-app}
\end{equation}

The exact effect on the accelerated potential can be exposed through the
Moreau envelope
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E(a):=\min_z\left\{
 F_\rho(z)+\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}2\norm{z-a}_2^2
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-moreau-envelope}
\end{equation}
Write $p(a)$ for the unique minimizer in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-moreau-envelope}.  Put
\begin{equation*}
 q:=\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\mathcal E}:=\kappa_{\mathrm A}\chi_{\mathrm A}
                  =\kappa_{\mathrm A}q^2.
\end{equation*}
The envelope is $\kappa_{\mathrm A}$-smooth and
$\mu_{\mathcal E}$-strongly convex, with minimizer $x^\star(\rho)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact estimate-sequence correction defect]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-estimate-defect}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence, define
\begin{align}
 z_t&:=x_t+\left(\frac1q-1\right)d_t,
 &\widetilde z_t
 &:=z_t-\frac{1+q}{q}r_t,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states}\\
 \Phi_t&:=\mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
       +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
        \norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2,
 &\mathfrak D_t
 &:=\norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
   -\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-potential-defect}
\end{align}
Then the safe center and the next momentum state obey the exact Nesterov
relations
\begin{align}
 \ell_t&=\frac{x_t+q\widetilde z_t}{1+q},
 \qquad
 x_{t+1}=\ell_t-\frac1{\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
                    \nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov}\\
 z_{t+1}
 &=(1-q)\widetilde z_t+q\ell_t
   -\frac q{\mu_{\mathcal E}}\nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{t+1}
 \leq(1-q)\Phi_t
   +\frac{(1-q)\mu_{\mathcal E}}2\mathfrak D_t.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $t\geq1$, with $e_t:=x^\star-x_t$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_t
 \leq\frac{1-q}{q}
 \left(\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2-\norm{e_t}_2^2\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope}
\end{equation}
Thus the correction defect is paid by monotone Euclidean progress, but the
black-box payment carries one factor $1/q=\Theta(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ too many for
an immediate accelerated contraction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The standard resolvent identities for an $\alpha$-strongly convex function
give
\begin{equation*}
 \nabla\mathcal E(a)
 =\kappa_{\mathrm A}(a-p(a)),
 \qquad
 L_{\mathcal E}\leq\kappa_{\mathrm A},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\mathcal E}
 =\frac{\alpha\kappa_{\mathrm A}}
        {\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A}}.
\end{equation*}
Since
$\beta_{\mathrm A}=(1-q)/(1+q)$, substitution of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states} gives the first identity in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov}.  The second is the definition of the
exact shifted minimizer.  Using
$d_{t+1}=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_t-r_t
-\nabla\mathcal E(\ell_t)/\kappa_{\mathrm A}$ in the definition of
$z_{t+1}$ gives \cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}.

Equations
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-corrected-nesterov,eq:aesp-cd-corrected-estimate-state}
are exactly one strongly convex Nesterov step initialized by
$(x_t,\widetilde z_t)$.  Combining smooth descent from $\ell_t$, convexity
between $\ell_t$ and $x_t$, and strong convexity between $\ell_t$ and
$x^\star$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal E(x_{t+1})-\mathcal E(x^\star)
 +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2\norm{z_{t+1}-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq(1-q)\left[
 \mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
 +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
       \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \right].
\end{equation*}
Adding and subtracting the uncorrected momentum distance proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}.

For the defect bound, put
\begin{equation*}
 a:=\frac{1-q}{q},
 \qquad
 h_t:=\frac{1+q}{q}r_t.
\end{equation*}
By \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction},
$0\leq h_t\leq a d_t$ coordinatewise.  Since
$z_t-x^\star=-e_t+ad_t$, direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak D_t
 &=\norm{h_t}_2^2+2\ip{e_t}{h_t}
    -2a\ip{d_t}{h_t}\\
 &\leq2a\ip{e_t}{d_t}\\
 &\leq a\left(\norm{e_{t-1}}_2^2-\norm{e_t}_2^2\right),
\end{align*}
where the first inequality uses $0\leq h_t\leq ad_t$ and $e_t\geq0$, and the
second uses $e_{t-1}=e_t+d_t$.  This is
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope}.
\end{proof}

The additive form \cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} is not the only
useful ledger.  Because the safeguard moves each momentum coordinate toward
the current monotone iterate, its loss can also be recorded multiplicatively.

\begin{lemma}[Multiplicative correction ledger]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-multiplicative-ledger}
Whenever $\Phi_t>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_t
 :=1+\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}\mathfrak D_t}{2\Phi_t}
 =\frac{
   \mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star)
   +\frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
      \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2}
  {\Phi_t}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-correction-inflation}
\end{equation}
Then $0\leq\gamma_t\leq2$, and $\gamma_t=1$ whenever $r_t=0$.
Consequently, for every $T\geq1$ before termination,
\begin{align}
 \Phi_T
 &\leq(1-q)^T\Phi_0\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_t,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-product-ledger}\\
 &\leq2^{N_T}(1-q)^T\Phi_0,
 \qquad
 N_T:=\card{\{0\leq t<T:r_t\neq0\}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-count-ledger}
\end{align}
More precisely, with
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-log-inflation}
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_T\leq\exp(-qT+\mathcal I_T)\Phi_0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-log-ledger}
\end{equation}
Thus any graph-uniform estimate
$\mathcal I_T=\softO(\log(\Phi_0/\epsobj))$ retains an
$\softO(q^{-1}\log(\Phi_0/\epsobj))$ outer iteration bound.  The cruder
correction count $N_T$ may replace $\mathcal I_T$ when it has the same type of
bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The safe chain and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction} imply
\begin{equation*}
 x_t\leq\widetilde z_t\leq z_t
 \qquad\text{coordinatewise}.
\end{equation*}
For a scalar, the squared distance to $x_i^\star$ is convex, so its maximum
on the interval $[x_{t,i},z_{t,i}]$ occurs at an endpoint.  Therefore
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\widetilde z_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq\norm{x_t-x^\star}_2^2
     +\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2.
\end{equation*}
Strong convexity of $\mathcal E$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\mu_{\mathcal E}}2
 \norm{x_t-x^\star}_2^2
 \leq\mathcal E(x_t)-\mathcal E(x^\star).
\end{equation*}
Substitution in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-correction-inflation} proves
$0\leq\gamma_t\leq2$.  If $r_t=0$, then
$\widetilde z_t=z_t$ and $\gamma_t=1$.  Rewriting
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} as
\begin{equation*}
 \Phi_{t+1}\leq(1-q)\gamma_t\Phi_t
\end{equation*}
and iterating proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-product-ledger,eq:aesp-cd-count-ledger}.  Finally,
$\log(1-q)\leq-q$ and
$\log\gamma_t\leq\log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}$ give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-log-ledger}.  If some $\gamma_t=0$, the one-step inequality
already gives $\Phi_{t+1}=0$, so the subsequent claims are immediate.
\end{proof}

The positive part of this ledger admits a sharper localization.  A correction
is called \emph{collateral} at coordinate $i$ when the common retraction clips
that coordinate even though its extrapolate is still below the optimum.

\begin{lemma}[Positive inflation is charged only to collateral clipping]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}
For $t\geq1$ before termination, put
\begin{equation*}
 e_t:=x^\star-x_t,
 \qquad
 a_q:=\frac{1-q}{q},
 \qquad
 c_q:=\frac{1+q}{q},
 \qquad
 h_t:=c_qr_t,
\end{equation*}
and define the collaterally clipped set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 :=\{i:r_{t,i}>0\ \text{and}\ y_{t,i}<x_i^\star\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-set}
\end{equation}
The note-scoped normalized collateral charge is
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 :=\frac{
  \displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}}
  \min\{e_{t,i}^2,\,2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}\}}
 {\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \log\max\{1,\gamma_t\}
 \leq\mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 \leq
 \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}}e_{t,i}^2}
      {\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2}
 \leq1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-positive-only-charge}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T
 \leq\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}
\end{equation}
For $t\geq2$, every quantity $h_{t,i}$ in the numerator has the exact
collapse representation
\begin{equation}
 h_{t,i}
 =\min\left\{
 a_qd_{t,i},
 \frac{c_q\kappa_{\mathrm A}}{\alpha}\sqrt{d_i}
 \norm{D_{A_t}^{-1/2}
 [\beta_{\mathrm A}s_{t-1}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})s_t]_+}_\infty
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-collapse}
\end{equation}
Thus \cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative} upper-bounds only the positive
log term using collateral coordinates, rather than counting all nonzero
corrections.  The analytical charge can nevertheless be positive on a benign
collateral round; for $t\geq2$ its clipping amplitude has the exact
sharp-collapse form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The definitions of $z_t$ and $\widetilde z_t$ give the coordinate
decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_t
 =\sum_i\mathfrak D_{t,i},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak D_{t,i}
 =h_{t,i}^2+2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}
   -2a_qd_{t,i}h_{t,i}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-coordinate-defect}
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction},
$0\leq h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$.  If $h_{t,i}>0$ and
$y_{t,i}\geq x_i^\star$, then
$\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{t,i}\geq e_{t,i}$ and
\begin{equation*}
 a_qd_{t,i}
 =c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{t,i}
 \geq c_qe_{t,i}\geq2e_{t,i}.
\end{equation*}
The last comparison is strict when $e_{t,i}>0$; when $e_{t,i}=0$,
$h_{t,i}>0$ implies $a_qd_{t,i}>0$, so in either case
$a_qd_{t,i}>2e_{t,i}$.  It follows from
$h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$ that
$\mathfrak D_{t,i}<0$.  Hence only indices in
$\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}$ can contribute positively.  On every such
index, again using $h_{t,i}\leq a_qd_{t,i}$,
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D_{t,i}
 \leq2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}-h_{t,i}^2
 \leq\min\{e_{t,i}^2,2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}\}.
\end{equation*}
The second inequality uses
$e_{t,i}^2-(2e_{t,i}h_{t,i}-h_{t,i}^2)
=(e_{t,i}-h_{t,i})^2\geq0$.

Strong convexity of the Moreau envelope gives
\begin{equation*}
 2\Phi_t
 \geq\mu_{\mathcal E}
 \left(\norm{e_t}_2^2+\norm{z_t-x^\star}_2^2\right).
\end{equation*}
If $\gamma_t\leq1$, the first inequality in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-positive-only-charge} is immediate.  Otherwise,
$\mathfrak D_t>0$, and $\log\gamma_t\leq\gamma_t-1$ together with the
preceding coordinate and strong-convexity bounds proves it.  The other two
inequalities follow directly from the numerator in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}.  Since $r_0=0$, summing proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.

Finally,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction,eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse} and
$c_q\beta_{\mathrm A}=a_q$ give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-collapse}.
\end{proof}

This is an a posteriori proof charge, not a locally computable certificate:
the set \cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-set} uses the unknown optimum.  It removes
the need to count every correction uniformly, but it may overcharge a benign
collateral round and does not yet bound the cumulative collateral fraction in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.  That packing problem is the
remaining outer lemma.

A first attempt to pack the charge is to divide the Euclidean progress
telescope in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope} by the current squared error.
This would suggest an estimate of the form
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^m \mathfrak C_t^{\mathrm{col}}
 \leq K\log\frac{\norm{e_0}_2^2}{\norm{e_m}_2^2}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-candidate}
\end{equation}
with an absolute, $\alpha$-independent constant $K$.  The next exact family
rules out this particular normalization, already on the first two nontrivial
stages.

\begin{proposition}[Euclidean log-error does not pack the collateral charge]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-fails}
Let $G$ consist of one edge, seed vertex $1$, fix $\rho=9/20$, and choose
\begin{equation}
 0<q\leq\frac1{100},
 \qquad
 \alpha:=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-parameters}
\end{equation}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the RPPR optimum has full support, while the
first two shifted solutions have the form
\begin{align}
 x^\star
 &=\left(\frac1{20}+\frac\alpha2,
          \frac1{20}-\frac\alpha2\right),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-optimum}\\
 x_1&=(u_1,0),
 &u_1&=\frac{11q^2}{30},\notag\\
 x_2&=(u_2,0),
 &u_2&=\frac{11q^2(7-4q)}{90}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates}
\end{align}
There is no correction at stage $1$, whereas stage $2$ is a full correction:
\begin{equation}
 r_1=0,
 \qquad
 r_2=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_2,
 \qquad
 d_2=(\delta,0),
 \qquad
 \delta:=\frac{22q^2(1-q)}{45}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-correction}
\end{equation}
Moreover, this second correction is collateral and harmful.  More precisely,
put
\begin{equation*}
 e:=x_1^\star-u_2,
 \qquad
 h:=a_q\delta=\frac{22q(1-q)^2}{45},
 \qquad
 E_j:=\norm{x^\star-x_j}_2^2.
\end{equation*}
Then $\mathcal B_2^{\mathrm{col}}=\{1\}$, $e>2h$, and
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak D_2&=h(2e-h)>0,
 &\gamma_2&>1,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-defect}\\
 \mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}
 &=\frac{2eh}
 {E_2+(e-h)^2+(x_2^\star)^2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-charge}
\end{align}
As $q\downarrow0$ through the admissible interval,
\begin{align}
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 \frac{\mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}{q}
 &=\frac{44}{9},
 &
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 \frac1{q^2}\log\frac{E_1}{E_2}
 &=\frac{88}{9},
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-local-limits}\\
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 q\frac{\mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}
         {\log(E_1/E_2)}
 &=\frac12,
 &
 \lim_{q\downarrow0}
 q\frac{\mathfrak C_1^{\mathrm{col}}+
         \mathfrak C_2^{\mathrm{col}}}
        {\log(E_0/E_2)}
 &=\frac27.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-packing-limits}
\end{align}
Consequently no $\alpha$-independent constant $K$ can make either the
pointwise charge by $\log(E_1/E_2)$ or the cumulative candidate
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-candidate} valid for all admissible runs.
More generally, any coefficient $K(q)=o(1/q)$ fails, including every
polylogarithmic dependence on $1/\alpha$.
This refutes only packing by the monotone Euclidean log-error.  It does not
refute a cumulative accelerated-scale bound using the collapse history, a
different potential, or a locally checkable surrogate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For this graph, with
$\lambda:=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $m:=(1-\alpha)/2$, the obstacle system is
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&-m\\-m&\lambda\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 c_\rho=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}11/20\\-9/20\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The sum and difference eigenvectors of $Q$ have eigenvalues $\alpha$ and
$1$, respectively.  Solving the two active KKT equations gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-optimum}; both coordinates are positive
because $q<1/3$.

In terms of $q$,
\begin{equation*}
 \lambda=\frac{1+2q^2}{2(1+q^2)},
 \qquad
 m=\frac1{2(1+q^2)},
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\mathrm A}=\frac{1-q^2}{1+q^2},
 \qquad
 \lambda+\kappa_{\mathrm A}=\frac3{2(1+q^2)}.
\end{equation*}
Solving the seed coordinate of the first two shifted obstacle problems gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates}.  These are the full shifted
solutions, because their inactive-coordinate obstacle residuals are
\begin{equation*}
 -\alpha\rho+m u_1
 =-\frac{4q^2}{15(1+q^2)}<0,
 \qquad
 -\alpha\rho+m u_2
 =-\frac{q^2(11q+1)}{45(1+q^2)}<0.
\end{equation*}
Here the first center is zero and the second center is
\begin{equation*}
 y_1=(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})x_1
 =\left(\frac{11q^2}{15(1+q)},0\right).
\end{equation*}
Its seed-coordinate lower residual equals
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{11q^2(1-q)(4q+1)}
 {60(1+q)(1+q^2)}>0,
\end{equation*}
so $\ell_1=y_1$ and $r_1=0$.  Substituting this center in the next shifted
solve gives $u_2$ and $\delta=u_2-u_1$ in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-iterates,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-correction}.

At stage $2$ the extrapolated seed coordinate is
\begin{equation*}
 y_{2,1}=\frac{11q^2(11-5q)}{90(1+q)}.
\end{equation*}
Its lower residual is
\begin{equation*}
 \zeta_{\rho,1}^{\mathrm{low}}(y_2)
 =\frac{11q^2(1-q)^2(5q-1)}
 {90(1+q)(1+q^2)}<0.
\end{equation*}
Thus the retraction magnitude is
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta_2
 =\frac{11(1-q)^2(1-5q)}{90(1+q)},
\end{equation*}
and direct subtraction gives
\begin{equation*}
 \Delta_2-\beta_{\mathrm A}\delta
 =\frac{11(1-q)^2(1-5q-4q^2)}{90(1+q)}>0.
\end{equation*}
The last sign holds throughout \cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-parameters}.
Therefore $\ell_2=x_2$ and the correction is full.

The exact collateral inequality follows from
\begin{equation*}
 180(1+q)(1+q^2)(x_1^\star-y_{2,1})
 =9+9q-143q^2+209q^3-242q^4+110q^5>0.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, on $q\leq1/100$ the right-hand side is at least
$9-143/10^4-242/10^8>0$.  Likewise,
\begin{equation*}
 180(1+q^2)(e-2h)
 =9-176q+297q^2-264q^3+198q^4-88q^5>0,
\end{equation*}
because it is at least
$9-176/100-264/10^6-88/10^{10}>0$ on the same interval.
Thus $\mathcal B_2^{\mathrm{col}}=\{1\}$ and the minimum in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-charge} is $2eh$.  A full correction has
$h_2=(h,0)$ and $\widetilde z_2=x_2$, which proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-defect,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-charge}.
Since $x_2\neq x^\star$, one has $\Phi_2>0$, and the positive defect gives
$\gamma_2>1$.

Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 e\longrightarrow\frac1{20},
 \qquad
 \frac hq\longrightarrow\frac{22}{45},
 \qquad
 E_0,E_1,E_2\longrightarrow\frac1{200}.
\end{equation*}
Since $E_1-E_2=2e\delta+\delta^2$,
$\delta/q^2\to22/45$, and
$E_0-E_2=2eu_2+u_2^2$ with $u_2/q^2\to77/90$, the elementary limit
$\log(1+v)/v\to1$ proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-local-limits,eq:aesp-cd-euclidean-family-packing-limits}.
\end{proof}

The correction count and normalized inflation are genuinely different
objects.  The next source-aligned family shows why the raw count cannot be
charged to support additions or bounded independently of terminal accuracy.
It simultaneously confirms that such a count is too coarse for the
multiplicative ledger: every correction in the family is benign.

\begin{proposition}[A singleton support retains logarithmically many corrections]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-singleton-corrections}
Let $G$ consist of one edge, let the seed be one endpoint, and choose
\begin{equation}
 0<q\leq q_0:=\frac{\sqrt{41}-5}{8},
 \qquad
 \alpha:=\frac{q^2}{1+q^2},
 \qquad
 \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\leq\rho<1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-parameters}
\end{equation}
For the exact safeguarded recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the RPPR optimum and every nonzero iterate are
supported only on the seed.  Let $e_t:=x_1^\star-x_{t,1}$ denote the scalar
error on that coordinate and define
\begin{equation}
 \vartheta:=\frac{2(1-q^2)}{3},
 \qquad
 u:=\frac{(1-q)(1+4q)}{3(1+q)},
 \qquad
 \xi:=\vartheta u.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-ratios}
\end{equation}
Then, for every $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 e_{2k}=\xi^ke_0,
 \qquad
 e_{2k+1}=\vartheta\xi^ke_0.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors}
\end{equation}
There is no correction at an odd stage, whereas every even stage $2k\geq2$
is a full correction:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{2k+1}&=0,&&k\geq0,\\
 r_{2k}&=\beta_{\mathrm A}d_{2k},&&k\geq1.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-schedule}
\end{equation}
Thus the raw count through $T$ stages is
\begin{equation}
 N_T=\left\lfloor\frac{T-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-count}
\end{equation}
Nevertheless, every one of these corrections satisfies
$\mathfrak D_{2k}<0$ and $\gamma_{2k}<1$, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_T=0
 \qquad\text{for every }T.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-zero-inflation}
\end{equation}

For fixed admissible $\rho$, reaching scalar error $\eta$ takes
$\Theta(\log(e_0/\eta))$ stages and incurs the same order of nonzero
correction calls.  Equivalently, since
$F_\rho(x_t)-F_\rho(x^\star)=((1+\alpha)/4)e_t^2$, reaching objective gap
$\epsobj$ incurs $\Theta(\log(1/\epsobj))$ corrections.  The seed entry and
the only edge are discovered once.  Each subsequent correction/rekey call
has degree work one, so this is cumulative correction work rather than
repeated support-discovery work.  It is a statement about the safeguarded
recurrence, not a lower bound for a different algorithm or a cached response
primitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let the seed be vertex $1$.  On this graph
\begin{equation*}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}
 (1+\alpha)/2&-(1-\alpha)/2\\
 -(1-\alpha)/2&(1+\alpha)/2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 c_\rho:=b-\alpha\rho\one
 =\alpha\begin{pmatrix}1-\rho\\-\rho\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Writing $\lambda:=(1+\alpha)/2$, the obstacle KKT conditions give
\begin{equation*}
 x^\star
 =\begin{pmatrix}\alpha(1-\rho)/\lambda\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the inactive-coordinate inequality is exactly
$\rho\geq(1-\alpha)/2$.  The same direct KKT check, or Stieltjes comparison,
shows that every shifted minimizer centered below $x^\star$ also has zero
second coordinate.  Thus the entire recurrence is scalar after the one-time
edge exposure.

Put $\kappa:=1-2\alpha$.  For a scalar safe center $\ell$, exact shifted
minimization gives
\begin{equation*}
 p(\ell)=\frac{\lambda x^\star+\kappa\ell}{\lambda+\kappa},
 \qquad
 x^\star-p(\ell)=\vartheta(x^\star-\ell),
 \qquad
 \vartheta=\frac{\kappa}{\lambda+\kappa}
            =\frac{2(1-q^2)}3.
\end{equation*}
Also $\beta_{\mathrm A}=(1-q)/(1+q)$.  Starting immediately after a full
correction, one shifted solve therefore changes the error from $e$ to
$\vartheta e$.  At the following odd stage,
\begin{equation*}
 x^\star-y
 =\bigl((1+\beta_{\mathrm A})\vartheta
        -\beta_{\mathrm A}\bigr)e
 =ue>0.
\end{equation*}
There is no correction, and the next shifted solve changes the error to
$\vartheta ue=\xi e$.

At the following even stage, the extrapolate instead satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 y-x^\star
 =\vartheta\bigl(\beta_{\mathrm A}
            -(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u\bigr)e,
 \qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm A}-(1+\beta_{\mathrm A})u
 =\frac{(1-q)(1-5q)}{3(1+q)^2}>0.
\end{equation*}
For this one-coordinate trial point, the retraction magnitude is
$\Delta=(\lambda/\alpha)(y-x^\star)$.  The condition that it removes the
entire extrapolation, $\Delta\geq\beta_{\mathrm A}d$, reduces after
cancelling positive factors to
\begin{equation*}
 4q^2+5q\leq1,
\end{equation*}
which is exactly $q\leq q_0$.  Hence $\ell=x$ at every even correction and
the same two-stage calculation repeats at every scale.  This proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors,eq:aesp-cd-singleton-schedule} and the
count in \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-count}.

At every correction put $a_q:=(1-q)/q$ and
$h:=((1+q)/q)r=a_qd$.  Its defect is
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak D=h(2e-h)<0,
\end{equation*}
because overshoot gives $\beta_{\mathrm A}d>e$ and hence
$a_qd=((1+q)/q)\beta_{\mathrm A}d>2e$.  Noncorrection stages have
$\gamma_t=1$, proving \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-zero-inflation}.  Finally,
$0<\xi<1$ and \cref{eq:aesp-cd-singleton-errors} give the logarithmic
accuracy statements.  The work statement follows because the seed vertex
has graph degree one and the shared model charges its adjacency scan.
\end{proof}

For a concrete rational instance, take $q=1/7$, so
$\alpha=1/50$, $\beta_{\mathrm A}=3/4$,
$\vartheta=32/49$, $u=11/28$, and $\xi=88/343$; every
$\rho\in[49/100,1)$ is admissible.  This exact example shows that the
collapse identity cannot remove the logarithm from the \emph{number} of
correction calls, even when the support is a singleton and never expands.
It also shows why $N_T$ must not replace $\mathcal I_T$ indiscriminately:
the normalized inflation correctly assigns zero cost to all of these
benign resets.

In the singleton family,
$\mathcal B_t^{\mathrm{col}}=\varnothing$ at every correction, so
\cref{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge} also proves
$\mathcal I_T=0$.  In contrast, the positive three-path defect below forces
$\mathcal B_3^{\mathrm{col}}\neq\varnothing$ and is charged by
$\mathfrak C_3^{\mathrm{col}}$.

It is tempting to hope that every safe correction decreases the momentum
distance, so that $\mathfrak D_t\leq0$ and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction} closes the proof.  The three-vertex
path already rules this out.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise momentum nonexpansion is false]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-momentum-expansion}
Take the three-vertex path, use an endpoint seed, and set
$\alpha=1/100$ and $\rho=1/1000$.  For the exact recurrence
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer}, the correction at $t=3$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D_3
 =\frac{
 105528900258547165289
 -31691781844105995447\sqrt{11}}
 {7072772362029000000}
 \in(0.0592,0.0593).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-three-path-defect}
\end{equation}
Hence the safeguard can increase the classical estimate-sequence momentum
distance even though all primal iterates and centers remain monotone below
$x^\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the path degrees $(1,2,1)$,
\begin{equation*}
 Q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 101/200&-99/(200\sqrt2)&0\\
 -99/(200\sqrt2)&101/200&-99/(200\sqrt2)\\
 0&-99/(200\sqrt2)&101/200
 \end{pmatrix},
 \quad
 c_\rho=
 \begin{pmatrix}999/100000\\-\sqrt2/100000\\-1/100000\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Here $q=1/\sqrt{99}=\sqrt{11}/33$.  The first three shifted obstacle
solutions are strictly positive, so they are obtained by solving their
$3\times3$ linear systems.  At $t=3$, only the endpoint has negative
one-sided trial residual; applying
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-lower-retraction,eq:aesp-cd-momentum-states} and simplifying
in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{11})$ gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-three-path-defect}.  The displayed interval follows, for
example, from $3.3166<\sqrt{11}<3.3167$.
\end{proof}

Thus the strictly outer problem is now sharper than generic perturbation
control.  By \cref{lem:aesp-cd-collateral-charge}, it is enough to pack the
normalized collateral charges in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative}.  The unit mass ledger
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-correction-ledger} and the Euclidean telescope
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defect-telescope} are insufficient by themselves: a proof
must additionally exploit the sharp-collapse identity
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-collapse}, which says that corrections are generated by
fast proximal-displacement modes.  This is the precise remaining potential
lemma.

\begin{corollary}[Accelerated work on a certified envelope]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope}
Assume a fixed envelope $U$ contains the optimal RPPR support, so minimizing
$F_\rho$ over $\R^U$ has the same solution and value as the full problem.
Run strongly convex Catalyst on this restricted objective with
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-parameters} and use
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle} at every stage.  To obtain
$F_\rho(x^{(T)})-F_\rho(x^\star)\leq\epsobj$, it is sufficient to take
\begin{equation*}
 T\leq
 \left\lceil\frac{2}{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}
 \log\left(\frac{2(F_\rho(x^{(0)})-F_\rho(x^\star))}{\epsobj}\right)
 \right\rceil.
\end{equation*}
The total degree work is consequently
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(\frac{\mathcal V_U}{\sqrt\alpha}
                  \log\frac1{\epsobj}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-certified-envelope-work}
\end{equation}
If an oracle supplies $U=S^\star(\rho)$, then
$\mathcal V_U\leq1/\rho$ and the bound becomes
\begin{equation}
 \softO\left(\frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}
                  \log\frac1{\epsobj}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-support-oracle-work}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition \cref{eq:aesp-cd-c2} is the relative Catalyst criterion of
\citet{lin2018catalyst}.  With
$\chi_{\mathrm A}=\alpha/(\alpha+\kappa_{\mathrm A})$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm A}=\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}/(2-\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}})$,
the standard strongly convex Catalyst estimate is
\begin{equation*}
 F_\rho(x^{(T)})-F_\rho(x^\star)
 \leq2\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm A}}}{2}\right)^T
       (F_\rho(x^{(0)})-F_\rho(x^\star)).
\end{equation*}
Combine this estimate with \cref{eq:aesp-cd-relative-work}.  All factors
other than $\mathcal V_U/\sqrt\alpha$ are logarithmic in the displayed
parameters.  The last claim uses the RPPR support-volume theorem.
\end{proof}

This closes the start-mass and outer-rate interaction on every fixed certified
envelope, while \cref{thm:aesp-cd-explored-volume} gives an oracle-free
trajectory-dependent result.  It also explains why the absolute interface
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-cumulative-interface} loses a power of the final tolerance:
it uses a one-shot $C^2/\varphi$ estimate where the relative oracle instead
uses geometric mass contraction.  For unrestricted Catalyst, the remaining
graph-uniform algorithmic task is to control the automatically explored
envelope.  The safeguarded recurrence \cref{eq:aesp-cd-safe-outer} already
supplies the stronger alternative: its envelope stays inside
$S^\star(\rho)$ and its stage-start masses telescope.  Its separate remaining
task is exactly the correction-defect amortization in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-defective-contraction}.

\begin{corollary}[Zero-start RPPR coordinate descent]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-zero-start}
For the unshifted objective, $C_\rho(0)\leq\alpha$.  Coordinate descent from
zero, stopped when $R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)<\alpha\epskkt$, therefore
returns
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(x-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty<\epskkt
\end{equation*}
with degree work at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha\epskkt}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-zero-start-work}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At zero, the only KKT violations are seed coordinates and
\begin{equation*}
 C_\rho(0)
 =\alpha\sum_i(s_i-\rho d_i)_+
 \leq\alpha\sum_i s_i=\alpha.
\end{equation*}
Apply \cref{thm:aesp-cd-inner-work} with $\kappa_{\mathrm A}=0$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{cd}}=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$, and
$\epsin=\alpha\epskkt$.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation record and remaining open question}

Any experiment for this proposal must record the shared graph and work model,
$\alpha$, $\rho$, $\epskkt$, $\epsin$, random seed, stopping formula, and code
version.  It should report both $R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)$ and the KKT-mass
decrease diagnostics.

\Cref{thm:aesp-cd-mass,thm:aesp-cd-inner-work,cor:aesp-cd-plugin} close the
inner local-monotonicity and oracle-correctness tasks.
\Cref{thm:aesp-cd-relative-oracle,cor:aesp-cd-certified-envelope} additionally
close the interaction between start mass and the accelerated outer rate on a
fixed certified envelope.  For the implementable safeguarded continuation,
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-outer-ledger} now proves oracle-free envelope control,
monotone support, and a dimension-free telescoping start-mass bound.  The
remaining central task is strictly the outer perturbation estimate isolated
by \cref{lem:aesp-cd-estimate-defect,lem:aesp-cd-multiplicative-ledger}.
Therefore this note does not yet claim the target
$\softO(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ for a fully implementable graph-uniform
algorithm: the Euclidean progress telescope alone loses one factor
$1/\sqrt\alpha$, and \cref{prop:aesp-cd-momentum-expansion} rules out
pointwise nonexpansion.  Moreover,
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-euclidean-packing-fails} shows that dividing that
Euclidean progress by the current error to form a logarithmic telescope still
loses an unbounded factor $1/q$.  The positive-log upper bound
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-collateral-cumulative} reduces a complete proof to packing
the normalized collateral charges at accelerated scale.  This remains an
a posteriori proof obligation because the charge uses $x^\star$; no local
certificate or cumulative bound is claimed.  By
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-singleton-corrections}, a raw count of correction calls
cannot be substituted: it can grow logarithmically with accuracy even when
there is only one support discovery, while all of its normalized inflation
terms vanish.
