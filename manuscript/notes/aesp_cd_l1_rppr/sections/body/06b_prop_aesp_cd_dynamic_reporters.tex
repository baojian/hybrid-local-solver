\begin{corollary}[Stable fixed-port kinetic reporters]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-stable-port-reporter}
Suppose every surviving frontier key factors through one fixed port
$t\in\mathbb R^r$ as
\begin{equation*}
 g_v(t)=a_v^{\mathsf T}t-b_v,\qquad a_v\geq0,
\end{equation*}
the safe trace makes $t$ coordinatewise nondecreasing, and each row is stable
between explicitly charged rekeys.  For $r=1$, a threshold heap reports every
positive row exactly.  For $r=2$, split the rows according to whether
$a_{v,2}>0$, $a_{v,2}=0<a_{v,1}$, or $a_v=0$.  The first stratum reduces to
dynamic planar extreme-point queries, the second to a threshold heap, and the
last to constants; hence every operation has logarithmic amortized cost.
For general fixed $r$, assign every nonconstant row to a stratum indexed by
one nonzero coefficient and let $\mathcal T_r(M)$ denote the cost of a
dynamic extreme-point operation in $\mathbb R^r$.  One stratum query either
returns a positive row or certifies that stratum empty; delete returned rows
after activation and repeat.  With $M$ row
exposures, $H$ charged rekeys, $K$ nonempty state updates/batches, and strict
equality left inactive, the reporter uses
\begin{equation*}
 O\!\left((M+H+|S^\star|+rK)\mathcal T_r(M)\right)
\end{equation*}
operations and asks the constrained Schur oracle only for the named
activations and batch validations.  In particular, when
$\mathcal T_r(M)=\operatorname{polylog}(2+M)$ and
$M+H+K=\widetilde O(\vol(S^\star))$, the query term in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-dynamic-schur-forest-work} is
$\widetilde O(\vol(S^\star))$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $r=1$, divide by the positive slope and store the activation threshold
$b_v/a_v$ in a heap.  Monotonicity means that a row, once extracted, never
returns.  In general, choose a canonical $j$ with $a_{v,j}>0$ and put
\begin{equation*}
 p_v=\bigl((a_{v,i}/a_{v,j})_{i\neq j},-b_v/a_{v,j}\bigr)
 \in\mathbb R^r,\qquad
 q_j(t)=\bigl((t_i)_{i\neq j},1\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Then $g_v(t)>0$ exactly when
$p_v^{\mathsf T}q_j(t)>-t_j$.  One dynamic extreme-point query per stratum
therefore either emits a positive row or certifies that stratum nonpositive.
For $r=2$
these are planar hull queries; zero-slope rows are the one-dimensional and
constant cases stated above.  Repeat until every stratum is nonpositive.
Every exposure, rekey, emitted label, and batch incurs only a constant number
of declared operations for fixed $r$.
\end{proof}

The fixed-port hypothesis is substantive.  It holds on paths, rooted spiders,
and stable separator interfaces, but a general bag hierarchy changes the
separator response seen by a frontier row.  The star breakpoint example
already prevents replacing its whole kinetic hull by one constant-size
quadratic cluster field.

On a rooted tree, a hierarchical scalar threshold eliminates the named-query
term altogether and, unlike an aggregate final-support solve, reproduces a
literal sequence of valid singleton admissions.

\begin{theorem}[Exact singleton threshold gate on a rooted tree]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-tree-singleton-threshold}
Let $H$ be SPD Stieltjes with off-diagonal graph a finite tree rooted at $o$,
and consider
\begin{equation*}
 \min_{z\geq0}\ \frac12z^{\mathsf T}Hz-\ell^{\mathsf T}z,
 \qquad \ell_o>0,\quad \ell_v\leq0\quad(v\neq o).
\end{equation*}
Maintain a connected rooted strict-interior face $U$, initially $\{o\}$, and
write $p(u)$ for the parent and $\beta_u:=-H_{p(u),u}>0$.  If the active
children $w$ of $u$ return responses
\begin{equation*}
 \beta_w z_w=s_w z_u+r_w,
\end{equation*}
put
\begin{equation}
 A_u:=H_{uu}-\sum_{w\in\mathcal C_U(u)}s_w,\qquad
 B_u:=\ell_u+\sum_{w\in\mathcal C_U(u)}r_w,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-tree-threshold-ab}
\end{equation}
and, for $u\neq o$,
\begin{equation*}
 s_u:=\frac{\beta_u^2}{A_u},\qquad
 r_u:=\frac{\beta_uB_u}{A_u}.
\end{equation*}
At $u$, form a keyed multiset as follows.  An inactive child $w$ contributes
$(-\ell_w/\beta_w,w)$.  An active child contributes its finite propagated
pair $(\tau_w,\operatorname{lab}_w)$.  Let
$(\theta_u,\operatorname{lab}_u)$ be the lexicographically tie-broken minimum,
or $(+\infty,\bot)$ if the multiset is empty, and propagate
\begin{equation}
 \tau_u:=\frac{A_u\theta_u-B_u}{\beta_u}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-tree-threshold-propagate}
\end{equation}
when finite.  The exact restricted root value is $z_o^U=B_o/A_o$.
If $\operatorname{lab}_o\neq\bot$ and $z_o^U>\theta_o$, admit that one
label and update its root path; otherwise terminate.  Every admitted label
has strictly positive current boundary key, all old coordinates are
coordinatewise nondecreasing, and termination returns the unique global
obstacle minimizer.

For single-seed RPPR in degree-scaled coordinates, this implementation has
no dynamic-Schur named-query charge and uses
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(
  \log(2+\Delta_\star)\left[
   \vol(S^\star)+
   \sum_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
  \right]
 \right)
 =\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{1+\vol(S^\star)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-tree-threshold-work}
\end{equation}
exact field operations, plus one final downward recovery.  It reproduces a
valid singleton positive-subset trace, not necessarily the canonical
simultaneous all-violations batches.  The statement is exact-real and does
not cover multiple positive-load components, cycles, or finite precision.
Its local bound assumes a promised tree with lazily available parent/child
records and $O(1)$ access to a revealed neighbor's degree and load; a global
tree-verification scan is not hidden in the bound.
Since RPPR also gives $\vol(S^\star)\leq1/\rho$, this is an unconditional
single-seed tree solve in
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ exact field operations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$A_u$ is the scalar Schur pivot of the active descendant block, hence is
positive.  Elimination gives the positive-slope affine relation
\begin{equation*}
 z_u=\frac{\beta_u z_{p(u)}+B_u}{A_u}.
\end{equation*}
The current key of an inactive child $w$ is
$\ell_w+\beta_wz_u$, so it is positive exactly when
$z_u>-\ell_w/\beta_w$.  Induction through the affine relations shows that
every propagated $\tau_u$ is exactly the parent-coordinate threshold at
which its stored descendant label becomes positive.  Thus the root minimum
below $z_o^U$ names a genuinely positive current boundary row.

After admitting it, inverse positivity of the enlarged principal Stieltjes
system makes the new coordinate positive and every old coordinate
nondecreasing, so strict interior is preserved.  If the root test fails, all
frontier keys are nonpositive.  A deeper inactive vertex has zero active
neighbors and load at most zero, so its key is also nonpositive.  The active
equalities and these inactive inequalities are precisely global obstacle
KKT.

Store $\sum s_w,\sum r_w$ and a heap with one current record per child.
Activating $v$ scans its adjacency once, initializes its summary, and replaces
its old inactive record at $p(v)$ by the active
$(s_v,r_v,\tau_v,\operatorname{lab}_v)$ record.  At every ancestor it replaces
the unique changed-child record and updates both aggregate sums before
recomputing the parent response and threshold.
This costs $O(\log(2+d_u))$ per visited ancestor and proves the first bound in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-tree-threshold-work}.  The single-seed RPPR support radius
satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 R_\star\leq1+
 \frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}
 {\log((1+\sqrt\alpha)/(1-\sqrt\alpha))}
 \leq1+
 \frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}{2\sqrt\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\sum_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
\leq R_\star\vol(S^\star)$, the second bound follows.  A final top-down use
of the affine responses materializes the solution in $O(\vol(S^\star))$
work.
\end{proof}

The same scalar messages close a unicyclic graph after paying for explicit
scans of its unique cycle.  The support-radius budget makes those scans
product-scale.

\begin{corollary}[Exact singleton gate on a single-source unicyclic graph]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-unicyclic-singleton}
Let $H$ be SPD Stieltjes with finite connected unicyclic off-diagonal graph
$G$, unique cycle $C$ of length $m$, root $o$, and
$\ell_v\leq0$ for $v\neq o$.  The obstacle problem in
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-tree-singleton-threshold} has an exact-real singleton
positive-subset gate using
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left((1+R_\star)\vol(S^\star)\log(2+\Delta_\star)\right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-unicyclic-singleton-work}
\end{equation}
field operations and $O(\vol(S^\star))$ records, where
$R_\star:=\max_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)$ and
$\Delta_\star:=\max_{v\in S^\star}d_v$, with $d_v$ the off-diagonal graph
degree.  If $\ell_o\leq0$, it returns zero.
For single-seed RPPR, the existing support-radius and volume bounds reduce
this to
$\widetilde O((1+\vol(S^\star))/\sqrt\alpha)$, hence
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$.

The trace is a legal singleton order rather than the canonical simultaneous
batch order.  The statement is exact-real and does not cover multiple
positive loads or cyclomatic number greater than one.  For a generic
Stieltjes problem, only the displayed $R_\star$-dependent work follows; the
$1/\sqrt\alpha$ specialization uses the RPPR radius lemma.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Any positive optimum component not containing $o$ would satisfy
$H_{KK}x_K=\ell_K\leq0$, contradicting
$x_K=H_{KK}^{-1}\ell_K\leq0$.  Thus it is enough to maintain a connected
strict-interior face $U$.  Until the cycle is fully
active, $H_U$ is a tree.  Use
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-tree-singleton-threshold} for every boundary vertex having
one active neighbor.  There is at most one inactive vertex $w$ with two
active neighbors, occurring when the inactive cycle gap has length one.
Exclude it from the ordinary child heaps and evaluate its exact key by two
root-to-coordinate message queries.  When scanning a new active row reveals
the second active incidence of the same boundary label, remove that label
from its ordinary heap and replace it by the unique double-frontier record,
including the corresponding root-path update.  Admit any ordinary or
double-frontier label with strict positive key.  If none exists, the active
equations and inactive inequalities are global KKT.  Each double-frontier
audit and transition costs $O(R_\star)$, and the two cached incidences plus
the active path identify the cycle-closing event without advance cycle
advice.

Once $C\subseteq U$, removing $C$ leaves rooted trees, each attached to one
cycle site.  Condition on the site coordinate and store the affine response
and next threshold of each active branch.  Summing the responses gives an SPD
cyclic-tridiagonal system on the $m$ cycle coordinates.  Solve it in $O(m)$
by path elimination to a $2\times2$ core and back substitution.  If a site
coordinate exceeds its minimum branch threshold, the named label has a
strictly positive current key; admit one and update only that branch route.
If no site exceeds its threshold, all frontier keys are nonpositive, and
deeper inactive vertices have nonpositive loads and no active neighbors, so
KKT again holds.  Equality remains inactive.

Adjacency scans and branch-route updates cost
$O((1+R_\star)\vol(S^\star)\log(2+\Delta_\star))$.  If the second phase is
reached, the whole cycle lies in $S^\star$.  With
$d=\operatorname{dist}(o,C)$, a farthest cycle vertex has distance at least
$d+\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, whence $m\leq2R_\star+1$.  Therefore all
$O(m)$ cycle rescans over at most $|S^\star|$ admissions, together with the
double-frontier audits and final recovery, fit the same bound.  The RPPR
specialization now follows from the radius bound used in
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-tree-singleton-threshold} and
$\vol(S^\star)\leq1/\rho$.
\end{proof}

The full-cycle scan can itself be made persistent on a cactus.  What matters
is not the cycle length but the number of distinct sites whose descendant
response streams remain live.

\begin{corollary}[Cactus cycles with bounded live articulation sites]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-cactus-live-sites}
Consider the single-positive-load problem of
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-unicyclic-singleton} on a promised cactus.  Once a cycle
block $B$ is fully active, let $L_B:=|B|$ and let $r_B$ be the maximum, over
the post-closure trace, of the number of distinct cycle articulation sites
simultaneously having at least one live descendant tree or block stream.
There is an exact-real legal singleton trace with work
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\log(2+\Delta_\star)\left[
   \vol(S^\star)+\sum_{v\in S^\star}\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
 \right]\right)+
 O\!\left(
  \sum_{v\in S^\star}
  \sum_{B\in\mathcal P(v)}(r_B+1)\log(2+L_B)
 \right),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-cactus-live-sites-work}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P(v)$ is the set of post-closure cycle blocks on the block
route from $v$ to the root; bridge and pre-closure route work is already in
the first term.  In particular,
if $\chi:=\max_B r_B=\operatorname{polylog}(2+\vol(S^\star))$, then single-seed
RPPR is solved in
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{(1+\chi)(1+\vol(S^\star))}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right).
\end{equation*}
Thus a chain of arbitrarily long cycles, which has one downstream live site
per ancestor block, meets the product scale.  For general cactus traces the
same construction gives the unconditional instance-sensitive bound obtained
by replacing each block factor by
$\min\{L_B,(r_B+1)\log(2+L_B)\}$; no
graph-uniform bound on unbounded $r_B$ is claimed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Aggregate all live child streams at one cycle site into one handled threshold
record.  Cut the active cycle and store the $3\times3$ affine transfer
matrices from the preceding proof in a balanced product tree.  A site
diagonal or load change updates one matrix in $O(\log L_B)$ work.  Conditional
on the parent articulation value $t$, inverse positivity gives every live site
an affine value $x_j(t)=a_jt+b_j$ with $a_j\geq0$, obtainable by a named prefix
query.  If $a_j>0$, its child threshold $\theta_j$ pulls back to
$(\theta_j-b_j)/a_j$ in the parent coordinate.  If $a_j=0$, the record is
immediate when $b_j>\theta_j$ and is otherwise infinite.  Scanning the $r_B$
live sites therefore returns the exact next labeled breakpoint and parent
response in $O((r_B+1)\log(2+L_B))$ work.

Before a block becomes fully active, its active portion is a pair of path arms
and uses the tree threshold messages plus the unique double-frontier check;
these bridge, path-arm, and pre-closure route costs are the first term of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-cactus-live-sites-work}.
At first closure, build the cycle product tree once from the cached path in
$O(L_B)$ work; cactus structure guarantees that the block is never enlarged.
All such builds and final back substitutions sum to
$\widetilde O(\vol(S^\star))$.  Each emitted label updates exactly the blocks
on its root route, proving \cref{eq:aesp-cd-cactus-live-sites-work}.  The number
of blocks on that route is at most one plus the graph distance, so bounded
$\chi$, the support-radius inequality, and
$\sum_v\operatorname{dist}(o,v)\leq R_\star\vol(S^\star)$ prove the RPPR
specialization.  On a root cycle, use the root site as the parent parameter
and close its remaining scalar equation at the top.  A full scan gives the
stated minimum for unbounded $r_B$.
\end{proof}

The single-positive-load hypothesis cannot be removed by merely running one
copy per active component, even for RPPR.  On the three-vertex path take
$\alpha=1/3$, $\rho=1/8$, and put source mass $1/2$ at each endpoint.  In
the degree-unscaled congruent coordinates the endpoint loads are $1/8$, the
middle load is $-1/12$,
the endpoint diagonal is $2/3$, and each edge coupling is $1/3$.  Each
singleton source component therefore has value $3/16$ and contributes only
$1/16<1/12$ to the middle key, so two independent reporters both stop.  The
joint middle key is $2/16-1/12=1/24>0$; after admitting it the exact solution
has middle value $1/24$ and endpoint values $5/24$.  Thus multiple sources
require a merge-aware forest response.
Under strict nonzero key margins, the finite-precision message
interface of \cref{prop:aesp-cd-dynamic-schur-precision} applies; without a
margin, the theorem remains exact-real.

\begin{problem}[Variable two-port block-chain reporter]
\label{prob:aesp-cd-variable-two-port-reporter}
On a locally revealed single-seed graph of treewidth two, combine the dynamic
constrained-Schur primitive with an exact positive-row reporter so that the
total number of factor reveals, active toggles, and named response queries is
$\widetilde O(1+\vol(S^\star))$, without a KKT margin or a supplied final
support.  Fixed ports are covered by
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-stable-port-reporter}; trees by
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-tree-singleton-threshold}; unicyclic graphs by
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-unicyclic-singleton}; bounded-productive-site cactus traces
by \cref{cor:aesp-cd-cactus-productive-sites}; all single-root cactus traces
by \cref{thm:aesp-cd-cactus-online-hysteretic-hld}; and bounded-size
biconnected blocks by separate block-response solutions.  The next uncovered
class is therefore a genuinely variable two-port series--parallel block, not
an unbounded cactus chain.  Nonradial recursive diamond and $2$-tree cores
have this variable-separator issue.
Local Schur updates can change dense response columns across many bags, while
the dynamic Schur structure answers only named queries.  This identifies a
missing hierarchical reporter interface, not an algorithmic lower bound; a
kinetic block hierarchy may still close the class.
\end{problem}

The fixed-mark linear algebra is not the obstruction.  On a supplied balanced
series--parallel decomposition, parallel composition adds two-port quadratic
summaries, while series composition eliminates the shared scalar by the Schur
formula in \cref{prop:aesp-cd-dynamic-schur-forest}.  Hence a named load,
mark, or response query changes $O(\log N)$ constant-size summaries.  What is
missing is an aggregate reporter under the changing two-coordinate port
states.  Even an active length-$L$ cycle has a logarithmic named-response
structure: cutting it to a path and writing
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{pmatrix}x_{i+1}\\x_i\\1\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
  a_i/c_i&-c_{i-1}/c_i&-b_i/c_i\\
  1&0&0\\0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}x_i\\x_{i-1}\\1\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
turns a site load or diagonal update, a prefix response, and a named
coordinate query into $O(\log L)$ updates or products in a balanced transfer
tree.  This avoids the full-cycle rescans in the naive block-chain accounting,
but it still does not report which of all site thresholds is positive.  The
following local RPPR core shows why the scalar threshold carried by a rooted
tree is insufficient.

\begin{proposition}[A two-port direction cannot be one scalar threshold]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-two-port-direction-stop}
Take $\alpha=1/2$ and the unweighted theta core with three internally disjoint
paths
\begin{equation*}
 u_1-s-b-u_2,\qquad u_1-a-t-u_2,\qquad u_1-q-u_2.
\end{equation*}
Condition on nonnegative port values $s,t$, keep $a,b,s,t$ active, and pin
$u_1,u_2,q$ at zero.  In the degree-unscaled RPPR system, the active equations
give
\begin{equation*}
 a=t/6-2\rho/3,\qquad b=s/6-2\rho/3,
\end{equation*}
while the two pinned terminal keys are
\begin{equation}
 g_1=s/4+t/24-5\rho/3,\qquad
 g_2=s/24+t/4-5\rho/3.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-two-port-direction-keys}
\end{equation}
At $(s,t)=(5\rho,5\rho)$, $a=b=\rho/6>0$ and
$g_1=g_2=-5\rho/24$.  The nonnegative update to $(6\rho,5\rho)$ makes
$(g_1,g_2)=(\rho/24,-\rho/6)$, whereas the update to
$(5\rho,6\rho)$ reverses the pair.  The remaining row $q$ has key $-\rho$.
Consequently a direction-free scalar next threshold with one fixed label
cannot summarize this two-port separator; an exact reporter needs at least a
two-dimensional envelope or normal-cone state.

This is a local interface obstruction with externally supplied port changes,
not a complete single-source trajectory or an algorithmic lower bound.  It
does not rule out the planar hull in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-stable-port-reporter} or a hierarchical projective reporter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $p=(1+\alpha)/2=3/4$ and $c=(1-\alpha)/2=1/4$.  The four active
degree-two equations immediately give the displayed values of $a,b$ after
conditioning on $s,t$.  Each pinned terminal has degree three and load
$-3\rho/2$; substituting its two nonzero active neighbors gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-two-port-direction-keys}.  The three evaluations are exact
rational arithmetic.  Since $q$ has degree two and both its neighbors are
pinned, its key is $-\rho$.
\end{proof}

The exact missing primitive can be stated without hiding it inside the Schur
solver.

\begin{proposition}[Series--parallel reduction to a meldable affine hull]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-reduction}
Let a height-$h$, size-$N$ series--parallel decomposition be supplied, expose
all $M$ pinned rows of the current graph, and perform $F_{\rm dyn}$ online factor
changes and $J$ legal
singleton admissions.  Suppose a persistent data structure
$\mathsf{MeldAffineHull}$ stores affine functions on a two-coordinate port
and supports, in $T(M)$ operations, bulk affine pullback, disjoint meld,
insert/delete, and exact strict argmax, without expanding its members.  Then
the exact obstacle trace can be maintained in
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(N+MT(M)+(F_{\rm dyn}+J)(h+1)T(M)\right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-work}
\end{equation}
field operations.  A nonpositive root maximum is the global KKT certificate.
Thus $h,T(M)=\operatorname{polylog}(2+N+M)$ and
$N+M+F_{\rm dyn}+J=\widetilde O(\vol(S^\star))$ would close the reporter term.

This is a conditional reduction, not an implementation claim.  Ordinary
two-port Schur summaries provide the quadratic response but not the required
bulk-transform-and-meld hull.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every cluster, store its two-port Schur energy and the handle containing
the pinned-row affine keys whose coordinates have their unique home and are
forgotten inside that cluster; cluster ports are excluded.  Parallel
composition adds the quadratic summaries and melds the handles.  In a series
composition, eliminating the shared free coordinate gives the Schur formulas
from \cref{prop:aesp-cd-dynamic-schur-forest}; its value is affine in the
parent port, so both child key sets undergo one bulk pullback before melding.
If the shared coordinate is pinned when it is forgotten, substitute zero and
insert its complete two-sided flux key exactly once; a shared parallel port
remains deferred.  The bulk pullback is a persistent lazy view under an
arbitrary, possibly rank-deficient affine map from child to parent ports.
An admission changes only the home-to-root path.  At the root, solve the
constant-dimensional response and apply a virtual top forget: every pinned
root port has its complete incident-flux key inserted or checked exactly once,
while active root ports are eliminated by the root solve.  Then ask for a
strict argmax; a positive label is a legal admission, while a nonpositive
maximum is exactly KKT.  Initial bottom-up construction and one final recovery
cost $O(N)$ and give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-work}.
\end{proof}

Standard dynamic planar convex hulls do not instantiate this oracle.  For
example, the structure of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.11169}{Jacob and Brodal} supports individual
point insertion, deletion, and extreme-point queries in logarithmic
amortized time.  The operation required above is different: one update may
apply a common projective transform to every row in a child handle and then
meld two arbitrarily interleaved transformed hulls without expanding either
handle.  Treating those rows as ordinary point updates costs linear work.
Thus the classical dynamic-hull theorem supports
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-stable-port-reporter}, where the row coordinates are stable,
but does not close \cref{prop:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-reduction}.

There is an unconditional exact fallback on a supplied balanced parse.  It
does not meld changed hulls; instead it periodically rebuilds immutable
canonical hulls and queries a cover of the changed parse paths.

\begin{proposition}[Square-root epoch reporter on a balanced series--parallel parse]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-reporter}
Let a size-\(N\), height-\(h=O(\log(2+N))\) two-terminal
series--parallel parse be supplied for the currently exposed graph.  Assign
every quadratic factor and pinned row to one home leaf, and suppose a trace
performs \(J\) home-leaf factor, load, threshold, or active-mark changes.  In
exact-real arithmetic, the current strict maximum pinned KKT key and a
maximizing label can be maintained in
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(N+J\sqrt N\right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-work}
\end{equation}
field operations and \(\widetilde O(N)\) space.  Hence a positive label gives
a legal singleton admission and a nonpositive maximum is the exact KKT
certificate for the exposed obstacle problem.

More generally, with a rebuild cap \(1\leq k\leq N\), the work is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(N+Jk+\frac{JN}{k}\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-tradeoff}
\end{equation}
If \(p\) is the number of distinct home leaves changed over the whole trace,
the same online choice \(k=\lceil\sqrt N\rceil\) has the sharper
instance-sensitive bound
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
   N+J\min\{p+1,\sqrt N\}
 \right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-productive}
\end{equation}
In particular, on an admission-only point-source trace, a block of structural
size \(N\) whose charged support radius satisfies
\(R_\star\geq\sqrt N\) fits the product ledger
\(\widetilde O(NR_\star)\), because \(J\leq |S^\star|\leq N\).
The same conclusion holds under the weaker instance promise
\(p+1\leq R_\star\).

The parse and its exposed factor universe are part of the input contract.
Online discovery, parse splice/rebalancing, and inactive ambient halo are not
paid by this proposition.  It is therefore an implementable fixed-envelope
fallback and a square-root partial result for variable two-port blocks, not a
universal local reporter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At an epoch boundary, build at every canonical parse cluster its exact
two-port quadratic Schur summary and the static convex hull of all pinned-row
affine keys homed and forgotten in that cluster.  Ports remain excluded, and
a virtual top forget handles the root ports exactly as in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-reduction}.  A row occurs in at most \(h+1\)
canonical hulls, so this snapshot costs \(\widetilde O(N)\).

After a home leaf changes, retire every cached hull on its leaf-to-root path
and update the quadratic summaries on that path.  If \(p<k\) distinct leaves
have changed since the snapshot, the union of their root paths has
\(O((p+1)h)\) maximal clean sibling clusters and explicit dirty leaves.
At every dirty series/forget node, also form explicitly the complete key of
any pinned separator row forgotten there; there are only
\(O((p+1)h)\) such rows.  These objects form a disjoint cover of all current
pinned rows.  Solve the
constant-dimensional root response, propagate the current two-port context
top-down through the dirty skeleton, and issue one ordinary static support
query to every clean-cluster hull; check each explicit dirty row directly.
This returns the exact global maximum in \(\widetilde O(p+1)\subseteq
\widetilde O(k)\) work.  Crucially, each clean hull stays in its own local
port coordinates: no transformed sibling meld is performed.

When the \(k\)-th distinct leaf is touched, rebuild all summaries and hulls
from the current exact factors and clear the dirty set.  Repeated changes at
one dirty leaf cost another query but do not earn rebuild credit.  There are
at most \(J/k\) completed rebuilds, so the initial snapshot, all queries, and
all rebuilds cost
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(N+Jk+(J/k)N\right).
\end{equation*}
Taking \(k=\lceil\sqrt N\rceil\) proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-work}.  Exact Schur elimination and the
home/forget invariant prove the KKT claim.  If the global number of touched
leaves satisfies \(p<k\), the first epoch never fills, so there is no rebuild
and every query costs only \(\widetilde O(p+1)\).  If \(p\geq k\), the
square-root bound applies.  Since
\(p+1\leq k+1\leq2\sqrt N+1\) at the boundary, the two cases give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-productive} up to a universal constant.
\end{proof}

The pullback part of this interface has more structure than an arbitrary
point update.

\begin{lemma}[Two-port Schur pullback is an order-monotone projective view]
\label{lem:aesp-cd-two-port-projective-pullback}
Let a child port satisfy $z=P\widehat z+u$ with $P\geq0$ entrywise, as in a
Stieltjes series elimination, and let a pinned key be
$g(z)=a^{\mathsf T}z-b$ with $a\geq0$.  Its pulled-back normal is
$\widehat a=P^{\mathsf T}a\geq0$.  On the stratum $a_2>0$, write
\begin{equation*}
 r:=a_1/a_2,\qquad c:=-b/a_2.
\end{equation*}
Whenever $P_{12}r+P_{22}>0$, the normalized dual row transforms as
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r=\frac{P_{11}r+P_{21}}{P_{12}r+P_{22}},
 \qquad
 \widehat c=\frac{c+r u_1+u_2}{P_{12}r+P_{22}}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-two-port-projective-pullback}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, this is the homogeneous projective action
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat r\\ \widehat c\\ 1\end{pmatrix}
 \sim
 \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
   P_{11}&0&P_{21}\\
   u_1&1&u_2\\
   P_{12}&0&P_{22}
 \end{pmatrix}}_{\mathcal P(P,u)}
 \begin{pmatrix}r\\ c\\ 1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Thus $r\mapsto\widehat r$ is increasing, decreasing, or constant according
to the sign of $\det P$; a whole ordered hull can retain the pullback as one
lazy projective view, with at most an order reversal.  Zero denominators and
$a_2=0$ form the usual endpoint strata.

Consecutive affine port maps compose without expanding the rows:
$\mathcal P(P_2,u_2)\mathcal P(P_1,u_1)
=\mathcal P(P_1P_2,P_1u_2+u_1)$.  Thus an entire series chain has one
constant-size lazy view.

This removes row-by-row resorting from the pullback operation.  It does not
implement the required meld: differently transformed child hulls can
interleave arbitrarily, and a hierarchy of unresolved unions makes strict
argmax branch into all descendants.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitution gives
$\widehat a=P^{\mathsf T}a$ and
$\widehat b=b-a^{\mathsf T}u$.  Dividing its two normal coordinates and its
intercept by $a_2(P_{12}r+P_{22})$ gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-two-port-projective-pullback}.  Moreover
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{d\widehat r}{dr}
 =\frac{\det P}{(P_{12}r+P_{22})^2},
\end{equation*}
whose sign is constant on the stratum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Slope-separated projective meld]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-slope-separated-projective-meld}
In \cref{prop:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-reduction}, suppose that after every lazy
pullback the two child hulls at a meld node have disjoint normalized-slope
intervals, allowing either child order, and are stored as balanced convex
chains with logarithmic bridge-tangent search.  Then the required bulk
pullback, disjoint meld, and strict positivity query have
$T(M)=O(\log(2+M))$.  Consequently the conditional work in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-work} becomes
\begin{equation*}
 O\!\left(N+M\log(2+M)
 +(F_{\rm dyn}+J)(h+1)\log(2+M)\right).
\end{equation*}
More generally, at most $\chi$ alternating sibling slope runs give
$T(M)=O(\chi\log(2+M))$ when the chains support persistent slope splits.
Endpoint strata are maintained separately.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The preceding lemma preserves or reverses each child slope order.  If every
slope in chain $A$ is below every slope in chain $B$, put
$f_A(t)=\max_{i\in A}(r_it+c_i)$ and define $f_B$ similarly.  Every
piecewise derivative of $f_B-f_A$ is positive, so the two envelopes cross at
most once.  Their union is therefore a prefix of $A$ followed by a suffix of
$B$.  A bridge-tangent search finds this transition logarithmically, and the
parent stores the two persistent child handles and the bridge.  Recomputing
one bridge at each changed ancestor gives the displayed bound.
Splitting into $\chi$ slope runs and joining them successively gives the
stated bounded-alternation extension.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine but restrictive GO: arbitrary transformed sibling slope
intervals can interleave, so it does not implement the general meld oracle.

\begin{proposition}[One child pullback can change linearly many parent facets]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-sp-alternating-meld-stop}
For every \(m\geq2\), there is a weighted two-terminal
series--parallel fan whose pinned affine keys can be partitioned between two
parallel children so that one common affine translation of the second child
changes \(m\) facets of the exact parent upper envelope.

Concretely, let the \(2m\) pinned keys be
\begin{equation*}
 \ell_k(x,y)=kx+y-k^2,qquad k=1,\ldots,2m,
\end{equation*}
put the even rows in one child and the odd rows in the other, and restrict
\(x\) to \([0,4m+1]\).  With no translation, all \(2m\) rows occur on the
upper envelope in alternating child order.  Replacing the odd child's port
by \(y-C\), where \(C=16m^2\), removes every odd facet; restoring \(C=0\)
inserts all \(m\) of them at once.

Hence an interface that materializes the merged parent chain or maintains
only a constant number of sibling bridges has \(\Omega(m)\) worst-case work
for one legal bulk pullback.  This is a representation lower bound for that
interface.  It does not rule out a lazy persistent
\(\mathsf{MeldAffineHull}\), an amortized kinetic structure, or the static
epoch fallback in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-sp-static-epoch-reporter}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The row \(k\) is the unique maximizer for
\(x\in(2k-1,2k+1)\), because for every \(j\neq k\),
\begin{equation*}
 \ell_k(x,y)-\ell_j(x,y)=(k-j)(x-k-j).
\end{equation*}
Thus all rows are exposed facets and their child parity alternates.  For an
odd \(k\), choose an adjacent even \(j\in\{k-1,k+1\}\cap[1,2m]\).  On the
declared interval,
\begin{equation*}
 |\ell_k(x,y)-\ell_j(x,y)|<8m+1<16m^2=C.
\end{equation*}
After translating the odd child by \(-C\), that adjacent even row strictly
dominates \(k\) everywhere, so no odd row remains on the parent envelope.

To realize the rows by an SPD Stieltjes fan, give internal row \(k\)
couplings \(-k\) and \(-1\) to the two ports, diagonal \(k+2\), and load
\(-k^2\); give each port diagonal one plus the sum of its incident coupling
magnitudes.  The matrix is strictly diagonally dominant, and the pinned KKT
key is exactly \(\ell_k\).  Splitting the length-two paths by parity is a
parallel series--parallel decomposition.  The child-port translation is the
bulk affine pullback allowed by the reporter interface.
\end{proof}

The hull cannot be replaced by a materialized constant-size obstacle response.
For a weighted series--parallel fan $K_{2,m}$ with ports $s,t$, let the
internal vertex $u_i$ have diagonal $i^2+2$, couplings $-i^2$ to $s$ and
$-1$ to $t$, and load $-i/m$.  Give the two ports diagonals
$1+\sum_i i^2$ and $m+1$.  The matrix is strictly diagonally dominant SPD
Stieltjes.  Conditional on $(s,t)$, the pinned key is
\begin{equation*}
 g_i=i^2s+t-i/m,
\end{equation*}
and eliminating the nonnegative $u_i$ contributes
$-(g_i)_+^2/[2(i^2+2)]$.  The break lines for $i\neq j$ meet at
\begin{equation*}
 (s,t)=\left(\frac1{m(i+j)},\frac{ij}{m(i+j)}\right)\in(0,1)^2,
\end{equation*}
and a third line $k$ passes through this point only if
$(k-i)(k-j)=0$.  Hence the exact planar piecewise-quadratic response has
$\Theta(m^2)$ cells: every line has a chord in the square, every pairwise
crossing lies inside it, and no three lines concur.  This rules out explicitly materializing the full
obstacle response at every composition node, but not the implicit hull oracle
in \cref{prop:aesp-cd-sp-meld-hull-reduction}.

A quantitative margin converts the reporter problem into monotone coordinate
level events.  The latter events are an additional interface; ordinary
point-query dynamic Schur does not emit them automatically.

\begin{proposition}[Separated RPPR keys from coordinate level events]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-separated-level-reporter}
Work in degree-unscaled coordinates $y=D^{-1/2}x$, where
\begin{equation*}
 \widehat H=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}D-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A,\qquad
 \widehat\ell=\alpha e_o-\alpha\rho d,\qquad
 c:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.
\end{equation*}
Along exact nested faces, write $y_i$ for the monotone active coordinates.
For a nonseed boundary row put
\begin{equation*}
 P_v:=c\sum_{i\in U\cap N(v)}y_i,\qquad
 T_v:=\alpha\rho d_v,\qquad g_v=P_v-T_v.
\end{equation*}
Assume that at every checkpoint every boundary row obeys the uniform
separation promise
\begin{equation}
 P_v\leq(1-\gamma)T_v
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 P_v\geq(1+\gamma)T_v,\qquad 0<\gamma\leq1.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-margin}
\end{equation}
If $c=0$, the signs are static.  Otherwise put
$\eta_{\rm lev}=\gamma/8$ and $\delta=\gamma\alpha\rho/(8c)$, and round every
$y_i$ down to zero or a level $\delta(1+\eta_{\rm lev})^k$.  Given a charged source
that reports every coordinate-level crossing in order, exact boundary signs
and a positive label can be maintained in
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm level}+
 O\!\left(
  \vol(S^\star)\,\gamma^{-1}
  \log\!\left(2+\frac{c}{\gamma\alpha\rho}\right)
  \operatorname{polylog}(2+\vol(S^\star))
 \right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-work}
\end{equation}
work.  Hence this reporter is soft-linear for constant or inverse-polylog
$\gamma$, provided the level-event source has the same bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The RPPR comparison bound gives $0\leq y_i\leq1/d_i\leq1$.  If
$\bar y_i$ is the rounded value and
$\bar P_v=c\sum_{i\in U\cap N(v)}\bar y_i$, then
\begin{equation}
 \bar P_v\leq P_v
 \leq(1+\eta_{\rm lev})\bar P_v+c d_v\delta
 =(1+\eta_{\rm lev})\bar P_v+\frac\gamma8T_v.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-envelope}
\end{equation}
On the negative branch of
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-margin}, the right endpoint in this interval
is at most
\begin{equation*}
 \bigl[(1+\gamma/8)(1-\gamma)+\gamma/8\bigr]T_v
 =\bigl(1-3\gamma/4-\gamma^2/8\bigr)T_v<T_v.
\end{equation*}
On the positive branch, solving the right inequality for $\bar P_v$ gives
\begin{equation*}
 \bar P_v\geq
 \frac{1+7\gamma/8}{1+\gamma/8}T_v>T_v.
\end{equation*}
Thus the rounded state certifies the exact sign.  A sign change between
separated checkpoints forces at least one coordinate level crossing, since
otherwise the same interval would certify both signs.

Each coordinate crosses at most
$O(\gamma^{-1}\log(2+c/(\gamma\alpha\rho)))$ levels.  A crossing updates the
accumulator of each currently exposed neighboring boundary row.  Summing the
adjacency work over active coordinates gives the second term in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-work}; heaps or dictionaries contribute only
the displayed polylogarithm.  A positive row is emitted once by violation
persistence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite KKT hysteresis removes the key-margin promise]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-finite-level-reporter}
In the setting of
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-separated-level-reporter}, fix a finite KKT tolerance
\(\epskkt>0\).  No separation assumption on the actual boundary keys is
needed.  For \(c>0\), put
\begin{equation*}
 \eta_{\rm fin}:=\min\!\left\{\frac12,
                 \frac{\epskkt}{2\rho}\right\},
 \qquad
 \delta_{\rm fin}:=\frac{\alpha\epskkt}{2c},
\end{equation*}
and round every monotone active coordinate down to zero or a level
\(\delta_{\rm fin}(1+\eta_{\rm fin})^k\).  Write \(\bar P_v\) for the
boundary response computed from these rounded coordinates.  Then every
named boundary row has the following exhaustive safe classification:
\begin{equation}
 \bar P_v>T_v
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_v>0
 \qquad\text{(safe support pivot)}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-level-pivot}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \bar P_v\leq T_v
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 g_v/d_v\leq\alpha\epskkt
 \qquad\text{(finite KKT certificate)}
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-level-stop}
\end{equation}
Thus an exhaustive charged coordinate-level event source gives a complete
finite reporter in
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm level}^{\rm fin}
 +\widetilde O\!\left(
  \vol(S^\star)
  \left(1+\frac{\rho}{\epskkt}\right)
  \log\!\left(2+\frac{c}{\alpha\epskkt}\right)
 \right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-finite-level-work}
\end{equation}
work.  Under the standard reduction
\(\rho=\epskkt=\epsppr/2\), the explicit reporter work outside the event
source is only
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
  \vol(S^\star)\log\!\frac1{\alpha\epsppr}
 \right).
\end{equation*}
The remaining missing primitive is therefore not a key-gap oracle: it is an
online mechanism that emits the monotone coordinate level crossings from a
compressed changing-face solve within the same bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the rounded state, exactly as in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-separated-level-envelope},
\begin{equation*}
 \bar P_v\leq P_v
 \leq(1+\eta_{\rm fin})\bar P_v+c d_v\delta_{\rm fin}.
\end{equation*}
Hence \(\bar P_v>T_v\) implies \(P_v>T_v\), proving the safe-pivot
branch.  On the complementary branch,
\begin{align*}
 P_v-T_v
 &\leq\eta_{\rm fin}T_v+c d_v\delta_{\rm fin}\\
 &=\alpha d_v\left(
     \eta_{\rm fin}\rho+\frac{\epskkt}{2}
   \right)
 \leq\alpha\epskkt d_v,
\end{align*}
which is the finite KKT branch.  The two branches are exhaustive, including
equality.

Since \(0\leq y_i\leq1\), each coordinate crosses
\begin{equation*}
 O\!\left(
  \eta_{\rm fin}^{-1}
  \log\!\left(2+\frac{c}{\alpha\epskkt}\right)
 \right)
 =O\!\left(
  \left(1+\frac\rho{\epskkt}\right)
  \log\!\left(2+\frac{c}{\alpha\epskkt}\right)
 \right)
\end{equation*}
levels.  Updating every exposed neighboring boundary accumulator at a
crossing and summing degrees over \(S^\star\) proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-finite-level-work}.  When \(c=0\), every off-root key is
the static negative load and no level structure is required.
\end{proof}

The explicit $C_{\rm level}$ is indispensable.  Merely knowing that each
finite run has some nonzero minimum key gap gives no uniform rate: the gap
may shrink with the instance, while a literal eager-refresh trace can still
change linearly many deferred keys over linearly many admissions.  Nor does a
standard dynamic-Schur point-query oracle reveal which old coordinate crossed
its next rounded level without either another kinetic structure or an old-face
scan.  Thus the proposition is a quantitative margin reduction, not a closure
of \cref{prob:aesp-cd-variable-two-port-reporter}.

Fixed coarse bins fail even on an actual single-source RPPR path.  Let
$w-o-v-z$ be a four-vertex path, take $\alpha=1/2$, $\rho=13/180$, and use
the degree-unscaled system above with source $o$.  On $U=\{o\}$,
$y_o=77/270\in[1/4,1/2)$, row $w$ has key $19/540>0$, and row $v$ has key
$-1/1080<0$.  After admitting $w$, the exact restricted root value is
$y_o=449/1530\in[1/4,1/2)$, while row $v$ has key $7/6120>0$.  Yet $w$ and
$v$ are not adjacent, and the only old coordinate affecting $v$ stayed in
the same factor-two bin.  Thus refreshing only the admitted row or fixed
dyadic coordinate crossings misses a genuine sign change; the
$1+\Theta(\gamma)$ resolution or an exact fallback in the proposition is
essential.

The next result records a sufficient condition under which restricted-face
scratch and boundary discovery do have the desired geometric accounting.

\begin{proposition}[Margin-certified geometric face batches]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batches}
Let $A_0\subset A_1\subset\cdots\subset S^\star$ be nested certified faces,
assume $\{v:c_v>0\}\subseteq A_0$, and let $p_A>0$ denote the exact
restricted solution on $A$.  For
$v\in\partial A$ define
\begin{equation*}
 g_v(A):=c_v-Q_{vA}p_A,
 \qquad
 L_v^\partial:=\sum_{i\in A}(-Q_{vi}).
\end{equation*}
Suppose the safe Chebyshev publication $z_A$ satisfies
$0\leq z_A\leq p_A$ and
$\norm{p_A-z_A}_\infty\leq\varepsilon_A$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 g_v(z_A)\leq g_v(A)
 \leq g_v(z_A)+L_v^\partial\varepsilon_A.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-boundary-key-interval}
\end{equation}
In particular, $g_v(z_A)>0$ certifies $v\in S^\star$.

Assume that for every proper face $A_j$ there is a batch
$B_j\subseteq\partial A_j$ such that, for fixed $0<\gamma\leq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \vol(B_j)\geq\gamma\vol(A_j),
 \qquad
 g_v(A_j)\geq\tau_jL_v^\partial\quad(v\in B_j),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_j\leq\frac{\tau_j}{2}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batch-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
Admit every vertex certified positive by the boundary scan.  Then
$\vol(A_{j+1})\geq(1+\gamma)\vol(A_j)$.  If the final face additionally
satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 g_v(z_{S^\star})+L_v^\partial\varepsilon_\star\leq0
 \qquad(v\in\partial S^\star),
\end{equation*}
one final scan certifies termination.  Restricted safe-Chebyshev work and
boundary scans therefore total
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
   \frac{\vol(S^\star)}{\gamma\sqrt\alpha}\right),
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batch-work}
\end{equation}
where the logarithms include the certified margins $\tau_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $Q_{vA}\leq0$ entrywise,
\begin{equation*}
 g_v(A)-g_v(z_A)
 =\sum_{i\in A}(-Q_{vi})(p_{A,i}-z_{A,i})
 \in[0,L_v^\partial\varepsilon_A],
\end{equation*}
which proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-boundary-key-interval}.  If $x_v^\star=0$,
the global obstacle KKT condition and $z_A\leq x_A^\star$ imply
$g_v(z_A)\leq0$; hence a positive lower key is a genuine support
certificate.  Under
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batch-hypothesis}, every $v\in B_j$ has
$g_v(z_{A_j})\geq\tau_jL_v^\partial/2>0$ and is admitted.  The face volumes
therefore grow geometrically.  Since one restricted solve and scan at face
$A_j$ costs
$\widetilde O(\vol(A_j)/\sqrt\alpha)$, summing the geometric series gives
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batch-work}.  The displayed upper boundary
certificate at the final face is the complementary KKT condition.
\end{proof}

The growth and margin hypotheses are substantive.  On a path prefix only
the next vertex is visible; an arbitrarily small positive key may neither
double volume nor meet a fixed work-normalized margin, while the following
vertex cannot be certified before that admission.  Thus delayed batching
can merge many already exposed frontier events, but it does not by itself
remove the serial-frontier obstruction in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-face-by-face-volume-stop}.

There is a different, trajectory-dependent escape: double a coordinate
envelope rather than requiring the certified active face itself to double.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional speculative-envelope doubling]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-doubling}
Let $U_0\subset U_1\subset\cdots\subset U_J$ be coordinate envelopes
containing $\{v:c_v>0\}$.  The restricted obstacle minimizer $p_U$ may
have active face $A_U=\supp(p_U)$ strictly smaller than $U$.  Assume a
restricted obstacle primitive publishes $z_U$ with
\begin{equation*}
 0\leq z_U\leq p_U,\qquad
 \norm{p_U-z_U}_\infty\leq\varepsilon_U
\end{equation*}
in
$\widetilde O(\vol(U)/\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm floor}})$ work, including its
active fallback.  Assume a charged exact fallback whose cost is included in
this bound, or a nonzero
boundary-margin promise resolves every interval in
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-boundary-key-interval} in finite work, either with positive
lower endpoint or nonpositive upper endpoint.

Here $\partial U$ means every outside neighbor of $U$.  If all upper
endpoints are nonpositive, the zero extension of $p_U$ is the
global obstacle optimum and the algorithm terminates.  Otherwise, anchor a
speculative graph search at the certified-positive vertices and choose the
next envelope so that
\begin{equation*}
 \vol(U_{j+1})\geq2\vol(U_j),
\end{equation*}
unless all source-containing ambient components reached by the search have
been exhausted; every selected certified-positive anchor must be included in
$U_{j+1}$.  Only the positive support of the next obstacle solve is
published.  Then there is at most one
nondoubling terminal expansion and
\begin{equation}
 J\leq1+\left\lceil\log_2
   \frac{\vol(U_J)}{\vol(U_0)}\right\rceil,\qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^J\vol(U_j)\leq3\vol(U_J).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-volume}
\end{equation}
Consequently discovery, restricted solves, and certificate scans cost
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(
   \frac{\vol(U_J)}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm floor}}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-work}
\end{equation}
This is a bound in the final explored volume, not in
$\vol(S^\star)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The boundary interval follows exactly as in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-geometric-face-batches}.  Nonboundary vertices outside
$U$ have no incident active coordinate and no source, so a closed boundary,
together with the restricted obstacle KKT conditions, is the global KKT
certificate.  Restricted Stieltjes comparison gives
$p_{U_j}\leq p_{U_{j+1}}\leq x^\star$ after zero padding, so publication
remains lower-safe.  Every nonterminal envelope doubles.  If graph search
exhausts all relevant source-containing components before doubling, the
following solve has a closed boundary and is terminal.  The geometric series proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-volume}; summing the assumed primitive
cost proves \cref{eq:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-work}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[RPPR decoy stops support-sensitive blind doubling]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-rppr-speculative-decoy}
Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and
\begin{equation*}
 0<\rho<\bar\rho_\alpha
 :=\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{\alpha^2+10\alpha+5}.
\end{equation*}
For every sufficiently large $M$, let $G_M$ contain the path
$s-a-u-h$, attach $M-1$ leaves to $h$, and seed $s$.  Thus
\begin{equation*}
 (d_s,d_a,d_u,d_h,d_z)=(1,2,2,M,1).
\end{equation*}
For all $M$ above a $\rho,\alpha$ dependent threshold, the exact RPPR support
is
\begin{equation*}
 S^\star=\{s,a,u\},\qquad \vol(S^\star)=5.
\end{equation*}
Nevertheless, the factor-two rule in
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-doubling}, even when graph search is
anchored only at a newly certified violation, can be forced to include $h$.
Its explored volume is then at least $M+5$, so
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\vol(U_J)}{\vol(S^\star)}\geq\frac{M+5}{5}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-speculative-decoy}
\end{equation}
Thus no graph-uniform replacement of explored volume by final support volume
is valid for blind factor-two prefetch.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $b_\alpha=(1-\alpha)/2$ and let
$T=\{s,a,u\}$.  The principal matrix $Q_T$ and the restricted right-hand side
$c_{\rho,T}=\alpha\mathbf e_{\mathrm{src}}-\alpha\rho\sqrt d_T$ are
independent of $M$.  At $\rho=0$, inverse positivity makes
$Q_T^{-1}(\alpha\mathbf e_{\mathrm{src}})$ strictly positive.
Put $p_\alpha=(1+\alpha)/2$ and use degree-scaled coordinates
$y=D_T^{-1/2}x$.  Direct elimination on the two prefixes gives scaled
next-boundary keys
\begin{equation*}
 \Gamma_a=-2\alpha\rho+b_\alpha y_s,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_u=-2\alpha\rho+b_\alpha y_a,
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
 y_s=\frac{\alpha(1-\rho)}{p_\alpha},
 \qquad
 y_a=\frac{\alpha[b_\alpha(1-\rho)-2p_\alpha\rho]}
 {2p_\alpha^2-b_\alpha^2}.
\end{equation*}
Here $\Gamma_v:=\sqrt{d_v}\,(c_\rho-Qx)_v$, so it has the same sign as the
ordinary KKT key.  The displayed restriction on $\rho$ is exactly the stricter condition
$\Gamma_u>0$ and also implies $\Gamma_a>0$.  Stieltjes inverse positivity
then makes the full restricted solution on $T$ strictly positive.

Let $x_T^\star=Q_T^{-1}c_{\rho,T}$.  At $h$, its exterior KKT key is
\begin{equation*}
 g_h(T)=-\alpha\rho\sqrt M
 +\frac{b_\alpha x_{T,u}^\star}{\sqrt{2M}}<0
\end{equation*}
once $M>b_\alpha x_{T,u}^\star/(\alpha\rho\sqrt2)$.  Every leaf of $h$ has
key $-\alpha\rho<0$.  Hence the zero extension of $x_T^\star$ satisfies the
global KKT conditions and $S^\star=T$.

Starting from $\{s\}$, admitting the certified vertex $a$ grows volume from
$1$ to $3$.  At $\{s,a\}$, the only continuation violation is $u$; admitting
it gives volume $5<2\cdot3$.  The unique outward neighbor of this new anchor
is $h$, so blind graph search toward volume six includes $h$ and immediately
pays its degree $M$, proving
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-rppr-speculative-decoy}.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of an accelerated
\emph{obstacle} primitive is not supplied by
\cref{thm:aesp-cd-safe-chebyshev-face}: that theorem accelerates a certified
interior linear face, whereas a speculative envelope may contain inactive
coordinates.  Moreover, explored volume cannot in general be charged to
restricted-optimum gain or final active volume.  A prefetched halo may remain
identically zero (zero gain) yet contain an arbitrarily high-degree decoy.
Implementations therefore need a hard degree-volume guard and a fallback;
without an independent bound on $\vol(U_J)$,
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-speculative-envelope-work} is not the desired universal
local theorem.

The RPPR support cap nevertheless yields a graph-uniform retained-volume
theorem.  It does not by itself bound the dynamic response work.

\begin{theorem}[Hard-capped discovery under a dynamic obstacle oracle]
\label{thm:aesp-cd-hard-cap-dynamic-oracle}
Put $V_\rho:=1/\rho$.  Suppose an exact dynamic obstacle oracle, on every
nested retained envelope $U$, returns its restricted minimizer $p_U$, the
exact key of every named boundary vertex, and a terminal recovery.  Let
$A_U:=\supp(p_U)$.  Its charged work $W_{\rm DS}(2V_\rho)$ must include all
factor reveals, active/pinned changes, named boundary reports, and recovery
while $\vol(U)\leq2V_\rho$.

Start from exactly the positive-load sources; their restricted minimizer has
full support.  At each
iteration, admit every boundary vertex with positive exact key.  Optionally
add speculative halo vertices.  Maintain a conservative ledger $h_U$ with
$\vol(U\setminus A_U)\leq h_U$, initially zero.  Before scanning any proposed
speculative row $W$, its degree metadata must certify the pessimistic guard
\begin{equation}
 h_U+\vol(W)\leq V_\rho.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-inactive-halo-guard}
\end{equation}
After the next restricted solve, release every newly active coordinate from
the ledger and retain any conservative overestimate.  If a speculative
candidate would violate the pessimistic guard, omit it and continue with the
certified positive anchors only.  Then, on every finite graph:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $A_U\subseteq S^\star(\rho)$ throughout and
      $\vol(U)\leq2/\rho$;
\item every nonterminal iteration activates at least one new vertex of
      $S^\star(\rho)$;
\item a boundary with no positive key is the global RPPR KKT certificate.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the protocol terminates exactly and has total charged work
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm DS}(2/\rho)+O(1/\rho).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-hard-cap-dynamic-work}
\end{equation}
In particular, an exposure-local oracle satisfying
$W_{\rm DS}(B)=\widetilde O(B/\sqrt\alpha)$ would give the intended universal
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ RPPR solve.  If the oracle, key
certificate, or finite-precision guard fails, retaining the last certified
lower checkpoint and switching to monotone RPPR coordinate descent preserves
correctness, but only the slower fallback bound remains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Restricted Stieltjes comparison gives $0\leq p_U\leq x^\star$, hence
$A_U\subseteq S^\star$ and $\vol(A_U)\leq V_\rho$.  A positive boundary key
cannot belong to an inactive global coordinate: padding $p_U$ by zero and
using $p_U\leq x^\star$ would contradict the global inactive KKT inequality.
After that vertex is inserted into the envelope it must be positive in the
new restricted minimizer; otherwise monotonicity of the old coordinates
would leave its already positive key positive.  Thus certified anchors are
charged to $A_U$, not to the inactive-halo budget.

Every positive-load source is active in its restricted obstacle minimizer;
otherwise its positive key would violate restricted KKT.  Hence the initial
ledger is zero.  Pessimistic charging before a speculative row is scanned and
releasing only certified active coordinates inductively preserve
$\vol(U\setminus A_U)\leq h_U\leq V_\rho$.  The guard and the support cap give
$\vol(U)\leq\vol(A_U)+\vol(U\setminus A_U)\leq2V_\rho$.
Every nonterminal iteration has a positive boundary key and hence grows the
finite true support.  If there is none, restricted active equalities,
nonpositive boundary keys, inclusion of every positive-load source, and
nonpositive loads away from the boundary are exactly the global obstacle KKT
conditions.
Each retained adjacency record is scanned once, giving the additive
$O(V_\rho)$ term; all response and reporting work is, by contract, inside
$W_{\rm DS}$.
\end{proof}

This theorem answers one part of the universal-bound question affirmatively:
an RPPR retained envelope of volume at most $2/\rho$ is always sufficient.
It does
\emph{not} manufacture a universal accelerated algorithm, because assuming
$W_{\rm DS}(B)=\widetilde O(B/\sqrt\alpha)$ is precisely the
serial-admission and frontier-reporting interface left open on general
graphs.  A degree lookup that itself scans a rejected high-degree row must
also be charged; the guard is useful only when the degree or row size is
known before materialization.
