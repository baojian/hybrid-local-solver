\section{Problem formulation}

The accelerated shifted analysis in this note specializes the shared model to
$\alpha\in(0,1/2)$; its frozen accelerated arm uses $\alpha<1/4$.  The
unshifted zero-start certificate and fallback cover
$1/4\leq\alpha\leq1$, so together the two algorithmic branches retain the
full shared range $0<\alpha\leq1$.  Both solve the same composite objective
$F_\rho$.  The final, note-scoped diagnostic target is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{R_{\mathrm{kkt},\rho}(x)}{\alpha}\leq\epskkt.
  \label{eq:aesp-cd-target}
\end{equation}
This is not identified with $\epsobj$, $\epspg$, or $\epsppr$.

\subsection{Point-source contract and the exact limit of linear reduction}

The oracle-free discovery and reporter results in this note take the canonical
point-source specialization
\begin{equation*}
 s=e_o.
\end{equation*}
Statements that use only the shared quadratic algebra may remain valid for a
general distribution.  Connected support and a single rooted exploration
order are invoked only under this point-source contract.  The support-radius
bound itself extends to distance from the whole source set, as the next
proposition shows.  Thus the genuine restriction is a one-root dynamic
discovery contract, not merely a notational normalization.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse-source support geometry is multi-rooted but still local]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-sparse-source-radius}
Let $s\geq0$, $\one^\top s=1$, and $T=\supp(s)$.  Every connected
component of the RPPR support $S^\star(\rho)$ intersects $T$, and
\begin{equation}
 \max_{v\in S^\star(\rho)}\operatorname{dist}(T,v)
 \leq
 1+\frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}
 {\log((1+\sqrt\alpha)/(1-\sqrt\alpha))}
 \leq
 1+\frac{\log_+((1-\alpha)/(\alpha\rho))}{2\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-sparse-source-radius}
\end{equation}
Consequently sparsity of $s$ creates at most $|T|$ source-rooted
components, all within the same square-root-scale radius.  What is lost
relative to $s=e_o$ is not spatial locality but the single rooted response
order: distinct components can contribute jointly to a previously inactive
row and then merge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Work first in the degree-unscaled congruence
$\widehat H=((1+\alpha)/2)D-((1-\alpha)/2)A$.
If an active connected component $C$ does not intersect $T$, its active
equations give
\begin{equation*}
 y_C^\star=\widehat H_{CC}^{-1}(-\alpha\rho d_C)<0,
\end{equation*}
because every principal Stieltjes inverse is nonnegative.  This contradicts
activity, so every component meets $T$.

For the radius, return to the shared normalized matrix $Q$.  Put
$b=\alpha D^{-1/2}s$ and
$\lambda_\alpha=(1-\sqrt\alpha)/(1+\sqrt\alpha)$.  The degree-$k$
Chebyshev inverse polynomial from the point-source proof satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{Q^{-1}-p_k(Q)}_2\leq\frac2\alpha\lambda_\alpha^{k+1}.
\end{equation*}
Moreover $\norm{b}_2\leq\alpha\norm{s}_1=\alpha$.  If
$\operatorname{dist}(T,v)=\ell$, locality makes
$(p_{\ell-1}(Q)b)_v=0$.  Obstacle comparison therefore gives exactly the
same Green bound as for one source,
\begin{equation*}
 0\leq x_v^\star\leq(Q^{-1}b)_v\leq2\lambda_\alpha^\ell.
\end{equation*}
Choose an active vertex at maximum source-set distance $R\geq1$.  It is
not a source, every neighbor has source-set distance at least $R-1$, and
its active KKT equality yields
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha\rho\sqrt{d_v}
 <\sum_{u\sim v}\frac{1-\alpha}{2\sqrt{d_ud_v}}x_u^\star
 \leq(1-\alpha)\sqrt{d_v}\lambda_\alpha^{R-1}.
\end{equation*}
Taking logarithms proves the first radius bound; the second uses
$\log((1+t)/(1-t))\geq2t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sparse linear PPR from point-source solvers]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-point-source-linear-reduction}
Suppose a point-source routine, for every $v$ and tolerance $\eta>0$, returns
$\widehat\pi^{(v)}$ satisfying
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{D^{-1}(\widehat\pi^{(v)}-\pi(e_v))}_\infty\leq\eta
\end{equation*}
in $\widetilde O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\eta))$ work.  For a distribution $s$ with
$T=\supp(s)$, put $A_s:=\sum_{v\in T}\sqrt{s_v}$.  Then weighted point-source
solves give a general-seed approximation with error $\epsppr$ in
\begin{equation}
 |T|+\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{A_s^2}{\sqrt\alpha\,\epsppr}
 \right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-linear-reduction}
\end{equation}
work.  Since $1\leq A_s^2\leq|T|$, this does not recover the desired additive
$|T|+\widetilde O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$ bound for a uniform $|T|$-point
source.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linearity of the unregularized system gives
\begin{equation*}
 \pi(s)=\sum_{v\in T}s_v\pi(e_v).
\end{equation*}
Choose $\eta_v=\epsppr/(A_s\sqrt{s_v})$.  The degree-normalized error of
$\sum_vs_v\widehat\pi^{(v)}$ is at most
$\sum_vs_v\eta_v=\epsppr$, while
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{v\in T}\frac1{\eta_v}=\frac{A_s^2}{\epsppr}.
\end{equation*}
Reading the source and merging its outputs costs the additive $|T|$ term.
The RPPR obstacle solution is nonlinear in $s$ and does not obey this
superposition; the exact three-vertex merge witness after
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-cactus-live-sites} shows why componentwise point-source
RPPR reporters can all stop while their joint middle key is positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The coarse-accuracy point-source regime is already closed]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-point-source-coarse-regime}
The active-set algorithm of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16339}{Wei and Yang}
computes, with high probability, a point-source ACL
$\epsppr$-approximate PageRank vector in
\begin{equation*}
 \widetilde O\!\left(\frac1{\epsppr^2}\right)
\end{equation*}
work, with support volume at most $2/\epsppr$.  Consequently, whenever
\begin{equation}
 \epsppr\geq\sqrt\alpha,
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-coarse-regime}
\end{equation}
their bound is no larger than the target
$\widetilde O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr))$.  Thus the only accuracy regime not
already covered by this comparison is
\begin{equation}
 \epsppr<\sqrt\alpha.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-hard-regime}
\end{equation}
Under the usual RPPR-to-PPR choice
$\rho=\epskkt=\epsppr/2$, this is the small-threshold regime
$\rho<\sqrt\alpha/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Wei and Yang use the nonlazy teleportation parameter $\bar\alpha$.  Setting
\begin{equation*}
 \bar\alpha=\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}
\end{equation*}
matches the shared lazy convention: if
$p=(1+\alpha)/2$ and $c=(1-\alpha)/2$, then
\begin{equation*}
 p\bigl(D-(1-\bar\alpha)A\bigr)=pD-cA,
 \qquad p\bar\alpha=\alpha.
\end{equation*}
Their ACL certificate writes the returned vector as the exact PageRank of
$e_o-r$, where $0\leq r\leq\epsppr d$.  Positivity of the PageRank operator
and stationarity on $d$ therefore give the shared degree-normalized error
bound.  Finally,
$1/\epsppr^2\leq1/(\sqrt\alpha\epsppr)$ is exactly
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-coarse-regime}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Existing point-source absolute-error methods do not close the hard regime]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-point-source-existing-hard-regime}
The point-source restriction aligns the note with several single-source
PageRank algorithms, but their published bounds do not already imply
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-hard-regime}.  More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The degree-normalized SSPPR algorithm of
  \href{https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2024.9}{Wei, Wen, and Yang}
  has expected work
  \begin{equation*}
   \widetilde O\!\left(
    \frac1{\bar\alpha\epsppr}
    \sqrt{\sum_t\frac{\pi(o,t)}{d_t}}
   \right)
   \leq \widetilde O\!\left(
    \frac1{\bar\alpha\epsppr}
   \right),
  \end{equation*}
  after restoring the teleportation parameter \(\bar\alpha\) that their
  statement treats as a constant.  Their candidate-discovery subroutine
  also assumes a \(\Theta(m)\)-time graph preprocessing.  With
  \(\bar\alpha=2\alpha/(1+\alpha)\), this is the classical
  \(\widetilde O(1/(\alpha\epsppr))\) dependence rather than the target.
\item The local Chebyshev method of
  \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10789}{Yang, Li, Liao, Lin, and Wang}
  proves, under its stated stability assumption, work
  \(O(K^2/\epsppr)\), where the PageRank Chebyshev truncation degree is
  \(K=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)\).  Thus its worst-case theorem again
  gives \(\widetilde O(1/(\alpha\epsppr))\).  The second factor \(K\)
  comes from distributing a safe truncation budget over all subset
  recurrences, not from the polynomial approximation degree itself.
\item The FISTA locality theorem of
  \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21138}{Fountoulakis and
  Mart\'inez-Rubio} does contain the accelerated core term
  \(\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))\), but only under a confinement
  hypothesis and with the additional boundary work
  \begin{equation*}
   O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\vol(B)}}{\rho\alpha^{3/2}}\right).
  \end{equation*}
  Their point-source star witness shows that standard FISTA can activate
  an arbitrarily high-volume off-support center, so this is not a
  graph-universal local bound.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the comparison in
\cref{cor:aesp-cd-point-source-coarse-regime} remains the strongest
unconditional external closure used by this note: existing point-source
methods remove neither the small-\(\alpha\) factor nor the one-sided local
reporter problem in the hard regime.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[APPR gives an optimal-volume envelope but not accelerated discovery]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope}
Let \(0<\alpha<1\), let \(x^{\mathrm{APPR}}(\rho)\) be any terminal output of
the classical APPR coordinate-push algorithm at normalized residual threshold
\(\alpha\rho d_i\), and let \(S^\star(\rho)\) be the support of the shared
RPPR solution.  Then
\begin{equation}
 S^\star(\rho)
 \subseteq \supp(x^{\mathrm{APPR}}(\rho))
 \subseteq S^\star\!\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\rho\right).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \vol\bigl(\supp(x^{\mathrm{APPR}}(\rho))\bigr)
 \leq \frac{2}{(1-\alpha)\rho}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-volume}
\end{equation}
For an arbitrary source distribution with $T=\supp(s)$, the same envelope
also obeys
\begin{equation}
 \max_{i\in\supp(x^{\mathrm{APPR}}(\rho))}\operatorname{dist}(T,i)
 \leq1+
 \frac{\log_+(2/(\alpha\rho))}
 {\log((1+\sqrt\alpha)/(1-\sqrt\alpha))}
 \leq1+\frac{\log_+(2/(\alpha\rho))}{2\sqrt\alpha}.
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-radius}
\end{equation}
In particular, throughout the accelerated range \(\alpha<1/2\), APPR
constructs a containing envelope of volume below \(4/\rho\).  Nevertheless,
its graph-universal degree work is
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\rho))\) in the worst case.  Thus the remaining
point-source problem is not to prove that an output-sized envelope exists;
it is to construct or reuse that envelope without the classical
\(1/\alpha\) repeated-push factor.  This separation already occurs at graph
radius one: for \(0<\rho\leq1/16\), on the center-seeded star with
\(m=\lfloor1/(8\rho)\rfloor\), the APPR envelope \(E\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 m\leq\vol(E)\leq2m,qquad
 \operatorname{rad}_o(E)\leq1,qquad
 W_{\mathrm{APPR}}>
 \frac{3}{32\alpha}\vol(E).
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-reuse-gap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two support inclusions are Theorem~10 of
\href{https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume22/20-029/20-029.pdf}{Ha,
Fountoulakis, and Mahoney}; their degree-unscaled variable is related to the
shared variable by a positive diagonal congruence and hence has identical
support.  Applying the shared support cap
\(\vol(S^\star(\eta))\leq1/\eta\) to the right inclusion with
\(\eta=(1-\alpha)\rho/2\) proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-volume}.  For a general source,
the same right inclusion and \cref{prop:aesp-cd-sparse-source-radius} at
parameter \(\eta\) give
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-radius}.  The classical APPR
upper bound and the matching center-seeded-star lower bound proved in the
parent manuscript give the final worst-case work statement.  Notice that
both the support sandwich and the source-set radius hold for general source
distributions; the point-source restriction makes the dynamic discovery
trace singly rooted.  For the final display, the root belongs to every APPR
support, so its degree gives \(\vol(E)\geq m\), while \(E\subseteq V\) gives
\(\vol(E)\leq2m\).  The parent star theorem gives
\(W_{\mathrm{APPR}}>3/(128\alpha\rho)\); since
\(2m\leq1/(4\rho)\), this is larger than
\(3\vol(E)/(32\alpha)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An APPR-envelope oracle closes semantic sparse-source PPR]
\label{cor:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-oracle}
\label{cor:aesp-cd-appr-envelope-oracle}
For any shared source distribution \(s\), fix \(0<\epsppr\leq1\), set
\(\rho=\epsppr/2\), and suppose the set
\begin{equation*}
 E=\supp(x^{\mathrm{APPR}}(\rho))
\end{equation*}
is supplied without charging its construction.  Strongly-convex accelerated
proximal gradient applied to \(F_\rho\) on the fixed coordinate envelope
\(E\), followed by zero padding, returns \(\widehat x\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(\widehat x-x^0)}_\infty\leq\epsppr
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-oracle-error}
\end{equation}
using
\begin{equation}
 |\supp(s)|+\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac{\vol(E)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right)
 \leq
 |\supp(s)|+\widetilde O\!\left(
  \frac1{\epsppr\sqrt\alpha}
 \right)
 \label{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-oracle-work}
\end{equation}
degree work in the accelerated range \(\alpha<1/2\).  No strict active or
dual margin and no exact-face identification are required for this semantic
PPR guarantee.  The point-source hypothesis is needed to turn the local
source-set geometry into one rooted discovery trace, not for the radius
bound or this oracle-supplied terminal solve.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope}, \(E\) contains
\(S^\star(\rho)\).  Therefore restricting the obstacle objective to \(E\)
does not change its minimizer.  The restricted Hessian still has spectrum in
\([\alpha,1]\), so accelerated proximal gradient drives the objective gap
below \(\alpha\epsppr^2/8\) in
\(\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)\) iterations.  Strong convexity then gives
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\widehat x-x^\star(\rho)}_2\leq\epsppr/2.
\end{equation*}
Since every degree is at least one,
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{D^{-1/2}(\widehat x-x^\star(\rho))}_\infty
 \leq\epsppr/2.
\end{equation*}
The RPPR comparison inequality
\(0\leq D^{-1/2}(x^0-x^\star(\rho))\leq\rho\one\), with
\(\rho=\epsppr/2\), proves
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-oracle-error}.  Each restricted
matrix application costs \(O(\vol(E))\), and
\cref{eq:aesp-cd-point-source-appr-envelope-volume} bounds that volume by
\(4/\rho=8/\epsppr\) when \(\alpha<1/2\).  Logarithmic accuracy factors are
absorbed by \(\widetilde O\), and reading the sparse source costs
\(O(|\supp(s)|)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Point-source structure is not closed under residual recursion]
\label{prop:aesp-cd-point-source-residual-recursion-stop}
Even an exact lower solve on the first point-source face can leave a residual
with several positive coordinates.  On the three-vertex path, take the
middle vertex as the source and put
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha=\frac13,
 \qquad \rho=\frac1{12}.
\end{equation*}
In degree-unscaled coordinates the root-only exact solve has
\begin{equation*}
 y_1=\frac{5}{24},
 \qquad
 \ell_\rho-\widehat H(y_1e_1)
 =\begin{pmatrix}1/24&0&1/24\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}.
\end{equation*}
Thus a bootstrap which recursively calls a point-source routine on the
unresolved residual leaves its hypothesis after one exact step.  For linear
PPR the two residual sources may still be superposed, but that pays the
source-allocation factor from
\cref{prop:aesp-cd-point-source-linear-reduction}.  For an obstacle
correction the map is nonlinear, so componentwise point-source solves are
not an exact replacement.  This proposition only stops a black-box
point-source recursion; it does not stop a reporter designed for the joint
nonnegative residual.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $p=2/3$, $c=1/3$, and the degree vector is $(1,2,1)$.  The middle
load is
$\ell_1=\alpha(1-2\rho)=5/18$, while
$\widehat H_{11}=2p=4/3$, giving $y_1=5/24$.  Each endpoint has load
$-\alpha\rho=-1/36$ and receives $cy_1=5/72$, so its residual is
$5/72-1/36=1/24$.  The active middle equation is exact.
\end{proof}
