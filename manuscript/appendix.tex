\section{Point Sources, Sparse Sources, and Nonlinearity}
\label{app:seed-scope}

The main theorem assumes $\vs=\eunit{v}$.  This appendix records exactly what
that restriction does and does not imply.

\begin{proposition}[Linear superposition with a mixture factor]
\label{prop:sparse-source-superposition}
Let $\vs$ be a nonnegative unit-mass distribution with support $K$.  Suppose a
point-source algorithm at vertex $v$ returns $\widehat\vpi^{(v)}$ with
degree-normalized error at most $\varepsilon_v$ and work
$\softO(1/(\sqrt\alpha\varepsilon_v))$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\vpi:=\sum_{v\in K}s_v\widehat\vpi^{(v)}
 \label{eq:sparse-source-composition}
\end{equation}
has error at most $\sum_{v\in K}s_v\varepsilon_v$.  Choosing the individual
tolerances optimally gives total work
\begin{equation}
 |K|+\softO\!\left(
   \frac{(\sum_{v\in K}\sqrt{s_v})^2}
        {\sqrt\alpha\epsppr}
 \right).
 \label{eq:sparse-source-mixture-work}
\end{equation}
The mixture factor lies in $[1,|K|]$ and equals $|K|$ for the uniform
distribution on $K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The PPR solution is linear in the load:
$\vpi(\vs)=\sum_{v\in K}s_v\vpi(\eunit{v})$.  The triangle inequality proves
the error statement.  Minimizing $\sum_v1/\varepsilon_v$ subject to
$\sum_vs_v\varepsilon_v\leq\epsppr$ gives
$\varepsilon_v=\epsppr/(\sqrt{s_v}\sum_u\sqrt{s_u})$ and
\cref{eq:sparse-source-mixture-work}.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives the stated range.
\end{proof}

RPPR does not admit the same composition.  Already at $\alpha=1$,
$\mQ=\mI$ and
\[
 (\vx_\rho^*(\vs))_i
 =\max\!\left\{\frac{s_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}-\rho\sqrt{d_i},0\right\},
\]
which is nonlinear in $\vs$.  Consequently independently discovered
point-source RPPR supports cannot simply be superposed to obtain the support
for a multi-source instance.  The rooted active-set geometry and the
point-source end-to-end bound in this paper should therefore not be read as a
free reduction of the stronger general-seed problem.
