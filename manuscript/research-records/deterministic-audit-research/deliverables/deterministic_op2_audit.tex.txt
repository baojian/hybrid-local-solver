\begin{abstract}
We record an independent audit of a deterministic solution to OP2 in the
supplied problem-definition note. The continuation and two-energy proof
was developed by the parallel task \emph{Prove conjecture 2 deterministically};
the present task checked its mathematical argument, local implementation
ledger, and bounded-arithmetic extension. We restate the proof for a
Dirichlet restriction, which justifies permanent degree pruning. We then
give a bounded component prepass, a deterministic boundary-forest spectral
certificate, and a directly certified accelerated component solver. The
fully charged bound remains $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ with
polylogarithmic objective-accuracy dependence. No randomized primitive is
used. Exact finite checks supplement the proof; this report is not a
machine-checked formal verification.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Provenance and scope.}
The main proof below is an audited restatement of the parallel task's
continuation argument, not a claim that this task independently originated
that argument. The degree-pruning and component refinements were developed
privately during this audit. Earlier private candidate algorithms, including
mass-preserving score repair, retain their original unresolved rate questions.
The requested research-duration ledger is maintained separately from the
mathematical verification record. Repository reference files were not edited.

\section{The precise target}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be finite, connected, simple, undirected and unweighted, with
original degrees $d_i\ge1$ and parameter $0<\alpha\le1$.
Put $D=\operatorname{diag}(d)$,
$w=D^{1/2}\one$, $c=(1-\alpha)/2$, and
\[
 Q=\alpha I+c(I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}),\qquad
 b=\alpha D^{-1/2}e_v.
\]
For $\rho>0$ define
\[
 F_\rho(x)=\tfrac12x^TQx-b^Tx+\alpha\rho\norm{D^{1/2}x}_1.
\]
Its unique optimum $x^*_\rho$ is nonnegative. On nonnegative vectors the
last term is $\alpha\rho w^Tx$. Standard Stieltjes/KKT properties give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard}
 \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I,\quad Q^{-1}\ge0,\quad Qw=\alpha w,
 \quad w^Tx^*_\rho\le1,\quad \vol(\supp x^*_\rho)\le1/\rho.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic OP2]\label{thm:main}
In the nonzero point-source regime $0<\rho<1/d_v$, there is a deterministic
local algorithm returning a sparse vector $\widehat x$ such that
\[
 0\le\widehat x\le x^*_\rho,\qquad
 F_\rho(\widehat x)-F_\rho(x^*_\rho)\le\eps,
 \qquad \vol(\supp\widehat x)\le1/\rho,
\]
with total work $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$. The suppressed factors
are polylogarithmic in the displayed inverse parameters, inverse objective
accuracy, and exposed or emitted words. No graph-wide preprocessing,
supplied support, uncharged boundary oracle, or randomized algorithm is used.
\end{theorem}

The work model is the reference's exact-real algebraic word model: every
scalar operation, comparison, state access/update, degree reply, adjacency
incidence inspection (first or repeated), and output word is charged.
For arbitrary $\rho>0$ include an additive constant in the work bound.
If $\rho d_v\ge1$, zero is exact. If $\alpha=1$, only the seed can be
nonzero, with degree density $(1/d_v-\rho)_+$.

\section{A safe fixed Dirichlet restriction}
Fix the final requested $\rho$ and define, only conceptually,
\[
 \mathcal A=\{i:d_i\le1/\rho\},\qquad M=Q_{\mathcal A\mathcal A}.
\]
The algorithm never enumerates $\mathcal A$. It classifies an encountered
vertex using its charged degree reply. It retains original degrees in all
formulas and never scans a row outside $\mathcal A$.

Every continuation regularizer $r\ge\rho$ has
$\supp(x^*_r)\subseteq\mathcal A$ by \eqref{eq:standard}; thus restricting
to $\mathcal A$ does not change those optima or their objective gaps.
Write $w=w_{\mathcal A}$ and $b=b_{\mathcal A}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dirichlet}
 \alpha I\preceq M\preceq I,\quad M^{-1}\ge0,\quad
 Mw\ge\alpha w,\quad |M|w\le w.
\end{equation}
The last inequality implies both
$|Mu|\le\gamma w$ when $|u|\le\gamma w$, and
$w^T|Mu|\le w^T|u|$. The degree-scaled adjacency on this restriction is
substochastic, not renormalized. Its column sums in mass coordinates are
at most one.

Let $x_r$ denote the restricted optimum for any $r>0$. Its KKT slack obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kkt}
 r^*=Mx_r-b+\alpha r w\ge0,\qquad r_i^*x_{r,i}=0,
 \qquad 0\le b-Mx_r\le\alpha r w.
\end{equation}
For the last assertion, use stationarity on positive coordinates and
nonpositive off-diagonals on zero coordinates. Weighted summation and
\eqref{eq:dirichlet} give mass at most one and support volume at most $1/r$.
Inverse positivity also proves $x_{r/2}\ge x_r$: if
$S=\{i:x_{r,i}>x_{r/2,i}\}$ were nonempty, the KKT inequalities and
Stieltjes signs would give
$M_{SS}(x_r-x_{r/2})_S\le-\alpha r w_S/2$,
contradicting $M_{SS}^{-1}\ge0$. Consequently the analytical
set $C_r=\supp(x_{r/2})$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
 \vol(C_r)\le2/r,\qquad i\notin C_r\Longrightarrow
 x_{r,i}=0,\quad r_i^*\ge\alpha r w_i/2.
\end{equation}
Here $x_{r/2}$ is the \emph{restricted} optimum; it need not equal the full
graph optimum when $r/2<\rho$. This distinction is necessary.

\section{One continuation stage}
At regularizer $r$, let $\lambda=\alpha r$. Assume the sparse baseline obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline}
 0\le\bar x\le x_r,\qquad s=b-M\bar x,
 \qquad0\le s\le4\lambda w.
\end{equation}
Put $m_s=w^Ts\le\alpha$ and define analytical comparators
\[
 t=M^{-1}s,\qquad \xi^*=x_r-\bar x.
\]
By \eqref{eq:dirichlet},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparators}
 0\le\xi^*\le t\le4r w,\qquad w^Tt\le m_s/\alpha\le1.
\end{equation}
Neither comparator is computed.

Choose dyadic $\theta\le1/2$ by halving until $\theta^2\le\alpha$; then
$\alpha/4\le\mu:=\theta^2\le\alpha$. Set $a=1-\theta$ and
\[
 \mathcal K=\{z:0\le z\le4r w,\ w^Tz\le m_s/\alpha\}.
\]
Starting at $\xi_0=z_0=0$, the exact stage is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iteration}
 \begin{aligned}
 y&=(\xi+\theta z)/(1+\theta),\\
 q&=az+\theta y-(My-s+\lambda w)/\theta,\\
 p&=\Proj_{\mathcal K}^{\rm Euclidean}(q),\\
 \xi^+&=a\xi+\theta p,\qquad z^+=p.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Its correction objective is $J(\xi)=\xi^TM\xi/2-(s-\lambda w)^T\xi$.
Translation by $\bar x$ preserves the original objective gap.
The implementation may replace $4r$ in the box by
$U=\max\{h,\max_i s_i/(\alpha w_i)\}\le4r$.
Indeed $M(Uw)\ge\alpha Uw\ge s$ still places $t$ and $\xi^*$ in the box
and preserves its upper-face sector inequality. All estimates below retain
the uniform upper bound $4r$.

\subsection{A comparison lemma}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:comparison}
In a Hilbert metric, let $f$ be one-smooth and $\mu$-strongly convex, with
$\theta^2=\mu$, $a=1-\theta$, and $y=(x+\theta z)/(1+\theta)$.
Set $q=az+\theta y-\nabla f(y)/\theta$ and $x^+=ax+\theta p$.
For any comparator $u$ satisfying $\ip{p-u}{q-p}\ge0$,
\[
 f(x^+)-f(u)+\tfrac\mu2\norm{p-u}^2
 \le a\{f(x)-f(u)+\tfrac\mu2\norm{z-u}^2\}
 -\tfrac{\mu\theta(1-\mu)}2\norm{z-y}^2.
\]
The comparator need not minimize $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $g=\nabla f(y)$. Since $x^+=y-g+\theta(p-q)$, smoothness yields
$f(x^+)\le f(y)-\norm g^2/2+\mu\norm{p-q}^2/2$.
The sector condition implies
$\norm{p-u}^2\le\norm{q-u}^2-\norm{p-q}^2$.
After addition, expand
$q-u=a(z-u)+\theta(y-u)-g/\theta$.
Apply strong convexity at $x$ and $u$ with weights $a$ and $\theta$, using
\[
 a(x-y)+\theta(u-y)=-\theta\{a(z-u)+\theta(y-u)\}.
\]
Finally use the convex-combination squared-norm identity and
$x-y=\theta(y-z)$. The remaining term is
$-\mu a\theta(1+\theta)\norm{z-y}^2/2$, which is the asserted term.
\end{proof}

The Euclidean projection supplies the sector condition for $u=\xi^*$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinaryenergy}
 E_k=J(\xi_k)-J(\xi^*)+\tfrac\mu2\norm{z_k-\xi^*}^2,
 \qquad E_{k+1}\le aE_k.
\end{equation}
Complementarity gives
$J(0)-J(\xi^*)=\tfrac12(\xi^*)^TM\xi^*$.
Mass at most one, $w_i\ge1$, and $M\preceq I$ therefore give $E_0\le1$.
Hence $O(\alpha^{-1/2}\log(1/\tau))$ steps suffice for gap $\tau$.

\section{The second energy and the cumulative work theorem}
The essential extra fact is a sector inequality in a different metric.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sector}
For the projection in \eqref{eq:iteration}, let $n=q-p$. Then
\[
 \ip{p-t}{Mn}=(Mp-s)^Tn\ge0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n=\eta w+n^{\rm up}-n^{\rm low}$ with nonnegative multipliers.
At a lower face, $p_i=0$ implies $(Mp-s)_i\le0$. At an upper face,
Stieltjes signs and $p\le4rw$ give
$(Mp)_i\ge4r(Mw)_i\ge4\lambda w_i\ge s_i$.
If the mass multiplier is active, $w^Tp=m_s/\alpha$ and
$w^T(Mp-s)\ge\alpha w^Tp-m_s=0$. Each normal contribution is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

Define the second objective and energy by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secondenergy}
 \mathcal A(\xi)=\tfrac12\norm{M\xi-s}^2+\lambda(Mw)^T\xi,
 \quad B_k=\mathcal A(\xi_k)-\mathcal A(t)
               +\tfrac\mu2\norm{z_k-t}_M^2.
\end{equation}
Its gradient in the $M$ metric is $M\xi-s+\lambda w$ and its metric
Hessian is $M$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:comparison}--\ref{lem:sector} yield
$B_{k+1}\le aB_k$. This energy may be negative; the comparison lemma
requires neither nonnegativity nor optimality of $t$.

Since $\norm s^2\le4\lambda m_s$, $t^TMt=t^Ts\le4r m_s$, and
$\mathcal A(t)=\lambda m_s$, we have $B_0\le3\lambda m_s$.
The linear term in $\mathcal A$ is nonnegative, so
$\norm{M\xi_k-s}^2\le8\lambda m_s$.
Also $q^*=s-M\xi^*=b-Mx_r$ obeys
$0\le q^*\le\lambda w$ and $w^Tq^*\le m_s$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:response}
 \norm{M(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2\le18\lambda m_s\le18\alpha^2r.
\end{equation}
This is a bound on the actual constrained trajectory. It is not an
unproved general claim of matrix-metric nonexpansiveness of projection.

\subsection{Selected signed flow}
Use mass coordinates $V=\theta D^{1/2}z$ and
$V^*=\theta D^{1/2}\xi^*$. Let $P=A_{\mathcal A\mathcal A}D_{\mathcal A}^{-1}$,
$K_0=(I+P)/2$, and $\beta_0=(1-\alpha)/(1+\theta)\le a$.
Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:iteration} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 V^{\rm raw}-V^*=\beta_0K_0(V-V^*)+H-R^*,\quad
 H=-\frac{D^{1/2}(M-\mu I)(\xi-\xi^*)}{1+\theta},\quad
 R^*=D^{1/2}r^*.
\end{equation}
For $e=(V-V^*)_+$ select $S_k=\{i:V^+_i>V_i^*\}$.
Lower normals vanish there; upper and mass normals only subtract.
Nonnegativity and column sums at most one for $K_0$ imply
\[
 \norm{e_{k+1}}_1+\sum_{i\in S_k}R_i^*
 \le\beta_0\norm{e_k}_1+\sum_{i\in S_k}H_{k,i}.
\]
Because $e_0=0$, telescoping and \eqref{eq:margin} yield
\[
 \tfrac\lambda2 D_{\rm out}\le\sum_k\sum_{i\in S_k}H_{k,i},\qquad
 D_{\rm out}=\sum_{k<K}\vol(\supp z_{k+1}\setminus C_r).
\]
The selected degree-volume is at most $D_{\rm out}+2K/r$. Therefore
\[
 \frac{\lambda^2}{4}D_{\rm out}^2
 \le(D_{\rm out}+2K/r)H_2,\qquad
 H_2=\sum_{k<K}\norm{D^{-1/2}H_k}^2\le18\alpha^2rK.
\]
The last step uses $0\preceq M-\mu I\preceq M$, commutation, and
\eqref{eq:response}. Solving this scalar inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
 \boxed{\sum_{k<K}\vol(\supp z_{k+1})\le148K/r.}
\end{equation}
It charges every repeated kinetic row, even when the trajectory leaves
the true support. The analytical core $C_r$ is never discovered by an oracle.

\section{Safe stage completion and continuation}
Choose $0<\delta\le\lambda/2$ and target
$\tau=\alpha\delta^2/8$. Suppose the actual stored nonnegative candidate
$\widetilde x$ has objective gap at most $\tau$. Compute one exact PG step
\[
 p=[\widetilde x-(M\widetilde x-b+\lambda w)]_+.
\]
Descent and strong convexity give
$\norm{p-x_r}\le\delta/2$. Moreover
$v=(I-M)(\widetilde x-p)$ is a valid orthant subgradient at $p$, with
$\norm v\le\sqrt{2\tau}\le\delta/2$.

For a dyadic density grid $h\le\min(\delta/2,\lambda/4)$, let
\[
 z_i=w_i\left\lfloor[p_i/w_i-\delta]_+\right\rfloor_h,
 \qquad\bar x^+=\max(\bar x,z).
\]
Then $0\le z\le x_r$ and $0\le x_r-z\le2\delta w$.
On a retained positive coordinate, uniform clipping decreases the matrix
product by at least $\alpha\delta w_i$, and grid rounding can raise it
by at most $hw_i$. Hence
\[
 (Mz-b)_i\le(-\lambda+\delta/2-\alpha\delta+h)w_i\le0.
\]
At zero coordinates the same conclusion follows from Stieltjes signs.
The coordinatewise maximum of two nonnegative subsolutions of $Mu\le b$
is again a subsolution. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repair}
 0\le\bar x^+\le x_r,\quad
 0\le b-M\bar x^+\le2\alpha r w,\quad
 F_r(\bar x^+)-F_r(x_r)\le2\delta^2/r.
\end{equation}
For the last inequality, support containment removes the KKT linear term,
$M\preceq I$, and $\vol(\supp x_r)\le1/r$ bound the squared error.

Start with $r_0=1/d_v$ and $\bar x=0$. Successive stages use
$r_j=\max(\rho,r_{j-1}/2)$. The first source is diffuse because
$r_1\ge r_0/2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:repair} implies
$s\le2\alpha r_jw\le4\alpha r_{j+1}w$ at the next stage, while
optimum monotonicity preserves the baseline order. At the final stage
halve $\delta$ until $2\delta^2/\rho\le\eps$.
There are logarithmically many stages and $\sum_j1/r_j=O(1/\rho)$.

\section{A realizable local ledger and bounded arithmetic}
In degree densities store the primal correction as a common scale times
normalized records. Averaging changes those records only on newly emitted
kinetic coordinates. Scanning their original rows updates the sparse matrix
response. Source records are supported on the baseline, its neighbors, and
the seed, with $O(1/r)$ records. Old kinetic exceptions are removed and new
ones installed; their costs are paid by the old and new kinetic volumes.

Euclidean projection onto the box and mass cap has density formula
\[
 z_i/w_i=\min\{U,(q_i/w_i-\eta)_+\}.
\]
Deterministic ordered trees store degree sums and weighted-key sums.
Two tail queries express clipped mass; a finite ordered-breakpoint search
finds an exact mass multiplier with polylogarithmic work per query.
Unexposed coordinates have zero state/source and negative raw values.
Only positive emitted coordinates cause row scans.

More explicitly, a tail at $t$ returns
$D(t)=\sum_{q_i/w_i>t}d_i$ and
$S(t)=\sum_{q_i/w_i>t}d_iq_i/w_i$. The clipped mass at multiplier $\eta$ is
\[
 S(\eta)-\eta D(\eta)
 -S(\eta+U)+(\eta+U)D(\eta+U).
\]
The two state trees induce at most four ordered breakpoint lists, accounting
for the two clipping levels and their respective common affine scales.
Binary searches using this mass evaluation identify adjacent breakpoints
around the target mass. On the resulting interval, mass is affine, so one
division gives the exact multiplier. Rank selection and tail evaluation
use tree heights; no numerical bisection to unspecified precision is used.

With dyadic rounding, query the \emph{closed} tail whose ideal density is
at least $h$. This emits exactly coordinates that remain positive after
rounding; tiny ideal outputs are not enumerated first. Scalar rebases scan
only retained records, with no adjacency inspection, and occur only
$O(\log(1/\tau))$ times per accelerated stage.

The full stage ledger is, up to polylogarithmic factors,
\[
 K(1+|\supp s|)+\sum_{k<K}\vol(\supp z_{k+1})
 +\vol(\supp\bar x)+1.
\]
It includes source construction, first and repeated incidences, state
maintenance, final materialization, terminal PG, output, and discarded
history. Summation with \eqref{eq:work} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Why bounded dyadic arithmetic preserves the theorem}
For actual stored states, model one step by a raw density error bounded by
$\kappa_r$, downward projection density error $\kappa_p$, and downward
primal density error $\kappa_x$. The two energies satisfy
\[
 E^+\le aE+\zeta,\qquad B^+\le aB+\zeta,\qquad
 \zeta=2\mu\kappa_r+\tfrac52\kappa_x
              +\tfrac52(\theta+\mu)\kappa_p.
\]
To check this, use mass at most one to pair uniform density errors with
comparators, and $|M|w\le w$ for the residual terms. Downward errors
preserve feasibility and decrease each positive linear mass penalty.

Here are the error estimates explicitly. A raw error bounded by
$\kappa_rw$ weakens either sector inequality by at most $2\kappa_r$, since
both projected point and comparator have mass at most one; in the $M$ metric
use $w^T|M(p-u)|\le w^T|p-u|\le2$. The comparison lemma therefore gains
at most $2\mu\kappa_r$. For a feasible downward displacement
$0\le d\le\eta w$, its mass is at most one and $\norm d^2\le\eta$.
The increase in either positional objective is at most $5\eta/2$:
the residual or gradient pairing is at most $2\eta$, the quadratic remainder
is at most $\eta/2$, and the positive linear penalty decreases.
Rounding the projected point changes the primal position by $\theta d$,
costing at most $5\theta\kappa_p/2$, and its kinetic squared distance
by at most $5\mu\kappa_p/2$. A subsequent primal floor costs at most
$5\kappa_x/2$. These are the stated constants.

The scalar-rebase implementation has
$\kappa_r\le2h/\theta$, $\kappa_p\le h$, and $\kappa_x\le10h$.
Indeed normalized neighbor-sum error is unchanged by point increments.
At a scalar rebase with scale $\sigma<1/2$, its per-degree magnitude obeys
$e^+\le\sigma e+2h$, so it stays below $4h$.
Multiplication by $c/[\theta(1+\theta)]$ gives the raw-error bound.
Before every step the retained scale is at least $1/2$, so normalized
position densities are at most $2U\le8$. Flooring the new scale loses at
most $8h$ in primal density, flooring its selected increment loses at most
$h$, and the optional primal rebase loses at most another $h$.
Thus $\Gamma:=\zeta/\theta\le29h/\theta$. Choose
$h\le\min\{\theta\tau/256,\theta\alpha^2r/29\}$, in addition to
terminal-repair bounds.
Then the ordinary error is at most $a^KE_0+\Gamma<\tau$ for a block
schedule with $a^KE_0\le\tau/2$. The squared-response bound gains only
$4\Gamma$. For completeness, put $B_C=K\vol(C_r)\le2K/r$,
let $D_o$ be the repeated kinetic volume outside $C_r$, and let
$\nu=2h\le\lambda/4$. The perturbed selected-flow inequality is
\[
 (\lambda/2-\nu)D_o
 \le\sqrt{H_2(D_o+B_C)}+\nu B_C,
 \qquad H_2\le22\alpha^2rK.
\]
This charges raw error on the selected core as well as outside it.
Young's inequality gives
$D_o+B_C\le16H_2/\lambda^2+4B_C\le360K/r$.

Persistent values can be integer grid counts. Weighted reporter keys have
a common scalar denominator, so degree denominators cancel in subtree
moments instead of accumulating an LCM. Source-energy sums can likewise be
rounded upward on a common denominator. Intermediate integer lengths are
polynomially bounded by logarithms of inverse parameters, inverse accuracy,
and exposed counts, together with the bit lengths of rational inputs,
labels, and degree replies. This is a bounded-rational
extension, not a claim about unchecked ordinary floating-point execution.
In an algebraic model without a unit-cost floor instruction, each bounded
grid count can be found by integer binary search with arithmetic and
comparisons; its additional cost is polylogarithmic in the same parameters.

\subsection{A shorter computable initial-energy bound}
Let $c_+=(s-\lambda w)_+$ and $m=m_s/\alpha$.
Complementarity and strong convexity imply, for $u=\xi^*$,
\[
 u^TMu=(s-\lambda w)^Tu\le c_+^Tu,\qquad
 E_0\le\frac{1+\mu/\alpha}{2}u^TMu.
\]
The bounds $u\le4rw$, $w^Tu\le m$, and
$\alpha\norm u^2\le u^TMu$ give three independently valid estimates:
\[
 E_0\le\frac{1+\mu/\alpha}{2}
 \min\left\{4r w^Tc_+,\;
 m\max_i\frac{c_{+,i}}{w_i},\;
 \frac{\norm{c_+}^2}{\alpha}\right\}.
\]
Taking the minimum with one shortens the certified block schedule while
leaving its trajectory and work theorem unchanged. The last estimate uses
Cauchy--Schwarz to solve $u^TMu\le\norm{c_+}\sqrt{u^TMu/\alpha}$.
For integer source counts with common denominator $C$, replace each
$a_i^2/d_i$ by its integer ceiling before summing; this rounds the squared
source norm upward and avoids accumulating unrelated degree denominators.

\subsection{Certified checkpoints without opening new PG rows}
For an actual candidate $x$, compute the exact PG point $p$ by scanning only
$\supp x$. In degree densities its affected records are the seed and
neighbors of those rows. All other allowed coordinates have $x=p=0$.
Although $p$ can activate vertices of very large total degree, their rows
are not needed to bound the subgradient:
\[
 v=(I-M)(x-p)\in\partial(F_r+\iota_{\R_+})(p),\qquad
 \norm v\le(1-\alpha)\norm{x-p}.
\]
If a computed upper bound $R^2\ge(1-\alpha)^2\norm{x-p}^2$ satisfies
$R^2\le\alpha^2\delta^2/4$, strong convexity implies
\[
 \norm{p-x_r}\le\delta/2,\qquad
 F_r(p)-F_r(x_r)\le R^2/(2\alpha)\le\tau.
\]
Consequently terminal clipping is already valid. This certificate concerns
$p$, not necessarily the pre-PG candidate $x$; the stage record states the
certified point explicitly.

Check only at steps $1,2,4,\ldots$ before the fixed horizon. The candidate
support at step $k$ is contained in the baseline and previously emitted
kinetic supports, of total volume $O(k/r)$. Summing at these checkpoints
costs $\widetilde O(K/r)$. Failed checks leave the trajectory unchanged;
a successful PG pass is reused for terminal repair.

To avoid a degree-denominator LCM in the norm, write each density displacement
as $a_i/(C d_i)$ and use
\[
 U_2=\frac1{C^2}\sum_i\left\lceil\frac{a_i^2}{d_i}\right\rceil
 \ge\norm{x-p}^2,\qquad R^2=(1-\alpha)^2U_2.
\]
The excess is at most the number of affected records divided by $C^2$.
For grid denominator $H$ in the integer continuation, one choice is
$C=2\operatorname{denom}(\alpha)H^2\operatorname{denom}(r)$.
All numerator additions are then integer additions on one denominator.

\section{Practical component acceleration}
Before continuation, explore the seed component after fixed degree pruning,
stopping before original scanned degree-volume exceeds $1/\rho$. A degree
is queried before deciding to scan its row. The entire prepass costs
$\widetilde O(1/\rho)$. If incomplete, continuation supplies the theorem.
If complete, its component $C$ contains the full optimum and
$\vol(C)\le1/\rho$. A singleton has an immediate exact diagonal solution.

\subsection{An exact solve on a fixed-size component}
If discovery completes and $|C|\le16$, the implementation optionally returns
the exact obstacle optimum. In degree densities the symmetric energy matrix
has $H_{ii}=(1+\alpha)d_i/2$, $H_{ij}=-c$ on internal edges, and load
$q_i=\alpha\mathbf1_{i=v}-\alpha\rho d_i$. Start with empty active set.
Whenever an inactive $j$ has $q_j-H_{jS}f_S>0$, append $j$ and extend an
exact $LDL^T$ factorization of $H_{SS}$. Its Schur pivot is positive, and
the new coordinate equals its positive violation divided by that pivot.
Old coordinates change by $-H_{SS}^{-1}H_{Sj}f_j\ge0$. Thus every active
coordinate stays positive and at most $|C|$ additions give the exact KKT
solution. No support margin, square root, or random pivot is required.

Incremental factorizations and triangular solves cost $O(|C|^3)$ in total,
with $O(|C|^2)$ storage. The limit sixteen is an enforced absolute constant;
discovery and assembly are separately charged. This preserves the main
word bound and the rational encoding bound. It is a final exact output,
not an intermediate nondyadic warm start. Larger or incomplete components
continue through the following proved routes. This shortcut is especially
useful for tiny closed graphs at extremely small $\alpha$.

\subsection{A deterministic boundary-forest certificate}
For a larger complete component, let $k_i=d_i-\deg_C(i)$. Each vertex
with $k_i>0$ is a root at initial distance $1/k_i$. Run a deterministic
shortest-path search with unit internal edge lengths. The parent forest
assigns each vertex $i$ a root $r(i)$ and distance
$\ell_i=\operatorname{depth}(i)+1/k_{r(i)}$. Define
\[
 L_r=\sum_{i:r(i)=r}d_i\ell_i,\qquad L=\max_r L_r.
\]
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz on the parent path gives
\[
 f_i^2\le\ell_i\left\{
 \sum_{\text{path edges}}(f_u-f_v)^2+k_{r(i)}f_{r(i)}^2\right\}.
\]
After multiplying by $d_i$ and summing, every edge coefficient is at most
its root load. Therefore
\[
 \sum_i d_i f_i^2\le L\left\{
 \sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(C)}(f_i-f_j)^2+\sum_i k_i f_i^2\right\}.
\]
This proves the computable spectral lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forestbeta}
 M_C\succeq\beta I,\qquad
 \beta=\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{2L}.
\end{equation}
With no boundary roots use $\beta=\alpha$. The construction costs
$\widetilde O(\vol(C))$ and uses neither randomization nor a linear solve.

\subsection{The direct component solver}
Use the same averaged coupling with dyadic $\theta^2\in(\beta/4,\beta]$,
but project onto the known component's density box $U=1/d_v$ and mass cap
one. These contain the optimum by inverse positivity. Full sparse products
and sorted-event waterfilling cost $\widetilde O(\vol(C))$ per step.
With exact raw products and downward density rounding $h$ in both states,
the ordinary energy satisfies $E^+\le aE+5h$. Taking
$h\le\theta\tau/16$ and $a^K\le\tau/2$ gives gap below $\tau$.
Terminal PG and \eqref{eq:repair} preserve the safe final output.
The total cost is
\[
 \widetilde O\left(\frac1\rho+\frac{\vol(C)}{\sqrt\beta}\right)
 \le\widetilde O\left(\frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right).
\]

At geometrically spaced iterations, one exact PG point $p$ has subgradient
norm at most $(1-\beta)\norm{x-p}$. If its squared bound is at most
$\beta^2\delta^2/4$, then $\norm{p-x^*_\rho}\le\delta/2$ and safe
terminal clipping is already valid. Otherwise continue to the certified
fixed horizon. The extra checks add only a logarithmic factor.
The norm uses the same rounded-up common-denominator sum as above, with
$C=2\operatorname{denom}(\alpha)H\operatorname{denom}(\rho)$ for the
component's directly stored grid states and with $1-\beta$ replacing
$1-\alpha$. Thus these certificates also avoid degree-denominator growth.

\subsection{A bounded region pilot with global acceptance}
If component discovery reaches its budget before completion, let $C$ be
exactly the scanned vertices. This is a connected seed-containing set with
$\vol(C)\le1/\rho$. Perform at most one preliminary solve on $Q_{CC}$,
treating all original edges leaving $C$ as Dirichlet boundary edges.
The forest lower bound $\beta_C\ge\alpha$ applies to this restricted matrix.
Its optimum has mass at most one and density at most $1/d_v$, so the same
component solver is applicable. Use local gap target
$\tau_C=\alpha^2\delta^2/16$ and additionally require
$h\le\alpha\delta\rho/16$.

Acceptance uses a \emph{global} PG point on $\mathcal A$, obtained by
extending the candidate by zero outside $C$. Its affected records include
neighbors in $\mathcal A\setminus C$. Use the previously proved test
$(1-\alpha)^2U_2\le\alpha^2\delta^2/4$, with $\alpha$ rather than
$\beta_C$. A passing test justifies the global clipping and objective
guarantee, including any retained coordinates outside $C$.
If no check passes by the prescribed local horizon, discard the pilot
iterate and start the proved continuation from zero, retaining only the
charged oracle cache.

This adds at most
$\widetilde O(\vol(C)/\sqrt{\beta_C})\le
\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ work. All partial-region assembly,
products, projections, global checks, discarded state, and fallback work
are included. Thus one preliminary solve preserves the OP2 bound.

If $C$ already contains the full optimum, its local objective gap is the
global gap. PG descent at the pilot horizon then gives
$\norm{x-p}^2\le2\tau_C=\alpha^2\delta^2/8$.
There are at most $1+2\vol(C)\le3/\rho$ affected records; the norm rounding
excess is at most $3h^2/(4\rho)\le3\alpha^2\delta^2/1024$.
Hence the global test passes by that horizon. This implication needs no
strict complementarity margin and does not assume in advance that $C$
contains the optimum.

\subsection{Integer component iterations}
The packaged component and pilot loops store density counts
$x_i/w_i=X_i/H$ and $z_i/w_i=Z_i/H$, with $\theta=1/T$.
Write $\alpha=A_\alpha/D_\alpha$, set $Y_i=TX_i+Z_i$, and put
$G=2D_\alpha HT(T+1)$. Direct substitution gives raw density
\[
 \frac{q_i}{w_i}=\frac{n_i}{Gd_i}-\alpha\rho T,
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n_i={}&2D_\alpha(T^2-1)d_iZ_i
 +[2D_\alpha-(D_\alpha+A_\alpha)T^2]d_iY_i\\
 &+(D_\alpha-A_\alpha)T^2\sum_{j\sim i,\,j\in C}Y_j
 +2A_\alpha HT^2(T+1)\mathbf1_{i=v}.
\end{aligned}
\]
After the exact box/cap projection is rounded to counts $Z_i^+$, update
$X_i^+=\lfloor((T-1)X_i+Z_i^+)/T\rfloor$.
This is algebraically the same trajectory as the rational component loop.

An exact integer mass test avoids unnecessary ordered-tree updates. Clear
the common shift into raw density $a_i/(Bd_i)$. The box projection mass is
$\sum_{\rm free}a_i/B+U\sum_{\rm upper}d_i$.
If this is at most one, direct integer floors give the rounded projection;
otherwise the deterministic reporter finds the mass multiplier. This full
region pass is charged because $\vol(C)\le1/\rho$. It is not applied to the
possibly much larger accumulated history of local continuation.

\subsection{Ordinary rational output without a square-root primitive}
A sparse density triple $(i,f_i,d_i)$ represents $x_i=\sqrt{d_i}f_i$ in
constant words. The optional \texttt{solve\_explicit} instead emits ordinary
rational coordinates using only arithmetic and comparisons. Run the density
solver with tolerance $\eps/4$, obtaining gap bound $B\le\eps/4$, and choose
dyadic $\eta>0$ with $\eta^2\le\eps/2$. For each emitted vertex, bisect
$[1,d_i]$ by squared-midpoint comparisons until its width is at most $\eta$.
The lower endpoint $\ell_i$ satisfies
$0\le\sqrt{d_i}-\ell_i\le\eta$; return $x'_i=\ell_i f_i$.
Then $0\le x'\le\widehat x\le x^*_\rho$ and
\[
 \norm{\widehat x-x'}^2\le\eta^2\sum_i f_i^2\le\eta^2,
\]
because $d_i\ge1$ and $\sum_i d_i f_i\le1$. Both output supports lie in
$\supp(x^*_\rho)$, so the KKT linear term vanishes. Therefore
\[
 F_\rho(x')-F_\rho(x^*_\rho)
 =\tfrac12\norm{x'-x^*_\rho}_Q^2
 \le2B+\norm{x'-\widehat x}_Q^2\le2B+\eta^2\le\eps.
\]
Every emitted degree is at most $1/\rho$, so conversion costs
$O(\log(1/\rho)+\log(1+1/\eps))$ operations per emitted record and no
additional graph query. Empty output needs no conversion. All interval
endpoints have polylogarithmic bit length; the charged OP2 bound is preserved.

\section{Relation to the previously explored obstacles}
The prior note audit identified several failures that the present proof
must address. The accelerated trajectory may leave the true support;
its cumulative kinetic volume, rather than support containment, is proved.
The second energy bounds the actual squared matrix response and does not
use an assumed uniform $\ell_1$ bound for signed Chebyshev propagation.
The Euclidean box/cap projection needs a matrix-metric sector only at the
specific inverse-source comparator, not global nonexpansiveness.
Sparse response records and deterministic ordered trees supply the charged
threshold search. PG checks inspect candidate rows before clipping, so
newly activated high-degree rows do not create an uncharged terminal scan.
No randomized SDD solve or randomized retry is part of the algorithm.

The supplied problem-definition conversion also yields OP1. In its distinct
accuracy namespace, set $\rho=\epsilon_{\rm PPR}/2$ and
$\eps\le\alpha\epsilon_{\rm PPR}^2/8$. Inverse positivity gives
$0\le Q^{-1}b-x^*_\rho\le\rho w$, and strong convexity gives
$\norm{\widehat x-x^*_\rho}\le\sqrt{2\eps/\alpha}$.
Therefore the degree-scaled PPR coordinate error is at most
$\rho+\sqrt{2\eps/\alpha}\le\epsilon_{\rm PPR}$ with work
$\widetilde O(1/(\sqrt\alpha\,\epsilon_{\rm PPR}))$.
This argument does not establish the separate nested-SDD reuse target OP3.
