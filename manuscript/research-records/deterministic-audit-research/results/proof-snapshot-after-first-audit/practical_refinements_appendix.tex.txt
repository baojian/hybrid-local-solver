\section{Practical refinements and bounded arithmetic}\label{app:refinements}

The exact-real theorem admits several useful refinements. We first give
a less conservative repair and a smaller correction mass cap. We then
prove a perturbation lemma and realize its hypotheses using bounded
rational arithmetic with explicit downward rounding. This last result
requires rational numerical inputs and accounts for their encoding lengths;
it is a separate extension of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, not an assertion about
unchecked floating-point arithmetic or a completed software implementation.

Throughout, $0<\alpha<1$, $0<r<1/d_v$, $\lambda=\alpha r$,
$\theta,\mu,a$ are as in \eqref{eq:theta}, and the baseline satisfies
\eqref{eq:baseline}. Write
\[
 x^*=x^*_r,\quad \xi^*=x^*-\xb,\quad
 s=b-Q\xb,\quad m_s=w^Ts,\quad t=Q^{-1}s.
\]
The quadratic $J_r$ denotes the full objective on the nonnegative orthant;
$J(\xi)=J_r(\xb+\xi)-J_r(\xb)$ denotes the correction objective.

\subsection{An end-stage projected-gradient repair}\label{app:pg}

\begin{lemma}\label{app:pg-repair}
Let $\widetilde x\ge0$ have objective gap at most $\tau$, and choose
$0<\delta\le\lambda/2$. If $\tau\le\alpha\delta^2/2$, the two operations
\[
 y=[\widetilde x-\nabla J_r(\widetilde x)]_+,
 \qquad z=[y-\delta w]_+
\]
give
\begin{equation}\label{app:repair-invariant}
 0\le z\le x^*,\quad Qz\le b,\quad
 0\le x^*-z\le2\delta w,\quad 0\le b-Qz\le2\lambda w.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\vol(\supp z)\le1/r$ and
$J_r(z)-J_r(x^*)\le2\delta^2/r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Delta=\widetilde x-y$. Projection optimality and $Q\preceq I$ give
\[
 J_r(\widetilde x)-J_r(y)\ge\tfrac12\norm\Delta^2,
 \qquad v=(I-Q)\Delta\in\partial(J_r+I_{\R_+^V})(y).
\]
Indeed the projection normal is
$\Delta-\nabla J_r(\widetilde x)$; adding it to $\nabla J_r(y)$ gives $v$.
Thus $y$ retains gap at most $\tau$,
$\norm{y-x^*}\le\sqrt{2\tau/\alpha}\le\delta$, and
$\norm v\le\sqrt{2\tau}\le\sqrt\alpha\delta\le\lambda/2$.
On $y_i>0$ the normal vanishes, so
$(Qy-b)_i=v_i-\lambda w_i\le0$. On $y_i=0$, Stieltjes signs give the
same conclusion. Since $w_i\ge1$, clipping by $\delta w$ puts $z$ below
$x^*$ with error at most $2\delta w$. On a positive retained coordinate,
$z\ge y-\delta w$ and nonpositive off-diagonals imply
$(Qz)_i\le(Qy)_i-\alpha\delta w_i\le b_i$; zero coordinates are immediate.
Equation~\eqref{eq:infty} and KKT now give the source upper bound.
Finally, $z-x^*$ is supported where the KKT slack vanishes, so its
objective error is $\norm{z-x^*}_Q^2/2\le2\delta^2/r$.
\end{proof}

At an intermediate stage, $\delta=\alpha r/2$ permits
$\tau=\alpha^3r^2/8$. The PG step is performed only after the accelerated
stage; inserting it inside the recurrence would require a new auxiliary
energy proof. Its local charge is one scan of the full candidate support,
whose volume is bounded by the baseline and emitted history. Compute $y$
on the exposed records and truncate immediately. Do not scan adjacency
lists of newly positive PG neighbors before this truncation.

There is also an independent certificate for an inexact terminal PG step.
For actual stored $x,y\ge0$ and an approximate gradient $\widetilde g$, set
\[
 \Delta=x-y,\quad e_g=\widetilde g-\nabla J_r(x),\quad
 e_p=\Delta-\widetilde g-n,
\]
where $n_i=0$ on $y_i>0$ and $n_i\le0$ on $y_i=0$.
Then $(I-Q)\Delta-e_g-e_p$ is a valid subgradient at $y$. Certified bounds
$D_0\ge\norm\Delta$, $E_g\ge\norm{e_g}$, and $E_p\ge\norm{e_p}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{app:terminal-certificate}
 R=(1-\alpha)D_0+E_g+E_p,
 \quad J_r(y)-J_r(x^*)\le\frac{R^2}{2\alpha},
 \quad \norm{y-x^*}\le\frac R\alpha.
\end{equation}
These are statements about the stored point. They require no new
$Q$-product on its positive support. A choice
$n_i=\min(\Delta_i-\widetilde g_i,0)$ at zero coordinates is explicit.
Unexposed zero coordinates have symbolically known positive gradient,
zero projected value, and zero certificate defect; they are not assigned
fictitious independently rounded errors.

\subsection{Source mass, monotone baselines, and a stronger work bound}
\label{app:mass}

The known scalar
\begin{equation}\label{app:eta}
 \eta=\frac{m_s}{\alpha}=1-w^T\xb
\end{equation}
is an exact correction mass budget. Replace the unit cap by
\[
 \calK_{r,\eta}=\{z:0\le z\le4rw,\ w^Tz\le\eta\}.
\]
Both $\xi^*$ and $t$ remain feasible, and $w^Tt=\eta$. The lower and upper
normal signs in Lemma~\ref{lem:sector} are unchanged. A mass normal
$\gamma w$ contributes exactly
$\gamma(\alpha\eta-m_s)=0$ when active. Hence both energy inequalities
and $\norm{Q(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2\le18\lambda m_s$ remain valid. In particular,
the full iterate $\xb+\xi_k$ now has mass at most one.

Define the optimum's mass deficit by
$\eta_*(q)=1-w^Tx_q^*$. KKT and $Qw=\alpha w$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{app:deficit-volume}
 \vol(\supp x_q^*)\le\frac{\eta_*(q)}q,\qquad q>0.
\end{equation}
To see this, $b-Qx_q^*$ is nonnegative, equals $\alpha q w_i$ on the
positive support, and has weighted mass $\alpha\eta_*(q)$. Since
$\xb\le x_r^*\le x_{r/2}^*$, the same analytical comparison set as before
satisfies $\vol(C_r)\le2\eta/r$. Its outside residual margin remains
$\lambda w_i/2$. No new support oracle is introduced. If one instead
defines a correction core, it gives the identical set
$\supp(x_{r/2}^*-\xb)=C_r$: equality of an allegedly missing positive
coordinate would force $x_{r,i}^*=x_{r/2,i}^*>0$, contradicting their
stationarity equations and the Stieltjes comparison at that coordinate.

For $K$ steps let $D_{\rm out}$ be the kinetic volume outside $C_r$,
$B_0=K\vol(C_r)$, and let $H_2$ be the squared response sum used in the
selected-flow proof. Then
\[
 \frac\lambda2D_{\rm out}
 \le\sqrt{(D_{\rm out}+B_0)H_2},\qquad
 B_0\le\frac{2\eta K}r,\qquad H_2\le18\lambda m_sK.
\]
Writing $Y=D_{\rm out}+B_0$, Young's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{app:mass-work}
 Y\le B_0+\frac{2\sqrt{YH_2}}\lambda
 \le B_0+\frac Y2+\frac{2H_2}{\lambda^2},
 \qquad
 \sum_k\vol(\supp z_{k+1})\le Y\le\frac{76\eta K}r.
\end{equation}
The other local operations inherit this factor:
$\vol(\supp\xb)\le\eta/r$ by \eqref{app:deficit-volume}, and the fixed
source has $O(1+\eta/r)$ exposed records. In the nonzero regime
$\eta/r\ge1$, so source refreshes, baseline scans, and materialization
are all covered by $\widetilde O(\eta K/r)$.

A safe repair can be made monotone by replacing its result $z$ with
\begin{equation}\label{app:max-repair}
 \xb_{\rm new}=\max(\xb,z).
\end{equation}
The maximum of two nonnegative Stieltjes subsolutions $Qx\le b$ is a
subsolution: at a coordinate selected from one vector, the diagonal
coordinate is unchanged and all other coordinates increase. Both vectors
lie below $x_r^*$, so \eqref{app:repair-invariant} and its error bound remain
valid. The quadratic gap bound follows again from $Q\preceq I$, not from
an unwarranted coordinatewise monotonicity of the $Q$-norm. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{app:repair-deficit}
 \eta_*(r)\le\eta_{\rm new}
 \le\eta_*(r)+2\delta\vol(\supp x_r^*)
 \le(1+\alpha)\eta_*(r).
\end{equation}

Each coordinate of $x_q^*$ is convex in $q$: a convex combination of
optimizers is a nonnegative supersolution at the interpolated parameter,
and therefore dominates its least supersolution. Thus $\eta_*$ is concave,
nondecreasing, and $\eta_*(0)=0$. In particular,
$\eta_*(R)/R\le\eta_*(r)/r$ for $R\ge r>0$.
For a stage at $r_j$ following repair at
$r_{j-1}\in[r_j,2r_j]$, equation~\eqref{app:repair-deficit} yields
\[
 \frac{\eta_j}{r_j}
 \le2(1+\alpha)\frac{\eta_*(r_{j-1})}{r_{j-1}}
 \le4\frac{\eta_*(\rho)}\rho.
\]
The initial zero baseline satisfies the same statement because
$x^*_{1/d_v}=0$ and $\eta_*(1/d_v)=1$.
Summing over the logarithmically many stages proves the refinement
\begin{equation}\label{app:strong-work}
 \widetilde O\!\left(\frac{\eta_*(\rho)}{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right)
\end{equation}
for exact arithmetic, with the stage-count logarithm included. The final
returned mass deficit estimates $\eta_*(\rho)$ within factor $1+\alpha$.

\subsection{A dimension-independent two-energy perturbation lemma}
\label{app:perturbation}

The following lemma permits changes of support caused by rounding. It
applies to either mass cap, using the conservative bound $w^Tz\le1$.
At the actual stored states $(\xi,z)$, form $y$ and $q=z^{\rm raw}$ by
\eqref{eq:iteration}. Model an iteration as
\begin{equation}\label{app:error-model}
 p_0=\Proj_{\calK}(q+u),\quad p=p_0+e_p,\quad
 \xi^+=a\xi+\theta p+e_x,
 \quad
 \begin{cases}
 |u|\le\kappa_r w,\\
 -\kappa_p w\le e_p\le0,\\
 -\kappa_x w\le e_x\le0,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $p,\xi^+\ge0$. Downward errors ensure feasibility. The ideal $p_0$
is analytical and need not be materialized on all its positive coordinates.

\begin{lemma}\label{app:two-energy}
For the energies $E,B$ of the main proof, the actual states in
\eqref{app:error-model} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{app:zeta}
\begin{gathered}
 E^+\le aE+\zeta,\qquad B^+\le aB+\zeta,\\
 \zeta=2\mu\kappa_r+\frac52\kappa_x
                  +\frac52(\theta+\mu)\kappa_p.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For uniform bounds put $\Gamma=\zeta/\theta$. Starting from zero,
\begin{equation}\label{app:perturbed-response}
 J(\xi_k)-J(\xi^*)\le a^k+\Gamma,
 \qquad \norm{Q(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2\le18\alpha^2r+4\Gamma.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Besides \eqref{eq:infty}, symmetry and $|Q|w=w$ imply
$w^T|Qv|\le w^T|v|$. Since $p_0,\xi^*,t$ have mass at most one,
\[
 |\ip{p_0-\xi^*}{u}|\le2\kappa_r,
 \qquad |\ip{p_0-t}{Qu}|\le2\kappa_r.
\]
The projection normal of $q+u$ has the usual sector signs in both
comparison metrics. Replacing it by $q-p_0$ therefore costs at most
$2\mu\kappa_r$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison}.

Set $\xi_0^+=a\xi+\theta p_0$ and
$d=\xi^+-\xi_0^+=\theta e_p+e_x\le0$, with
$|d|\le\kappa_d w$, $\kappa_d=\theta\kappa_p+\kappa_x$.
Its lost mass is at most one, so $\norm d^2\le\kappa_d$.
Also $w^T|Q\xi_0^+-s|\le1+\alpha\le2$.
Quadratic expansion, $|Qd|\le\kappa_dw$, and $Q\preceq I$ show that
each of $J$ and $\mathcal A$ increases by at most $5\kappa_d/2$;
their positive mass penalties decrease under $d\le0$.
The mirror error has $\norm{e_p}^2\le\kappa_p$. Expanding either
mirror-distance term costs at most $5\mu\kappa_p/2$, because the
absolute weighted mass of the difference from its comparator is at most
two. Adding these bounds proves \eqref{app:zeta}. Summing the geometric
recurrences gives the first assertion in \eqref{app:perturbed-response}.
The auxiliary proof of \eqref{eq:response}, with its added energy
$\Gamma$, gives the second.
\end{proof}

For varying errors, $\Gamma$ is replaced by the explicitly maintained
convolution $\Gamma_{k+1}=a\Gamma_k+\zeta_k$, $\Gamma_0=0$.
The lemma uses mass bounds rather than a Euclidean sum of uniform errors
over unknown vertices.

To preserve the work bound, set $\nu=\theta\kappa_r$. On a selected
coordinate $p_i>\xi_i^*$ one has $p_{0,i}>\xi_i^*$; its lower normal is
absent, while upper normals, the mass normal, and downward $e_p$ only
subtract from the selected flow. The raw error contributes at most
$\nu d_i$. Thus, with $B_0=K\vol(C_r)\le2K/r$,
\begin{equation}\label{app:perturbed-flow}
 \frac\lambda2D_{\rm out}
 \le\sqrt{(D_{\rm out}+B_0)H_2}
       +\nu(D_{\rm out}+B_0),\quad
 H_2\le K(18\alpha^2r+4\Gamma).
\end{equation}
If $\nu\le\lambda/4$, absorb its outside term and apply Young's
inequality to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{app:rounded-work}
 \sum_k\vol(\supp p_k)
 \le\frac{16H_2}{\lambda^2}+\frac{8K}r
 \le\frac{360K}r\qquad(\Gamma\le\alpha^2r).
\end{equation}
No minimum positive activation margin is assumed. Downward primal error
affects the response through \eqref{app:perturbed-response} and introduces
no separate term into the raw flow identity.

\subsection{A bounded dyadic realization with scalar rebases}
\label{app:dyadic}

Let $0<\tau\le\alpha^2r$, and choose a dyadic grid $h$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{app:grid-block}
 0<h\le\min\{1/8,\theta\tau/256\},\qquad
 K=Tq,\quad T=1/\theta,\quad 2^q\ge2/\tau.
\end{equation}
Take the smallest suitable integer $q$. Since
$(1-\theta)^T<1/2$, this gives $a^K\le\tau/2$ without retaining a
growing exact power. Additional conditions $h\le\delta/2,\lambda/4$
may be imposed explicitly for terminal repair.

Store degree densities as $\xi_i/w_i=\sigma X_i$ and $Z_i=z_i/w_i$,
where $\sigma,X_i,Z_i$ are nonnegative multiples of $h$. At iteration
boundaries $1/2\le\sigma\le1$. Maintain primal neighbor records $L_i$,
initially $L_i=\sum_{j\sim i}X_j$, and exact current-kinetic and baseline
neighbor sums. A changed $X_j$ scatters its \emph{actual dyadic increment}
to all $L_i$ with $i\sim j$. The response density is evaluated as
\begin{equation}\label{app:neighbor-response}
 (q_0-\mu)X_i-cL_i/d_i,\qquad q_0=(1+\alpha)/2.
\end{equation}
It equals $[D^{-1/2}(Q-\mu I)D^{1/2}X]_i$ when $L$ is exact. The source
is always computed exactly from the dyadic baseline, using
\[
 s_i/w_i=\alpha\mathbf1_{i=v}/d_i-q_0\xb_i/w_i
                +(c/d_i)\sum_{j\sim i}\xb_j/w_j.
\]

Use the exact ordered reporter with these response records to determine
the box/mass multiplier. If the computed raw density is
$\sigma k_i-\lambda/\theta$ and the multiplier is $\gamma$, emit only
the \emph{closed} tail
\begin{equation}\label{app:closed-tail}
 k_i\ge(\lambda/\theta+\gamma+h)/\sigma.
\end{equation}
For each emitted vertex set $p_i/w_i=\lfloor p_{0,i}/w_i\rfloor_h$.
Here $\lfloor t\rfloor_h=h\lfloor t/h\rfloor$, and the ideal projection
is onto the chosen box and mass cap. Since $h\le4r$, condition
\eqref{app:closed-tail} is exactly the condition for a positive stored
output, including equality at $h$. Enumerating all positive ideal outputs
before rounding would incur uncharged work and is not the algorithm.

Remove the old kinetic/source exceptions, put
$\sigma'=\lfloor a\sigma\rfloor_h$, and on emitted coordinates update
\begin{equation}\label{app:dyadic-update}
 X_i\longleftarrow
 \left\lfloor X_i+\frac{\theta(p_i/w_i)}{\sigma'}\right\rfloor_h.
\end{equation}
Scatter the resulting changes to $L$. The scale is positive because
$a\sigma\ge1/4$ and $h\le1/8$. At the old iteration boundary
$X_i\le2U$, $U=4r\le4$. Scale rounding loses at most $2Uh$ in physical
density; \eqref{app:dyadic-update} loses at most another $h$.
Set $z\leftarrow p$ and form its exact neighbor sums by the same charged
scan of the emitted support.

If $\sigma'<1/2$, independently replace all exposed primal records by
\begin{equation}\label{app:scalar-rebase}
 X_i\longleftarrow\lfloor\sigma'X_i\rfloor_h,
 \qquad L_i\longleftarrow\lfloor\sigma'L_i\rfloor_h,
 \qquad \sigma\longleftarrow1.
\end{equation}
Both replacements use their old values. Do not scatter the $X$ changes
in this rebase: that would count the transformation twice. Do not scale
the current-kinetic or baseline neighbor sums. Rebuild keys and their
exceptions from the resulting records. If $\sigma'\ge1/2$, instead set
$\sigma\leftarrow\sigma'$, refresh all touched base keys, and then install
the new kinetic/source exceptions. The rebase adds at most $h$ of
downward primal density error, so altogether
\begin{equation}\label{app:rounding-constants}
 \kappa_p=h,\qquad \kappa_x\le(2U+2)h\le10h.
\end{equation}
Nonnegativity of the construction and downward errors preserve feasibility.

The neighbor records now have a controlled error
$e_i^L=L_i-\sum_{j\sim i}X_j$. Exact scattered increments preserve this
error between rebases. At a rebase the individual floor losses give
\[
 e_i^{L,+}=\sigma'e_i^L+
       \sum_{j\sim i}\ell_j-\ell_i^L,
 \qquad 0\le\ell_j,\ell_i^L<h.
\]
Since $\sigma'<1/2$, the invariant
$|e_i^L|/d_i\le4h$ follows from
$|e_i^{L,+}|/d_i\le\sigma'|e_i^L|/d_i+2h$.
The only approximate response term is the primal neighbor sum, so its
raw density error is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{app:raw-rounding}
 \frac{|u_i|}{w_i}
 \le\frac{\sigma c|e_i^L|}{\theta(1+\theta)d_i}
 \le\frac{2h}{\theta}=\kappa_r.
\end{equation}
Every state needed by this argument remains represented through the stage;
unexposed coordinates have zero state and source and cannot be emitted.

Equations~\eqref{app:zeta}, \eqref{app:rounding-constants}, and
\eqref{app:raw-rounding} imply
\[
 \Gamma\le\frac{29h}{\theta}<\frac\tau8,
 \qquad \theta\kappa_r=2h\le\lambda/4.
\]
Thus \eqref{app:grid-block} certifies gap below $\tau$ for the actual
stored candidate and the kinetic bound \eqref{app:rounded-work}.
This conclusion permits approximate primal neighbor sums; it does not
silently treat them as exact products.

All rebase operations are charged scalar/tree passes over exposed records.
A scale interval starts at one and each step decreases scale by at most
$\theta+h$; crossing below $1/2$ needs at least $1/[2(\theta+h)]$ steps.
There are therefore at most $4\theta K+1=O(1+\log(1/\tau))$ rebases.
The number of retained records is bounded by source exposure and the
cumulative emitted adjacency volume. Consequently the passes cost only
an additional allowed logarithmic factor. They inspect no adjacency lists.
Ordinary scatters and the final full-candidate scan remain separately paid.

\subsection{Encoding lengths and completion of continuation}
\label{app:encoding}

For completeness, the rational implementation does not accumulate an
iteration-dependent product denominator. Write
\[
 \alpha=A_0/B_\alpha,\qquad\theta=1/T,\qquad
 h=1/H,\qquad\sigma=S/H,
\]
and express dyadic densities and neighbor records as integers over $H$.
Let $M_i=\sum_{j\sim i}Z_j$. Multiplying the lazy key by its degree gives
\begin{equation}\label{app:weighted-key}
 \begin{split}
 W_i:=d_i k_i={}&-
 \frac{(q_0-\mu)d_iX_i-cL_i}{\theta(1+\theta)}\\
 &+\frac1\sigma\left\{
 a d_iZ_i-\frac{(q_0-\mu)d_iZ_i-cM_i}{1+\theta}
                  +\frac{d_i s_i/w_i}{\theta}\right\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The degree denominator cancels in every term. All $W_i$ have denominators
dividing the common integer $2B_\alpha HT(T+1)S$. Their physical values
$\sigma W_i$ have a common denominator dividing
$2B_\alpha H^2T(T+1)$. Include the represented denominator of $r$ for
the common shift and box height. Thus weighted subtree moments do not
form an accumulated least common multiple of vertex degrees. Comparisons
of unweighted keys $W_i/d_i$ use only the additional encoding of one degree.
Remove all old exceptions before installing new ones, so only the current
scale denominator is retained.

On an affine interval of the clipped waterfill, if $I$ is the free set
and $U_0$ the saturated set, its mass multiplier is
\[
 \gamma=
 \frac{\sum_{i\in I}d_i q_i^{\rm den}
                  +U\sum_{i\in U_0}d_i-M}{\sum_{i\in I}d_i},
\]
where $q_i^{\rm den}$ is raw density and $M$ is either $1$ or $\eta$.
Empty free sets are handled by the exact breakpoint comparison. The
division introduces only the encoding length of a degree sum. Ideal
projected coordinates are immediately rounded to the dyadic grid, so
this denominator never enters the next stored vector. The reporter uses
the finite ordered breakpoint search of the main proof; it does not
bisect until an unknown activation margin is resolved.

The stored normalized densities are bounded (at most $32$ even before
rebase); neighbor sums add at most the encoding lengths of exposed counts
and degrees. Across stages take nested nonincreasing dyadic grids, so an
old baseline is represented exactly on the new grid. Therefore all
integer temporaries have bit length bounded by
\[
 O\!\left(L_{\rm input}+\log H+\log T+
          \log(N+2)+\log(d_{\max}+2)\right),
\]
up to fixed constant factors for intermediate products. Here
$L_{\rm input}$ includes numerical parameter and vertex-label encodings,
$N$ is the number of exposed records, and $d_{\max}$ the largest exposed
degree. There is no linear growth in the number of iterations. Exact
integer arithmetic and deterministic comparison trees thus add only
polynomial factors in these logarithmic encoding lengths to the charged
operation count.

Finally choose, at each continuation stage,
\begin{equation}\label{app:final-budgets}
 0<\delta\le\alpha r/2,\qquad
 \tau=\alpha\delta^2/8,\qquad
 h\le\min\{1/8,\theta\tau/256,\delta/2,\lambda/4\}.
\end{equation}
At the final stage also require $2\delta^2/r\le\epsilon$.
Run the block schedule and compute an \emph{exact} end-stage PG step from
the actual materialized full candidate. This one terminal scan must not
reuse approximate $L$ as an exact gradient. Its PG point $y$ has
$\norm{y-x^*}\le\delta/2$ and a valid subgradient of norm at most
$\sqrt{2\tau}\le\delta/2$. Set
\[
 z_i/w_i=\left\lfloor[y_i/w_i-\delta]_+\right\rfloor_h,
 \qquad \xb_{\rm new}=\max(\xb,z).
\]
The loss is at most $\delta/2+\delta+h\le2\delta$ in density. On a
retained positive coordinate,
\[
 (Qz-b)_i\le(-\lambda+\delta/2-\alpha\delta+h)w_i\le0;
\]
here uniform clipping helps and additional downward grid rounding raises
the $Q$-product by at most $hw_i$. Zero coordinates follow from Stieltjes
signs. Thus \eqref{app:repair-invariant} and the monotone maximum argument
hold for the actual dyadic baseline. It supplies a source in
$[0,4\alpha r_{\rm next}w]$ for every factor-two next stage and the
required final objective error.

This bounded-rational construction retains the original
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ local operation count, with the
additional explicitly described encoding factors in a bit model. The
sharper bound \eqref{app:strong-work} was proved above for the exact
trajectory; the conservative rounded ledger \eqref{app:rounded-work}
already suffices for the original bound. These arithmetic arguments are
distinct from tests of any particular implementation and do not certify
ordinary fixed-precision floating-point execution.
