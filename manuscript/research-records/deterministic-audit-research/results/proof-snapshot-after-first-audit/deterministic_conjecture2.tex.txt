\begin{abstract}
We give a deterministic continuation algorithm for the second conjecture in
the supplied problem-definition note. Its charged local work is
$\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ for additive objective accuracy,
and its returned vector lies coordinatewise below the exact optimum.
The algorithm uses accelerated steps with a degree-scaled box and a mass cap,
a sparse exact waterfilling reporter, and a certified truncation between
regularization stages. A second quadratic energy, justified by a projection
inequality for one analytical comparator, bounds the cumulative residual
response and hence every repeated kinetic-support scan. No randomized
primitive or restricted linear-system solver is used.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Draft status.} The complete mathematical proof has passed
two independent end-to-end audits and a separate audit of the computational
model and local work charges. The exact continuation implementation has
also passed independent tests. Bounded-precision implementation work is
ongoing. This draft does not assert completion of the requested minimum
research duration. Only the authorized \texttt{problem\_definitions/main.tex}
was read from the manuscript; this document is stored outside that repository.

\section{Statement and model}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be the finite connected simple unweighted undirected graph in
the supplied definition, with degrees $d_i\ge1$, adjacency matrix $A$, and
$D=\diag(d)$. Fix the point seed $v$, $0<\alpha\le1$, and $\rho>0$. Write
\[
 w=D^{1/2}{\bf1},\quad c=\frac{1-\alpha}{2},\quad
 Q=\alpha I+c(I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}),\quad
 b=\alpha D^{-1/2}e_v.
\]
The objective and optimum are
\[
 F_\rho(x)=\frac12x^TQx-b^Tx+\alpha\rho\norm{D^{1/2}x}_1,
 \qquad x^*_\rho=\arg\min_x F_\rho(x).
\]
All inequalities between vectors below are coordinatewise.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $0<\rho<1/d_v$ and $\epsilon>0$, the deterministic algorithm in
Section~\ref{sec:schedule} returns a sparse vector $\widehat x$ satisfying
\[
 0\le\widehat x\le x^*_\rho,\qquad
 F_\rho(\widehat x)-F_\rho(x^*_\rho)\le\epsilon,
 \qquad \vol(\supp\widehat x)\le1/\rho,
\]
in $\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$ charged local work. The hidden
factors are polylogarithmic in the inverse parameters, inverse accuracy,
and exposed or emitted words. There is no polynomial dependence on the
total graph size or free preprocessing, support, or boundary oracle.
For unrestricted $\rho>0$, the bound is
$\widetilde O(1+1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem uses the reference's exact-real algebraic word model. Scalar
arithmetic, comparisons, stored-state reads and writes, degree replies,
all first and repeated adjacency entries, and emitted words are charged.
Bit complexity and floating-point stability are separate questions.
Computation uses degree densities $f_i=x_i/w_i$, with rational coefficients
when the parameters are rational. An output value may retain its exact
constant-size radical representation $f_i\sqrt{d_i}$. No square-root or
logarithm primitive is needed for the iteration schedule.

If $\rho d_v\ge1$, return zero after querying $d_v$. If $\alpha=1$, return
only $x_v=(1-\rho d_v)_+/\sqrt{d_v}$. Henceforth $0<\alpha<1$ and
$0<\rho<1/d_v$.

\section{Obstacle facts and analytical comparison support}
The supplied standard properties give
\[
 \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I,\quad Q^{-1}\ge0,\quad Qw=\alpha w,
 \quad x^*_r\ge0,\quad w^Tx^*_r\le1,\quad\vol(\supp x^*_r)\le1/r.
\]
On nonnegative vectors put $\lambda=\alpha r$ and
$J_r(x)=x^TQx/2-(b-\lambda w)^Tx$. Its KKT slack obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kkt}
 r^*=Qx^*_r-b+\lambda w\ge0,\quad r_i^*x^*_{r,i}=0,
 \qquad 0\le b-Qx^*_r\le\lambda w.
\end{equation}
The optimum is coordinatewise nonincreasing in $r$. For example, it is the
least nonnegative supersolution of $Qy\ge b-\alpha r w$. To verify this,
on $I=\{i:x^*_{r,i}>y_i\}$ the Stieltjes signs give
$Q_{II}(x^*_r-y)_I\le0$; inverse positivity forces $I$ empty.

Let $C_r=\supp(x^*_{r/2})$, used only in analysis. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
 \vol(C_r)\le2/r,\qquad i\notin C_r\Longrightarrow
 x^*_{r,i}=0\ \text{and}\ r_i^*\ge\lambda w_i/2.
\end{equation}
Indeed both optima vanish at $i$, and their order with the nonpositive
off-diagonals implies $(Qx^*_r)_i\ge(Qx^*_{r/2})_i$.
The degree-coordinate matrix
\[
 D^{-1/2}QD^{1/2}=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}I-cD^{-1}A
\]
has absolute row sums one. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infty}
 |u|\le\gamma w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |Qu|\le\gamma w.
\end{equation}

\section{A stage with a diffuse residual source}
At regularization $r$, assume a sparse baseline satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline}
 0\le\xb\le x^*_r,\qquad s=b-Q\xb,\qquad 0\le s\le4\lambda w.
\end{equation}
Let $m_s=w^Ts=\alpha(1-w^T\xb)\le\alpha$. Define the analytical vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t}
\begin{gathered}
 t=Q^{-1}s=x^*_0-\xb,\qquad \xi^*=x^*_r-\xb,\\
 0\le\xi^*\le t\le4r w,\qquad w^Tt=m_s/\alpha\le1.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Inverse positivity proves these inequalities; neither vector is computed.

Choose $\theta$ by halving from $1/2$ until $\theta^2\le\alpha$. Set
$\mu=\theta^2$ and $a=1-\theta$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \alpha/4<\mu\le\alpha,\qquad \sqrt\alpha/2<\theta\le\sqrt\alpha.
\end{equation}
Use the explicit convex correction set
\[
 \calK_r=\{z:0\le z\le4r w,\ w^Tz\le1\}.
\]
Starting at $\xi_0=z_0=0$, apply only the following ordinary averaging steps:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iteration}
 \begin{aligned}
 y_k&=\frac{\xi_k+\theta z_k}{1+\theta},\\
 z_k^{\rm raw}&=a z_k+\theta y_k-\frac{Qy_k-s+\lambda w}{\theta},\\
 z_{k+1}&=\Proj_{\calK_r}^{\rm Euclidean}(z_k^{\rm raw}),\\
 \xi_{k+1}&=a\xi_k+\theta z_{k+1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
No original-objective line search or intermediate primal cleanup is added.
Such changes would require another proof for the auxiliary energy below.

\subsection{An accelerated comparison lemma}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:comparison}
In a Hilbert metric, let $f$ be one-smooth and $\mu$-strongly convex,
$\theta^2=\mu$, $a=1-\theta$, and $y=(x+\theta z)/(1+\theta)$.
Set $g=\nabla f(y)$, $q=az+\theta y-g/\theta$, and $x^+=ax+\theta p$.
For any comparator $t$ satisfying $\ip{p-t}{q-p}\ge0$,
\[
 f(x^+)-f(t)+\frac\mu2\norm{p-t}^2
 \le a\left(f(x)-f(t)+\frac\mu2\norm{z-t}^2\right)
 -\frac{\mu\theta(1-\mu)}2\norm{z-y}^2.
\]
The comparator need not minimize $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $x^+=y-g+\theta(p-q)$, smoothness gives
$f(x^+)\le f(y)-\norm g^2/2+\mu\norm{p-q}^2/2$.
The sector inequality gives
$\norm{p-t}^2\le\norm{q-t}^2-\norm{p-q}^2$.
Add these and expand $q-t=a(z-t)+\theta(y-t)-g/\theta$.
The strong-convexity inequalities at $x$ and $t$, with weights $a$ and
$\theta$, apply because
\[
 a(x-y)+\theta(t-y)=-\theta\{a(z-t)+\theta(y-t)\}.
\]
Finally
\[
 \norm{a(z-t)+\theta(y-t)}^2
 =a\norm{z-t}^2+\theta\norm{y-t}^2-a\theta\norm{z-y}^2
\]
and $x-y=\theta(y-z)$. The remaining negative term is
$-\mu a\theta(1+\theta)\norm{z-y}^2/2$, as asserted.
\end{proof}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} in Euclidean metric to
$J(\xi)=\xi^TQ\xi/2-(s-\lambda w)^T\xi$ and comparator $\xi^*$.
Projection supplies its sector inequality. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary}
 E_{k+1}\le aE_k,\qquad
 E_k=J(\xi_k)-J(\xi^*)+\frac\mu2\norm{z_k-\xi^*}^2.
\end{equation}
By complementarity and $w^T\xi^*\le1$,
\[
 E_0=\tfrac12\norm{\xi^*}_Q^2+\tfrac\mu2\norm{\xi^*}^2\le1.
\]
Maintaining the scalar bound $a^k$ and stopping at $a^k\le\tau$ certifies
objective gap at most $\tau$ in at most
$K\le1+\theta^{-1}\log(1/\tau)$ steps.

\section{The second energy bounds the nonlinear response}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sector}
For a projection output $p$ in \eqref{eq:iteration}, with
$n=z^{\rm raw}-p$, the comparator in \eqref{eq:t} satisfies
$\ip{p-t}{Qn}\ge0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n=\eta w+n^{\rm up}-n^{\rm low}$ for the Euclidean projection
normals, with all multipliers nonnegative. Their contributions to
$(Qp-s)^Tn$ are separately nonnegative. At $p_i=0$, $(Qp)_i\le0\le s_i$.
At $p_i=4rw_i$, Stieltjes signs and $p\le4rw$ give
$(Qp)_i\ge4\alpha r w_i\ge s_i$. If $\eta>0$, $w^Tp=1$ and its
contribution is $\eta(\alpha-m_s)\ge0$.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aux}
 \mathcal A(\xi)=\tfrac12\norm{Q\xi-s}^2+\alpha\lambda w^T\xi.
\end{equation}
Its gradient in the $Q$ metric is $Q\xi-s+\lambda w$, exactly the gradient
used in \eqref{eq:iteration}. Its metric Hessian is $Q$, with eigenvalues
in $[\alpha,1]\subseteq[\mu,1]$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:comparison}
and~\ref{lem:sector} therefore yield
\[
 B_{k+1}\le a B_k,\qquad
 B_k=\mathcal A(\xi_k)-\mathcal A(t)+\tfrac\mu2\norm{z_k-t}_Q^2.
\]
This energy may be negative: $t$ is not an optimizer of $\mathcal A$.
The comparison lemma does not require either property.

The diffuse-source bounds imply
\[
 \norm s^2\le4\lambda m_s,\quad \mathcal A(t)=\lambda m_s,
 \quad t^TQt=t^Ts\le4r m_s,
\]
so $B_0\le3\lambda m_s$. Since the mass term in \eqref{eq:aux} is
nonnegative, $\norm{Q\xi_k-s}^2\le8\lambda m_s$ for every $k$.
Furthermore $q=s-Q\xi^*=b-Qx^*_r$ satisfies
$0\le q\le\lambda w$ and $w^Tq\le m_s$, hence $\norm q^2\le\lambda m_s$.
We obtain the central uniform bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:response}
 \boxed{\norm{Q(\xi_k-\xi^*)}^2\le18\lambda m_s\le18\alpha^2r.}
\end{equation}
This is a bound for the actual constrained nonlinear trajectory.
It does not use a general claim that Euclidean projection is $Q$-nonexpansive.

\section{Selected signed flow charges repeated support work}
Set $P=AD^{-1}$, $K_0=(I+P)/2$, and use mass coordinates
\[
 u=D^{1/2}\xi,\quad V=\theta D^{1/2}z,\quad
 u^*=D^{1/2}\xi^*,\quad V^*=\theta u^*,\quad
 \beta_0=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\theta}\le a.
\]
A direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rawflow}
 V^{\rm raw}-V^*=\beta_0K_0(V-V^*)+H-R^*,\quad
 H=-\frac{D^{1/2}(Q-\mu I)(\xi-\xi^*)}{1+\theta},\quad
 R^*=D^{1/2}r^*.
\end{equation}
Here $K_0$ is nonnegative and column stochastic. Write
$e=(V-V^*)_+$ and $n^-=(V^*-V)_+$, and select
$E_k=\{i:V_{k+1,i}>V_i^*\}$. Lower projection normals vanish there;
upper and mass normals subtract nonnegative mass. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selected}
 \norm{e_{k+1}}_1+\sum_{i\in E_k}R_i^*
 \le\beta_0\norm{e_k}_1+\sum_{i\in E_k}H_{k,i}.
\end{equation}
No total unselected positive flux replaces the signed term.
Since $e_0=0$, \eqref{eq:margin} and telescoping give
\[
 \frac\lambda2 D_{\rm out}\le\sum_k\sum_{i\in E_k}H_{k,i},\qquad
 D_{\rm out}=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\vol(\supp z_{k+1}\setminus C_r).
\]
The total selected degree-volume is at most $D_{\rm out}+2K/r$.
Cauchy's inequality yields
\[
 \frac{\lambda^2}{4}D_{\rm out}^2
 \le(D_{\rm out}+2K/r)H_2,\qquad
 H_2=\sum_k\norm{D^{-1/2}H_k}^2.
\]
As $0\preceq Q-\mu I\preceq Q$ and these matrices commute,
\eqref{eq:response} gives $H_2\le18\alpha^2rK$.
The scalar quadratic inequality implies
$D_{\rm out}\le8H_2/\lambda^2+2K/r$. Adding the support inside $C_r$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
 \boxed{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\vol(\supp z_{k+1})\le148K/r.}
\end{equation}
Every repeated adjacency scan is included in this sum. All comparison
vectors and sets in its proof remain uncomputed analytical objects.

\section{A certified repair of approximate stages}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:repair}
Suppose $\widetilde x\ge0$ has objective gap at most
$\tau=\alpha^3\delta^2/2$ at regularization $r$, where
$0<\delta\le\alpha r/2$. Set $\xb^+=[\widetilde x-\delta w]_+$.
Then
\[
 0\le\xb^+\le x^*_r,\quad
 0\le b-Q\xb^+\le2\alpha r w,\quad
 \vol(\supp\xb^+)\le1/r,
\]
and $F_r(\xb^+)-F_r(x^*_r)\le2\delta^2/r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Strong convexity gives $\norm{\widetilde x-x^*_r}\le\eta=\alpha\delta$.
Since $w_i\ge1$ and $\eta\le\delta$,
\[
 0\le x^*_r-\xb^+\le(\delta+\eta)w\le2\delta w.
\]
On a positive coordinate of $\xb^+$, Stieltjes signs and
$\xb^+\ge\widetilde x-\delta w$ give
\[
 (Q\xb^+)_i\le(Q\widetilde x)_i-\alpha\delta w_i
 \le(Qx^*_r)_i\le b_i.
\]
At a zero coordinate $(Q\xb^+)_i\le0\le b_i$. Thus the repaired source
is exactly nonnegative. Equation~\eqref{eq:infty} and \eqref{eq:kkt} give
$b-Q\xb^+\le\alpha r w+2\delta w\le2\alpha r w$.
Support containment proves the volume bound. Complementarity removes the
linear term in the objective difference, which is at most
$\norm{\xb^+-x^*_r}^2/2\le2\delta^2/r$.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete schedule and output}\label{sec:schedule}
Let $r_0=1/d_v$ and initialize $\xb=0$. For successive stages set
$r_j=\max\{\rho,r_0/2^j\}$ and stop at the first $r_j=\rho$.
The first source is $s=b\le2\alpha r_1 w$, so \eqref{eq:baseline} holds.
At each intermediate stage set $\delta_j=\alpha r_j/2$. At the final
stage halve this starting value until also
$\delta_j^2\le\epsilon\rho/2$. Set $\tau_j=\alpha^3\delta_j^2/2$.
Run \eqref{eq:iteration} until the maintained scalar $a^k$ is at most
$\tau_j$, materialize $\widetilde x=\xb+\xi_k$, and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:repair}.

For the next regularization $r_{j+1}\ge r_j/2$, the repaired source obeys
$0\le s\le2\alpha r_jw\le4\alpha r_{j+1}w$. Optimum monotonicity preserves
$\xb\le x^*_{r_{j+1}}$. This proves all stage invariants by induction.
At the final stage Lemma~\ref{lem:repair} gives objective gap at most
$2\delta_j^2/\rho\le\epsilon$, with safe support containment.

There are $O(1+\log(1/\rho))$ stages. Their iteration counts are
$O(\alpha^{-1/2}L)$ for a fixed sum $L$ of the allowed parameter and
accuracy logarithms, while $\sum_j1/r_j=O(1/\rho)$.
The geometric sum and \eqref{eq:work} give the desired total kinetic work.
There is no exact-old-solution or smallest-complementarity-margin assumption.

\section{The local exact reporter and arithmetic ledger}
We describe a sufficient implementation, allowing conservative logarithmic
factors. Represent the degree densities by $\xi_i/w_i=\sigma X_i$, with
$\sigma=a^k$, and maintain the normalized response
$R=(Q-\mu I)\xi/\sigma$. Let $T_z=(Q-\mu I)z$.
The exact raw-density key is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:key}
 \begin{aligned}
 K_i={}&-\frac{R_i}{\theta(1+\theta)w_i}
 +\frac1\sigma\left(\frac{az_i}{w_i}
 -\frac{(T_z)_i}{(1+\theta)w_i}+\frac{s_i}{\theta w_i}\right),\\
 \frac{z_i^{\rm raw}}{w_i}={}&\sigma K_i-\lambda/\theta.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Only the sparse bracketed exceptions change when the common scale changes.
Ordinary averaging changes normalized $X$ only on the newly emitted
kinetic support. A scan of those lists updates its response. Reverting
and reinstalling the previous and new kinetic exceptions costs their
charged support volume. Refreshing the fixed source exceptions every
iteration costs $O(|\supp s|)$ key operations, also paid.

Use a deterministic balanced tree storing key order, cardinalities,
degree weights, and weighted key sums. With $U=4r$, projection is
\[
 \frac{z_i}{w_i}=\min\{U,(\sigma K_i-\lambda/\theta-\eta)_+\},
 \qquad\eta\ge0.
\]
Let $T(q)=\sum_i d_i(K_i-q)_+$ and
$q=(\lambda/\theta+\eta)/\sigma$. The projected mass is
$\sigma\{T(q)-T(q+U/\sigma)\}$, computable by two tree-tail queries.
If its value at $\eta=0$ exceeds one, locate its root among the two
translated ordered breakpoint lists $\{K_i\}$ and $\{K_i-U/\sigma\}$.
Deterministic rank selection in their implicit merge and binary search
among $2N$ breakpoint ranks take $O(\log^3(N+2))$ operations. Solve the
remaining affine equation exactly. Flat intervals and ties admit a valid
multiplier without any numerical support-margin search.

Enumerate only keys with positive projected output. An unexposed vertex
has zero state and source and strictly negative raw value, so cannot be
selected. Each newly exposed degree is queried once and cached. An inactive
boundary source vertex incurs only scalar key updates until it becomes
kinetic-active; its adjacency list is not recursively scanned.

The fixed source is assembled from the baseline support, its neighbors,
and the seed. After repair this costs $O(\vol(\supp\xb)+1)$ and has
$|\supp s|=O(1/r+1)$ records. The full per-stage ledger is
\[
 O\!\left(\left[K(1+|\supp s|)+\sum_k\vol(\supp z_k)
 +\vol(\supp\xb)+1\right]\operatorname{polylog}(N+2)\right).
\]
Final materialization visits only the old baseline and the union of emitted
kinetic supports, already charged by \eqref{eq:work}. Repair leaves support
volume at most $1/r$. Building the next source scans that repaired support.
Discarding obsolete state is charged to the same history. There is no
repeated scan of old primal coordinates during the iterations, hidden
restricted solve, or global terminal certificate.

The exposed records and cached adjacency entries are themselves bounded
by the charged history. Summing the ledger over the geometric schedule
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

All vertex-state maps and sets can use deterministic balanced comparison
trees, with the same permitted logarithmic factors. No expected-time hashing
bound is needed. The early exact rational Python prototype used dictionaries
for convenience. The standalone implementation uses deterministic AVL maps
and sets; its structural counters are not a claim about Python bit costs.

\section{Verification record and scope}
Separate fresh proof files contain the single-comparator metric argument,
the selected squared-flow reduction, the approximate repair, and independent
audits of their composition. Earlier exact prototypes checked the lazy
state representation and mass-waterfilling against full rational iterations.
The enlarged box and sparse-source reporter passed an independent exact suite
of 6,250 projection cases and 198 sparse-state checks. The complete
continuation wrapper passed 24 exact cases, 38 pre-repair and repaired-stage
comparisons, and 1,398 correction iterations. These include non-dyadic
parameters and an inactive degree-1,000 boundary whose adjacency list is
never scanned. No computational experiment is needed by the mathematical
theorem.

The theorem above is an arithmetic-word result. The following appendix
supplies a separate bounded-arithmetic extension for rational inputs,
including directed rounding, scalar rescaling, and coefficient lengths.
A successful exact Fraction test alone is not a bit-complexity or
production-runtime guarantee.
