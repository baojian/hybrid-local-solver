## Proof dependencies and scope qualifications

The source-to-result chain is explicit: graph/Stieltjes facts and KKT → a computably repaired diffuse baseline → Euclidean objective contraction and the second-metric response bound → selected signed-flow support-volume accounting → the local reporter and geometric continuation. The use of the optimum, its half-regularization support, and `Q^{-1}s` is analytical only. None appears as an oracle call or an uncharged preprocessing step. The extra safe-underestimate and support guarantees are conclusions, not additional input assumptions.

The baseline repair tolerance in the main proof, the improved projected-gradient repair tolerance in `app:pg-repair`, and the rounded terminal tolerance in `app:final-budgets` are different sufficient budgets for their respective algorithms. They all certify the same final original objective gap. In particular, an optional checkpoint in `app:checkpoints` returns only when the independent certificate meets the current stage objective tolerance; otherwise it falls back to the proved schedule. `app:independent-gap` is a sufficient objective-gap certificate derived from an original-objective subgradient. An inconclusive residual certificate does not replace or weaken OP2.

The stronger mass-deficit bound in `app:strong-work` and `app:rounded-deficit` supplements the original worst-case bound. The quantity `eta_*(rho)` is used to express the analysis; the algorithm uses the exactly known baseline/source mass. No optimum-mass or total-volume query is assumed. The optional singleton check in `app:singleton` verifies an exact seed-only KKT solution and otherwise executes the complete fallback with both costs charged; it is not a singleton-support restriction on the result.

## Three distinct arithmetic/performance claims

1. **Exact-real theorem:** arbitrary real `alpha`, `rho`, and positive objective tolerance within the canonical ranges, in the source's stipulated unit-cost arithmetic word model. This is the direct affirmative OP2 result.
2. **Bounded rational realization:** `app:refinements`, `app:error-model`, `app:grid-block`, `app:final-budgets`, and `app:explicit-complexity` require rational encoded numerical inputs and implement controlled dyadic rounding with exact integer arithmetic. For the default wrappers, schedules 232–255 state word/arithmetic `O(L^3/(rho sqrt(alpha)))`, adjacency `O(L/(rho sqrt(alpha)))`, and a conservative bit-arithmetic `O(B^3 L^3/(rho sqrt(alpha)))`, with parameter/encountered-graph encoding lengths included in `B`. Integer quotient/remainder is explicitly separated from the arbitrary-real model. Extra internal work of a user-provided graph oracle is also explicitly additional. Arbitrarily fine manually supplied grids or extra manual stepping/materialization are not silently covered by the default-wrapper bound.
3. **Practical performance:** exact arithmetic tests and saved timings can support implementation correctness and measured performance on their cases. They do not establish numerical stability of ordinary fixed-precision floating point, guarantee a particular runtime on every graph, or prove superiority to every existing solver. The main scope paragraph and refinements 519–527 make this separation explicit. No such production claim is necessary to answer OP2.

**Finding:** I found no scope mismatch, missing canonical parameter case, graph-class narrowing, or hidden input-oracle assumption in the reviewed statements. No change is required for source conformity. The final public claim should retain the words “deterministic,” “additive objective gap,” and “fully charged local work in the stated exact-real model,” with the bounded-rational implementation and empirical performance reported separately.
