\subsection{Source-aligned notation}
\label{subsec:source-aligned-notation}

This section follows the notation of
\citet{fountoulakis2026complexity}. In particular, vectors and matrices are
written as plain italic symbols \(x,s,A,D,Q\), as in that paper, rather than
using the boldface convention employed elsewhere in this manuscript. We record
the source convention completely so that later algorithms can state any
translation explicitly.

The source uses \(\varepsilon\) in two different contexts: as an objective-gap
target in its theoretical bounds and as a proximal fixed-point residual
tolerance in its experiments. We retain both definitions below, but do not
identify them. The repository has not yet adopted either one as its canonical
stopping rule.

\paragraph{Sets, graph, and volume.}
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be an undirected, unweighted graph with
\(V=[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). For \(S\subseteq V\), the notation is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \(|S|\) is the cardinality of \(S\), and
    \(S^c:=[n]\setminus S\) is its complement;
    \item \(\mathcal{N}(S)\) is the set of vertices adjacent to at least one
    vertex in \(S\);
    \item
    \[
        \partial S
        :=
        \mathcal{N}(S)\setminus S,
        \qquad
        \operatorname{Ext}(S)
        :=
        V\setminus(S\cup\partial S)
    \]
    are the vertex boundary and exterior of \(S\);
    \item \(d_i:=|\mathcal{N}(\{i\})|\) is the degree of vertex \(i\), with
    \(d_i>0\) for every \(i\);
    \item
    \[
        \vol(S):=\sum_{i\in S}d_i,
        \qquad
        d_{\min,\partial S}:=\min_{j\in\partial S}d_j
    \]
    are the degree volume of \(S\) and the minimum boundary degree.
\end{itemize}

The adjacency matrix is \(A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\),
\(D:=\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\) is the diagonal degree matrix, and
\(I\in\R^{n\times n}\) is the identity matrix. All optimization vectors are
column vectors in \(\R^n\). The support of a vector is
\[
    \supp(x):=\{i\in[n]:x_i\neq 0\}.
\]
We use \(\norm{\cdot}_2\), \(\norm{\cdot}_1\), and
\(\norm{\cdot}_\infty\) for the Euclidean, \(\ell_1\), and maximum norms,
respectively. Matrix inequalities such as \(M\preceq N\) use the Loewner
order.

\paragraph{Seed and PageRank matrices.}
The seed distribution satisfies
\[
    s\in\R_+^n,
    \qquad
    \langle\mathbf{1},s\rangle=1.
\]
The single-seed specialization is \(s=e_v\), where \(e_v\) is the \(v\)-th
standard basis vector. For a teleportation parameter
\(\alpha\in(0,1]\), define the symmetric normalized Laplacian and the shifted
PageRank matrix by
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    :=
    I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2},
    \qquad
    Q
    :=
    \alpha I+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\mathcal{L}
    =
    \frac{1+\alpha}{2}I
    -
    \frac{1-\alpha}{2}D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}.
    \label{eq:source-pagerank-matrices}
\end{equation}
Since \(0\preceq\mathcal{L}\preceq2I\),
\[
    \alpha I\preceq Q\preceq I.
\]

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The \(\ell_1\)-regularized PageRank problem}
{The l1-regularized PageRank problem}}
\label{subsec:source-rppr}

The smooth PageRank quadratic is
\begin{equation}
    f(x)
    :=
    \frac{1}{2}\langle x,Qx\rangle
    -
    \alpha\langle D^{-1/2}s,x\rangle,
    \qquad
    \nabla f(x)=Qx-\alpha D^{-1/2}s.
    \label{equ:f(x)}
\end{equation}
For a sparsity parameter \(\rho>0\), define
\[
    g_\rho(x)
    :=
    \alpha\rho\norm{D^{1/2}x}_1.
\]
The source often abbreviates \(g_\rho\) as \(g\). Its central
\(\ell_1\)-regularized personalized PageRank problem is
\begin{equation}
    \min_{x\in\R^n} F_\rho(x),
    \qquad
    F_\rho(x)
    :=
    f(x)+g_\rho(x).
    \label{equ:F(x)}
\end{equation}

The matrix bound in \cref{eq:source-pagerank-matrices} makes \(f\)
\(1\)-smooth and \(\alpha\)-strongly convex. Consequently \(F_\rho\) has a
unique minimizer, denoted by
\[
    x^\star(\rho)
    :=
    \argmin_{x\in\R^n}F_\rho(x).
\]
When \(\rho\) is fixed, we abbreviate \(x^\star(\rho)\) as \(x^\star\), and
write
\[
    S^\star
    :=
    \supp(x^\star),
    \qquad
    I^\star
    :=
    [n]\setminus S^\star.
\]
The source records that \(x^\star(\rho)\geq0\) coordinatewise and invokes the
sparsity guarantee
\(\vol(S^\star)\leq1/\rho\)
\citep{fountoulakis2019variational,fountoulakis2026complexity}.

The coordinatewise optimality conditions used by the source are
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_i f\bigl(x^\star(\rho)\bigr)
    \in
    \begin{cases}
        \{-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\},
            & x_i^\star(\rho)>0,\\
        [-\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},0],
            & x_i^\star(\rho)=0.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:source-rppr-kkt}
\end{equation}
For the initialization \(x_0=0\), the initial objective gap is
\[
    \Delta_0
    :=
    F_\rho(0)-F_\rho(x^\star)
    \leq
    \frac{\alpha}{2}.
\]

\subsection{Composite optimization and FISTA notation}
\label{subsec:source-fista-notation}

For a generic composite objective \(F=f+g\), \(L>0\) is the Euclidean
smoothness constant of \(f\), and \(\mu>0\) is the Euclidean
strong-convexity constant of \(F\). For RPPR,
\[
    L=1,
    \qquad
    \mu=\alpha,
    \qquad
    \eta:=\frac{1}{L}=1,
    \qquad
    \beta
    :=
    \frac{\sqrt{L/\mu}-1}{\sqrt{L/\mu}+1}
    =
    \frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha}}{1+\sqrt{\alpha}}.
\]

For any proper convex function \(g\), its scaled proximal operator is
\[
    \prox_{\eta g}(x)
    :=
    \argmin_{y\in\R^n}
    \left\{
        \eta g(y)+\frac{1}{2}\norm{y-x}_2^2
    \right\}.
\]
Starting from \(x_{-1}=x_0=0\), the source writes strongly-convex FISTA as
\begin{equation}
    y_k
    =
    x_k+\beta(x_k-x_{k-1}),
    \qquad
    x_{k+1}
    =
    \prox_{\eta g}\bigl(y_k-\eta\nabla f(y_k)\bigr),
    \qquad
    k\geq0.
    \label{eq:source-fista}
\end{equation}
For \(g=g_\rho\), the coordinate update is the weighted
soft-thresholding rule
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+1,i}
    =
    \sign\bigl(y_{k,i}-\eta\nabla_i f(y_k)\bigr)
    \max\left\{
        \left|y_{k,i}-\eta\nabla_i f(y_k)\right|
        -
        \eta\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},
        0
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:source-fista-coordinate}
\end{equation}
ISTA is the specialization \(y_k=x_k\), equivalently \(\beta=0\).

\subsection{Degree-weighted work and source stopping rule}
\label{subsec:source-work-and-stopping}

The source charges \(d_i\) work whenever the neighborhood of vertex \(i\) is
scanned. For an iterate pair \((y_k,x_{k+1})\), its symmetric per-iteration
work measure and cumulative work are
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{work}_k
    :=
    \vol(\supp(y_k))
    +
    \vol(\supp(x_{k+1})),
    \qquad
    \operatorname{Work}(N)
    :=
    \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{work}_k.
    \label{eq:source-degree-work}
\end{equation}
This counts neighbor accesses with repetition and is distinct from iteration
count.

The source experiments use the unit-step proximal-gradient map and its
fixed-point residual
\begin{equation}
    T_{\alpha,\rho}(x)
    :=
    \prox_{g_\rho}\bigl(x-\nabla f(x)\bigr),
    \qquad
    r_{\mathrm{pg}}(x)
    :=
    \norm{x-T_{\alpha,\rho}(x)}_\infty.
    \label{eq:source-fixed-point-residual}
\end{equation}
They stop when \(r_{\mathrm{pg}}(x_k)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pg}}\).
We add the subscript ``pg'' to distinguish this experimental tolerance from
the theoretical objective-gap target
\[
    F_\rho(x_N)-F_\rho(x^\star)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obj}}.
\]
The source itself writes both quantities as \(\varepsilon\).

\subsection{Over-regularization and locality notation}
\label{subsec:source-locality-notation}

The source compares the base problem (A), with regularization \(\rho\), and
the over-regularized problem (B), with regularization \(2\rho\):
\[
    g_A(x)
    :=
    \alpha\rho\norm{D^{1/2}x}_1,
    \qquad
    g_B(x)
    :=
    2\alpha\rho\norm{D^{1/2}x}_1,
\]
\[
    F_A:=f+g_A,
    \qquad
    F_B:=f+g_B,
    \qquad
    x_A^\star\in\argmin_xF_A(x),
    \qquad
    x_B^\star\in\argmin_xF_B(x).
\]
Their active and inactive sets are
\[
    S_A:=\supp(x_A^\star),
    \qquad
    S_B:=\supp(x_B^\star),
    \qquad
    I_B:=[n]\setminus S_B.
\]
The spurious active set of FISTA relative to the base support is
\[
    \widetilde{A}_k
    :=
    \supp(x_{k+1})\cap S_A^c.
\]
With this notation, the source decomposes degree-weighted work as
\[
    \operatorname{Work}(N)
    =
    O\left(
        N\vol(S_A)
        +
        \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\vol(\widetilde{A}_k)
    \right).
\]

For the forward-gradient map
\[
    u(x):=x-\eta\nabla f(x),
\]
the (B)-problem soft-threshold and the degree-normalized complementarity slack
of an inactive coordinate \(i\in I_B\) are
\[
    \lambda_i^{(B)}
    :=
    2\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i},
    \qquad
    \gamma_i^{(B)}
    :=
    \frac{
        \lambda_i^{(B)}
        -
        |\nabla_i f(x_B^\star)|
    }{\sqrt{d_i}}
    =
    2\alpha\rho
    +
    \frac{\nabla_i f(x_B^\star)}{\sqrt{d_i}}.
\]
For the base problem, the analogous threshold is
\(\lambda_i^{(A)}:=\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\).
The superscripts on \(\lambda_i^{(A)}\) and \(\lambda_i^{(B)}\) are added here
to keep the two problems distinct; the source writes \(\lambda_i\) and lets
the active problem determine whether the threshold is
\(\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\) or \(2\alpha\rho\sqrt{d_i}\).
The global inactive-set margin sometimes used in the source is
\[
    \gamma
    :=
    \min_{i\in I^\star}\gamma_i.
\]

The source further defines
\[
    A_{\mathrm{spur}}(y)
    :=
    \supp\left(
        \prox_{\eta g_B}\bigl(u(y)\bigr)
    \right)
    \cap I_B,
\]
\[
    I_B^{\mathrm{small}}
    :=
    \left\{
        i\in I_B:
        \gamma_i^{(B)}<\rho\alpha
    \right\},
    \qquad
    I_B^{\mathrm{large}}
    :=
    \left\{
        i\in I_B:
        \gamma_i^{(B)}\geq\rho\alpha
    \right\}.
\]
The source denotes \(A_{\mathrm{spur}}(y)\) simply by \(A(y)\); the renamed
version here avoids collision with the adjacency matrix \(A\).

A confinement set \(\mathcal{B}\) satisfies
\(\widetilde{A}_k\subseteq\mathcal{B}\) for every \(k\). For a candidate core
\(S\supseteq S_B\), the principal choice is
\[
    \mathcal{B}:=\partial S,
    \qquad
    \supp(x_k)\subseteq S\cup\partial S.
\]
Thus the notation distinguishes the core \(S\), its boundary
\(\mathcal{B}\), and its exterior \(\operatorname{Ext}(S)\).

\subsection{Auxiliary symbols used in the source analysis}
\label{subsec:source-auxiliary-notation}

For completeness, the remaining recurring analytical symbols in
\citet{fountoulakis2026complexity} are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \(b:=\alpha D^{-1/2}s\), so that
    \(\nabla f(x)=Qx-b\);
    \item \(q:=1-\sqrt{\mu/L}=1-\sqrt{\alpha}\), the geometric contraction
    factor used in the FISTA proof;
    \item
    \[
        N_{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obj}}}
        :=
        \left\lceil
            \frac{
                \log(\Delta_0/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obj}})
            }{
                \log\bigl(1/(1-\sqrt{\mu/L})\bigr)
            }
        \right\rceil,
    \]
    the sufficient iteration count for the stated objective-gap target;
    \item
    \[
        M
        :=
        \frac{8\Delta_0}{\mu}
        \left(
            (1+\beta)^2(1-\sqrt{\mu/L})+\beta^2
        \right),
    \]
    an iterate-distance constant in the source proof;
    \item \(\mathcal{B}'\subseteq\mathcal{B}\), the subset of confined
    coordinates whose (B)-margin is at least \(\rho\alpha\);
    \item \(R\), any uniform bound satisfying
    \(\norm{y_k-x_B^\star}_2\leq R\);
    \item \(\Delta x:=x_A^\star-x_B^\star\), used in the
    regularization-path comparison;
    \item \(w_k:=y_k-\eta\nabla f(y_k)=u(y_k)\), an alternative name for the
    forward point.
    \item \(\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}(S)\), used only in the comparison
    with prior work, is the number of edges in the subgraph induced by \(S\).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{High-degree cutoff.}
The source uses \(R\) in the sufficient nonactivation condition
\[
    d_i
    \geq
    \left(\frac{LR}{\alpha\rho}\right)^2.
\]
For RPPR, \(L\leq1\) and the source obtains \(R\leq\sqrt{20}\), giving the
more concrete cutoff \(d_i\geq20\alpha^{-2}\rho^{-2}\).

\paragraph{Breakpoint examples.}
The source uses two short graph families to show that the global margin
\(\gamma\) can vanish.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item In the center-seeded star, \(G\) has \(m+1\) vertices, center \(c\)
    with \(d_c=m\), leaves \(\ell\) with \(d_\ell=1\), and seed \(s=e_c\).
    It defines
    \[
        \rho_0:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2m},
        \qquad
        x^\star:=x_c^\star e_c,
        \qquad
        x_c^\star
        :=
        \frac{2\alpha(1-\rho m)}
        {(1+\alpha)\sqrt{m}},
    \]
    and the leaf slack
    \[
        \gamma_\ell
        :=
        \frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}(\rho-\rho_0).
    \]
    \item In the endpoint-seeded path,
    \(G=P_{m+1}\) has vertices \(1,\ldots,m+1\), seed \(s=e_1\), and
    \[
        \rho_0:=\frac{1-\alpha}{3+\alpha},
        \qquad
        x^\star:=x_1^\star e_1,
        \qquad
        x_1^\star
        :=
        \frac{2\alpha(1-\rho)}{1+\alpha}.
    \]
    The degree-normalized slack at vertex \(2\) is
    \[
        \gamma_2
        :=
        \frac{\alpha(3+\alpha)}
        {2(1+\alpha)}(\rho-\rho_0).
    \]
\end{itemize}
In both families, \(\gamma=0\) at \(\rho=\rho_0\).

\paragraph{Leaf-seeded star lower bound.}
In the source's lower-bound construction, \(G(m)\) has \(n=m+1\) vertices:
center \(w\), seed leaf \(v\), and remaining leaves
\(u_1,\ldots,u_{m-1}\). Here
\[
    \rho_0
    :=
    \frac{1-\alpha}
    {m(1+\alpha)+(1-\alpha)},
    \qquad
    x^\star
    :=
    x_v^\star e_v,
    \qquad
    x_v^\star
    :=
    \frac{2\alpha(1-\rho)}{1+\alpha}.
\]
At the breakpoint \(\rho=\rho_0\), the proof also names the one-step
candidate
\[
    x^+
    :=
    \prox_{\alpha\rho_0\norm{D^{1/2}\cdot}_1}\bigl(u(y)\bigr).
\]
The symbols
\[
    e_k:=x_{k,v}-x_v^\star,
    \qquad
    \widetilde e_k:=y_{k,v}-x_v^\star,
    \qquad
    a:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
\]
denote the one-dimensional seed-coordinate errors and recurrence coefficient.
The lower-bound objective-gap threshold is
\[
    \varepsilon_0(\alpha)
    :=
    \frac{
        \alpha^3(1-\alpha)^4\beta^4
    }{
        2(3+\alpha)^2
    }.
\]
These instance-specific symbols are not part of the generic RPPR definition,
but including them makes the catalogue cover the paper's proof-local
notation as well as its main formulation.
