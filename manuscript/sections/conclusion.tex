Classical APPR has worst-case degree-weighted work
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\epsappr))\), even on an unweighted center-seeded star and
independently of the legal active-vertex ordering. The construction isolates
the mass-processing and high-degree rescanning mechanisms that accelerated and
hybrid local solvers must overcome.

For regularized PageRank, the original full-batch ISTA also admits an exact
algorithm-specific characterization.  Thresholded coordinate ISTA removes
the batch method's stale-scan logarithm, and the resulting coordinate method
and its coarse-to-fine batch hybrid both have tight
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\rho))\) worst-case work at fixed relative accuracy.  This
is our first fully matched local hybrid result, but it does not improve the
dependence on \(\alpha\).

The coarse-forward-push/SOR analysis explains why changing only the handoff
scale is insufficient.  Its monotone phase is tight at the desired balancing
scale, while the fixed-relaxation FIFO tail revisits a long spider in a
triangular wave and pays \(\Omega(1/(\alpha\epsppr))\).  A square-root
improvement therefore requires a different tail mechanism---for example
nonbacktracking state, elimination on an explored tree, varying polynomial
relaxation, or an accelerated continuation rule that controls active volume.
An algorithm-independent local-oracle lower bound and a graph-uniform
\(\widetilde O(1/(\rho\sqrt\alpha))\) matching method remain open.
