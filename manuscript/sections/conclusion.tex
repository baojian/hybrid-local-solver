The accelerated local running-time targets for personalized PageRank
approximation and regularized PageRank optimization can be attained in one
framework. The explicit bias and objective-error conversion transfers the
RPPR algorithms to degree-normalized PPR accuracy. Two mechanisms provide
the RPPR work bound. Deterministic continuation controls the
cumulative support volume of a constrained accelerated trajectory using two
comparison energies and a signed-flow argument. Randomized threshold
batching controls the number of complete support-safe principal systems
through a block-Cholesky decay theorem. Both mechanisms include discovery,
repeated numerical work, boundary maintenance, certification, and sparse
output in the running-time bound.

The deterministic construction also admits a bounded-arithmetic realization.
Its proof uses directed rounding and controlled response errors, so exact
threshold equalities and arbitrarily small positive coordinates do not
create an unaccounted numerical search. This extension is a correctness and
complexity statement for the specified rounded algorithm. Establishing a
useful fixed-precision implementation and comparing practical constants
remain separate tasks; this paper makes no empirical speed claim.

Several questions remain. The randomized bound retains a minimum between
support-cardinality and spectral-depth estimates; an equally sharp
support-adaptive deterministic bound would further clarify their tradeoff.
Reusing numerical work across nested faces may improve the randomized
algorithm beyond repeated nearly-linear solves, but must also maintain all
boundary information locally. More broadly, the optimal joint dependence
on regularization, teleportation, and accuracy is not determined by the
present upper bounds. The locality counterexamples for unmodified
accelerated methods also leave room to characterize precisely which
constraints or graph structures make classical recurrences efficient.
