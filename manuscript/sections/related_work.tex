\paragraph{Local PageRank and variational locality.}
The push method of \citet{andersen2007using} made PPR a canonical local graph
primitive: its work is controlled by residual mass and a degree-normalized
threshold rather than by the ambient number of edges.  Variational RPPR
recasts locality as weighted $\ell_1$ regularization and proves the support
volume bound used here \citep{fountoulakis2019variational}.  Classical
first-order acceleration alone does not settle the local problem.  Momentum
can create spurious activity, and even a support-monotone method can revisit
an increasingly large face.  The COLT open problem of
\citet{fountoulakis2022open} asks for a square-root dependence on the PPR
condition parameter without losing the sparse regularized structure.

\paragraph{From accelerated faces to cheap faces to few faces.}
The closest sequence of results is more informative than a flat list of
complexities.  \citet{martinezrubio2023accelerated} introduced a sparse
accelerated active-set algorithm: it keeps the active set inside the optimal
support and applies accelerated optimization on successive restricted
problems.  Under its internal-volume convention, the stated leading work is
\[
 \softO\!\left(
   \frac{|\mathcal S_\rho^*|\,\widetilde{\vol}(\mathcal S_\rho^*)}
        {\sqrt\alpha}
   +|\mathcal S_\rho^*|\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*)
 \right).
\]
An endpoint-path audit shows that the factor $|\mathcal S_\rho^*|$ is real
for the literal repeated-face algorithm.  For sufficiently small $\rho$, the
optimal support is the whole path, while every proper seed prefix has only
the next path vertex on its boundary; exact positive-residual expansion
therefore adds one coordinate per stage and accumulates quadratic prefix
volume.  This does not make the factor an information-theoretic barrier.

\citet{wei2026simple} take the next step.  They preserve true-support
activation but solve every supplied degree-scaled face with a nearly-linear
SDD method, obtaining
$\softO(|\mathcal S_\rho^*|\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*))$ RPPR work in the
comparable unweighted setting.  This removes the explicit
$1/\sqrt\alpha$ inner-iteration factor from each face, but the outer method
may still rebuild and rescan $|\mathcal S_\rho^*|$ faces.

There is also an exact primal--dual correspondence between their residual
test and our flow language.  In the non-lazy convention of
\citet{wei2026simple}, with $L_a=D-(1-a)A$ and
$r(x)=e_s-a^{-1}L_ax$, the slack of the corresponding grounded-flow vertex
constraint is
\begin{equation}
 L_ax-a(e_s-\rho d)=a(\rho d-r(x)).
 \label{eq:wei-yang-dual-slack}
\end{equation}
Hence their activation test $r(x^{(S)})(v)>\rho d(v)$ is exactly a violated
dual inequality.  Their paper states and exploits the residual/KKT form in
its RPPR support argument, but it does not introduce a flow variable, a dual
energy objective, or the grounded-flow optimization problem.  Our explicit
dual therefore clarifies the separation structure; the new complexity claim
remains the threshold-batch theorem, not the standard act of taking a
Fenchel dual.

Our result changes the outer counting unit.  Threshold admission plus the
Cholesky-decay theorem bounds the number of complete exposed faces by
$\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$, and each face is solved in nearly-linear local
volume.  The progression is summarized in \cref{tab:lineage}; logarithms and
the papers' slightly different internal-volume definitions are suppressed.

This comparison also locates the novelty.  The randomized SDD primitive is
not ours, and randomization itself is not the contribution.  We use the
supplied-matrix solver of \citet{koutis2011nearly} as a black box.  The new
ingredient is the threshold-batch depth theorem and its integration with
support-safe, residual-certified local discovery.  The same distinction
applies to Wei--Yang: their innovation is the simple active-set reduction and
the use of nearly-linear supplied-face solves, not the invention of the SDD
solver.

\paragraph{Obstacle and complementarity methods.}
The RPPR KKT system is a monotone linear complementarity problem with a
Stieltjes matrix.  Classical principal-pivot, projected-gradient, policy-
iteration, and reduced-space conjugate-gradient methods give finite
termination or fast convergence on a supplied global problem.  Their usual
complexities count global tests, full policy systems, or one solve per pivot;
they do not provide adjacency-list locality or an output-volume bound.  Our
grounded-flow dual is likewise an instance of standard Fenchel duality, and
primal--dual active-set methods are classical for obstacle problems
\citep{karkkainen2003augmented}.  We do not claim either fact as new.  Our
safe-pivot lemma uses the same inverse-positivity geometry, while the local
dual separation interface, threshold-batch depth, and boundary charging are
specific to the PageRank setting.

\paragraph{AESP--CD and the two-stage viewpoint.}
The present algorithm grew from attempts to continue AESP/CD-style
acceleration across a changing active set.  The final proof takes a cleaner
route: it rebuilds a certified SDD state on only
$\softO(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ faces and carries no momentum across a batch.  It is
therefore best viewed as a threshold-batched active-set lane compatible with
an AESP--CD portfolio, not as a randomized proof of the original momentum
recurrence.  The strict handoff is similarly modular: Stage~II may be a
principal SDD solve for the theorem, while SOR, APPR, or a structural direct
solver can be raced as implementation choices without changing the Stage~I
certificate.

\paragraph{Randomization and derandomization.}
All support admissions in our method are deterministic consequences of a
checked residual inequality.  Randomness only determines whether a proposed
face solve passes that check within a capped number of trials.  Hence a
constant-failure run followed by a full restart is Las Vegas in the
exact-real model.  Ordinary deterministic CG immediately gives a weaker
$\softO(1/(\rho\alpha))$ route.  Obtaining the same nearly-linear face cost
deterministically, together with finite-precision and coefficient-bit
guarantees, is outside the present theorem.
