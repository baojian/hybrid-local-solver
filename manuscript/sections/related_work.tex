Andersen, Chung, and Lang introduced the lazy APPR push operation and proved
that its degree-weighted work is at most
\(1/(\alpha\epsappr)\) through a residual-mass argument
\citep{andersen2007using}. Some later local-solver work describes this
dependence as \(\Theta(1/(\alpha\epsappr))\); for example,
\citet{zhou2024iterative} uses that notation while restating the classical
one-sided mass-decrease argument. Our
\cref{thm:appr-star-lower-bound} supplies the explicit matching instance and
proves the lower bound directly in the same adjacency-list work model.

The APPR stopping rule is algorithmic: it terminates when every residual
coordinate is smaller than \(\epsappr d_u\). By contrast, variational
PageRank methods express locality through an \(\ell_1\)-regularized objective
\citep{fountoulakis2019variational}. We keep these parameters distinct, but
\cref{prop:appr-rppr-bridge} shows that APPR's exact target, after symmetric
degree scaling, is the unregularized member of the RPPR family used in
\cref{sec:problem-formulation}.
