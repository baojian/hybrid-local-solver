Local graph algorithms aim to answer seed-centered questions without scanning
an entire graph. Approximate personalized PageRank (APPR) is the canonical
example: its residual-push implementation has degree-weighted work
\(\bigO(1/(\alpha\epsappr))\), independent of the total graph size
\citep{andersen2007using}. This guarantee is also the natural baseline for
local optimization methods that seek a better dependence on the PageRank
parameter \(\alpha\).

We first make that baseline precise. In
\cref{thm:appr-star-lower-bound}, we give a center-seeded star on which every
legal ordering of APPR pushes incurs
\(\Omega(1/(\alpha\epsappr))\) adjacency-list work. Together with the classical
upper bound, this proves a tight worst-case complexity of
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\epsappr))\). The proof separates the obstruction into two
parts: APPR must process \(\Omega(1/\alpha)\) cumulative residual mass, and the
star forces a constant fraction of that mass through a vertex of degree
\(\Theta(1/\epsappr)\). These mechanisms clarify what an accelerated or hybrid
local solver must change to improve the worst-case bound.

We next turn to the variational, \(\ell_1\)-regularized PageRank problem.  For
the original full-batch ISTA, we remove an unnecessary
\(\log(1/\alpha)\) from the published upper analysis and prove matching
lower bounds.  With general teleportation distributions, its exact worst-case
order is
\[
    \Theta\!\left(
      \frac{1}{\alpha\rho}
      \left[1+\log\frac{1}{\delta\rho}\right]
    \right),
\]
where \(\delta\) is the relative KKT accuracy.  The extra
\(\log(1/\rho)\) is a stale full-batch scanning cost, not an intrinsic
problem barrier.  A residual-thresholded coordinate implementation removes
it: for every fixed \(\delta\), both that method and a coordinate-to-batch
coarse-to-fine hybrid have exact worst-case work
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\rho))\).  Their matching lower bound is again a
center-seeded star and holds for every violating-coordinate ordering.

Our unregularized two-stage experiment combines coarse forward push with a
signed, optimally relaxed SOR tail.  The coarse phase has exact order
\(\Theta(1/(\alpha\tau))\) at tolerance \(\tau\), independently of the
active ordering.  This justifies the balancing scale
\(\tau=\Theta(\epsppr/\sqrt\alpha)\), but it does not make the complete
method uniformly accelerated.  A long spider forces
\(\Omega(1/(\alpha\epsppr))\) work for the fixed-relaxation FIFO tail.
Thus an effective hybrid must change the propagation mechanism, not only its
handoff tolerance.
