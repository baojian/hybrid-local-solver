Personalized PageRank assigns relevance to vertices near a seed through a
geometrically stopped random walk. Its local computation poses a basic
algorithmic question: can a well-conditioned numerical method operate at the
scale of the requested output, even when the input graph is much larger?
The classical push algorithm provides one answer. It computes a sparse
approximation using $O(1/(\alpha\epsappr))$ degree-weighted work, where
$\alpha$ is the teleportation parameter and $\epsappr$ is a normalized
residual tolerance \citep{andersen2006local,andersen2007using}. Its updates
are local because each activated vertex carries enough residual mass to pay
for the incident edges it scans.

Acceleration offers a tempting improvement. The symmetric PageRank system
has spectrum in $[\alpha,1]$, so global accelerated gradient, conjugate
gradient, and Chebyshev methods need only a square-root dependence on
$1/\alpha$ in their iteration counts. Yet even one full matrix-vector
product can read the entire graph. In a local algorithm, the meaningful
quantity is the sum of the costs of all updates and discoveries. A short
iteration sequence and a sparse final answer do not bound that sum.

\paragraph{The regularized PageRank question.}
Weighted $\ell_1$ regularization turns this issue into a sparse convex
optimization problem. For a seed vertex $v$, consider
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\vx\in\R^n}
 F_\rho(\vx):=
 \tfrac12\vx^\trans\mQ\vx-\alpha\eunit{v}^\trans\mD^{-1/2}\vx
           +\alpha\rho\norm{\mD^{1/2}\vx}_1,
 \qquad
 \mQ=\alpha\mI+\tfrac{1-\alpha}{2}\bm{\mathcal L}.
 \label{eq:intro-rppr}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{\mathcal L}$ is the symmetric normalized graph Laplacian and
$\mD$ contains the original degrees. The optimum is nonnegative and its
support has degree volume at most $1/\rho$
\citep{fountoulakis2019variational}. Local proximal gradient exploits this
structure in $\widetilde O(1/(\alpha\rho))$ work. At COLT 2022,
\citet{fountoulakis2022open} proposed the accelerated running-time target
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(\frac1{\rho\sqrt\alpha}\right),
 \label{eq:intro-target}
\end{equation}
with only logarithmic dependence on optimization accuracy and no dependence
on the ambient graph size.

We attain this target deterministically and randomly in a fully charged
local model. The deterministic result uses an accelerated constrained
correction method with regularization continuation. The randomized result
uses threshold-batched active sets and certified nearly-linear SDD solves.
These are two different mechanisms for achieving the same running-time
scale. The conclusion is an algorithmic existence result for regularized
PageRank. The original motivation also asked whether familiar accelerated
iterations preserve small supports. Standard FISTA can fail that property
\citep{fountoulakis2026complexity}; our theorem leaves that negative result
intact.

\paragraph{What prevented earlier analyses from reaching this bound?}
There are two distinct costs to control. Momentum may activate vertices
outside the optimal support, including vertices of very large degree.
Alternatively, a support-safe method may repeatedly solve the problem on a
growing sequence of faces. The accelerated sparse method of
\citet{martinezrubio2023accelerated} controls the first difficulty, but its
bound includes the number of support discoveries. The recent method of
\citet{wei2026simple} makes each supplied face nearly linear-time using an
SDD solver; it retains a bound proportional to the number of those faces.
Our deterministic proof controls the cumulative cost of the actual
accelerated trajectory. Our randomized proof bounds how many complete
faces require numerical processing.

\paragraph{Deterministic local acceleration.}
At regularization scale $r$, we maintain a certified baseline
$\overline\vx\leq\vx_r^*$ and a diffuse residual source
$\vh=\vb-\mQ\overline\vx$ satisfying
$\vzero\leq\vh\leq4\alpha r\mD^{1/2}\one$.
The unknown remaining correction lies in an explicit degree-scaled box
with a computable mass cap. We run a projected accelerated recurrence on
this set. The recurrence has a primal correction and a kinetic vector;
only nonzero kinetic coordinates require graph-row updates.

The analysis applies one accelerated comparison identity in two metrics.
In the Euclidean metric it proves objective convergence. In the PageRank
metric, the same projection satisfies a second comparison inequality,
thanks to the box, the mass cap, and the Stieltjes sign pattern. This gives
a uniform bound on the squared matrix response along the actual trajectory.
For the work analysis, the support of the less regularized optimum
$\vx_{r/2}^*$ serves as an analytical comparison set of volume at most
$2/r$. The algorithm never computes that set. Outside it, the KKT slack
has a uniform lower bound. A selected signed-flow telescope charges every
kinetic activation outside the set to the squared response and proves
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=1}^{K}\vol(\supp\vz_k)\leq76K/r.
 \label{eq:intro-kinetic-bound}
\end{equation}
This bound includes arbitrary excursions and repeated visits.

A lazy representation of the primal correction and a deterministic ordered
threshold reporter realize this charge without rescanning the entire primal
history each iteration. After a stage, one projected-gradient step followed
by a downward correction produces a safe baseline for the next smaller
regularizer. Each stage has $K=\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ iterations,
and the reciprocal regularizers sum geometrically to $O(1/\rho)$.
Together these facts give \cref{eq:intro-target}. No supplied-face linear
solver is used.

\paragraph{Randomized threshold batching.}
The same optimization problem is a nonnegative Stieltjes obstacle problem.
Every exactly solved reachable face lies below the optimum, and a negative
outside slack identifies a true support coordinate. We admit all violations
above a certified common threshold. Order the unknown optimal support by
these batches and factor its principal matrix in that order. A block
Cholesky argument turns the remaining objective gap into an elimination
vector's squared tail. A well-conditioned block chain, obtained by retaining
only neighboring Cholesky blocks, permits a Chebyshev inverse-decay estimate.
It yields $\widetilde O(1/\sqrt\alpha)$ batches independently of graph
diameter, optimal-support cardinality, and unknown complementarity margins.
The factorization is used in the proof; the algorithm does not construct it.

Each supplied principal system has $O(\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*))$ nonzeros
after degree scaling. A randomized SDD routine is checked by an exact local
residual certificate before any admission or output is accepted. Failed
certifications cause retries, giving a Las Vegas implementation. If
$k_*=|\mathcal S_\rho^*|$ and $V_*=\vol(\mathcal S_\rho^*)$, its expected
work satisfies the more informative bound
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde O\!\left(V_*\min\{k_*,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\right)
 \leq\widetilde O\!\left(
      \min\{\rho^{-2},\rho^{-1}\alpha^{-1/2}\}\right).
 \label{eq:intro-random-adaptive}
\end{equation}
The cardinality bound follows because a nonempty batch adds a new vertex.
This retains the small-support regime of the recent active-set analysis
while adding the accelerated envelope.

\paragraph{Computation and consequences.}
Both main results count first and repeated adjacency inspections, degree
queries, arithmetic, dictionaries, threshold maintenance, numerical state,
verification, and sparse output. They require no graph-wide preprocessing.
The baseline model is exact-real algebraic words. For the deterministic
algorithm, we additionally give a rounded rational implementation and prove
the same local operation bound, with explicit encoding factors in bit cost.
The rounded proof controls the stored trajectory and uses a finite threshold
search; it does not appeal to exact support identification after numerical
convergence.

Finally, a regularization-bias bound converts additive RPPR objective
accuracy into semantic PPR error. Both algorithms achieve
$\widetilde O(1/(\epsppr\sqrt\alpha))$ work for error
$\norm{\mD^{-1}(\widehat\vpi-\vpi)}_\infty\leq\epsppr$.
We also prove a separate inner-face transfer for applications that retain
only a discovered vertex set and then solve ordinary PPR on it. The main
scope is a point source on a finite connected simple unweighted undirected
graph. General seed inputs and their additional costs are discussed in
\cref{app:seed-scope}.
